# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND EVIDENCE-BASED SYNTHESIS REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report analyzes patent evidence relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) with emphasis on low-acrylonitrile embodiments, using the provided manifest patent US11347156B2 as the sole primary source. The patent is not a synthesis patent for NBR production; instead, it discloses an electro-conductive member architecture in which NBR is used as one of the diene-based rubbers in a matrix-domain rubber composite. The extracted evidence is still relevant to low-acrylonitrile NBR because it explicitly identifies several commercial NBR grades with nitrile contents spanning 15% to 41.5%, including a low-nitrile example at 15% and another at 18.0%, and it compares these materials in terms of solubility parameter, viscoelastic response, and electro-conductive member performance. The patent’s core technical teaching is that pairing different diene rubbers, including NBR and SBR, can create a matrix-domain structure with improved mechanical recovery and stable discharge behavior when the SP-value difference and tan δ ratio are controlled. The evidence also shows that NBR can be used either as the matrix or as the domain rubber, but the patent does not disclose NBR polymerization conditions, monomer feed ratios, initiator systems, or emulsion/solution synthesis routes. Accordingly, the report treats the patent as an indirect source for low-acrylonitrile NBR composition benchmarking rather than a direct synthesis disclosure. The major formulation trend is the use of NBR as a commercially supplied rubber grade with specified nitrile content, combined with carbon black, sulfur, and vulcanization accelerators to build electro-conductive layers. The major process trend is compounding and kneading rather than polymerization, with performance governed by rubber pairing, phase-separated morphology, and viscoelastic matching rather than by in situ NBR manufacture.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence base for this run does not include a true NBR synthesis patent; instead, it provides a formulation-oriented disclosure where NBR is used as a functional rubber phase in an electro-conductive electrophotographic member. The most useful low-acrylonitrile signal is the explicit disclosure of an NBR grade with 15% nitrile content, alongside other grades at 18.0%, 27.5%, 35%, 40.0%, and 41.5%. For low-acrylonitrile NBR work, the patent supports using commercially prepared NBR grades in the ~15–18% nitrile range when balancing elasticity, phase behavior, and electrical performance. No polymerization route, monomer feed ratio, or initiator system is disclosed in the extracted evidence.
