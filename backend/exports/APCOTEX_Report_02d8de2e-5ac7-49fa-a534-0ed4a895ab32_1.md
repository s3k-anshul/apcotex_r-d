# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS LANDSCAPE FROM PATENT EVIDENCE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), focusing on precursor NBR composition, hydrogenation routes, and process strategies that improve molecular-weight control, residual unsaturation, and manufacturability. Across the selected patents, the target material is consistently defined as a nitrile rubber derived from at least one conjugated diene and at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile, typically acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymers, which are then hydrogenated partially or selectively to reduce residual double bonds and improve heat and oil resistance. The landscape shows two major synthesis themes: (1) direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution or in specialized reactors using homogeneous catalysts, and (2) precursor engineering via solution polymerization, RAFT-mediated polymerization, or functionalized NBR architectures that are subsequently hydrogenated or coupled.

The hydrogenation patents emphasize catalyst choice and reactor/process intensification. US7666950B2 discloses hydrogenation in a spinning disk reactor with hydrogen pressure from 3.5 to 100 bar and broad catalyst families including rhodium- and ruthenium-based systems. EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 focus on simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis using ruthenium or osmium catalysts, enabling lower molecular weight HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distributions and low residual double bond content in a single step. These disclosures highlight the tradeoff between hydrogenation efficiency, metathesis-driven chain scission, and viscosity control. In contrast, EP2478021B1 and EP2554558A1 address the upstream NBR synthesis problem by solution polymerization in organic solvents or solvent mixtures, using controlled radical polymerization concepts and chain-transfer/modifier systems to tune molecular weight before hydrogenation. EP2623518A1 and US20140357801A1 extend this precursor-engineering approach by introducing alkyne- or tetrazole-functional RAFT-derived NBRs that can be post-coupled, with hydrogenation remaining an optional downstream step.

Overall, the evidence indicates that the most robust HNBR manufacturing strategies combine controlled NBR precursor synthesis, careful management of molecular weight and viscosity, and hydrogenation under catalysts/processes that preserve polymer integrity while achieving low residual unsaturation.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7666950B2 focuses on direct hydrogenation of NBR in a spinning disk reactor, while EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 use simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis to lower molecular weight during HNBR formation.
- Catalyst systems differ substantially: US7666950B2 broadly cites rhodium/ruthenium/titanium and other metals; EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 center on ruthenium/osmium metathesis-type catalysts; the precursor patents EP2478021B1, EP2554558A1, EP2623518A1, and US20140357801A1 rely on radical/RAFT polymerization rather than hydrogenation catalysts.
- Hydrogenation-process patents emphasize residual double bond reduction and viscosity control, with reported HNBR RDB values dropping to near zero in EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2; precursor patents instead emphasize monomer ratios, solvent systems, conversion, and molecular-weight control before hydrogenation.
- EP2478021B1 and EP2554558A1 both use acrylonitrile/butadiene solution polymerization, but EP2478021B1 highlights modifier-controlled synthesis in DMAc, whereas EP2554558A1 emphasizes mixed-solvent systems with a main solvent fraction of 70 to 99.9 vol%.
- EP2623518A1 and US20140357801A1 introduce functional RAFT chain-transfer agents to create alkyne- or tetrazole-functional NBRs, enabling post-polymer coupling; these are not direct HNBR synthesis routes but are relevant to precursor architecture and downstream property tuning.
- Across the hydrogenation patents, lower catalyst loading and shorter reaction times are associated with lower residual double bond content and lower or moderate Mooney viscosity, while higher or less selective catalyst systems can leave higher RDB and higher viscosity.
- Coagulation/isolation methods are explicitly described in the precursor patents as ethanol precipitation or solvent removal, whereas the hydrogenation patents focus more on reactor configuration and catalyst performance than on isolation chemistry.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that HNBR synthesis is best approached through either optimized direct hydrogenation of NBR or through precursor engineering followed by hydrogenation. The strongest direct HNBR evidence supports ruthenium-based or related homogeneous catalyst systems, with process intensification in a spinning disk reactor and hydrogen pressures in the 3.5 to 100 bar range. For simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes, the evidence favors ruthenium/osmium catalysts that can deliver low residual double bond content and molecular weights in the 20,000 to 250,000 range with Mooney viscosity between 1 and 50. For precursor synthesis, solution polymerization in organic solvents, often with RAFT or modifier control, is repeatedly used to tune molecular weight and functionality before hydrogenation. Based on the evidence, the most defensible synthesis strategy for HNBR is controlled NBR precursor preparation with acrylonitrile/butadiene compositions in the typical 15 to 40 wt% nitrile and 60 to 85 wt% diene range, followed by hydrogenation using a selective homogeneous catalyst system under conditions that minimize viscosity rise and preserve molecular-weight distribution.
