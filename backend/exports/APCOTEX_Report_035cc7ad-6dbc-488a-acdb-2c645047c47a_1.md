# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON NITRILE RUBBER METATHESIS AND COMPOSITION-CONTROLLED FEEDSTOCKS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews two primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) in the provided manifest, with emphasis on how low-ACN NBR feedstocks are handled in downstream processing rather than direct low-ACN copolymer synthesis. Both patents define NBR broadly as a copolymer or terpolymer of an α,β-unsaturated nitrile and a conjugated diene, optionally with additional copolymerizable monomers, and both focus on metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber prior to hydrogenation. The technical target in this landscape is not a new monomer recipe for low acrylonitrile incorporation, but rather the preparation of lower-molecular-weight NBR intermediates that can be hydrogenated to HNBR while preserving processability and avoiding excessive viscosity rise.

US7875683B2 discloses metathetic degradation of nitrile rubber using a metal-carbene metathesis catalyst combined with a transition-metal additive of general formula M(OZ)m, aiming to improve degradation efficiency and lower molecular weight. The patent emphasizes the challenge of producing sufficiently low-Mn NBR feedstocks for hydrogenation and notes practical limits around Mooney viscosity and work-up. EP2028194B1 discloses a related metathesis process using specific ruthenium/osmium complex catalysts, with comparative examples showing that a Grubbs III catalyst outperforms a Grubbs II catalyst at the same ruthenium loading and 1-hexene co-olefin level, giving lower Mw and narrower PDI in shorter times.

Across the two patents, the dominant process trend is olefin metathesis of NBR in the presence of a co-olefin, followed by hydrogenation, with catalyst selection being the main lever for improving activity, lowering molecular weight, and narrowing dispersity. No patent in the manifest directly discloses a low-acrylonitrile copolymerization recipe; instead, they provide enabling degradation/process technology for NBR grades that may include low-ACN feedstocks.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Both patents address NBR degradation by olefin metathesis rather than direct low-ACN copolymerization; neither discloses a monomer-feed recipe for lowering acrylonitrile incorporation.
- US7875683B2 uses a metathesis catalyst plus a transition-metal additive M(OZ)m, while EP2028194B1 uses defined ruthenium/osmium complex catalysts without the additive strategy.
- EP2028194B1 provides explicit example data with 1-hexene co-olefin, 40 °C reaction temperature, and catalyst loadings of 23 ppm and 57 ppm Ru; US7875683B2 provides no numbered examples or comparable operating data in the extracted evidence.
- EP2028194B1 demonstrates that Grubbs III outperforms Grubbs II at identical Ru loading, giving lower Mw and narrower PDI; US7875683B2 instead claims improved degradation through catalyst/additive synergy but without example-level comparison in the extraction.
- Both patents frame the practical target as lowering NBR Mooney viscosity and molecular weight before hydrogenation, with the background noting a feedstock Mooney viscosity around 30 and a degraded target below 30 ML 1+4 at 100 °C in US7875683B2.
- Co-olefin use is explicit in EP2028194B1 (1-hexene, 2.0 phr), while US7875683B2 mentions low molecular weight 1-olefins generally but does not provide a specific example in the extracted text.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence indicates that the most relevant synthesis route for low-acrylonitrile NBR in this manifest is not direct low-ACN monomer copolymerization, but metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of NBR feedstocks prior to hydrogenation. The strongest operational evidence comes from EP2028194B1, where a Grubbs III catalyst with 1-hexene at 40 °C and 23-57 ppm Ru gives substantially lower Mw and narrower PDI than Grubbs II. US7875683B2 supports the same general process direction and adds a transition-metal additive strategy to improve metathesis efficiency. Based on the evidence, the recommended process window is ruthenium-based metathesis catalysis, optional low-olefin co-olefin addition, and careful control of catalyst activity to drive NBR to below 30 Mooney units or below 70,000 g/mol Mn before hydrogenation.
