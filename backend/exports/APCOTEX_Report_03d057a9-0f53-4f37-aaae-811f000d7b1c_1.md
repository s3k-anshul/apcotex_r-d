# HYDROGENATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON HYDROGENATION, METATHESIS-ENABLED MOLECULAR WEIGHT CONTROL, AND HNBR-CONTAINING FORMULATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a 2010–2024 patent set relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR) and closely related HNBR-containing formulations. The target is defined as nitrile rubber precursors that are hydrogenated, partially hydrogenated, or highly saturated to reduce residual unsaturation and improve heat, ozone, and oil resistance while preserving processability and molecular weight retention. Across the selected patents, two major technical directions emerge. First, several patents focus on direct or indirect synthesis of HNBR from NBR by hydrogenation, including latex hydrogenation, solution/cement hydrogenation, and hydrogenation after metathesis-based chain scission to lower Mooney viscosity before saturation. These disclosures emphasize catalyst choice, hydrogen pressure, temperature, and the need to control residual double bond content and molecular weight distribution. Second, multiple patents use HNBR as a functional ingredient in downstream compositions rather than as the primary synthesis target, including swellable packers, electroconductive silver pastes, toothed belts, and battery electrode dispersants. These formulation patents are still relevant because they specify HNBR grades, hydrogenation levels, and processing windows that indirectly define useful HNBR property envelopes.

The landscape shows recurring use of ruthenium-based metathesis catalysts, often with salt additives or alkaline earth chlorides, to depolymerize NBR prior to hydrogenation and thereby obtain lower-Mooney HNBR with narrower molecular weight distribution. Other patents disclose conventional emulsion polymerization of NBR followed by palladium-catalyzed hydrogenation in latex or solution, with hydrogen pressures around 3 MPa and temperatures near 50°C in representative examples. In formulation-oriented patents, HNBR is selected for improved thermal stability, low residual unsaturation, and compatibility with fillers, SAPs, silver powders, or polyamide resins. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that combine controlled NBR polymerization, selective hydrogenation, and, where needed, metathesis-assisted molecular weight reduction to balance processability with final elastomer performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Two patents directly address HNBR precursor modification by metathesis before hydrogenation: US7951875B2 and US7737233B2. Both use ruthenium-based metathesis chemistry to reduce molecular weight, but US7951875B2 provides more concrete operating data (80°C, 0.05-0.25 phr catalyst, ethylene or 1-hexene co-olefin), while US7737233B2 emphasizes catalyst system design and broader process windows.
- Direct hydrogenation disclosures in EP2692788B1 use latex or solution hydrogenation with palladium catalysts at 3.0-3 MPa and 50°C, whereas the metathesis patents focus on pre-hydrogenation chain scission to lower Mooney viscosity.
- EP2692788B1 and EP1813835B1 both use highly saturated nitrile rubber in formulations, but EP2692788B1 targets polyamide-containing crosslinkable compositions and EP1813835B1 targets toothed belts with unsaturated carboxylic acid metal salts, silica, and short fibers.
- US10385647B2, US20180022951A1, EP3331072B1, EP1813835B1, and US10731020B2 are formulation patents rather than synthesis patents; they define useful HNBR property windows such as hydrogenation level, alkali metal content, and thermal stability in end-use systems.
- US20180022951A1 specifies HNBR with 50 wt% nitrile group and 25 mg/100 mg iodine value in a silver paste, while EP3331072B1 specifies partially hydrogenated nitrile rubber with 20-50 wt% hydrogenated conjugated diene repeat units for conductive dispersion.
- US10731020B2 is the least synthesis-relevant to HNBR, but it confirms that hydrogenated nitrile rubber is acceptable as a base rubber in vulcanization-molded lightweight articles alongside EPDM, NBR, and other elastomers.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports two practical HNBR synthesis strategies: direct hydrogenation of NBR precursors in latex or solution, and metathesis-assisted molecular weight reduction followed by hydrogenation. For direct hydrogenation, the clearest disclosed conditions are palladium-catalyzed hydrogenation at about 3.0-3 MPa and 50°C, with latex coagulation and drying after hydrogenation. For low-Mooney, processable HNBR, the strongest evidence favors ruthenium-catalyzed metathesis of NBR with a co-olefin, using catalyst loadings in the 0.05-0.25 phr range and temperatures around 80°C, followed by hydrogenation at 60-150°C. Across the formulation patents, useful HNBR grades are those with high hydrogenation, low residual unsaturation, low alkali metal content, and sufficient thermal stability for brine, battery, belt, and conductive paste applications. The recommended synthesis direction from the evidence is to control NBR feedstock composition first, then reduce molecular weight by metathesis if low Mooney viscosity is needed, and finally hydrogenate under moderate pressure and temperature to preserve molecular weight distribution and achieve the desired HNBR performance.
