# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND POST-PROCESSING PATENT LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary US patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR): one focused on removal of ruthenium catalyst residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber, and two focused on producing low molecular weight HNBR with narrower molecular weight distributions. Across the set, the target material is consistently defined as nitrile rubber derived from conjugated dienes and α,β-unsaturated nitriles that is selectively hydrogenated to improve heat, oil, and chemical resistance while retaining processable elastomeric properties. The patents collectively address two major manufacturing challenges: controlling molecular weight/Mooney viscosity during hydrogenation and removing residual transition-metal catalysts after synthesis.

The first patent discloses purification of optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions using functionalized macroreticular ion-exchange resins, emphasizing low ruthenium content in the recovered polymer. It is relevant to downstream HNBR quality because catalyst residue control is critical for color, stability, and compatibility with subsequent processing. The second patent introduces a two-step route in which nitrile rubber is first subjected to metathesis in the presence of ruthenium or osmium compounds, optionally with a co-olefin and solvent, and then hydrogenated to yield lower-MW HNBR with reduced polydispersity. The third patent advances this concept further by performing simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in a single step using specific ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems, enabling low residual double bond content and lower Mooney viscosity.

Major trends across the landscape include use of homogeneous transition-metal catalysts, co-olefin-assisted chain scission, and explicit control of molecular weight, Mooney viscosity, and residual unsaturation. Process conditions disclosed in the examples include solution-phase operation, elevated temperatures, hydrogen pressure, catalyst loading in phr, and post-reaction purification by filtration, evaporation, drying, or ion exchange. Overall, the evidence points to a technical direction centered on controlled depolymerization/hydrogenation chemistry followed by rigorous catalyst-removal strategies to produce commercially useful HNBR grades for seals, hoses, and other demanding elastomer applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2 and US9150669B2 both target low molecular weight HNBR, but US7919563B2 uses a two-step metathesis-then-hydrogenation sequence, whereas US9150669B2 performs simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in one step.
- US7919563B2 explicitly uses co-olefins such as ethylene and 1-hexene, with 500 psi ethylene and 80° C. in the examples; US9150669B2 also allows optional co-olefin but the example data emphasize catalyst comparison rather than co-olefin identity.
- Catalyst systems differ: US7919563B2 claims Ru/Os compounds for metathesis, while US9150669B2 focuses on specific Ru/Os formula (I) catalysts and compares Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson's catalyst systems.
- US9150669B2 reports direct hydrogenation performance via residual double bond content over 60-240 min, reaching 0.4% RDB for HNBR 1 at 240 min; US7919563B2 reports final polymer properties such as Mw, Mn, PDI, and Mooney viscosity after metathesis/hydrogenation.
- US7846995B2 is not a synthesis patent but a purification patent; it complements the other two by addressing removal of ruthenium residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions after synthesis.
- Across the set, solution-phase processing is common, but only US7846995B2 emphasizes post-reaction cleanup by ion exchange, while the other two emphasize molecular-weight control during formation of HNBR.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows a coherent HNBR manufacturing strategy: first control chain length and residual unsaturation through metathesis-assisted hydrogenation, then remove catalyst residues to obtain a cleaner product. The strongest synthesis evidence supports solution-phase Ru/Os-catalyzed processes at elevated temperature, with optional co-olefins and hydrogen, to reach low Mw, low Mooney viscosity, and low RDB. Based on the examples, effective parameter windows include about 80° C., catalyst loadings in the 0.041-0.060 phr range for hydrogenation/metathesis systems, and sufficient reaction time to drive RDB below 1% in the best case. For product purification, functionalized macroreticular ion-exchange resins with particle diameters of 0.2 to 2.5 mm and treatment of rubber solutions containing 5 to 500 ppm Ru are supported by the evidence. Overall, the evidence favors a combined approach of controlled catalytic depolymerization/hydrogenation followed by rigorous metal-removal purification to produce commercially useful HNBR for seals, hoses, and other high-performance elastomer applications.
