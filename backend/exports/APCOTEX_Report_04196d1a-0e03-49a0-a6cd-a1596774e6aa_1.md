# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT HNBR PROCESS PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes five primary patents focused on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, process intensification, and post-reaction purification. The target material is hydrogenated NBR derived from acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber, with emphasis on low residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil resistance, and better processability through control of molecular weight, Mooney viscosity, and catalyst residues. Across the selected patents, two major synthesis strategies dominate: direct selective hydrogenation of NBR using Ru/Os-based catalyst systems, and single-step or tandem metathesis/hydrogenation routes that reduce molecular weight while hydrogenating the polymer backbone. A third theme is downstream purification, especially removal of ruthenium residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using functionalized ion-exchange resins.

US10500578B2 and EP2473281B1 are the most process-centric disclosures for low-molecular-weight HNBR. They describe catalyst compositions based on ruthenium or osmium complexes, often paired with vinyl ether co-catalysts, enabling simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation or metathesis followed by hydrogenation. These routes are designed to produce HNBR with reduced Mw, narrower PDI, and low residual double bond content. US10500578B2 provides detailed comparative examples showing the effect of catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether on hydrogenation rate and molecular-weight retention. EP2473281B1 similarly emphasizes one-step production of HNBR with Mw 20,000-250,000 and Mooney viscosity 1-50.

US7772328B2 is distinct in that it focuses on low-molecular-weight HNBR made by olefin metathesis of an already hydrogenated nitrile rubber framework, targeting low Mooney viscosity and narrow molecular-weight distribution rather than the hydrogenation catalyst itself. US9150669B2 reinforces the same single-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation concept but highlights alternative catalyst structures and performance benchmarks. US7846995B2 addresses a practical manufacturing issue: removal of ruthenium-containing catalyst residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using macroreticular functionalized ion-exchange resins. Together, the patents show a coherent development path from catalyst design and reaction control to purification and product stabilization for industrial HNBR manufacture.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10500578B2 and US9150669B2 both focus on simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis of nitrile rubber using Ru/Os catalyst systems, but US10500578B2 emphasizes catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether and detailed hydrogenation profiles, while US9150669B2 emphasizes the general catalyst class and product-property windows.
- EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 disclose the same core one-step low-molecular-weight HNBR concept, with overlapping target ranges for Mw (20,000-250,000), Mooney viscosity (1-50), and low PDI; EP2473281B1 additionally frames the process as a low-molecular-weight HNBR preparation method with explicit catalyst comparison data.
- US7772328B2 differs from the simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis patents by focusing on metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber followed by hydrogenation, rather than a single-step hydrogenation/metathesis process.
- US7846995B2 is not a synthesis patent but a purification patent; it complements the synthesis disclosures by addressing ruthenium residue removal from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions using ion-exchange resins.
- Across the process patents, co-olefin use is explicitly disclosed in US7772328B2, US10500578B2, EP2473281B1, and US9150669B2, with vinyl ethyl ether appearing as a key co-catalyst/co-olefin in the catalyst-pretreatment and metathesis-enabled routes.
- Temperature control is centered around 120° C. in US10500578B2 and around 80° C. in US7772328B2, while EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 emphasize product-property targets more than a single fixed temperature in the extracted evidence.
- Hydrogen pressure is explicitly high in US10500578B2 (4.137 MPa, and 6.895 MPa in one example), whereas US7772328B2 highlights co-olefin pressure conditions (500 psi ethylene) during metathesis-based chain scission.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two practical synthesis routes to hydrogenated NBR: direct selective hydrogenation of NBR with Ru/Os catalysts, and metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by or concurrent with hydrogenation. The strongest process guidance comes from US10500578B2, EP2473281B1, and US9150669B2, which collectively indicate that Ru/Os catalyst systems, vinyl ether pretreatment or co-olefin assistance, temperatures around 120° C., and hydrogen pressures in the 4-7 MPa range can deliver high hydrogenation degrees and low residual double bond content while maintaining manageable molecular weight. For low-Mw HNBR, the evidence favors catalyst systems that combine metathesis activity with hydrogenation activity and that can be tuned by co-catalyst pretreatment. For product cleanup, ruthenium-removal ion-exchange treatment is recommended to improve final purity. Overall, the best-supported synthesis target is HNBR with Mw roughly 20,000-250,000, Mooney viscosity below 50, and residual unsaturation below about 5% when high-performance applications are desired.
