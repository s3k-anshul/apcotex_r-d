# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT HNBR PROCESS LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes eight patents focused on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) synthesis, precursor preparation, and post-hydrogenation purification. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated nitrile rubber derived from NBR, typically an acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymer optionally containing additional comonomers. Across the patent set, the central technical objective is to reduce residual unsaturation while controlling molecular weight, Mooney viscosity, polydispersity, and catalyst residues so that HNBR retains heat, ozone, oil, and aging resistance with improved processability. The landscape divides into three major themes: direct hydrogenation of NBR in specialized reactors, one-step or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation routes to low-molecular-weight HNBR, and downstream catalyst-removal methods for improving aging performance and color.

US7666950B2 discloses hydrogenation of NBR in a spinning disk reactor, emphasizing lower hydrogen pressure than conventional batch systems and use of homogeneous rhodium/ruthenium catalysts. US20210340285A1 focuses on solution hydrogenation of partially unsaturated NBR using Ru, Pd, or Rh compounds at 60-200°C and 0.7-15 MPa, with explicit attention to minimizing residual catalyst and improving aging. EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 are the most process-intensive low-MW HNBR disclosures: both describe simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in the presence of hydrogen and ruthenium/osmium catalysts, enabling single-step reduction of molecular weight and residual double bonds. US8362154B2 and US7951875B2 are precursor-degradation patents that reduce NBR molecular weight by metathesis before or during hydrogenation, using Grubbs-type catalysts, co-olefins such as 1-hexene or ethylene, and controlled catalyst loadings. US9023914B2 provides a complementary route by preparing NBR in organic solvent with modifiers, enabling tailored precursor architecture before optional hydrogenation. US7846995B2 addresses the purification problem by removing Ru residues from optionally hydrogenated NBR using functionalized ion-exchange resins.

Overall, the evidence supports solution-phase hydrogenation or combined metathesis/hydrogenation as the dominant routes, with catalyst choice, solvent, and post-treatment being key levers for achieving low-RDB, low-MW HNBR suitable for demanding elastomer applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7666950B2 emphasizes reactor intensification via spinning disk hydrogenation, whereas US20210340285A1 and US9150669B2 focus on solution hydrogenation with noble-metal catalysts and explicit residue control.
- US9150669B2 and EP2473281B1 both target low-MW HNBR by simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation; both use ruthenium/osmium catalyst families, but EP2473281B1 reports Hoveyda/Arlt/Wilkinson comparisons while US9150669B2 frames the process more broadly with formula (I) catalysts.
- US8362154B2 and US7951875B2 are precursor-degradation routes: both use metathesis with co-olefins to lower NBR molecular weight before or during hydrogenation, but US7951875B2 provides more explicit solvent/co-olefin/process data and final HNBR property tables.
- US8362154B2 shows Grubbs III outperforming Grubbs II at 23 and 57 ppm Ru, while US7951875B2 demonstrates that ethylene or 1-hexene and catalyst loading from 0.05 to 0.25 phr can drive Mw down to 54 kg/mol and Mooney viscosity to 3.
- US9023914B2 is upstream of the HNBR step: it prepares tailored NBR in organic solvent with modifiers, enabling controlled precursor architecture before hydrogenation, unlike the other patents that focus on hydrogenation or purification.
- US7846995B2 is downstream of synthesis and addresses Ru removal rather than polymer formation; it complements the hydrogenation patents by improving aging and color through ion-exchange purification.
- Across the hydrogenation patents, the most common catalyst families are rhodium, ruthenium, and palladium compounds, with hydrogenation temperatures spanning 60 to 200 °C and pressures from 3.5 bar up to 15 MPa depending on reactor and catalyst system.
- Residual unsaturation control is strongest in the simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation patents, where RDB values are driven below 1% in the best examples, while direct hydrogenation patents emphasize catalyst recovery and aging stability.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that HNBR can be produced by three main routes: direct hydrogenation of NBR, simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation to lower molecular weight during saturation, and precursor tailoring followed by hydrogenation. For the most process-efficient low-MW HNBR, the strongest evidence supports one-step metathesis/hydrogenation using ruthenium/osmium catalysts in solution, with catalyst loadings in the low-ppm to low-phr range and temperatures around 23 to 80 °C in the examples. For conventional hydrogenation, solution-phase Ru/Rh/Pd systems at 60 to 200 °C and 0.7 to 15 MPa are disclosed, but catalyst residue removal is essential to preserve aging performance. Reactor intensification via spinning disk hydrogenation offers a lower-pressure alternative, while ion-exchange purification is a practical finishing step for reducing Ru residues. Based on the evidence, the most robust synthesis strategy for low-MW HNBR is controlled NBR precursor preparation followed by simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation or direct solution hydrogenation with subsequent catalyst removal, aiming for low residual double bonds, Mw below about 250,000 g/mol, and Mooney viscosity in the 1 to 50 range.
