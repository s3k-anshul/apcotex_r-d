# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: EMULSION, SOLUTION, AND FUNCTIONALIZED COPOLYMER ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and closely related styrene-butadiene copolymers. Across the manifest, the target is defined by reduced styrene fraction relative to conventional SBR, with disclosed embodiments spanning emulsion SBR, solution SBR, modified/functionalized SBR, and tapered or low-molecular-weight styrene-butadiene copolymers. The selected patents collectively emphasize control of styrene content, monomer feed ratio, sequence distribution, molecular weight, and post-polymerization functionalization as levers to tune tire-relevant properties such as wet grip, wear resistance, silica affinity, processability, and storage modulus.

Two major polymerization families dominate the landscape. Emulsion polymerization patents (US8530017B2, US9328176B2, EP2986647B1) use water as the continuous medium, with rosin/fatty-acid soaps or reactive emulsifiers, redox initiator systems, mercaptan chain-transfer agents, and low-temperature batch operation around 7–10 °C. These disclosures focus on introducing polar or reactive functionality during emulsion SBR synthesis, then using coagulation, washing, stripping, and drying to isolate the rubber. Solution polymerization patents (US11591428B2, US10174133B2, US11292862B2) use hydrocarbon solvents such as hexane, organolithium initiation, and controlled monomer feeds or terminal modification. These routes provide tighter control over styrene incorporation, microstructure, molecular weight, and functional end groups, while downstream water use is limited to coagulation or solvent-removal workup where disclosed.

A recurring formulation trend is the use of the resulting SBR in tire tread compounds with silica and/or carbon black, often alongside process oil, silane coupling agents, sulfur, and accelerators. Several patents explicitly target improved filler interaction or reduced oil demand, while maintaining or lowering styrene content into ranges consistent with low-styrene SBR embodiments. Overall, the patent set shows that low styrene SBR is not defined by a single synthesis route; rather, it is enabled by feed composition control and by functionalization strategies that preserve performance despite reduced styrene fraction.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US11591428B2 and US10174133B2 are solution-polymerized SBR-type materials in hydrocarbon solvent, whereas US8530017B2, US9328176B2, and EP2986647B1 are emulsion/latex processes in water.
- The lowest explicitly disclosed styrene fractions in the manifest are 17% bound styrene in US11292862B2 Sample 7 and 21.0 wt% bound styrene in EP2986647B1 Example 6; US11591428B2 reports 25.7-26.6% styrene in tapered copolymers.
- US11292862B2 uniquely combines continuous solution polymerization with in-line purification of monomers, modifier addition, and steam stripping, while US10174133B2 emphasizes terminal capping and silane-based post-modification.
- US8530017B2 and US9328176B2 both use water-based emulsion polymerization with redox initiators and mercaptan chain-transfer agents, but US8530017B2 focuses on reactive emulsifiers, whereas US9328176B2 focuses on epoxy acrylate functionalization and ring opening.
- EP2986647B1 differs from the other emulsion patents by explicitly incorporating vegetable-oil-based extender oils and reporting oil-extended functionalized SBR latex and dry rubber properties.
- Across the manifest, styrene content is controlled either by monomer feed ratio in solution polymerization or by emulsion recipe design; functionalization is used to recover silica affinity, processability, and tread performance at reduced styrene levels.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The selected primary patents show two complementary routes to low styrene SBR: water-based emulsion polymerization with functional or reactive emulsifiers, and hydrocarbon-solution polymerization with precise feed control and post-polymerization modification. The strongest direct low-styrene disclosures are US11292862B2 (17% bound styrene in a solution polymerized modified conjugated diene polymer) and EP2986647B1 (21.0 wt% bound styrene in a functionalized oil-extended SBR), while US11591428B2 provides tapered SBR at 25.7-26.6% styrene and the emulsion patents demonstrate how polar functionality and oil extension can preserve tire performance. Overall, the manifest indicates that low styrene fraction is achieved not by a single recipe but by coordinated control of monomer ratio, sequence distribution, molecular weight, and functionalization/workup strategy.
