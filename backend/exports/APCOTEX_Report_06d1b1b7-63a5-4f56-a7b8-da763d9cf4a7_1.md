# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: COMPOSITION CONTROL, DEGRADATION, AND SWELLABLE RUBBER APPLICATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile-rubber processing. The target material is defined as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile fraction relative to conventional grades, typically pursued to improve low-temperature flexibility while retaining sufficient oil resistance. The patent landscape here is not centered on direct low-ACN copolymer synthesis; instead, it spans downstream molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber and formulation of nitrile-elastomer-containing swellable rubber compositions. EP2145681B1 addresses metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber using ruthenium or osmium metathesis catalysts combined with a boron-trifluoride adduct activator. The disclosure is relevant because it enables lower-Mooney, more processable nitrile rubber feedstocks, which can be useful when tailoring NBR grades for improved handling or for subsequent hydrogenation/degradation workflows. US10385647B2 focuses on swellable rubber compositions containing a nitrile elastomer, butadiene rubber, sulfur curing agent, silica, and powder super absorbent polymer (SAP), with explicit embodiments using nitrile rubber elastomers and hydrogenated nitrile elastomers. Although not a synthesis patent for low-ACN NBR, it provides formulation/process guidance showing how nitrile elastomers are incorporated into high-temperature, high-pressure swellable packers and how composition, SAP loading, and curing conditions affect performance. Across the two patents, the major trends are: use of metathesis chemistry to reduce molecular weight and improve processability; use of nitrile elastomer blends in demanding oilfield swellable systems; and strong emphasis on temperature, pressure, and swelling-performance windows rather than direct control of acrylonitrile content. Overall, the evidence suggests that low-ACN NBR synthesis itself is not directly disclosed in these primary patents, but the patents provide adjacent technical routes for modifying nitrile rubber properties and for deploying nitrile elastomers in formulations where low-temperature flexibility, swelling behavior, and processability are critical.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2145681B1 is a nitrile-rubber modification patent focused on metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction, whereas US10385647B2 is a formulation patent for swellable packers using nitrile elastomer blends.
- EP2145681B1 discloses catalyst-system chemistry with ruthenium/osmium metathesis catalysts and BF3-adduct activators; US10385647B2 discloses no polymerization catalyst system, instead emphasizing SAP-filled rubber compounding and curing.
- EP2145681B1 provides molecular-weight and Mooney-viscosity context for nitrile rubber processing; US10385647B2 provides Mooney viscosity for a hydrogenated nitrile elastomer (about 47 to about 72) and percent hydrogenation (about 70% to about 91%).
- US10385647B2 contains extensive formulation/process parameters such as 125-175 phr or 170-210 phr powder SAP, 330°F curing, 70 psi, and 2.5 h cure time; EP2145681B1 does not provide comparable formulation data in the extracted evidence.
- Neither patent directly discloses low-acrylonitrile NBR copolymerization or explicit acrylonitrile-content control; both are adjacent technologies that inform property tailoring and downstream use of nitrile rubbers.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence does not directly disclose a dedicated low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis route, but it does identify two useful adjacent strategies: metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber and high-performance nitrile-elastomer compounding for swellable packers. For low-ACN NBR development, the most actionable synthesis guidance remains outside these patents: control acrylonitrile feed ratio during butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymerization, then tune molecular weight and Mooney viscosity to the application. Based on the disclosed evidence, lower molecular weight and moderate Mooney viscosity are desirable for processability, while nitrile-elastomer formulations for oilfield service benefit from robust curing and carefully selected SAP loading. Recommended evidence-based targets are a controlled nitrile-rubber feedstock with reduced molecular weight, Mooney viscosity in a processable range, and formulation compatibility with high-temperature brine exposure if the material is intended for swellable applications.
