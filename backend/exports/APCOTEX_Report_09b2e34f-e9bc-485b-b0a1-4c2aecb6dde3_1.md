# PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT: LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR-RELATED SYNTHESIS, MODIFICATION, AND PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a seven-patent landscape relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related NBR processing/modification technologies. The target is defined as NBR compositions with reduced acrylonitrile content, typically sought to tune flexibility, glass transition temperature, processability, and downstream performance in tires, seals, belts, charging members, and adhesive systems. The patent set is heterogeneous: only one patent directly addresses NBR molecular-weight reduction by olefin metathesis, while the others contribute enabling chemistry or application contexts that rely on NBR as a base elastomer or functional component.

The most directly relevant disclosure is US9346908B1, which treats NBR (explicitly 38 mol % acrylonitrile in the examples) with a loaded ruthenium olefin-metathesis catalyst and 1-hexene co-olefin in chlorobenzene at room temperature for two hours to reduce molecular weight and improve processability. The catalyst is immobilized on a magnetic carrier via a grafted pyridine-containing polymer, enabling magnetic recovery and reuse. This patent is important because it demonstrates a practical route for modifying NBR properties without changing monomer feed composition, but it does not itself teach low-acrylonitrile polymerization.

The remaining patents are application- and process-oriented. US8053512B2 and EP2057205B1 focus on elastomer backbone modification and metathesis-based segment interchange, respectively, using unsaturated polymers and catalysts to alter morphology, hysteresis, or block architecture. US10317811B2 and US9740133B2 disclose charging members whose electroconductive elastic layers contain NBR and/or NBR-based particle aggregates, with explicit control of surface roughness, protrusion height, particle size, and vulcanization/polishing conditions. US11131058B2 and EP2522704B1 use carboxy-modified NBR or NBR-modified epoxy resin in adhesive/treatment systems, showing how NBR chemistry is leveraged in cord treatment and structural adhesive sheets.

Across the landscape, the dominant trends are controlled catalyst-mediated modification, solvent-based processing, magnetic catalyst recovery, and formulation engineering around NBR content rather than direct low-ACN copolymerization. For low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis specifically, the evidence supports monomer-ratio control during copolymerization as the primary route, while the patents here mainly provide downstream processing and modification strategies that can be combined with low-ACN base polymers.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9346908B1 is the only patent that directly processes NBR as the target polymer; it uses a 38 mol % acrylonitrile NBR solution, 1-hexene co-olefin, and a metathesis catalyst to reduce molecular weight rather than to alter ACN content.
- US8053512B2 and EP2057205B1 both rely on unsaturated elastomer backbones and catalyst-mediated modification, but they focus on backbone functionalization or segment interchange, not monomer-ratio control during NBR synthesis.
- US10317811B2 and US9740133B2 both use NBR in electroconductive charging members; the disclosed NBR in these examples has 35.0% bonded acrylonitrile, and performance is governed by filler/particle morphology, vulcanization, polishing, and UV treatment.
- US11131058B2 and EP2522704B1 use NBR-derived materials in adhesive/treatment formulations, specifically carboxy-modified NBR latex and NBR-modified epoxy resin, emphasizing formulation compatibility rather than polymerization conditions.
- Across the set, no patent explicitly discloses low-acrylonitrile NBR copolymerization feed ratios or emulsion/solution polymerization recipes for making low-ACN NBR; the evidence is strongest for post-polymerization modification and application engineering.
- Process conditions that recur in the relevant patents include room-temperature metathesis in chlorobenzene/toluene, 95-105 °C catalyst-loading or segment-interchange steps, methanol precipitation, THF extraction, and vulcanization/heat-curing steps for NBR-containing formulations.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence base shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR itself is not directly synthesized in the provided patents; instead, the landscape is dominated by NBR post-processing, catalyst-mediated molecular-weight reduction, and NBR-based formulation engineering. The most technically relevant disclosure is US9346908B1, which demonstrates that NBR at 38 mol % acrylonitrile can be efficiently degraded by olefin metathesis using a recoverable magnetic catalyst system, improving processability while preserving narrow PDI. For low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis, the patents do not provide direct monomer-feed recipes, so the recommended synthesis strategy remains controlled copolymerization of butadiene with a reduced acrylonitrile feed, followed by careful control of conversion and molecular weight. Based on the broader evidence, practical downstream processing parameters include solvent-based handling, catalyst recovery where applicable, and formulation tuning via fillers, particle aggregates, and curing conditions to exploit the softer, more flexible character expected from lower-ACN NBR grades.
