# CARBOXYLATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND SYNTHESIS-RELEVANT PROCESS FEATURES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to carboxylated nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how carboxylated or acid-modified nitrile rubbers are used and processed in heat-activable adhesive systems. The primary patent in the manifest, US20100147462A1, does not disclose a standalone polymerization recipe for making the carboxylated NBR itself; instead, it describes a heat-activable adhesive tape for flexible printed circuit boards based on an acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer combined with an epoxy resin. The patent is nevertheless highly relevant because it explicitly identifies the target elastomer class, states the acrylonitrile content range, and shows how carboxylated NBR is formulated to achieve heat activation, solvent processability, high-temperature flow control, and solder-bath resistance.

The disclosed formulation strategy centers on using the acid-modified nitrile rubber as both elastomer and reactive component, eliminating the need for an additional nonpolymer hardener. The weight ratio of modified NBR to epoxy resin is kept above 1.5, which is presented as important for maintaining flexibility while enabling crosslinking during hot lamination and subsequent bonding at elevated temperature. The patent also indicates that the nitrile rubber may be at least partly hydrogenated and that the acrylonitrile content can range from 15% to 50 wt%. Example data show solution coating from butanone, lamination at 100°C, hot pressing at 200°C and 1.3 MPa for 1 hour, and solder-bath testing at 288°C for 10 seconds.

Overall, the patent landscape in this manifest is formulation- and process-oriented rather than monomer-feed-oriented. The key trend is the use of carboxylated NBR as a reactive, high-viscosity elastomer in epoxy-containing adhesive systems for electronics, where thermal stability, low flow, and solvent-based coating are critical. For synthesis planning, the evidence supports selecting an acrylonitrile-rich NBR backbone, introducing acid or acid anhydride functionality, and formulating with epoxy resin at elastomer-rich ratios to obtain heat-activable, crosslinkable materials.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The available evidence supports carboxylated NBR as a reactive elastomer for heat-activable, epoxy-containing adhesive systems rather than a detailed monomer-by-monomer synthesis disclosure. The most important evidence-based parameters are an acrylonitrile content of 15% to 50 wt%, acid or acid anhydride modification, and an elastomer-to-epoxy weight ratio greater than 1.5, with no additional nonpolymer hardener. For process implementation, solvent coating from butanone, lamination at 100 °C, and hot pressing at 200 °C and 1.3 MPa for 1 hour are the clearest disclosed operating conditions. The patent indicates that these choices improve bond strength, thermal stability, and solder-bath resistance while preserving processability.
