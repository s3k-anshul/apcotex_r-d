# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) PATENT LANDSCAPE: SYNTHESIS, LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT CONTROL, AND PERFORMANCE-ORIENTED FORMULATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how precursor NBR is hydrogenated, selectively degraded, or formulated to deliver improved processability and end-use performance. The target material is HNBR defined by partial to near-complete saturation of the butadiene-derived backbone while retaining nitrile functionality, with key attributes including residual unsaturation, molecular weight, Mooney viscosity, gel content, and retention of mechanical properties after hydrogenation. Across the selected patents, two major synthesis directions emerge. First, several documents focus on direct hydrogenation or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems, often in the presence of hydrogen and sometimes a co-olefin, to reduce molecular weight and narrow molecular-weight distribution in a single step. These routes aim to preserve polymer microstructure while lowering Mooney viscosity and residual double-bond content. Second, other patents address downstream formulation and processing of HNBR, including peroxide-cured compositions, microgel-containing vulcanizates, belt compounds, and residue-removal processes that improve manufacturability and purity after metathesis/hydrogenation. The landscape shows repeated use of solution-phase processing, catalyst tuning, and additive control to balance hydrogenation efficiency against gelation, corrosion, and catalyst residue issues. Representative formulation trends include use of unsaturated carboxylic acids/salts, peroxides, microgels, carbon black or silica fillers, and polyfunctional co-crosslinkers to improve hardness, low-temperature flexibility, LLC resistance, and high-temperature durability. Overall, the patents collectively indicate that successful HNBR synthesis depends on controlling backbone saturation and molecular weight while minimizing side reactions and preserving processability for seals, belts, hoses, and related elastomeric parts.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Several patents converge on low-Mooney HNBR targets: US7772328B2 and US7951875B2 report products down to Mooney 3–43, while EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 claim broader HNBR windows of 1–50 Mooney.
- The synthesis patents split into two routes: sequential metathesis then hydrogenation (US7772328B2, US7951875B2, US7737233B2) versus simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation in hydrogen (EP2473281B1, US9150669B2).
- Catalyst systems differ: US7737233B2 adds alkaline earth metal chlorides to ruthenium/osmium metathesis catalysts; EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 use ruthenium/osmium complexes for one-step hydrogenation/metathesis; US8957161B2 emphasizes salt additives to accelerate metathesis.
- Process conditions in the low-MW patents are solution-based, often using co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, with reactor temperatures around 80°C in the example sets and catalyst loadings from 0.05 to 0.25 phr in US7772328B2/US7951875B2.
- Residual unsaturation control is explicit in EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2, where example products reach RDB values as low as 0.4% after 240 min, whereas the metathesis-only patents focus more on molecular-weight reduction before hydrogenation.
- Downstream formulation patents (US9321910B2, US9243683B2, EP2700692B1) show that HNBR synthesis is only part of the technology stack; peroxide cure, microgels, fillers, and plasticizers are used to tailor heat resistance, cold resistance, stiffness, and LLC resistance.
- US7846995B2 addresses a different but essential manufacturing issue: removal of ruthenium residues from HNBR solutions, with 5–1000 ppm Ru in solution and 33% removal demonstrated in Example 2A.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that hydrogenated NBR technology is dominated by two synthesis philosophies: controlled NBR metathesis followed by hydrogenation, and true one-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation under hydrogen. The strongest evidence for practical HNBR synthesis supports solution-phase processing, ruthenium-based single-site catalysts, optional co-olefins, and feedstocks with moderate Mooney viscosity so that the final HNBR reaches low Mooney values, low residual double bond content, and narrow molecular-weight distribution. Based on the evidence, the most robust synthesis targets are NBR feed Mooney viscosity around 24 to 40, catalyst loading in the low phr or 0.1 to 0.3 wt% range, reaction temperatures near 80°C in the example sets, and hydrogenation sufficient to drive diene saturation toward 95 to 100% when high-performance HNBR is desired. For downstream use, peroxide cure systems, microgels, and carefully balanced fillers are important to translate the polymer into seals, belts, hoses, and gaskets with the required heat, oil, and coolant resistance. Residue-removal steps should also be considered when ruthenium catalysts are used, because catalyst purification is a practical requirement for commercial HNBR manufacture.
