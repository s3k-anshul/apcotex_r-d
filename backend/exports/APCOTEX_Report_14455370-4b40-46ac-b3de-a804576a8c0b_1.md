# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION CONTROL, LOW-VISCOSITY GRADES, AND DOWNSTREAM PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile-rubber technologies. The target is defined here as NBR grades with reduced acrylonitrile content or compositions/processes that enable low-ACN performance through monomer feed control, molecular-weight reduction, or formulation strategies that improve processability and end-use properties. Across the selected patents, the landscape spans three major technical directions: (1) direct synthesis or modification of nitrile rubber to lower molecular weight and viscosity, including olefin-metathesis degradation followed by hydrogenation; (2) composition engineering of highly saturated or carboxylated nitrile rubbers with polyamide, silica, fillers, and cross-linking systems to balance oil resistance, heat resistance, rollability, and compression set; and (3) downstream compounding or masterbatch approaches that improve dispersion of cross-linkers or additives and suppress foaming or viscosity rise during processing.

The most synthesis-relevant disclosures are the metathesis-based patents, which show that nitrile rubber can be reacted in solution with ruthenium or related metathesis catalysts, often in chlorinated aromatic solvents, to reduce molecular weight and narrow dispersity before optional hydrogenation. These routes are directly relevant to low-ACN NBR because they address processability limits associated with high Mooney viscosity while preserving nitrile-rubber chemistry. Other patents focus on highly saturated nitrile rubbers containing 15–60 wt% acrylonitrile units, often with carboxylated comonomers such as mono-n-butyl maleate, and demonstrate that blending with polyamide resin and using peroxide or amine-based cross-linking systems can improve rollability and high-temperature strength. Several patents also emphasize controlled compounding conditions, including twin-screw extrusion at 220–320°C, Banbury mixing, and masterbatch preparation at or above the melting point of the amine cross-linker.

Overall, the evidence suggests that the most useful synthesis window for low-acrylonitrile NBR development is not a single monomer recipe but a combination of composition control, low-viscosity generation, and formulation optimization. The strongest recurring trends are reduced molecular weight, controlled acrylonitrile content, selective hydrogenation, and carefully tuned cross-linking/dispersion strategies to preserve oil resistance while improving processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473279B1 and EP2057205B1 both use metathesis chemistry, but EP2473279B1 focuses on degrading nitrile rubber to lower molecular weight before hydrogenation, whereas EP2057205B1 uses metathesis to interchange segments between polymers and form meta-block copolymers.
- US10280298B2 and US9382391B2 both rely on highly saturated nitrile rubber with carboxyl functionality, but US10280298B2 emphasizes polyamide blending and peroxide cure, while US9382391B2 emphasizes masterbatching an aromatic polyvalent amine cross-linker for better dispersibility.
- EP3323850B1 and US8034881B2 are both formulation patents for nitrile-rubber compounds, but EP3323850B1 centers on silica/plasticizer/polyamine systems for low heat generation, whereas US8034881B2 centers on cyclic amidine additives to improve compression set and heat resistance.
- EP2671914B1 is the most latex- and filler-dispersion-oriented patent, using layered inorganic fillers with controlled particle-size distributions, while the other composition patents rely more on carbon black, silica, or polyamide reinforcement.
- EP2561011B1 is not a nitrile-rubber synthesis patent, but it uniquely includes a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer as a matrix for CNT/graphene masterbatch processing, linking nitrile rubber to conductive composite manufacturing.
- US9306208B2 and US10317811B2 are application/process patents rather than NBR synthesis patents; US9306208B2 concerns electrochemical electrodes with coated lithium powders, while US10317811B2 concerns charging rollers using A-BNR surface layers and UV roughening.
- Across the synthesis-relevant patents, acrylonitrile content is typically controlled in the 15 to 60 wt% range for highly saturated nitrile rubbers, with low-viscosity/processability improvements achieved by metathesis, hydrogenation, or formulation architecture rather than by extreme changes in ACN content.
- Processing temperatures vary widely: metathesis/hydrogenation routes use 22°C to 138°C in solution, while compounding routes use 130°C to 320°C, and twin-screw extrusion commonly appears at 220°C or higher for polyamide-containing systems.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR development is best approached as a combined composition-and-process problem rather than a single monomer specification. The strongest synthesis evidence comes from metathesis/hydrogenation routes that reduce molecular weight and improve processability, while the strongest formulation evidence comes from highly saturated nitrile rubber systems that use carboxylated and non-carboxylated components, polyamide reinforcement, and carefully selected cure packages. For practical low-ACN NBR synthesis, the evidence supports maintaining acrylonitrile in the lower end of the disclosed nitrile-rubber ranges, using controlled emulsion polymerization at low temperature, and then tailoring molecular weight by metathesis or related post-polymerization modification. For downstream processing, the most robust formulations use low-surface-area silica or polyamide reinforcement, with peroxide or amine-based cross-linking systems selected to match the intended cure route. Overall, the recommended evidence-based strategy is: low-temperature emulsion copolymerization of butadiene/acrylonitrile with optional carboxyl comonomer, followed by selective hydrogenation or metathesis-based viscosity reduction, then compounding with a cure package optimized for rollability, compression set, and high-temperature strength.
