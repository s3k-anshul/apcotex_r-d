# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS CONTROL: PATENT LANDSCAPE REVIEW

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six patents focused on the preparation of hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) from nitrile rubber (NBR), with emphasis on selective hydrogenation, simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis, and catalyst-mediated molecular-weight control. Across the landscape, the target material is consistently defined as an NBR-derived elastomer in which the butadiene-derived double bonds are partially to highly hydrogenated while nitrile functionality is retained, enabling improved heat, ozone, and oil resistance. The patents collectively address two major manufacturing challenges: achieving high residual-double-bond conversion without gelation or nitrile reduction, and reducing the molecular weight/Mooney viscosity of HNBR to improve processability.

Two broad process families dominate. The first is direct hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous noble-metal catalysts, especially ruthenium, osmium, rhodium, or palladium systems, often in organic solvent and under elevated hydrogen pressure and temperature. US20180223005A1 and EP3827029A1 exemplify this route, with the former emphasizing catalyst composition design using Ru/Os complexes and vinyl-containing co-catalysts, and the latter focusing on catalyst residue management and aging stability through controlled noble-metal loading. The second family uses metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction, either as a pre-hydrogenation degradation step or as a simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation process. US7919563B2, EP2473281B1, and US9079979B2 show that Ru/Os metathesis catalysts can be used with co-olefins and hydrogen to generate lower-Mw HNBR in a single step or staged sequence, while US8957161B2 provides supporting catalyst-system chemistry relevant to metathesis activation.

Key formulation/process trends include use of chlorobenzene or MCB-type aromatic solvents, hydrogen pressures from about 4.137 to 8.4 MPa in the examples, temperatures from 80 to 140°C, and catalyst loadings in the low-phr range or ppm metal range. Co-catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether, use of 1-hexene as co-olefin, and careful control of catalyst identity strongly influence hydrogenation rate, molecular-weight retention, and final Mooney viscosity. Overall, the evidence supports Ru/Os-based catalyst systems and metathesis-enabled process designs as the most effective routes to low-Mw, processable HNBR.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US20180223005A1 and EP3827029A1 focus on direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution, whereas US7919563B2, EP2473281B1, and US9079979B2 emphasize metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction before or during hydrogenation.
- The metathesis-enabled patents use Ru/Os catalyst families and often include co-olefins such as 1-hexene or ethylene; US20180223005A1 instead relies on catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether to tune hydrogenation activity.
- Operating temperatures overlap in the 120-140 °C range in the examples, but EP3827029A1 claims a broader 60-200 °C window and 0.7-15 MPa hydrogen pressure range, while the example-rich hydrogenation patents commonly use 4.137-8.4 MPa.
- US7919563B2 and EP2473281B1 explicitly target low molecular weight and low Mooney viscosity HNBR, with EP2473281B1 reporting Mw as low as 113,371 g/mol and Mooney viscosity 33.3, and US7919563B2 reporting Mw as low as 59,000 g/mol and Mooney viscosity 3.
- US9079979B2 shows that Grubbs III outperforms Grubbs II and Wilkinson's catalyst in early hydrogenation kinetics, while US20180223005A1 shows that catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether can improve hydrogenation rate and preserve molecular weight.
- EP3827029A1 is distinct in prioritizing catalyst residue control and aging stability, highlighting ppm-level noble-metal windows rather than metathesis or chain-shortening chemistry.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that HNBR synthesis is best controlled by either highly selective direct hydrogenation with Ru/Os/Rh/Pd catalysts or by metathesis-assisted chain shortening followed by hydrogenation, with the latter route being most effective for low-Mw, low-Mooney products. The strongest evidence supports Ru-based catalyst systems, hydrogenation at roughly 120-140 °C, hydrogen pressures around 4-8.4 MPa in the examples, and co-catalyst/co-olefin strategies such as vinyl ethyl ether pretreatment or 1-hexene/ethylene addition to tune activity and molecular weight. For cleaner product and better aging, catalyst residue should be minimized, with EP3827029A1 indicating practical ppm windows for Ru, Pd, and Rh. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are: Ru/Os-based catalyst chemistry, aromatic solvent media such as MCB/chlorobenzene, hydrogenation near 140 °C, hydrogen pressure near 8.4 MPa where high conversion is needed, and metathesis-enabled molecular-weight reduction when low Mooney viscosity is the priority.
