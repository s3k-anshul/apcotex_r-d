# HIGH ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION-CONTROLLED NITRILE RUBBER AND RELATED LATEX/COMPOSITE PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys five primary patents that intersect with high-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) synthesis, processing, or use in latex/composite systems. The target is defined as NBR enriched in acrylonitrile, typically pursued to increase oil resistance, chemical resistance, and performance in demanding elastomer applications, while managing tradeoffs in gel content, particle size, processability, and interfacial adhesion. The patent set is heterogeneous: one patent is directly centered on nitrile rubber suspension/latex handling after hydrogenation and precipitation (EP2325238B1), two patents disclose acrylonitrile-containing copolymer latexes used in glove or dip-molding applications (US9388302B2 and US20200299532A1), one patent discloses ABS graft polymerization with explicit acrylonitrile feed control but is only indirectly relevant to NBR chemistry (US8299175B2), and one patent focuses on CNT/graphene/polymer composites that includes a nitrile resin base and a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer as a matrix component (EP2561011B1). Across the landscape, the dominant polymerization route remains emulsion polymerization, often with staged monomer addition, emulsifier control, and chain-transfer regulation to tune particle size, gel content, and final latex stability. For high-acrylonitrile NBR specifically, the most relevant evidence emphasizes monomer composition control rather than a dedicated high-ACN NBR recipe: acrylonitrile contents of 30-35 wt% appear in glove latex examples, while the ABS patent uses 4-20 parts by weight acrylonitrile with 17-40 parts styrene and a staged butadiene latex preparation. Process trends include two-step monomer feed strategies, use of emulsifiers and mercaptan chain-transfer agents, coagulation/aging conditions for powder recovery, and latex blending or lamination to balance antistatic behavior, softness, and adhesion. The evidence suggests that practical high-ACN NBR development is most strongly supported by emulsion-polymerized latex systems with controlled feed ratios, moderate temperatures, and downstream coagulation or precipitation protocols that preserve particle integrity and manage emulsifier residue.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2325238B1 focuses on post-synthesis precipitation and suspension stabilization of HXNBR, whereas US9388302B2 and US20200299532A1 focus on latex composition and article formation rather than polymer backbone synthesis.
- US9388302B2 is the clearest high-acrylonitrile disclosure, with 30 wt% and 35 wt% acrylonitrile in nitrile-butadiene latex formulations; US20200299532A1 uses nitrile rubber blends but does not disclose acrylonitrile content.
- US8299175B2 uses staged emulsion polymerization with 4-20 parts by weight acrylonitrile in an ABS graft system, showing monomer-feed control principles but not an NBR product.
- Emulsion polymerization is the dominant synthesis route across the relevant patents; EP2325238B1 instead emphasizes solvent precipitation of already-formed HXNBR into aqueous suspension.
- Emulsifier and chain-transfer control are explicit in US8299175B2 and US9388302B2; EP2325238B1 emphasizes amine-assisted precipitation and pH control; US20200299532A1 emphasizes latex blending, coagulant dipping, chlorination, and curing.
- The most relevant high-ACN composition window in the evidence is 30-35 wt% acrylonitrile in US9388302B2, which aligns with high-ACN NBR definitions and is more directly applicable than the ABS or composite patents.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that high-acrylonitrile NBR development is best supported by emulsion-polymerized latex systems with explicit monomer-ratio control, staged feed strategies, and careful downstream coagulation or dip-molding processing. Among the patents reviewed, US9388302B2 provides the most direct high-ACN NBR composition evidence, with 30 wt% and 35 wt% acrylonitrile formulations, while US8299175B2 reinforces the importance of staged monomer addition, particle size, gel content, and chain-transfer control in nitrile-containing emulsion systems. EP2325238B1 contributes practical precipitation/suspension handling for HXNBR-derived materials, and US20200299532A1 shows how nitrile-rich latex blends can be converted into antistatic articles with acceptable mechanical properties. Recommended evidence-based synthesis parameters are: emulsion polymerization; acrylonitrile in the 30-35 wt% range for high-ACN grades; butadiene as the major comonomer; controlled use of emulsifier and mercaptan chain-transfer agent; staged monomer addition to manage particle size and gel; and coagulation/drying conditions matched to the intended latex or article format.
