# HYDROGENATED NBR: CATALYTIC HYDROGENATION, SIMULTANEOUS METATHESIS-HYDROGENATION, AND CATALYST-RESIDUE CONTROL PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes five primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, with emphasis on routes that reduce residual unsaturation, control molecular weight, and improve processability or product stability. The target material is HNBR derived from nitrile rubber (NBR), typically a copolymer of a conjugated diene and an alpha,beta-unsaturated nitrile, with hydrogenation used to improve heat resistance, oil resistance, and oxidative stability while preserving the nitrile rubber microstructure. The selected patents span two major process families: (1) simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis of NBR using ruthenium/osmium-based catalysts to lower molecular weight and narrow molecular weight distribution in a single step, and (2) conventional hydrogenation of NBR using noble-metal catalysts followed by catalyst-removal or catalyst-management strategies to improve aging performance and product quality.

Across the landscape, the metathesis-hydrogenation patents (US7919563B2, US9150669B2, EP2473280B1) focus on producing low-molecular-weight HNBR with reduced Mooney viscosity and narrower polydispersity. They disclose catalyst families based on Ru/Os complexes, optional co-olefins, hydrogen atmosphere, and reaction temperatures around 80 degrees C in the examples, with catalyst loadings reported in phr and metal contents in ppm. The examples show that catalyst identity strongly affects hydrogenation rate, residual double bond (RDB) decay, and final Mooney viscosity. In contrast, US7846995B2 and EP3827029A1 address downstream catalyst residue control and direct hydrogenation quality, respectively. US7846995B2 uses functionalized ion-exchange resin treatment to remove ruthenium-containing residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions, while EP3827029A1 defines hydrogenation windows for Ru, Pd, or Rh catalysis and links low catalyst residue to improved aging, reduced discoloration, and lower gel growth.

Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis by selective hydrogenation of NBR in solution, with optional simultaneous chain scission via metathesis for viscosity reduction, followed by rigorous catalyst-removal or residue-minimization steps to preserve aging stability and appearance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2, US9150669B2, and EP2473280B1 all target simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis of NBR to reduce molecular weight and Mooney viscosity, but they differ in catalyst families and comparative performance data.
- US7919563B2 reports catalyst loadings of 0.05 to 0.25 phr and co-olefin use at 500 psi or 6.9×10^5 Pa, with 80 degrees C as the reactor temperature in the examples.
- US9150669B2 uses lower catalyst loadings of 0.041 to 0.060 phr and includes a 1.0 phr co-catalyst in one formulation; its tables show RDB dropping from 100% to as low as 0.4% by 240 min.
- EP2473280B1 reports catalyst loadings of 0.055 to 0.080 phr and metal contents of 65.6 to 91.3 ppm, with Grubbs III outperforming Grubbs II and Wilkinson's catalyst in early hydrogenation rate.
- Across the three metathesis-hydrogenation patents, final Mw values fall in the approximate 113,371 to 195,304 g/mol range in the examples, with Mooney viscosity spanning 3.0 to 69.3 depending on catalyst system and conditions.
- US7846995B2 is distinct because it does not modify the polymer backbone; instead it removes Ru residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using ion-exchange resin at about 100 degrees C for 66 hours.
- EP3827029A1 shifts from catalyst choice to process windows, specifying 60 to 200 degrees C, 700000 to 15000000 pascals, and 1 to 24 hours for hydrogenation with Ru, Pd, or Rh catalysts.
- The combined evidence suggests that low residual unsaturation is best achieved by Ru-based hydrogenation/metathesis under moderate temperature and controlled catalyst loading, followed by residue-removal or residue-minimization steps to protect aging properties.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows two complementary strategies for HNBR manufacture: one-step simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis to lower molecular weight and viscosity, and post-reaction catalyst cleanup or catalyst-residue minimization to improve aging and appearance. The strongest synthesis evidence supports Ru-based catalyst systems, optional co-olefin use, and reaction temperatures near 80 degrees C for metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction, while EP3827029A1 defines broader hydrogenation windows of 60 to 200 degrees C, 700000 to 15000000 pascals, and 1 to 24 hours. For practical HNBR synthesis, the evidence favors catalyst loadings in the low phr range, careful control of metal ppm, and aggressive residue removal when noble-metal catalysts are used. The best-supported route is selective hydrogenation of NBR in solution, with simultaneous metathesis when low-MW HNBR is desired, followed by purification to reduce Ru/Rh/Pd residues and preserve aging stability.
