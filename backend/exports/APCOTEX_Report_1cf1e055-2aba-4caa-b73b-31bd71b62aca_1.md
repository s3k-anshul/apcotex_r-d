# HYDROGENATED NITRILE BUTADIENE RUBBER (HNBR): HYDROGENATION ROUTES, CATALYST SYSTEMS, AND POST-TREATMENT TRENDS ACROSS SELECTED PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes nine primary patents focused on hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR) and closely related hydrogenation/manufacturing routes. The selected landscape spans direct hydrogenation of NBR latex in water, solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR in organic solvent, simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes that reduce molecular weight while saturating residual double bonds, and downstream purification/removal of noble-metal residues from HNBR solutions. Across the set, the target material is consistently HNBR or an HNBR precursor intended to yield HNBR, with the key target attribute being degree of hydrogenation or residual double-bond content. The patents collectively show that high hydrogenation levels are achievable by tuning catalyst identity, catalyst loading, temperature, hydrogen pressure, and reaction time, while avoiding gel formation and preserving processability.

A major process split is evident. One cluster uses aqueous latex hydrogenation without organic solvent, exemplified by ruthenium/osmium-type catalysts in water-containing polymer dispersions and by direct hydrogenation of NBR latex with Hoveyda-Grubbs-type catalysts. Another cluster uses monochlorobenzene or other organic solvents for solution hydrogenation, often with Ru, Rh, or Pd catalysts and sometimes with phosphine co-catalysts. A third cluster combines hydrogenation with metathesis to lower molecular weight and narrow molecular-weight distribution, using Ru or Os complexes designed to promote simultaneous chain scission and saturation. The final cluster addresses post-hydrogenation cleanup, especially removal of ruthenium residues by functionalized ion-exchange resins, reflecting the importance of catalyst residue control for aging performance and product quality.

Formulation and process trends include use of low ppm noble-metal catalyst levels, operation at elevated temperatures (roughly 100-155°C in the examples), hydrogen pressures from tens of bar up to about 1500 psi in latex systems, and careful control of residual unsaturation to below about 5% in several solution examples. Water is central only in the latex-based patent; in the others it is absent from the core reaction medium and appears, when present, only in analytical or downstream handling contexts. Overall, the patent set shows a mature HNBR technology space balancing saturation efficiency, molecular-weight control, and catalyst-residue management.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9657113B2 is the only aqueous-latex hydrogenation patent in the set; the others with direct synthesis evidence are solution-phase in monochlorobenzene or acetone-based solution handling.
- US9657113B2 achieves 98-99% hydrogenation in 0.5-10 h depending on catalyst loading, temperature, and pressure, while US9150669B2 / EP2473281B1 report residual double-bond contents down to 0.4-0.7% after 240 min in simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis.
- US20210340285A1 and EP3827029A1 emphasize catalyst residue control and ageing performance rather than reporting a specific hydrogenation degree for the target HNBR in the extracted evidence.
- US7846995B2 is a downstream purification patent: it treats already hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions to remove Ru residues, not to perform hydrogenation.
- EP2868676A1 and EP3063192B1 are formulation/property patents that define hydrogenation thresholds for HNBR-type copolymers (50% or more, preferably 75% or 85% or more) but do not disclose a synthesis route in the extracted evidence.
- The metathesis-assisted patents (US9150669B2 and EP2473281B1) uniquely couple hydrogenation with molecular-weight reduction, yielding lower Mw and lower Mooney viscosity than the comparison catalyst system.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, HNBR is produced by two dominant routes: direct hydrogenation of NBR latex in water and solution-phase hydrogenation in organic solvent, including one-pot hydrogenation/metathesis systems that simultaneously lower molecular weight. The strongest synthesis disclosures are US9657113B2, which demonstrates near-quantitative latex hydrogenation with HG2 catalyst, and US9150669B2 / EP2473281B1, which show that Ru/Os catalyst systems can drive residual double bonds to very low levels while reducing Mw and Mooney viscosity. Other patents in the set focus on catalyst-residue control, ageing stability, or formulation/property definitions for hydrogenated nitrile copolymers, reinforcing that HNBR manufacturing is tightly coupled to downstream purification and performance requirements. The extracted evidence supports a broad target hydrogenation window from at least 50% to near-complete saturation, with practical examples reaching 98-99% hydrogenation or residual double-bond contents below 1%.
