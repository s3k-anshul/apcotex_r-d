# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SYNTHESIS, COMPOSITION CONTROL, AND APPLICATION-DRIVEN FORMULATION TRENDS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) with emphasis on low-acrylonitrile embodiments and adjacent NBR manufacturing/formulation strategies. The selected landscape spans one electro-conductive member patent that explicitly uses NBR as a matrix/domain rubber system, and three NBR composition patents focused on hydrogenated NBR, NBR/PVC blends, and NBR-based rubber-metal laminates. Although not all patents are direct low-ACN synthesis disclosures, each contributes evidence on how acrylonitrile content is selected, constrained, or leveraged in NBR materials design.

Across the set, the most explicit acrylonitrile-content disclosures appear in the hydrogenated NBR composition patent, which defines hydrogenated NBRs at 25–44 wt% acrylonitrile content and further narrows preferred embodiments to 30–40 wt%. The electro-conductive member patent discloses NBR as one of the paired diene rubbers in a matrix/domain architecture, with examples listing commercial NBR grades spanning 15.0–41.5 wt% nitrile content, including a low-nitrile grade at 15%. The NBR/PVC sealing rubber patent centers on a polymer blend containing NBR and PVC, with a PVC content window of 15.70–17.50 wt% and examples using a high-nitrile NBR polymer at 42.5 wt% nitrile content. The rubber-metal laminate patent uses an NBR polymer with 34 wt% nitrile content and emphasizes carbon-black architecture and solvent-based coating formulation rather than monomer synthesis.

From a process perspective, the patents are dominated by compounding, kneading, vulcanization, and laminate/coating operations rather than direct NBR polymerization. The only synthesis-adjacent NBR identity control is through selection of commercial NBR grades or blends with specified nitrile content. Major formulation trends include sulfur or peroxide curing, use of plasticizers, carbon black/silica reinforcement, and careful tuning of filler type and amount to balance oil resistance, compression set, surface smoothness, and mechanical recovery. Water is not a polymerization medium in these disclosures; when present, it is only implicit or absent from the extracted evidence. Overall, the evidence supports low-ACN NBR as a composition-selection problem more than a new polymerization route problem in the retrieved patents.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7772305B2 is the only patent that explicitly defines an acrylonitrile-content window for the target material, at 25–44 wt% AN, with a preferred 30–40 wt% range; its examples center on 36 wt% AN hydrogenated NBR.
- US9688834B2 discloses a conventional NBR polymer at 34% nitrile content in a laminate formulation, placing it within the low-to-mid ACN range but without a synthesis claim.
- US11347156B2 lists multiple commercial NBR grades spanning 15% to 41.5% nitrile content, including a low-nitrile 15% grade, but uses them as functional rubbers in a matrix/domain electro-conductive member rather than as a synthesis target.
- US8846804B2 uses a high nitrile NBR polymer at 42.5% nitrile content and an NBR/PVC blend polymer; it is composition-focused and does not disclose a low-ACN synthesis route.
- All four patents are compounding- and performance-driven rather than monomer-feed polymerization disclosures; none provide direct emulsion or solution copolymerization conditions for making low-ACN NBR.
- Water is absent from the extracted process evidence for all four patents; the disclosed operations are kneading, solvent dissolution, molding, coating, and vulcanization.
- The strongest low-ACN-relevant numerical evidence is the 15% nitrile NBR grade in US11347156B2 and the 34% nitrile NBR in US9688834B2, while US7772305B2 provides the clearest target window for hydrogenated NBR compositions.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The retrieved patents indicate that low-acrylonitrile NBR is treated primarily as a composition-selection and formulation problem rather than a newly disclosed polymerization process. The most directly useful target window in the set is the 25–44 wt% acrylonitrile range in US7772305B2, with practical examples around 36 wt% AN, while US11347156B2 and US9688834B2 demonstrate that commercial NBR grades at 15–34% nitrile content are actively used in functional rubber systems. For synthesis-oriented work, the evidence does not provide direct copolymerization conditions; therefore, recommended parameters must be inferred cautiously from the disclosed material selections: use NBR grades in the low-to-mid ACN range, control filler and plasticizer balance, and select curing systems matched to the intended application. The patents collectively support sulfur or peroxide curing, careful carbon-black architecture, and solvent-based or solid-state compounding as the dominant downstream processing strategies for low-ACN NBR materials.
