# PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT: HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS, DEGRADATION, AND POST-TREATMENT TECHNOLOGIES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes nine patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on routes that create, modify, or purify hydrogenated NBR materials. The target material is defined as nitrile rubber derived from conjugated dienes and α,β-unsaturated nitriles that has been partially to highly hydrogenated to reduce residual unsaturation and improve heat, ozone, oil, and aging resistance. Across the patent set, two major technology directions emerge. First, several patents focus on controlled molecular-weight reduction of NBR prior to hydrogenation by olefin metathesis using ruthenium catalysts, co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, and solution-phase processing in monochlorobenzene. These routes aim to produce lower-Mooney, narrower-distribution HNBR suitable for seals, hoses, and other molded articles. Second, other patents address formulation and post-processing of HNBR, including partially hydrogenated NBR blended with silicone rubber, peroxide-cured HNBR compositions containing microgels and unsaturated carboxylic acids, and catalyst-residue removal using functionalized ion-exchange resins. A separate catalyst-system patent shows that alkaline-earth metal chlorides can accelerate metathesis of NBR, while another demonstrates that salt additives can strongly influence metathesis efficiency and molecular-weight reduction. The landscape also includes a metathesis-catalyst patent that explicitly claims modified NBR depolymerization and hydrogenation uses, underscoring the centrality of ruthenium carbene catalysts for HNBR precursor modification. Overall, the evidence points to solution metathesis as the dominant precursor-engineering strategy, with hydrogenation typically performed using Wilkinson-type Rh catalysts or related homogeneous systems. Process trends include high-temperature solution reactions, inert-atmosphere handling, catalyst loading optimization, and downstream purification to reduce residual Ru. Formulation trends emphasize balancing low-temperature flexibility, compression set, and high-temperature durability through selective hydrogenation level control and reinforcing or compatibilizing additives.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 both disclose solution-phase NBR metathesis followed by hydrogenation, using monochlorobenzene, ethylene or 1-hexene co-olefins, and high-pressure hydrogenation with Wilkinson-type catalyst systems.
- Both low-molecular-weight HNBR patents report the same substrate composition basis: 34 mol % acrylonitrile NBR with Mooney viscosity 35, but they differ in emphasis on shaped-article utility versus polymer-property optimization.
- EP2580260A1 shifts from synthesis to formulation, using partially hydrogenated NBR in silicone blends and emphasizing low-solvent processing and compression-set improvement rather than precursor depolymerization.
- US9321910B2 and US5569719A both focus on compounding/vulcanization of HNBR, but US9321910B2 uses microgels and peroxide/unsaturated-carboxylic-acid systems, whereas US5569719A uses sulfur vulcanization with tellurium dithiocarbamate promoter.
- US8957161B2 and US7737233B2 both address metathesis degradation of NBR with ruthenium catalysts; US7737233B2 specifically claims alkaline-earth chloride additives, while US8957161B2 compares multiple salts including LiBr, LiCl, LiI, and CaCl2.
- US7846995B2 is distinct in targeting post-reaction purification, showing that HNBR made by metathesis/hydrogenation can be treated with functionalized ion-exchange resins to reduce residual Ru.
- Across the metathesis-focused patents, solution chlorobenzene/monochlorobenzene is the dominant solvent, room temperature or 80°C are common metathesis conditions, and catalyst loading is typically expressed in phr or mg scale.
- Hydrogenation-specific disclosure is strongest in US5569719A and the low-molecular-weight HNBR patents, where hydrogenation level is linked to iodine value, residual double bonds, and final Mooney viscosity.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that hydrogenated NBR is most effectively accessed through controlled precursor engineering of NBR by metathesis, followed by hydrogenation and, where needed, purification or formulation tailoring. The strongest synthesis evidence supports solution metathesis of NBR in monochlorobenzene/chlorobenzene with ruthenium carbene catalysts, co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, and subsequent hydrogenation under high H2 pressure using Wilkinson-type catalysts. For property control, the evidence favors low residual unsaturation, lower Mooney viscosity, and narrower molecular-weight distribution; in the formulation space, partial hydrogenation levels around 95% and careful promoter/additive selection improve heat build-up, compression set, and high-temperature durability. Recommended evidence-based synthesis parameters are: metathesis in monochlorobenzene at about 80° C. or room temperature with controlled catalyst loading, hydrogenation at 1200 psi H2 and 138° C. when using homogeneous Rh catalysts, and post-treatment to reduce residual Ru when catalyst carryover is a concern.
