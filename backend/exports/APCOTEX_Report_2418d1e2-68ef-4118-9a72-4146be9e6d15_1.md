# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT HNBR PROCESS LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, with emphasis on routes that control residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and processability. The target material is HNBR derived from nitrile rubber (NBR), typically a copolymer of a conjugated diene and an α,β-unsaturated nitrile, optionally containing additional comonomers. Across the selected patents, two major manufacturing themes emerge. The first is direct hydrogenation of NBR, including reactor/process innovations such as spinning disk reactors and homogeneous hydrogenation catalysts, aimed at lowering hydrogenation severity, reducing equipment cost, and improving throughput. The second is a one-step or tandem metathesis/hydrogenation strategy that simultaneously cleaves unsaturation and saturates the polymer backbone to produce low-molecular-weight HNBR with narrower molecular weight distributions and low residual double bond (RDB) content.

The landscape shows that conventional batch hydrogenation of NBR/HNBR is constrained by mass-transfer limitations, high hydrogen pressures, and viscosity increase during hydrogenation. Patents addressing this problem disclose hydrogen pressures from 3.5 to 100 bar in spinning disk reactors, and in prior art discussions note typical batch pressures of 65 to 90 bar. Other disclosures focus on catalyst selection, including rhodium- and ruthenium-based homogeneous systems, Wilkinson-type catalysts, and metathesis-active Ru/osmium complexes used in the presence of hydrogen and optional co-olefins. A parallel set of patents addresses downstream purification, especially removal of ruthenium residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using functionalized ion-exchange resins.

Formulation and application trends also appear: low-Mooney HNBR grades are targeted for seals, hoses, bearing pads, and flexible pipes, while peroxide-vulcanized HNBR compounds with controlled scorch time are used for bonding to PVDF liners in aggressive oilfield service. Overall, the evidence supports synthesis routes that combine controlled precursor composition, selective hydrogenation or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation, and post-treatment purification to achieve low-RDB, low-Mooney HNBR suitable for demanding elastomer applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7666950B2 focuses on direct hydrogenation of NBR in a spinning disk reactor, whereas US7951875B2, US7772328B2, EP2473281B1, and US9150669B2 emphasize metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction combined with hydrogenation.
- EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 are the clearest single-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation disclosures; both target Mw 20,000 to 250,000, Mooney 1 to 50, and low MWD, with hydrogen present and optional co-olefin.
- US7951875B2 and US7772328B2 disclose very similar experimental conditions: 15 wt% or 6 wt% cement, 80°C reactor temperature, 0.05-0.25 phr catalyst loadings, 6.9×10^5 Pa ethylene, and 1-hexene as an alternative co-olefin.
- The metathesis/hydrogenation patents report substantial property reductions, including Mw down to 59,000 and Mooney viscosity down to 3 in the experimental series, showing stronger molecular-weight control than direct hydrogenation-only process claims.
- US7846995B2 is a downstream purification patent rather than a synthesis patent; it addresses Ru residue removal from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using ion-exchange resin, which complements the Ru-catalyzed synthesis routes.
- US7923518B2 is a precursor-quality patent: it controls NBR coagulation residues, calcium/chlorine content, and storage stability, which can influence hydrogenation feedstock quality but does not itself hydrogenate the polymer.
- US10641418B2 is application-driven rather than synthesis-driven; it uses HNBR with below 2% RDB and 30-40% acrylonitrile in peroxide-vulcanized bonded pipe layers, highlighting the property requirements that synthesis must meet.
- Across the set, the most common precursor composition is NBR based on butadiene and acrylonitrile, with optional comonomers such as fumaric acid, maleic acid, acrylic acid, methacrylic acid, and alkyl acrylates/methacrylates.
- Catalyst trends split between homogeneous hydrogenation catalysts for direct HNBR formation and Ru/osmium metathesis-active complexes for tandem molecular-weight reduction plus hydrogenation; post-treatment purification then removes residual Ru.
- Process trends show that lower solids content, controlled temperature, and catalyst loading are used to tune Mw, PDI, and RDB, while downstream applications demand low Mooney, low RDB, and peroxide-curable formulations.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows two dominant synthesis strategies for HNBR: direct selective hydrogenation of NBR, and tandem metathesis/hydrogenation to generate low-molecular-weight HNBR with narrow molecular weight distribution. For direct hydrogenation, the most process-relevant disclosure is the spinning disk reactor approach with hydrogen pressures from 3.5 to 100 bar, which is intended to reduce mass-transfer limitations and equipment cost. For low-Mooney HNBR, the strongest evidence supports simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation in the presence of hydrogen and Ru- or osmium-based catalysts, with target products in the Mw 20,000 to 250,000 range, Mooney 1 to 50, and low residual double bond content. Experimental data indicate that catalyst loading, solids content, co-olefin choice, and temperature materially affect Mw, PDI, and RDB. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are: use butadiene/acrylonitrile-based NBR precursors, maintain controlled precursor purity and storage stability, apply Ru-based simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation when low molecular weight is desired, and follow with ruthenium residue removal by ion exchange when necessary. For end-use performance, HNBR with acrylonitrile content around 30-40% and residual double bond content below 2% is consistent with demanding oilfield and flexible-pipe applications.
