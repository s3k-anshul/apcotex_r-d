# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS, MOLECULAR-WEIGHT CONTROL, AND CATALYST-REMOVAL PATENT LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how NBR precursors are converted, modified, or post-treated to improve processability, residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and catalyst cleanliness. The target material is hydrogenated NBR, including partially and highly hydrogenated grades, where the key performance drivers are saturation level, thermal stability, oil resistance, and manageable Mooney viscosity. Across the patent set, two major technical themes emerge. First, several patents address direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution using homogeneous or heterogeneous transition-metal catalysts, with explicit attention to hydrogen pressure, temperature, catalyst composition, and co-catalyst pretreatment to control hydrogenation rate and suppress gelation or excessive molecular-weight drift. Second, other patents use metathesis or segment-interchange chemistry to reduce molecular weight before or during hydrogenation, thereby producing lower-viscosity HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distributions and improved processability. A third theme is downstream purification: one patent focuses on removing residual hydrogenation catalyst from HNBR cements by chelation, semi-coagulation, and washing, while another addresses phosphine/phosphine oxide and halogen residues in HNBR made with phosphine-containing hydrogenation catalysts. The landscape also includes application-driven disclosures where HNBR is selected for swellable packers, transmission belts, and other elastomeric systems, reinforcing the importance of controlled hydrogenation degree and low residual impurities. Overall, the evidence indicates that the most effective HNBR synthesis strategies combine controlled catalyst chemistry, moderate-to-high hydrogenation pressure, elevated solution-phase temperatures, and post-hydrogenation purification or pre-hydrogenation molecular-weight reduction to achieve a balance of saturation, low Mooney viscosity, and robust end-use performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7951875B2 and US7772328B2 are closely aligned: both use metathesis-first, hydrogenation-second processing to lower HNBR molecular weight and Mooney viscosity, with ethylene or 1-hexene as co-olefin and 80 °C metathesis conditions.
- US10508156B2 differs from the metathesis-first patents by focusing on direct hydrogenation of NBR at 120-140 °C and 4.137-8.4 MPa H2, using Ru/Os-type catalyst compositions and vinyl ether pretreatment to improve hydrogenation efficiency.
- EP2057205B1 uses partially hydrogenated HNBR as a metathesis partner to build meta-block copolymers, showing that HNBR can be a reactive intermediate rather than only a final product.
- US20190119411A1 and EP3090002B1 are downstream purification/control patents: the former removes palladium residues by chelation and semi-coagulation, while the latter manages phosphine/phosphine oxide and halogen residues from hydrogenation catalyst systems.
- US10385647B2 is formulation-driven rather than synthesis-driven, but it confirms that HNBR grades with about 70-91% hydrogenation and 9-30% unsaturation are selected for swellable packers requiring high-temperature and high-pressure performance.
- US11480231B2 also is application-focused and references HNBR as a suitable elastomer for high-temperature, in-oil belt service, but it does not disclose synthesis conditions.
- US8013215B2 and US9896424B2 are outside the HNBR synthesis space; they do not teach nitrile rubber hydrogenation, though they contain general polymer or functionalization chemistry.
- Across the HNBR-relevant patents, the most repeated process variables are elevated temperature, solvent-based processing, transition-metal catalysts, and post-reaction purification or molecular-weight control to balance saturation with processability.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports three practical synthesis directions for HNBR: direct solution hydrogenation of NBR with Ru/Os/Rh-type catalysts under elevated hydrogen pressure and temperature; metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction before hydrogenation to obtain lower-Mooney, narrower-distribution HNBR; and rigorous post-hydrogenation purification to remove catalyst residues and phosphine-derived impurities. For process development, the strongest evidence favors hydrogenation around 120-140 °C and 4.137-8.4 MPa H2, with catalyst pretreatment using vinyl ethyl ether or related co-catalysts to improve activity and reduce catalyst loading. Where low viscosity is critical, metathesis with ethylene or 1-hexene at about 80 °C before hydrogenation is a validated route. For end-use quality, residual catalyst removal by chelation/semi-coagulation and control of phosphine/phosphine oxide residues are important to preserve vulcanizate performance. Overall, the evidence points to a combined strategy of controlled hydrogenation degree, molecular-weight management, and residue cleanup as the best route to high-performance HNBR.
