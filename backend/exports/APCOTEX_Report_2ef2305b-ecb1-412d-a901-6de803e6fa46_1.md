# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: EMULSION, SOLUTION, AND FUNCTIONALIZED STYRENE-BUTADIENE COPOLYMER ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes ten primary patents relevant to styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) preparation, with emphasis on low-styrene embodiments and process routes that control styrene incorporation while preserving processability and tire performance. The selected patents span aqueous emulsion polymerization, organic-solvent solution polymerization, and post-polymerization functionalization/oil-extension strategies. Across the landscape, the target material is consistently a styrene-butadiene copolymer or elastomer, but the disclosed styrene levels vary widely: some patents explicitly use styrene in the 10–50 wt% range or around 25 wt%, while others disclose lower or moderate styrene fractions in solution SBR feeds (e.g., 30–40 wt% styrene in monomer charge) and functionalized emulsion SBRs with styrene around 21 wt% in the final polymer. Several patents focus less on lowering styrene per se and more on improving silica affinity, wet traction, rolling resistance, or processability through reactive emulsifiers, reactive polyols, epoxy-acrylate comonomers, silane modifiers, or oil extension. The dominant emulsion routes use water as the polymerization medium with rosin/fatty-acid emulsifier systems, redox or peroxide initiators, mercaptan chain-transfer agents, and low-temperature batch operation near 7–10 °C. The solution routes use hydrocarbon solvents such as hexane or cyclohexane, organolithium initiation, polar modifiers, and in some cases branching or coupling chemistry, followed by solvent removal and oil extension. Downstream water use is common in emulsion patents for coagulation, washing, and drying, while solution patents use water mainly for steam stripping or solvent removal. Overall, the patent set shows that low-styrene SBR development is not limited to a single synthesis platform; rather, it is achieved through careful monomer feed design and by integrating functional comonomers or post-modification steps that compensate for reduced styrene content with improved filler interaction and tire-relevant performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Emulsion-route patents (US8530017B2, US9328176B2, US8455600B2, EP2986647B1) all use water as the polymerization medium and typically rely on rosin/fatty-acid emulsifiers, peroxide/redox initiators, mercaptan chain-transfer agents, and low temperatures around 7-10 °C.
- Solution-route patents (US6858683B2, US20140256858A1, EP2657262B1) use hydrocarbon solvents such as cyclohexane or hexane, organolithium initiation, and solvent stripping; water appears only in downstream stripping/workup.
- The explicitly low-styrene feed disclosure among the primary SBR synthesis patents is strongest in US6858683B2, where a continuous solution example uses 3 wt% styrene in the feed; EP2657262B1 uses 30 wt% styrene feed and US20140256858A1 uses 40 wt% styrene feed.
- Functionalization strategies differ: US8530017B2 uses reactive emulsifiers, US9328176B2 uses epoxy-acrylate monomer plus aqueous ring-opening, US8455600B2 uses reactive polyols, and EP2986647B1 uses GMA plus vegetable-oil extension.
- US20170260340A1 and EP2424740B1 are downstream compounding/masterbatch or silane-modification patents rather than SBR synthesis patents; they are relevant to processing and filler interaction but do not define low-styrene SBR synthesis conditions.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, low-styrene SBR is realized mainly through emulsion or solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene with careful feed control, then enhanced by functional comonomers, reactive emulsifiers, or post-polymerization extension to recover performance lost by reducing styrene. The most explicit low-styrene feed disclosure appears in the solution-process patent US6858683B2, while the emulsion patents emphasize silica affinity and tire performance through functionalization rather than extreme styrene reduction. Water is central in the emulsion patents and appears only as a downstream stripping/workup medium in the solution patents.
