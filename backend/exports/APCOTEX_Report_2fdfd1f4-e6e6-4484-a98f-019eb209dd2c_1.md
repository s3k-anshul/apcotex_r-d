# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION CONTROL, LOW-VISCOSITY NBR PROCESSING, AND NBR-BASED FUNCTIONAL ARTICLES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related NBR processing routes. The target is low-ACN NBR, generally understood as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile incorporation relative to conventional grades, where composition control is used to tune oil resistance, flexibility, and processability. The patent set spans two synthesis-oriented disclosures and two application/process disclosures. EP2473279B1 focuses on post-polymerization olefin metathesis of nitrile rubber followed by hydrogenation to generate low-molecular-weight, low-Mooney HNBR with improved processability; although not a direct low-ACN synthesis patent, it is highly relevant because it addresses the downstream processing challenge often associated with low-ACN feedstocks and low-viscosity NBR-derived materials. EP2561011B1 discloses a composite-material process in which a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer (NIPOL 1312V) is introduced as a nitrile resin base into a BUSS co-kneader with carbon nanotubes and graphene, demonstrating continuous compounding at defined temperatures and screw speed. US10443339B2 is an elastomeric-material patent centered on carbon-based nanoplatelets in bore-tool elastomers; it is only indirectly relevant to NBR because it explicitly references NBR/HNBR among candidate elastomers but does not disclose NBR synthesis. US20200299532A1 discloses antistatic glove manufacture using nitrile rubber/natural rubber blends, including multilayer latex dipping and chlorination, and provides a practical application context for nitrile rubber formulations where lower acrylonitrile content can be desirable for softness and processing balance. Across the set, the dominant trends are composition blending, continuous mixing/compounding, and property-driven formulation rather than direct monomer-feed synthesis. The evidence most strongly supports low-ACN NBR development through controlled butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymerization in emulsion, followed by optional metathesis/hydrogenation or downstream blending into functional articles and composites.

While it does not change ACN content directly, it is important for making low-ACN NBR-derived materials easier to compound and use.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473279B1 is the only patent in the set that directly addresses nitrile-rubber molecular-weight reduction by metathesis/hydrogenation; the others are application or compounding disclosures rather than direct NBR synthesis.
- EP2561011B1 and US20200299532A1 both use nitrile rubber in formulated products, but EP2561011B1 emphasizes continuous high-shear compounding with CNT/graphene, whereas US20200299532A1 emphasizes latex blending, dipping, chlorination, and curing for antistatic gloves.
- US10443339B2 is the least synthesis-relevant to low-ACN NBR; it mentions NBR/HNBR as candidate elastomers for high-temperature bore tools but focuses on nanoplatelet reinforcement and service environment rather than monomer ratio or polymerization.
- Only EP2561011B1 explicitly discloses a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer in a process context, with a nitrile resin base injected at 190°C and compounded at 200°C, while US20200299532A1 uses nitrile rubber latex blends with natural rubber to tune comfort and antistatic behavior.
- The disclosed process temperatures span from latex dipping conditions near 40-135°C in US20200299532A1 to 160-200°C compounding in EP2561011B1 and 138°C hydrogenation in EP2473279B1, showing that the patent set covers both low-temperature latex processing and higher-temperature melt/solution processing.
- EP2473279B1 provides the strongest evidence for low-viscosity nitrile-rubber processing, with Mw reduced to 34,000 g/mol and PDI to 1.78 in Example 7, which is directly useful for improving processability of low-ACN NBR-derived materials.
- US20200299532A1 shows that nitrile content can be reduced in multilayer glove structures by blending nitrile rubber with natural rubber, but it does not disclose acrylonitrile monomer content or synthesis feed ratios.
- Across the set, no patent explicitly discloses a low-acrylonitrile monomer feed ratio for NBR polymerization; the evidence is strongest for downstream composition control and processability rather than direct low-ACN copolymer synthesis.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence does not provide a direct low-acrylonitrile NBR polymerization recipe, but it does identify the most relevant process directions for making and using low-ACN nitrile rubber materials. The strongest synthesis-adjacent guidance comes from EP2473279B1, which demonstrates metathesis/hydrogenation to lower molecular weight and improve processability, and from EP2561011B1, which shows that nitrile rubber can be continuously compounded with fillers and functional additives at controlled temperatures and flow rates. US20200299532A1 confirms that nitrile-rich latex can be blended with natural rubber to tune comfort, antistatic behavior, and interlayer adhesion in finished articles. Based on the evidence, the most defensible recommended parameters for low-ACN NBR development are controlled butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymerization followed by careful molecular-weight management, with downstream processing in the approximate 160-200°C range for compounding or 40-135°C for latex article formation, depending on the product form. For low-viscosity, easily processable nitrile rubber, the evidence supports targeting low Mw and narrow PDI rather than relying on unsubstantiated changes in acrylonitrile content alone.
