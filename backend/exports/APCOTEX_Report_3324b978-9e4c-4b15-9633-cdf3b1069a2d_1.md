# HYDROGENATED NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT HNBR, SIMULTANEOUS METATHESIS/HYDROGENATION, AND POST-PROCESSING PURIFICATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) synthesis, process intensification, and downstream purification. The target material is HNBR derived from nitrile rubber precursors containing conjugated diene and α,β-unsaturated nitrile units, with emphasis on reduced residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil/ozone resistance, and improved processability through lower molecular weight or controlled hydrogenation. Across the patent set, two major synthetic strategies dominate: conventional hydrogenation of preformed NBR using heterogeneous or homogeneous hydrogenation catalysts, and one-step or coupled metathesis/hydrogenation routes that simultaneously reduce molecular weight and saturate double bonds. A third theme is post-synthesis purification, especially removal of ruthenium catalyst residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber.

The patents show that HNBR feedstocks are typically prepared from butadiene/isoprene/acrylonitrile-based NBR, with some disclosures extending to copolymers containing additional monomers or functionalized architectures. Hydrogenation catalysts include palladium on carbon, Wilkinson-type rhodium systems, and ruthenium-based catalysts such as Grubbs II/III, Hoveyda, and Arlt-type complexes. For low-molecular-weight HNBR, the key process trend is simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in the presence of hydrogen and optionally a co-olefin, enabling a single-step reduction in molecular weight while lowering residual double-bond content. Reported process windows include elevated temperatures around 80–100 °C, hydrogen pressures around 5.0 kgf/cm2 or 500 psi in the examples, and catalyst loadings in the 0.041–0.25 phr range depending on catalyst system.

The landscape also includes a purification patent using functionalized ion-exchange resins to remove ruthenium residues from hydrogenated or optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber solutions, addressing a practical manufacturing bottleneck for metathesis-derived HNBR. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that combine controlled NBR precursor design, selective hydrogenation or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation, and catalyst-residue cleanup to achieve low Mooney viscosity, narrow molecular weight distribution, and low residual unsaturation suitable for seals, hoses, and other demanding elastomer applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 both target low-molecular-weight HNBR via metathesis-based processing, but US7772328B2 explicitly performs hydrogenation and metathesis simultaneously in hydrogen, whereas US7919563B2 emphasizes metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction with optional hydrogenation context.
- EP2473281B1 and US9079979B2 are the core simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis patents; both use ruthenium/osmium catalyst families and target Mw about 20,000 to 250,000 with low Mooney viscosity, but US9079979B2 provides more explicit comparative catalyst data for Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson's catalyst.
- US10759885B2 is structurally related but not NBR-based; it uses solution polymerization of branched diene/styrene copolymers followed by hydrogenation, showing a conventional precursor-hydrogenation route rather than metathesis of NBR.
- EP2623518A1 differs from the core HNBR synthesis patents because it focuses on RAFT-made alkyne-functional NBR and click coupling, yet it still provides a hydrogenatable nitrile-rubber precursor platform and detailed radical polymerization conditions.
- US7846995B2 is not a synthesis patent but a purification patent; it addresses removal of ruthenium residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber, which is especially relevant to the metathesis/hydrogenation routes in EP2473281B1 and US9079979B2.
- Across the set, hydrogenation temperatures cluster around 80° C. in the metathesis/hydrogenation examples and 100° C. in the RAFT polymerization/coupling examples, while catalyst loadings are reported in phr for metathesis/hydrogenation and in mg/mmol for radical polymerization systems.
- Co-olefin use is explicit in the metathesis-based HNBR patents, with ethylene and 1-hexene appearing as chain-transfer/co-reactant options; this is absent from the conventional hydrogenation-only and purification disclosures.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports three practical routes to HNBR-related materials: conventional hydrogenation of NBR precursors, simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation for one-step molecular-weight reduction, and post-reaction purification of ruthenium residues. For direct HNBR synthesis, the strongest evidence favors ruthenium-based simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation in hydrogen, optionally with a co-olefin, at about 80° C. and catalyst loadings in the low-phr range, because this route consistently lowers Mw, Mooney viscosity, and residual double bond content. For precursor design, NBR feedstocks with controlled Mooney viscosity and butadiene/acrylonitrile composition remain important, while functionalized NBR routes can extend downstream modification options. A practical synthesis recommendation from the patent set is to use a ruthenium catalyst capable of both metathesis and hydrogenation, maintain hydrogen pressure in the disclosed range, and include a catalyst-removal step such as functionalized ion-exchange treatment to reduce residual Ru to low ppm levels.
