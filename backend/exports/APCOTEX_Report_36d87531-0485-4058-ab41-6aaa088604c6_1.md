# LOW-STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION, EMULSION, FUNCTIONALIZATION, AND BINDER-FORMULATION ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes nine primary patents relevant to low-styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) synthesis, modification, and downstream use. The target is a styrene-butadiene elastomer biased toward butadiene, typically with reduced styrene fraction relative to conventional SBR grades, while preserving processability and performance. Across the selected patents, the dominant technical themes are solution anionic copolymerization in hydrocarbon solvent, emulsion/latex polymerization in water, post-polymerization functionalization or grafting, and formulation strategies that improve handling or end-use properties without increasing styrene content. Several patents explicitly disclose styrene-butadiene copolymers with styrene contents in the low-to-moderate range, including 3 wt%, 20.2 wt%, 21.3 wt%, 21.7 wt%, 22.1 wt%, 25 wt%, 25.7 wt%, and 27.0 wt%, depending on embodiment and application. The landscape also includes patents where SBR is not the central synthetic target but is used as a benchmark binder or rubber component in a broader formulation context.

The major polymerization approaches are: (1) solution anionic polymerization in nonpolar hydrocarbon solvents such as cyclohexane, hexane, methylcyclohexane, or n-hexane, often with lithium alkyl initiators, randomizers/polar additives, and post-polymerization coupling or branching; (2) aqueous emulsion polymerization for cross-linked SBR microsphere binders, using deionized water, emulsifiers, oxidizing/reducing systems, and extensive demulsification/washing workup; and (3) grafting or functionalization of preformed SBR to improve filler interaction, processability, or battery-binder performance. Process trends include precise control of monomer feed ratio, conversion, temperature, and residence time; use of chain-end modification or branching agents to tune macrostructure; and downstream water-based coagulation, stripping, washing, or extraction steps. Overall, the selected patents show that low-styrene SBR development is driven less by a single composition window than by integrated control of polymer microstructure, branching, and post-treatment to achieve tire or binder performance with reduced styrene fraction.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Solution-polymerized SBR patents (US6858683B2, EP3030430B1, US12037432B2, US5902856A) generally use hydrocarbon solvents and lithium-based initiators, while US12129321B2 is the only clearly aqueous emulsion/latex route among the selected set.
- The lowest explicitly disclosed styrene levels in the selected primary patents are 3 wt% feed in US6858683B2 and 7.0 wt% in US7312264B2; several other patents sit in the 20-27 wt% range, including 21.7 wt% (US7612144B2), 25 wt% (EP3030430B1), 25.7 wt% (US7312264B2 starting SBR), and 27.0 wt% (US12037432B2).
- US12129321B2 uses deionized water as the polymerization medium and then salt coagulation, acid treatment, extraction, and washing, whereas the solution patents rely on steam stripping, solvent removal, or coagulation in alcohol.
- Initiator systems vary substantially: n-butyllithium and related organolithiums dominate the solution patents, sulfur-containing lithio initiators are central in US7612144B2, and peroxide/redox emulsion initiation is used in US12129321B2.
- Post-polymerization workup is a major trend across the set: steam stripping and water squeezing in US5902856A and EP3030430B1, hot-water solvent removal in US12037432B2, and coagulation/drying in alcohol for US7612144B2 and US7829624B2.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, low-styrene SBR is represented mainly through solution-polymerization patents that tightly control monomer feed, conversion, and post-treatment, plus one aqueous emulsion binder patent. The most directly low-styrene disclosures are the 3 wt% styrene feed in US6858683B2 and the 7.0 wt% styrene starting polymer in US7312264B2, while several other patents disclose SBR in the 20-27 wt% styrene range and focus more on processability, branching, or functionalization than on lowering styrene itself. Overall, the landscape emphasizes solvent-based anionic polymerization with lithium initiators, followed by branching, coupling, grafting, or stabilization, and a separate waterborne latex route for microsphere binders.
