# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE AND SYNTHESIS REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews two primary patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) synthesis and composition control. The target is defined as SBR materials with reduced styrene incorporation, typically achieved by controlling the butadiene-to-styrene feed ratio and the polymerization route. The patent landscape spans two distinct approaches: a solution-polymerized hydroxyl-terminated SBR used as a soft segment in polyurethane elastomers, and a patent explicitly directed to preparing SBR having low combined styrene content. Together, they show that low-styrene SBR can be addressed either as a standalone polymerization objective or as a tailored intermediate for downstream elastomer systems.

US20190112458A1 discloses an anionic solution polymerization route using a dilithium initiator, cyclohexane/tetrahydrofuran solvent system, and ethylene oxide end-capping to produce hydroxyl-terminated SBR. The examples vary the butadiene-to-styrene ratio, active lithium ratio, temperature, and reaction time, yielding SBR soft segments with molecular weights from 1000 to 8000 and glass-transition temperatures around -20 to -30 °C. Although the patent is framed around polyurethane elastomers, it provides explicit solution-polymerization recipes and styrene feed control that are directly relevant to low-styrene SBR design.

EP3243844A4 is more directly aligned with the target, as its title and description explicitly state a method for preparing styrene-butadiene rubber having low combined styrene content. The extracted evidence is largely bibliographic and classification-based, but it clearly identifies aqueous emulsion polymerization as the process family and low combined styrene content as the objective. Overall, the evidence suggests that low styrene SBR synthesis is primarily governed by monomer feed ratio control, polymerization route selection, and downstream end-use requirements such as tire performance, with solution and emulsion routes both represented in the patent set.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US20190112458A1 uses solution polymerization in cyclohexane with a dilithium initiator and ethylene oxide end-capping, while EP3243844A4 is classified under aqueous emulsion polymerization with emulsifying agents.
- Both patents target styrene-content reduction, but US20190112458A1 discloses explicit butadiene:styrene feed ratios of 100:25 to 100:35, whereas EP3243844A4 provides only title/classification evidence for low combined styrene content without numerical recipe data in the extracted block.
- US20190112458A1 includes detailed temperatures of 45-55 °C for SBR synthesis and 0 °C for initiator formation; EP3243844A4 does not expose temperatures, pressures, initiators, or chain-transfer agents in the extracted evidence.
- US20190112458A1 uses post-polymerization acid treatment and ethanol precipitation, while EP3243844A4 does not disclose coagulation or workup steps in the extracted evidence.
- The two patents represent complementary routes to low-styrene SBR: a solution-polymerized hydroxyl-terminated SBR intermediate versus a direct low-combined-styrene emulsion SBR process.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two viable low-styrene SBR synthesis directions: explicit solution polymerization with controlled butadiene/styrene feed ratio, and aqueous emulsion polymerization aimed directly at low combined styrene content. The strongest actionable parameters come from US20190112458A1, where butadiene:styrene ratios of 100:25 to 100:35, synthesis temperatures of 45-55 °C, and controlled active-lithium ratios are disclosed. EP3243844A4 confirms that low combined styrene content is also pursued via emulsion polymerization, but the extracted evidence lacks detailed operating conditions. For low styrene SBR development, the best-supported synthesis strategy is to tightly control monomer feed ratio and polymerization route, with solution polymerization offering the most explicit recipe data in the provided corpus.
