# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION-POLYMERIZED AND MODIFIED STYRENE-BUTADIENE COPOLYMERS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents centered on styrene-butadiene rubber/copolymer materials relevant to low styrene SBR. The selected patents span two major synthesis families: solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene in hydrocarbon solvent, and post-polymerization modification of solution-made styrene-butadiene copolymers with functional modifiers. Across the set, the target material is not a block styrenic elastomer but a random or tapered styrene-butadiene copolymer designed for tire-reinforcement performance, with styrene content explicitly controlled in the low-to-moderate range. One patent discloses a low molecular weight tapered styrene-butadiene copolymer with total styrene content of 20 to 30 wt% and a random/tapered sequence distribution intended to replace process oil in rubber compounds. Another patent discloses a modified styrene-butadiene copolymer prepared by anionic solution polymerization in hexane followed by terminal modification with a silane-containing modifier, emphasizing filler affinity and processability. The third patent discloses a modified conjugated diene-based polymer prepared by polymerizing styrene and butadiene in hydrocarbon solvent with an organometallic catalyst system, then modifying the active polymer with an alkoxysilane-based modifier; it explicitly targets a styrene-butadiene random copolymer with 50 to 95 wt% diene and 5 to 50 wt% aromatic vinyl in one embodiment. Major process trends include hydrocarbon-solvent solution polymerization, organolithium or mixed catalyst initiation, use of polar modifiers/additives, controlled monomer feed and temperature, and downstream termination/coagulation/drying steps. Water is not part of the core polymerization medium in any of the three patents, but appears in workup or recovery steps in two patents. Overall, the landscape emphasizes composition control, sequence distribution, and post-polymerization functionalization rather than simply lowering styrene content alone, making these patents technically relevant to low styrene SBR synthesis and tire-compound performance optimization.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US11591428B2 is the only patent that explicitly claims a low-styrene range for the target material, with total styrene content of 20 to 30 wt% and preferred 22 to 27 wt%.
- US10174133B2 and US10676543B2 both use solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene in hydrocarbon solvent, but neither explicitly discloses the styrene content of the target copolymer in the extracted evidence.
- US11591428B2 emphasizes tapered sequence distribution and low molecular weight (Mn 30,000 to 80,000), whereas US10174133B2 focuses on post-polymerization silane modification and US10676543B2 focuses on trans-1,4-rich conjugated diene structure plus alkoxysilane modification.
- All three patents rely on hydrocarbon-solvent solution processes rather than emulsion polymerization for the disclosed examples.
- Water is used only in downstream recovery/workup in US10174133B2; US11591428B2 uses coagulation in isopropanol, and US10676543B2 discloses alcohol quench without water in the extracted evidence.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, the strongest direct low-styrene disclosure is US11591428B2, which claims a styrene-butadiene copolymer containing 20 to 30 wt% styrene and preferred 22 to 27 wt% styrene, with controlled styrene sequence distribution and low molecular weight. The other two patents are technically relevant because they disclose solution-polymerized styrene-butadiene copolymers and post-polymerization functionalization routes that are compatible with composition control, but they do not explicitly establish a low-styrene target in the extracted evidence. Collectively, the patents show that low-styrene SBR development in this set is centered on hydrocarbon-solution polymerization, controlled monomer feed/sequence architecture, and downstream functionalization or compounding to improve tire-relevant properties.
