# PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR HYDROGENATED NITRILE RUBBER (HNBR) SYNTHESIS, MODIFICATION, AND FORMULATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys a focused patent set relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), defined as nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) in which the diene-derived unsaturation has been selectively reduced to improve heat, ozone, oil, and aging resistance. The selected patents span three major technical themes: direct hydrogenation of NBR to HNBR, post-hydrogenation purification and molecular-weight control, and downstream compounding/formulation strategies that exploit HNBR’s saturated backbone. Across the landscape, the core synthetic route remains solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous transition-metal catalysts, especially rhodium- and ruthenium-based systems, with process emphasis on catalyst choice, hydrogen pressure, temperature, and residual double-bond control. Several patents also address simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation or metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction to produce low-Mooney HNBR with improved processability.

The formulation patents show that HNBR performance is strongly tuned by filler package, peroxide cure system, and co-crosslinking agents. One cluster targets high-temperature vulcanizates using microgels, unsaturated carboxylic acids/salts, and peroxides to improve modulus retention and demolding behavior. Another cluster focuses on intake-manifold and sealing applications, balancing acrylonitrile content, iodine number, ester plasticizer level, peroxide loading, and optional polyfunctional unsaturated compounds to optimize oil/fuel resistance and low-temperature flexibility. A separate composition strategy blends HNBR with unhydrogenated NBR to reduce cost while maintaining sealing performance, and another patent removes ruthenium catalyst residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using functionalized ion-exchange resins.

Overall, the evidence indicates that the most robust HNBR synthesis routes are homogeneous hydrogenation of NBR in solution, often with selective catalysts and controlled hydrogenation to high conversion of diene units, followed by purification to reduce residual metal. For application-oriented HNBR compounds, the most consistent trends are peroxide curing, careful control of residual unsaturation/iodine value, and filler/plasticizer optimization to balance heat resistance, oil resistance, cold flexibility, and processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- The patent set splits into synthesis/purification patents (US7919563B2, US7772328B2, US9150669B2, US5569719A, EP2519350B1, US7846995B2) and formulation patents (US9321910B2, US7772305B2, US20090258980A1, EP2700692B1).
- Direct HNBR synthesis patents emphasize solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR, often with homogeneous Rh/Ru catalysts or Pd/SiO2, while the metathesis-based patents use Ru/Os catalysts and co-olefins to reduce molecular weight before or during hydrogenation.
- Residual unsaturation control is a recurring theme: US5569719A targets iodine value 15; US7772305B2 and US20090258980A1 specify iodine number windows of 32-65; EP2700692B1 requires iodine value 30 mg/100 mg or less; US9150669B2 reports RDB reduction from 100% to below 1% in one catalyst system.
- Catalyst systems differ substantially: Pd/SiO2 in US5569719A, Ru/Os metathesis catalysts in US7919563B2 and US7772328B2, Ru hydrogenation/metathesis catalysts in US9150669B2, and functionalized ion-exchange resins for Ru removal in US7846995B2.
- Process conditions are most explicit in the metathesis/hydrogenation patents: 80°C and 6.9×10^5 Pa ethylene in US7919563B2; 80°C and 0.05-0.25 phr catalyst in US7772328B2; 60-150°C under high pressure in EP2519350B1; 66 h at ca. 100°C for Ru removal in US7846995B2.
- Formulation patents consistently use peroxide curing, with optional polyfunctional unsaturated coagents, and filler systems based on carbon black, silica, or mixed fillers to tune hardness, LLC resistance, and processability.
- US9321910B2 is distinct in using microgels plus unsaturated carboxylic acids/salts to improve high-temperature vulcanizate properties, whereas EP2700692B1 focuses on low-hardness static seals with MT carbon black and cocrosslinking agent.
- US20090258980A1 and US7772305B2 both exploit HNBR/NBR blending to reduce cost and adjust iodine number, but US20090258980A1 explicitly blends in unhydrogenated NBR while US7772305B2 centers on HNBR composition windows and plasticizer selection.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR as the core HNBR synthesis route, with selective Rh- or Pd-based hydrogenation giving low residual unsaturation and acceptable molecular weight, and Ru/Os metathesis routes offering an alternative for molecular-weight reduction and processability control. For practical HNBR manufacture, the most defensible parameter windows from the patents are high hydrogenation of diene units (about 95-100% in one formulation patent), low iodine value/residual double bond content, and careful control of catalyst loading and temperature in the 80°C to 150°C range depending on the route. For downstream performance, peroxide curing with optional polyfunctional unsaturated coagents, controlled acrylonitrile content, and filler selection (MT carbon black, silica, or mixed systems) are repeatedly shown to govern oil resistance, heat resistance, low-temperature flexibility, and roll processability. If the goal is a robust HNBR synthesis strategy, the strongest evidence favors homogeneous hydrogenation of NBR followed by catalyst removal, with metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction as a useful adjunct when low Mooney viscosity is required.
