# POLYMERIZATION / SYNTHESIS PATENT RESEARCH REPORT
## Low Acrylonitrile NBR

### 1. Abstract
The patent landscape for Low Acrylonitrile NBR (Nitrile Butadiene Rubber) reveals a focus on improving synthesis methods to achieve lower acrylonitrile content while maintaining or enhancing the material's properties. Major scientific approaches include emulsion polymerization and bulk polymerization, with recurring variables such as reaction temperature, pressure, and the ratio of acrylonitrile to butadiene. Trends indicate a push towards more environmentally friendly and cost-effective production methods. The extracted data highlights the importance of catalyst selection, initiator systems, and post-polymerization treatments in controlling the final product's characteristics.

### 2. Research Scope & Selection Method
Patents were qualified based on their direct relevance to the synthesis or polymerization of Low Acrylonitrile NBR. Only patents that demonstrated a clear focus on the production of NBR with reduced acrylonitrile content were included. The selection method ensured that all analyzed patents provided detailed information on the synthesis process, including reaction conditions and material compositions.

### 4. Cross-Patent Comparison
Comparing the patents, the acrylonitrile content ranges from 15% to 18%, with reaction temperatures between 50°C. The butadiene content is consistently high, ranging from 80% to 85% of the initial monomer mixture. The Mooney viscosity of the final product is reported in one patent, with a value of 45 MU.

### 5. Key Technical Trends
A key trend observed is the use of specific catalysts and initiator systems to control the polymerization process and achieve the desired acrylonitrile content. Another trend is the variation in reaction conditions, such as temperature and pressure, to optimize the synthesis process. The use of post-polymerization treatments to enhance the material's properties is also noted.
