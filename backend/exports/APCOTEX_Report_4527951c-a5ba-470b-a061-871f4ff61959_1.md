# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: COMPOSITION CONTROL, CONTINUOUS FEED STRATEGIES, AND EMULSION POLYMERIZATION ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how acrylonitrile content is controlled during copolymer synthesis and how process design affects composition uniformity, conversion, and downstream properties. The target material is low-ACN NBR, generally understood as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile content relative to standard grades, often in the approximate 10–18 wt% range and typically below about 20 wt% ACN. The patent set spans three distinct but related approaches: a modern controlled emulsion/block-copolymer platform that explicitly incorporates nitrile-rubber blocks containing acrylonitrile and butadiene; a continuous emulsion copolymerization process designed to manage monomer ratio drift and achieve high bound acrylonitrile; and an older grafting/latex-based route that uses preformed NBR latex as a rubber phase in impact-resistant nitrile polymer compositions.

Across the landscape, the dominant technical theme is monomer composition control. US5770660A shows that incremental butadiene addition in multiple reactors can maintain a more constant acrylonitrile/butadiene ratio and produce NBR with about 48.8–59.5 wt% bound acrylonitrile, demonstrating the importance of feed staging and conversion management. US7592409B2 is not a direct low-ACN NBR patent, but it is relevant because it discloses emulsion polymerization with in-situ emulsifier formation and block architectures that include a nitrile-rubber B block composed of acrylonitrile and 1,3-butadiene; this supports controlled emulsion methods that could be adapted to low-ACN NBR block or seed-latex synthesis. US3426102A discloses preformed NBR latexes with acrylonitrile/butadiene ratios such as 40/60 and 25/75 used as impact modifiers in aqueous polymerization, illustrating early latex-based NBR preparation and the role of low-to-moderate ACN rubber phases in property tuning.

Overall, the evidence favors emulsion-based synthesis, careful monomer feed scheduling, and use of emulsifier/initiator systems that preserve latex stability while controlling composition drift. For low-ACN NBR, the most transferable parameters are staged monomer addition, moderate temperatures in the 45–60 C range for conventional emulsion routes, and maintaining a stable aqueous phase with suitable emulsifier and chain-transfer control.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US5770660A is the only patent in the set that directly targets acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymerization as the primary product; it uses continuous staged butadiene addition to control monomer ratio drift and achieve about 48.8–59.5 wt% bound acrylonitrile.
- US3426102A discloses preformed NBR latexes with explicit acrylonitrile/butadiene ratios of 60/40 and 40/60, plus a 25/75 rubber latex used in graft/impact resin systems; its process is batch emulsion polymerization at 45–60 C with emulsifier, initiator, and mercaptan chain transfer agent.
- US7592409B2 is mechanistically relevant but not a direct NBR patent; it teaches in-situ emulsifier formation and controlled emulsion/block polymerization, including B blocks containing acrylonitrile and butadiene/isoprene, which could be adapted to controlled NBR architectures.
- Compared with the older batch emulsion route in US3426102A, US5770660A provides stronger composition control by staged monomer feeding and multiple reactors, reducing the monomer-ratio swing that normally occurs as butadiene is depleted.
- US3426102A uses conventional emulsion ingredients such as GAFAC RE-610, potassium persulfate or azo-bis-isobutyronitrile, and t-dodecyl/n-dodecyl mercaptan; US5770660A emphasizes process staging rather than a novel catalyst system.
- US7592409B2 operates at 65–75 C in microemulsion/RAFT/NMP-like systems with oleic acid, potassium persulfate, tripotassium phosphate, and potassium hydroxide, whereas US5770660A and US3426102A are conventional NBR emulsion processes centered on monomer feed and latex stability.
- For low-acrylonitrile NBR specifically, the most transferable lesson is from US5770660A: control the acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratio and avoid complete butadiene depletion to preserve uniform composition.
- US3426102A suggests that low-to-moderate ACN rubber phases can be prepared at 45 C with about 8 pH and 33% solids, which may be useful for low-ACN latex design and downstream blending.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis is best approached through emulsion polymerization with tight monomer-feed control and stable latex formation. The strongest direct guidance comes from US5770660A, which demonstrates that staged butadiene addition across multiple reactors can maintain a more uniform acrylonitrile/butadiene ratio and avoid the composition drift that degrades properties in conventional batch NBR. US3426102A provides older but still useful evidence that acrylonitrile/butadiene latexes can be made at 45 C with emulsifier, initiator, and mercaptan chain-transfer control, including a 40/60 acrylonitrile/butadiene rubber latex that is within the low-to-moderate ACN regime. US7592409B2 is not a direct NBR patent, but it supports controlled emulsion strategies and block/seed architectures that could be adapted to low-ACN nitrile rubber systems. Based on the evidence, recommended synthesis parameters are emulsion polymerization, staged or incremental monomer feed, moderate temperatures around 45–60 C for conventional NBR latex routes, and careful maintenance of unconverted monomer ratio to prevent butadiene depletion and composition drift.
