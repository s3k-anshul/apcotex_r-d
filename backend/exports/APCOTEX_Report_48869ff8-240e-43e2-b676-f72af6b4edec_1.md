# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION AND EMULSION ROUTES, FUNCTIONALIZATION, AND TIRE-COMPOUND PERFORMANCE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and styrene-butadiene copolymer synthesis, with emphasis on embodiments that can support low-styrene grades or composition control. The selected patents span both solution polymerization and emulsion polymerization, plus downstream functionalization strategies that improve tire-relevant properties without changing the core SBR identity. Across the landscape, the dominant synthesis themes are anionic solution polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent with n-butyllithium initiation, controlled monomer feed of styrene and 1,3-butadiene, terminal modification or coupling, and post-polymerization functionalization using silane-, carbinol-, or carboxyl-containing reagents. In parallel, emulsion-state radical polymerization in water is used to prepare functional SBRs and related copolymers, often with emulsifiers, redox initiators, mercaptan chain-transfer agents, and aqueous stripping/drying workup.

The target definition here is low styrene fraction in SBR, but the extracted evidence rarely states a dedicated low-styrene claim; instead, it provides explicit styrene contents or feed ratios for specific SBR embodiments. The most compositionally informative disclosures are EP3056520B1 and EP2989129B1, which report styrene contents around 24.7-25.4 wt% for solution-polymerized, silane-containing or carbinol-terminated SBRs, and EP3070120B1 and US9328176B2, which disclose emulsion SBR systems with styrene feeds or resulting styrene contents ranging from 20 wt% to 40 wt% depending on embodiment. US10174133B2 and US5902856A focus more on solution blending, terminal modification, and molecular-weight architecture than on styrene fraction itself, but they remain relevant because they disclose SBR synthesis and downstream processing routes used in tire compounds.

Overall, the patent set shows two major process trends: solution SBR routes that enable tighter control of microstructure, molecular weight, and terminal functionality, and emulsion SBR routes that use water as the polymerization medium and are readily adapted to polar-functional monomers. Formulation trends emphasize silica-filled tread compounds, use of silanes and coupling agents, and property optimization for rolling resistance, wet grip, abrasion resistance, and processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Solution-polymerized SBR patents US10174133B2, EP2989129B1, and EP3056520B1 use hydrocarbon solvents (hexane/normal hexane) and n-butyllithium-based anionic initiation, whereas EP3070120B1 and US9328176B2 use aqueous emulsion polymerization with water, emulsifiers, and redox/organic peroxide initiators.
- Explicit styrene-content disclosures cluster around about 24.7-25.4 wt% in EP2989129B1 and EP3056520B1, while EP3070120B1 discloses a lower 20 wt% styrene SBR embodiment (Polymer 2) and higher 40-50 wt% polymers for other embodiments.
- US9328176B2 and EP3070120B1 both use 1,500 mL water and 24 h at 10° C. in emulsion examples, but US9328176B2 emphasizes functionalization via epoxy-acrylate ring opening, whereas EP3070120B1 emphasizes silica-compatible functional SBR with glycidyl methacrylate.
- US10174133B2 focuses on modifier chemistry and terminal butadiene capping rather than styrene fraction control, while US5902856A focuses on blending terminal-modified and high-molecular polymers and reports ST/V ratios rather than a direct low-styrene target.
- Water is central to the polymerization medium in EP3070120B1 and US9328176B2, but in US10174133B2, EP2989129B1, EP3056520B1, and US5902856A it appears only in downstream stripping, coagulation, or drying steps.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, low-styrene SBR is most directly supported by the solution-polymerized examples in EP2989129B1 and EP3056520B1, which explicitly disclose styrene contents around 24.7-25.4 wt%, and by the emulsion route in EP3070120B1, which includes a 20 wt% styrene SBR embodiment. The remaining patents are relevant because they establish industrially important SBR synthesis and processing platforms—solution anionic polymerization, emulsion polymerization, terminal modification, and silica-compound formulation—that can be used to tune composition and performance. Overall, the evidence shows that low-styrene SBR is achieved through controlled monomer feed and process selection rather than through a single dedicated low-styrene claim, with tire-performance optimization driven by functionalization, molecular-weight control, and downstream compounding.
