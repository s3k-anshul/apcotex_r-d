# LOW MOLECULAR WEIGHT NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR CONTROLLED SOLUTION AND PLASMA-POLYMER PROCESSES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patents relevant to low molecular weight nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on synthesis routes that deliberately reduce chain length, lower viscosity, or create functional low-MW intermediates. The patent set spans two distinct technical directions. EP2478021B1 discloses solution free-radical polymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile in organic solvent using a modifier/regulator system, with explicit adjustment of regulator concentration to obtain low molecular weight NBR suitable for downstream RAFT-based block/terpolymer construction. The examples show both conventional NBR preparation and deliberate low-MW product formation, including a reported Mn of 12,000 g/mol for a RAFT macromer. US10400053B2 is not a synthesis patent for NBR itself, but it is relevant to low-MW NBR compounding because it describes plasma polymerization of hydrocarbon monomers on carbon particles to create active fillers for rubber blends, including NBR-containing formulations. Together, the patents illustrate the broader process landscape around low-viscosity NBR: molecular-weight control by chain-transfer/regulator chemistry in solution polymerization, conversion control and staged monomer addition, and downstream filler-surface engineering to improve processability and composite performance. Across the evidence, the dominant polymerization themes are radical solution polymerization in organic solvent, use of RAFT/regulator chemistry, oxygen exclusion, butadiene pressurized feed, elevated temperature initiation, and solvent-based isolation by precipitation. The broader formulation/process trend is the move away from conventional emulsion-polymerized NBR toward more controllable organic-solution routes that can generate lower-MW, functionalized, or block-capable NBR intermediates while preserving compatibility with later blending or hydrogenation steps.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2478021B1 is a direct low-MW NBR synthesis patent, while US10400053B2 is a downstream rubber-filler modification patent rather than a polymerization route.
- EP2478021B1 uses organic-solution free-radical polymerization of butadiene/acrylonitrile with a regulator/RAFT-type modifier; US10400053B2 uses solvent-free RF plasma polymerization of acetylene on carbon particles.
- EP2478021B1 explicitly controls molecular weight via regulator amount, including 2.22 phm to obtain low-MW NBR and Mn 12,000 g/mol; US10400053B2 does not disclose NBR molecular-weight control, but reports NBR compound Mooney viscosity of 45 in a rubber blend context.
- EP2478021B1 operates at 100°C and 3 bar butadiene feed with 9 h reaction times; US10400053B2 uses plasma conditions such as 250 W, 200 mTorr, 1 h, and 13.56 MHz RF frequency.
- EP2478021B1 isolates polymer by ethanol precipitation and vacuum drying; US10400053B2 characterizes coated fillers by TGA and XPS rather than polymer isolation.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports solution free-radical polymerization with regulator/chain-transfer control as the most direct route to low molecular weight NBR. EP2478021B1 provides the strongest guidance: use organic solvent solution polymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile, maintain oxygen-free conditions, feed butadiene at 3 bar, initiate at 100°C, and increase regulator loading to lower molecular weight, with a demonstrated low-MW product at Mn 12,000 g/mol. US10400053B2 is complementary rather than synthetic, showing that plasma-modified carbon fillers can be used in rubber blends, including NBR-containing systems, to tune composite properties. For low-MW NBR synthesis, the best-supported parameters are organic-solution radical polymerization, explicit regulator dosing, controlled monomer feed, and precipitation isolation; for formulation, plasma-treated fillers may help optimize processability and composite performance.
