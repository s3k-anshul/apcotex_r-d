# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This technical report synthesizes patent research data for Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR), analyzing six primary patents published between 2011 and 2017. No competitor patents or external website sources were identified in the provided dataset. The primary patents investigate advanced emulsion polymerization and post-polymerization coagulation techniques to optimize the chemical, physical, and vulcanization properties of nitrile rubbers. Key technical approaches include the precise control of monomer ratios (such as acrylonitrile and butadiene), the use of molecular weight regulators like tertiary dodecyl mercaptan (TDM) added incrementally as 'boosters' during polymerization, and the implementation of specific redox initiator systems (e.g., iron(II) chloride versus iron(II) sulfate) to regulate conversion rates and polymer microstructure. Furthermore, significant process trends focus on the coagulation stage, where the choice of salt (such as calcium chloride, magnesium chloride, or sodium chloride) and the adjustment of latex pH prior to coagulation are shown to critically influence the ion indices, Mooney scorch, vulcanization rate, and mechanical properties (e.g., stress at 300% elongation) of the resulting rubber. This synthesis highlights a highly controlled, multi-stage process paradigm designed to yield nitrile rubbers with tailored Mooney viscosities, narrow glass transition temperature ranges, and improved aging resistance.

#### Patent 1

**Patent Details**
- Patent Number: US9650502
- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of nitrile rubbers
- Assignee: Not disclosed
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2017
- Material Type: Nitrile Rubber Latex
- Relevance to target: Provides detailed emulsion polymerization recipes and incremental modifier addition strategies to prepare nitrile rubber latexes with controlled acrylonitrile content.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method2017-05-16_US9650502B2_Process_for_the_preparation_of_nitrile_rubbers_Not_disclosed_US_2017_https://patents.google.com/patent/US9650502B2**

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Latex A, B, and C were prepared with bound ACN contents of 28.1%, 33.9%, and 27.9% respectively.
- Polymerization was carried out at 7°C and short-stopped at 70% conversion after 7 hours using hydroxylamine sulfate.
- Coagulation was performed using magnesium sulfate, followed by washing at pH 11.5-11.8 with potassium hydroxide and drying at 100°C.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates a precise low-temperature emulsion polymerization process with incremental TDM additions to control molecular weight and bound ACN content.

**Trend Analysis:**
- Emulsion polymerization is typically conducted at low temperatures (e.g., 7-8°C) and stopped at conversions ranging from 70% to 86% to prevent gel formation and maintain desired polymer properties.
- Incremental addition of molecular weight regulators (such as TDM boosters) is a common strategy to control polymer chain length and bound acrylonitrile distribution.
- Post-polymerization latex treatment, specifically pH adjustment (using KOH or HCl) and the choice of coagulation salt (NaCl, CaCl2, or MgSO4), critically determines the cation concentration and ion indices of the final rubber.
- Compounding and physical testing consistently utilize 1.5 L laboratory mixers and vulcanization at 160°C under 120 bar pressure to evaluate Mooney scorch, vulcanization rates, and tensile properties.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The analyzed patent landscape for Low Acrylonitrile NBR reveals a highly sophisticated process chain spanning low-temperature emulsion polymerization, precise molecular weight regulation, and optimized coagulation chemistry. Key findings demonstrate that bound acrylonitrile content (typically 27% to 36%) and polymer properties are tightly controlled via TDM booster additions and specific redox initiator systems (such as FeCl2 vs FeSO4). Furthermore, post-polymerization steps—specifically latex pH adjustment and the selection of coagulation salts (magnesium chloride, calcium chloride, or sodium chloride)—are critical levers to control the rubber's ion indices, which in turn dictate Mooney scorch, vulcanization kinetics, and vulcanizate mechanical properties (e.g., stress at 30% and 300% elongation). No competitor patents or external website sources were present in the dataset, indicating a highly focused primary patent analysis. Future research should address the optimization of these coagulation parameters to achieve the ideal balance of processability and mechanical performance.
