# LOW MOLECULAR WEIGHT NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON CONTROLLED MOLECULAR-WEIGHT REDUCTION, SOLUTION POLYMERIZATION, AND POST-POLYMER DEGRADATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to low molecular weight nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on routes that deliberately reduce molecular weight, lower viscosity, or improve processability. The primary target is low-MW NBR or NBR-derived materials with controlled chain length, typically achieved either during polymerization by solution/emulsion control and chain-transfer/modifier strategies, or after polymerization by olefin-metathesis degradation. The landscape is broader than classical NBR synthesis alone: several patents address low-viscosity or low-MW polymer architectures that are conceptually transferable to NBR processing, while others directly disclose NBR depolymerization or molecular-weight reduction.

The most directly relevant disclosures are the solution-phase NBR patent EP2478021B1, which uses free-radical polymerization of butadiene/acrylonitrile in organic solvent with a modifier (regulator) and demonstrates deliberate low-MW NBR preparation by increasing regulator loading to 2.22 phm, and US9346908B1, which reduces NBR molecular weight by olefin-metathesis using ruthenium catalysts on magnetic carriers, enabling catalyst recovery and reuse. EP2478021B1 also shows that solution polymerization can be used to prepare NBR terpolymers and RAFT-derived low-MW macromers, with polymerization at 100°C and 3 bar butadiene. US9346908B1 demonstrates that 6 wt% NBR/chlorobenzene solutions treated with 2 wt% 1-hexene and 5 wt% loaded catalyst at room temperature for 2 h can yield lower-MW NBR while preserving recoverability.

Other patents in the set are not NBR synthesis patents per se, but they contribute relevant process concepts: controlled solution polymerization with precise molecular-weight tuning (US10167348B2), associative/telechelic architectures for viscosity control (US11905379B2), living radical graft copolymerization with low-MW side chains (US9988480B2), and adiabatic bulk polymerization with chain-transfer control (US10465017B2). Together, these disclosures indicate that low-MW NBR can be approached through solvent-based radical polymerization with regulator/chain-transfer control, or through post-polymer metathesis degradation, with isolation by precipitation or magnetic separation and with strong emphasis on viscosity/processability management.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2478021B1 and US9346908B1 are the most directly relevant NBR patents: EP2478021B1 controls NBR molecular weight during solution polymerization with a regulator, while US9346908B1 lowers the molecular weight of preformed NBR by olefin-metathesis degradation.
- EP2478021B1 uses acrylonitrile/1,3-butadiene solution polymerization in DMAc with free-radical initiator plus DoPAT regulator; US9346908B1 uses chlorobenzene NBR solutions with 1-hexene and loaded ruthenium metathesis catalysts.
- EP2478021B1 explicitly reports low-MW NBR by increasing regulator amount to 2.22 phm and gives Mn = 12,000 g/mol for a RAFT macromer; US9346908B1 reports Mw reduction from 126,049 to 39,054-45,925 after one metathesis treatment.
- EP2478021B1 relies on precipitation in ethanol and vacuum drying for isolation; US9346908B1 isolates product by methanol precipitation and recovers catalyst magnetically.
- US10167348B2, US11905379B2, US9988480B2, and US10465017B2 are not NBR-specific but collectively reinforce the importance of solution polymerization, chain-transfer control, living radical control, and staged thermal management for molecular-weight tuning.
- US10731046B2 and US20200354606A1 are formulation/process references rather than NBR synthesis disclosures; both emphasize low-viscosity or printable compositions, which is relevant to downstream handling of low-MW NBR-like materials.
- US8470957B2 is the least directly related to NBR chemistry, but it contributes a low-oligomer, feed-controlled microreactor strategy that is conceptually aligned with controlled low-MW product formation.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two dominant routes to low molecular weight NBR: controlled solution polymerization with a regulator/chain-transfer strategy, and post-polymer molecular-weight reduction by olefin metathesis. EP2478021B1 is the clearest synthesis patent for low-MW NBR formation, showing that increasing regulator loading in DMAc solution at 100°C and 3 bar butadiene can deliberately suppress molecular weight. US9346908B1 provides a complementary degradation route, where 6 wt% NBR solutions treated with 2 wt% 1-hexene and 5 wt% magnetic loaded ruthenium catalyst at room temperature for 2 h yield substantially lower Mw and allow catalyst recovery. For practical low-MW NBR synthesis, the strongest evidence favors solvent-based control, oxygen exclusion, moderate temperature, and either elevated regulator loading during polymerization or metathesis-based chain scission after polymerization. The broader patent set reinforces that precise molecular-weight control, low-viscosity formulation, and recoverable catalyst/process design are the key enabling parameters.
