# PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT: LOW MOLECULAR WEIGHT NBR AND RELATED LOW-VISCOSITY POLYMER SYSTEMS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a patent set returned for the target concept of low molecular weight NBR, but the retrieved primary patents are largely adjacent rather than direct disclosures of low-MW nitrile-butadiene rubber synthesis. Across the manifest, only US12146207B2 explicitly includes NBR as a rubber component in degradable wellbore diverters/seals, while EP2389295B1 concerns functionalized diene rubbers in silica-filled tire tread compounds, EP2921509B1 discloses liquid ethylene/alpha-olefin copolymers prepared by solution polymerization with bridged metallocene catalysts, US10465017B2 describes essentially adiabatic bulk polymerization of silicone-containing copolymers using thermal initiators and chain-transfer agents, and US10161556B2 uses HNBR as a high-temperature insulation material in subsea pipelines. None of the retrieved patents provides a direct, dedicated route to low molecular weight NBR via controlled polymerization, depolymerization, or chain-transfer engineering. However, the set is still informative because it highlights the process levers most relevant to low-MW elastomer design: solution polymerization, catalyst/initiator selection, chain-transfer control, thermal management, and formulation-driven viscosity or stiffness tuning. EP2921509B1 is the closest process analogue in terms of deliberately targeting liquid, low-Mn polymer products with narrow molecular-weight distribution, while US10465017B2 demonstrates staged adiabatic polymerization and explicit chain-transfer use to manage conversion and polymer properties. EP2389295B1 and US10161556B2 show how diene-rubber performance is often governed more by compounding and end-use requirements than by direct low-MW synthesis. US12146207B2 confirms that NBR can be used as a degradable elastomeric component in temporary sealing systems, but it does not disclose NBR molecular-weight control. Overall, the landscape suggests that low molecular weight NBR development is most likely to rely on solution polymerization with controlled chain transfer, carefully selected initiator systems, and tight temperature control, rather than on the formulations disclosed in these retrieved patents.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2921509B1 is the only patent in the set that explicitly targets liquid, low-Mn polymer products; its disclosed Mn range spans 2080 to 5385 in the examples, with Mw/Mn around 1.81-1.89 and low bromine number (<0.1 in the inventive examples).
- US10465017B2 uses staged essentially adiabatic polymerization and explicit chain-transfer/initiator management to tune polymer inherent viscosity from 0.25 to 0.62 dl/g, illustrating a molecular-weight-control strategy that is conceptually transferable to low-MW NBR.
- EP2389295B1 is formulation-driven rather than synthesis-driven: it reports Mooney viscosity values of 55.6-80.0 ME and mixing at 70-154°C, showing how diene-rubber processability is adjusted downstream of polymerization.
- US10161556B2 and US12146207B2 are application patents rather than synthesis patents; both mention NBR/HNBR as functional elastomeric materials, but neither provides a route to low molecular weight NBR.
- Across the set, solution polymerization appears only in EP2921509B1 and in the background discussion of EP2389295B1; the other patents rely on bulk/adiabatic polymerization or end-use compounding.
- No patent in the manifest discloses a dedicated NBR chain-transfer recipe, acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratio, or NBR-specific molecular-weight target; the closest transferable controls are catalyst/initiator loading, hydrogen or chain-transfer use, and temperature management.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The retrieved patent set does not contain a direct low molecular weight NBR synthesis disclosure, but it does identify the most relevant control concepts for achieving such a material: solution polymerization, staged radical or catalytic initiation, chain-transfer management, and tight thermal control. EP2921509B1 provides the strongest low-MW process analogue through liquid copolymer production with Mn in the low-thousands and narrow dispersity, while US10465017B2 demonstrates how staged adiabatic polymerization and chain-transfer agents can be used to tune polymer viscosity. For low molecular weight NBR development, the evidence supports targeting solution polymerization with deliberate molecular-weight suppression, low-to-moderate temperatures, and explicit chain-transfer/initiator control, followed by formulation or compounding only after the desired low-viscosity backbone is obtained.
