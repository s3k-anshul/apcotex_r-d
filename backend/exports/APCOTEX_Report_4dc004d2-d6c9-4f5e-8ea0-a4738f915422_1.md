# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR SYNTHESIS AND COMPOSITION CONTROL

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and adjacent process technologies. The target material is NBR with reduced acrylonitrile (ACN) content relative to conventional grades, typically to improve low-temperature flexibility while retaining oil resistance. The patent set spans two direct NBR preparation routes and one enabling metathesis technology that supports downstream molecular-weight adjustment of NBR prior to hydrogenation or property tuning. EP2316860B1 discloses emulsion polymerization of at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile with at least one conjugated diene using a redox initiator system containing a peroxo oxidant, a reducing agent, and Fe(II) chloride; the specification emphasizes conventional emulsion NBR manufacture and the use of metal-salt/redox initiation. US9023914B2 teaches free-radical solution polymerization of NBR in organic solvents with specific modifier substances, enabling control of molecular architecture, molecular weight, and polydispersity; its examples include acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratios such as 37/63 phm and a lower-ACN terpolymer at 30/50 phm ACN/butadiene. US8507398B2 is not a direct NBR synthesis patent, but it is highly relevant because it provides ruthenium- or osmium-carbene metathesis catalysts for metathesis of nitrile rubber, supporting post-polymerization chain modification or molecular-weight reduction strategies that can be important when tailoring low-ACN NBR for hydrogenation or viscosity control. Across the landscape, the dominant process themes are emulsion versus solution polymerization, redox or radical initiation, use of chain-transfer/modifier chemistry for molecular-weight control, and the possibility of subsequent hydrogenation or metathesis-based adjustment. The evidence supports low-ACN NBR synthesis by controlled copolymerization of butadiene with acrylonitrile, with process selection strongly influencing polymer architecture, viscosity, and downstream processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2316860B1 and US9023914B2 both address direct NBR formation, but EP2316860B1 is an emulsion process using a peroxo/reducing-agent/Fe(II) chloride redox system, whereas US9023914B2 uses free-radical solution polymerization in organic solvent with specific modifiers.
- US9023914B2 explicitly discloses a lower-ACN formulation at 30 phm acrylonitrile and 50 phm butadiene, while EP2316860B1 emphasizes general NBR composition ranges rather than a specific low-ACN recipe.
- EP2316860B1 relies on emulsion polymerization and conventional chain-transfer/initiator concepts from prior art, whereas US9023914B2 emphasizes organic-solvent synthesis and modifier incorporation to control molecular weight and polydispersity.
- US8507398B2 is not a polymerization patent, but it is process-enabling for NBR by providing metathesis catalysts that can be used for chain modification or molecular-weight reduction before or after hydrogenation.
- Across the set, temperature control is explicit in US9023914B2 at 100 °C and in EP2316860B1’s cited prior art at 20-30 °C; US8507398B2 demonstrates metathesis at room temperature in one example and over 5-60 min time windows.
- Only US9023914B2 directly demonstrates a composition shift toward lower acrylonitrile content; the other patents mainly contribute process control or downstream modification tools.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is most directly achieved by controlled copolymerization of butadiene with acrylonitrile, with process choice strongly affecting molecular weight and processability. Among the primary patents, US9023914B2 is the most directly useful for low-ACN synthesis because it discloses solution polymerization with an explicit lower-ACN example (30 phm ACN, 50 phm butadiene) and modifier-based control of polymer architecture. EP2316860B1 supports conventional emulsion NBR manufacture using a redox/Fe(II) chloride initiation system, which is industrially relevant for NBR production but less specific to low-ACN composition tuning. US8507398B2 adds a complementary metathesis toolkit for post-polymerization molecular-weight adjustment and chain modification. Based on the evidence, the most defensible synthesis strategy for low-ACN NBR is controlled butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymerization, with acrylonitrile held at the lower end of the NBR range and molecular weight managed by modifier or chain-transfer chemistry; solution polymerization offers the clearest disclosed route for architecture control, while emulsion polymerization remains the standard industrial route.
