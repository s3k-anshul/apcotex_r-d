# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) PREPARATION PROCESSES: CATALYST-ENABLED SIMULTANEOUS HYDROGENATION AND METATHESIS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents focused on the preparation of hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) from nitrile rubber precursors. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated NBR with controlled residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil/oxidative resistance, and reduced molecular weight or narrower molecular-weight distribution relative to conventional HNBR. Both patents address the same core transformation: selective hydrogenation of acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber in the presence of hydrogen, but they emphasize catalyst-enabled process intensification rather than conventional stepwise hydrogenation. EP2473280B1 discloses a simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis route using ruthenium- or osmium-based catalysts of general formulae (I)–(III), with examples comparing Grubbs II, Grubbs III, and Wilkinson’s catalyst. The patent highlights faster reduction of residual double bonds and the ability to obtain HNBR with lower molecular weight and narrower distribution in a single step. US9150669B2 similarly discloses a simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis process, but focuses on alternative catalyst systems of formula (I) and explicitly frames the invention as a route to low-molecular-weight HNBR with low residual double bond content. Across the landscape, the dominant process trend is catalytic hydrogenation coupled to metathesis, using ruthenium-based complexes and optional co-olefin to tune chain scission and molecular weight. The disclosed examples emphasize catalyst identity, catalyst loading in phr, metal content in ppm, reaction time, and residual double-bond conversion profiles as the key process variables. Overall, the patents indicate that HNBR synthesis can be improved by selecting metathesis-active hydrogenation catalysts that simultaneously reduce unsaturation and control molecular weight, enabling a single-step preparation of HNBR with targeted viscosity and performance properties.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Both patents use simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis of nitrile rubber as the core HNBR-forming strategy, rather than conventional post-hydrogenation alone.
- EP2473280B1 emphasizes Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson's catalyst comparisons, while US9150669B2 emphasizes Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson's catalyst systems.
- Catalyst loadings are reported in phr in both patents, but EP2473280B1 spans 0.055-0.080 phr and US9150669B2 spans 0.041-0.060 phr in the main example.
- Both patents report 65.6 ppm metal content for most samples; EP2473280B1 also includes a 91.3 ppm case, whereas US9150669B2 keeps all compared samples at 65.6 ppm.
- EP2473280B1 shows very rapid RDB reduction to 0.4-0.5% by 120 min for the best catalysts, while US9150669B2 shows 0.6% at 120 min for HNBR 1 and slower conversion for HNBR 2 and HNBR 3.
- US9150669B2 achieves lower Mw for the best sample (113371 g/mol) than EP2473280B1's best listed sample (135772 g/mol), indicating stronger molecular-weight reduction under its catalyst set.
- Both patents support the same process trend: ruthenium-based metathesis-active hydrogenation catalysts can simultaneously lower unsaturation and molecular weight to produce HNBR with improved processability.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports a catalyst-driven, single-step route to HNBR from nitrile rubber using simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis. The most effective disclosed conditions combine ruthenium-based catalysts with low phr loadings, metal contents around 65.6 ppm, and reaction times of 120-240 min, yielding residual double bond contents near or below 1% and substantially reduced molecular weight. Based on the examples, the most favorable synthesis window is a metathesis-active hydrogenation catalyst system that balances rapid unsaturation removal with controlled chain scission, with EP2473280B1 favoring Grubbs III-type systems and US9150669B2 favoring Hoveyda-type systems. For HNBR synthesis, the evidence recommends prioritizing ruthenium catalyst selection, maintaining low catalyst loading, and monitoring RDB and Mw as the key endpoints for process optimization.
