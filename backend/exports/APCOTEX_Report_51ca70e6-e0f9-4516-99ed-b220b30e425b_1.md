# NBR-RELATED PATENT LANDSCAPE: POLYMERIZATION, COMPOSITION CONTROL, AND PROCESS-DRIVEN RUBBER PERFORMANCE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents that mention nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) in distinct technical contexts: EP2507313B1, which focuses on accelerator-free elastomeric glove films based on carboxylated acrylonitrile-butadiene latex, and US10317811B2, which uses NBR as a vulcanized rubber component in an electroconductive charging roller. Although neither patent is a dedicated NBR polymerization patent, both provide useful evidence on NBR formulation, compounding, and downstream processing constraints relevant to NBR-based materials. EP2507313B1 is the more synthesis-relevant document because it discloses the latex composition architecture, including acrylonitrile/butadiene/carboxylic acid ratios, methacrylic acid premixing, pH adjustment, total solids control, and sulfur/accelerator-free crosslinking using metal oxides. The patent emphasizes self-crosslinked or methacrylic-acid-modified carboxylated nitrile latex for glove dipping, followed by drying and thermal curing, with performance benefits such as reduced sulfur residues and improved aging behavior. US10317811B2 is not a synthesis patent in the classical sense, but it discloses an NBR-containing unvulcanized rubber composition for extrusion molding of a charging roller, followed by air vulcanization and ultraviolet surface treatment. The examples show how NBR is compounded with carbon black, zinc oxide, zinc stearate, calcium carbonate, sulfur, and TBzTD, and how process variables such as take-up rate, vulcanization temperature, and UV dose affect surface hardness, viscosity, and image performance. Across the two patents, the dominant trends are latex-based formulation control, careful management of crosslinking chemistry, and process conditions that tune mechanical properties rather than monomer synthesis itself. For NBR synthesis and manufacturing, the evidence supports the importance of controlling acrylonitrile content, carboxylation or functional modification where needed, and downstream cure strategy to balance elasticity, durability, and surface performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2507313B1 is centered on carboxylated acrylonitrile-butadiene latex formulation for glove films, whereas US10317811B2 uses NBR as a compounded rubber in a charging roller; the former is latex-dipping oriented and the latter is extrusion/vulcanization oriented.
- EP2507313B1 explicitly controls acrylonitrile/butadiene/carboxylic acid composition and latex solids/pH, while US10317811B2 emphasizes filler and cure-package loading (carbon black, ZnO, sulfur, TBzTD) rather than monomer composition.
- EP2507313B1 targets sulfur- and accelerator-free crosslinking using metal oxide and methacrylic-acid/self-crosslinking concepts; US10317811B2 uses conventional sulfur/TBzTD vulcanization followed by UV surface oxidation.
- Temperature profiles differ substantially: EP2507313B1 reports glove drying at 50-70°C and 80-120°C with curing at 150°C, while US10317811B2 molds at 100°C and vulcanizes at 160°C for 1 hour before UV irradiation.
- EP2507313B1 focuses on latex total solids of 18-30% and pH 9-10, whereas US10317811B2 reports a solid rubber compounding route with parts-by-mass formulation and process variables such as 107% take-up rate and 9000 mJ/cm2 UV dose.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows two distinct but complementary NBR-use pathways: accelerator-free carboxylated nitrile latex films for dipped gloves and conventionally vulcanized NBR compounds for functional rollers. For NBR synthesis and manufacturing, the most actionable parameters are acrylonitrile content in the 26-30 wt% range, controlled latex solids of 18-30%, pH 9-10, and carefully selected crosslinking chemistry; for compounded articles, the key variables are filler loading, sulfur/TBzTD cure package, vulcanization temperature, and post-cure surface treatment. Based on the patents, the best-supported synthesis/manufacturing strategy for NBR-related products is to tightly control monomer composition and then tailor cure chemistry to the end-use: metal-oxide/self-crosslinking routes for accelerator-free gloves, and conventional sulfur-based vulcanization plus surface oxidation for engineered roller applications.
