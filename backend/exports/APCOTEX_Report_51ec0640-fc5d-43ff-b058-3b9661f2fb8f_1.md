# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS, DEGRADATION, AND PROPERTY-OPTIMIZATION PATENT LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how precursor NBR is hydrogenated, how HNBR molecular weight and residual unsaturation are controlled, and how downstream formulations are tuned for high-temperature performance. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated nitrile-butadiene rubber, typically defined by reduced residual double bonds, improved heat/oil/ozone resistance, and processability suitable for seals, hoses, belts, and related elastomeric articles. Across the selected patents, two major synthesis themes emerge. First, direct hydrogenation of NBR is disclosed using homogeneous catalysts such as Wilkinson-type rhodium systems or ruthenium-based catalysts, often in solution and sometimes with co-catalysts or additives to manage selectivity and catalyst removal. Second, a more advanced route combines metathesis-based chain scission with hydrogenation, enabling lower molecular weight HNBR with narrower molecular weight distribution and improved processing behavior. These metathesis/hydrogenation processes use ruthenium or osmium carbene catalysts, sometimes with co-olefins, and in one case are accelerated by alkaline earth metal chloride additives. A third cluster of patents does not change the polymer backbone chemistry but instead improves HNBR compound performance through formulation: peroxide curing with unsaturated carboxylic acids/salts, microgels, non-reinforcing mineral fillers, basic silicates, and phosphine/phosphine oxide management. The landscape therefore spans precursor polymer design, catalytic hydrogenation, simultaneous metathesis-hydrogenation, and post-polymer compounding strategies. The strongest technical trends are toward lower Mooney viscosity, lower residual double bond content, narrower polydispersity, and improved compression set or high-temperature mechanical retention. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that either preserve backbone integrity while hydrogenating selectively, or intentionally reduce molecular weight before/while hydrogenating to improve processability, followed by formulation strategies that preserve modulus and aging resistance in demanding automotive and industrial applications.

- Example 3-2: Benzyl bromide led to non-detectable TPP in isolated HNBR and 0.94 wt.% TPP=O.
- Example 3-3: No reagent addition gave 1.33 wt.% TPP and 2.17 wt.% TPP=O in isolated HNBR.
- Example 3: The examples show that reagent choice affects residual phosphine and phosphine oxide levels in HNBR.

**Technical Relevance**
This patent is relevant because it addresses a practical issue in HNBR synthesis: residual phosphine/phosphine oxide and halogen contamination after hydrogenation. Such residues can strongly affect vulcanizate modulus and compression set, so the patent informs purification and catalyst-selection strategies.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US2473280B1 and the metathesis-degradation patents (US7772328B2, US7919563B2, US7737233B2, US8957161B2) all target lower molecular weight HNBR precursors, but EP2473280B1 does so in a single simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis step, whereas US7772328B2 and US7919563B2 use metathesis degradation followed by hydrogenation.
- US2473280B1 reports HNBR Mw of 20,000 to 250,000 and Mooney viscosity of 1 to 50, while US7737233B2 describes post-metathesis HNBR typically at Mw 30,000 to 250,000 and Mooney 3 to 50; the latter is a broader catalyst/process disclosure rather than a single optimized example set.
- US7772328B2 and US7919563B2 show strong catalyst/co-olefin dependence: ethylene at 6.9×10^5 Pa and 1-hexene at 150 g are both used, with reactor temperature 80°C and catalyst loadings of 0.05 to 0.25 phr.
- EP2473280B1 uses hydrogenation catalysts such as Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson’s catalyst with catalyst amounts around 0.055 to 0.080 phr and metal contents of 65.6 to 91.3 ppm, emphasizing catalyst choice as a control knob for RDB reduction.
- US9321910B2 and EP3196239A1 are formulation patents rather than synthesis patents: both start from hydrogenated nitrile rubber and focus on high-temperature vulcanizate performance, using microgels or filler/silicate combinations to improve compression set and aging resistance.
- EP3090002B1 differs from the other HNBR patents by focusing on residual phosphine/phosphine oxide and halogen content after hydrogenation; it highlights that catalyst residue management is important for final vulcanizate properties.
- US8957161B2 provides a salt-additive strategy for metathesis catalyst activation, which is mechanistically relevant to the metathesis-based HNBR route but is demonstrated on model metathesis substrates rather than HNBR itself.
- US7737233B2 and EP2473280B1 both rely on ruthenium/osmium carbene metathesis catalysts, but EP2473280B1 integrates hydrogenation into the same process, whereas US7737233B2 focuses on catalyst systems and process rationale.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that the most effective HNBR synthesis strategies are either selective hydrogenation of NBR with careful residue control or metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by hydrogenation, including one-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation. For practical HNBR manufacture, the evidence supports using NBR feedstocks with manageable Mooney viscosity, ruthenium- or osmium-based metathesis catalysts, and co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene when chain scission is desired. For high-performance vulcanizates, peroxide curing combined with unsaturated carboxylic acids/salts, microgels, or non-reinforcing mineral filler/basic silicate packages improves compression set and thermal aging. A technically supported synthesis window is HNBR with Mw roughly 20,000 to 250,000, Mooney viscosity below about 50, and low residual double bond content, followed by formulation strategies that preserve modulus and durability at 130 to 175°C.
