# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This technical report synthesizes polymerization and synthesis methodologies for High Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR) based on a selected portfolio of seven key patents published between 2011 and 2024. High Acrylonitrile NBR is defined by a bound acrylonitrile (ACN) content typically exceeding 30%, which imparts superior oil, fuel, and chemical resistance compared to low or medium ACN counterparts. The patent landscape reveals a strong emphasis on emulsion polymerization techniques, particularly cold emulsion processes conducted at 7°C to 8°C to minimize polymer branching and gel formation. Key formulation trends include the use of advanced redox initiator systems (such as hydroperoxides paired with reducing agents and iron catalysts like FeCl2 or FeSO4) and precise molecular weight control via incremental dosing of chain-transfer agents (e.g., tertiary dodecyl mercaptan). Furthermore, a major process trend centers on post-polymerization coagulation chemistry, where the choice of salt (magnesium chloride vs. calcium chloride), latex pH adjustment, and the incorporation of additives like gelatin are shown to critically influence the polymer's ion index, Mooney scorch, vulcanization kinetics, and mechanical properties. This report provides a comprehensive cross-patent comparison and detailed extraction of experimental recipes to guide future research and development in high-performance NBR synthesis.

#### Patent 2

**Patent Details**
- Patent Number: US9650502
- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of nitrile rubbers
- Assignee: Not disclosed
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2017-05-16
- Polymer Type: Nitrile Rubber (NBR)
- Relevance to target: Discloses emulsion polymerization recipes for NBR latices (Latex A, B, C) with varying acrylonitrile content.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**
- Polymerization process: Emulsion polymerization
- Monomer system: Acrylonitrile and butadiene
- Monomer ratio: Latex A: 500 g acrylonitrile to 4.37 kg butadiene; Latex B: 815 g acrylonitrile to 4.0 kg butadiene
- Water amount: 9.92 kg deionized water
- Emulsifier: Potassium stearate and DNMK (sodium salt of disproportionated rosin acid)
- Emulsifier loading: 0.53 kg of 5.4 wt% potassium stearate solution, 0.440 kg of DNMK
- Initiator: Diisopropylbenzene hydroperoxide (DIHP) and sodium formaldehyde sulfoxylate (FSS)
- Initiator loading: 6 g DIHP, 139 g FSS aqueous solution
- Catalyst / activator: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence.
- Chain-transfer agent: Tertiary dodecyl mercaptan (TDM)
- Chain-transfer dosage: Initial 18.72 g (Latex A) or 21.85 g (Latex B), with booster additions of 2.34 g or 5.45 g TDM at specific conversions (37% and 59%)
- Polymerization temperature: 7°C
- Pressure: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence.
- pH: Latex A: 8.5, Latex B: 8.5, Latex C: 8.6
- Reaction time: 7 hours
- Conversion: Interrupted at 70% conversion using hydroxylamine sulfate
- Coagulation conditions: Latex added to 43 kg water and 4 kg saturated magnesium sulfate solution
- Post-treatment: Washing with deionized water adjusted to pH 11.5-11.8 with KOH (14 g) at 45°C, filtering, and drying at 100°C for 24 hours
- Raw polymer properties: Mooney viscosity (ML 1+4 @ 100°C) of 60.5 (Latex A), 32.1 (Latex B), and 29.0 (Latex C); bound ACN of 28.1% (Latex A), 33.9% (Latex B), and 27.9% (Latex C)

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Latex A was prepared using 500 g acrylonitrile, 4.37 kg butadiene, and 18.72 g TDM, yielding 28.1% bound ACN and a Mooney viscosity of 60.5.
- Latex B was prepared with a higher initial ACN charge of 815 g and 4.0 kg butadiene, resulting in 33.9% bound ACN and a Mooney viscosity of 32.1.
- Polymerization was conducted at 7°C, initiated by DIHP/FSS, and short-stopped at 70% conversion after 7 hours using hydroxylamine sulfate.
- Coagulation was achieved using magnesium sulfate, followed by washing at pH 11.5-11.8 and drying to less than 1% residual humidity.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates precise control of bound acrylonitrile content and Mooney viscosity through incremental chain-transfer agent (TDM) dosing and low-temperature emulsion polymerization.
