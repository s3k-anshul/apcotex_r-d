# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION-CONTROLLED SYNTHESIS, LATEX PROCESSING, AND POST-MODIFICATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile-group-containing elastomers. The target material is defined as an NBR copolymer with reduced acrylonitrile content, typically below about 25 wt% and often in the 15–24 wt% range, where lower ACN improves low-temperature flexibility while retaining useful oil/fuel resistance. The selected patents span three distinct technical directions: direct NBR latex processing and coagulation control, controlled emulsion polymerization of nitrile-containing block copolymers, and post-polymerization hydrogenation of diene-based NBR latexes.

EP2238176B1 focuses on nitrile rubber containing acrylonitrile and 1,3-butadiene units, with very low chlorine content and improved latex coagulation behavior using polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) as a co-precipitant. The examples emphasize reduced salt demand for coagulation, rapid vulcanization, and low metal corrosivity of vulcanizates. EP3063192B1 discloses nitrile-group-containing copolymer rubbers with 10–60 wt% nitrile units, 31–89 wt% diene units, and 1–9 wt% acrylate-type termonomers, with hydrogenation levels of 50% or more; it is more aligned with HNBR-type materials than low-ACN NBR, but it provides composition-control and emulsion-processing insights. US7592409B2 teaches controlled emulsion polymerization using in-situ generated emulsifiers and controlled radical methods to make block copolymers containing acrylonitrile/isoprene or acrylonitrile/butadiene segments, demonstrating microemulsion formation, rapid conversion, and seed-latex block growth. US9657113B2 discloses direct hydrogenation of NBR latex using Ru/Os-based catalysts, showing that 66/34 butadiene/acrylonitrile latex can be hydrogenated efficiently in water without organic solvent.

Across the landscape, the dominant process themes are emulsion or latex-based synthesis, careful monomer composition control, low-salt or in-situ emulsifier strategies, and process conditions that preserve latex stability while enabling downstream vulcanization or hydrogenation. For low-ACN NBR synthesis, the strongest evidence supports emulsion polymerization with controlled feed/composition management, followed by coagulation routes that minimize ionic contamination and preserve cure performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2238176B1 and US9657113B2 both start from NBR latex, but EP2238176B1 focuses on coagulation/isolation and impurity control, whereas US9657113B2 focuses on direct hydrogenation of the latex.
- EP2238176B1 discloses acrylonitrile/butadiene NBR with very low chlorine content (4–25 ppm), while US9657113B2 uses a latex containing 66% butadiene and 34% acrylonitrile; neither patent targets very low ACN explicitly, but both are composition-relevant to NBR processing.
- EP3063192B1 uses much higher nitrile contents (10–60 wt%, preferably 34–43 wt%) and is therefore closer to HNBR-type chemistry than low-ACN NBR; it adds 1–9 wt% acrylate termonomer and requires 50%+ hydrogenation.
- US7592409B2 is the only patent in the set that explicitly teaches controlled emulsion polymerization and in-situ emulsifier formation, making it the most process-relevant for composition-controlled low-ACN NBR synthesis.
- US9657113B2 demonstrates that NBR latexes can be hydrogenated efficiently at 100–120°C and 500–1500 psi H2 with Ru/Os catalysts, but this is a downstream modification route rather than a synthesis route for low-ACN NBR.
- EP2238176B1 emphasizes PVA-assisted coagulation with MgCl2 at 20°C followed by heating to 90°C, showing a low-salt isolation strategy that may help preserve rubber quality after emulsion polymerization.
- US7592409B2 uses 65–75°C microemulsion polymerization with potassium persulfate, tripotassium phosphate, and potassium hydroxide, illustrating a controlled aqueous polymerization platform that could be adapted to acrylonitrile/butadiene feed control.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is most plausibly produced by emulsion polymerization with tight monomer-feed/composition control, followed by low-contamination coagulation and drying. Among the patents reviewed, US7592409B2 provides the strongest general synthesis platform for controlled aqueous polymerization, while EP2238176B1 provides the most directly relevant NBR isolation/coagulation strategy and US9657113B2 confirms that NBR latexes with substantial acrylonitrile content can be processed in water without solvent. EP3063192B1 is more relevant as a nitrile-elastomer composition reference than as a low-ACN NBR route. Based on the evidence, a practical low-ACN NBR process would favor emulsion polymerization of butadiene/acrylonitrile with controlled feed, moderate temperatures in the 65–75°C range, and coagulation using polyvalent salts with PVA or similar co-precipitants to minimize electrolyte demand and preserve vulcanization performance.
