# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND PROCESS/COMPOSITION EVIDENCE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents that intersect with low-acrylonitrile nitrile rubber chemistry, but they do so in different ways. US7592409B2 is primarily a controlled emulsion polymerization patent for styrene-based block copolymers that explicitly includes a nitrile-rubber block described as randomly distributed acrylonitrile and 1,3-butadiene; it is relevant as a process platform demonstrating aqueous microemulsion polymerization, in-situ emulsifier formation, and block-copolymer architectures containing NBR-like segments. US9624333B2 is more directly aligned with low-acrylonitrile NBR because it discloses ABS/graft-copolymer systems whose graft sheath contains acrylonitrile and/or methyl methacrylate, while the graft substrate is a butadiene-rich rubber prepared by aqueous emulsion polymerization. Across the two patents, the dominant synthesis motif is emulsion polymerization in water, often with potassium persulfate initiation, potassium hydroxide or bicarbonate buffering, and surfactant/soap stabilization. The first patent emphasizes controlled radical polymerization in microemulsion/latex form with in-situ surfactant generation and seed-latex block growth; the second emphasizes conventional aqueous emulsion polymerization of butadiene/styrene rubber substrates followed by agglomeration and grafting. For low-acrylonitrile NBR, the most important evidence is the explicit use of acrylonitrile as a comonomer in a rubber block or graft sheath and the ability to tune rubber composition through feed strategy and aqueous emulsion processing. However, neither patent is a dedicated low-ACN NBR grade patent; instead, they provide enabling process and composition windows that can support low-acrylonitrile rubber design, especially where acrylonitrile is present at controlled, relatively modest levels in a butadiene-rich copolymer. The landscape therefore points to aqueous emulsion routes, staged monomer addition, and composition control as the main levers for low-ACN NBR-related materials.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7592409B2 focuses on controlled aqueous emulsion/microemulsion polymerization with in-situ emulsifier formation and explicitly includes acrylonitrile-containing rubber blocks, whereas US9624333B2 uses conventional aqueous emulsion polymerization of a butadiene/styrene graft substrate followed by grafting with an acrylonitrile-containing sheath.
- US7592409B2 discloses block-copolymer architecture (A-B-A triblock) and controlled radical methods (RAFT/NMP-type conditions), while US9624333B2 discloses graft copolymer architecture with agglomerated rubber substrate and SAN-type sheath.
- Both patents rely on water as the polymerization medium, but US7592409B2 emphasizes microemulsion/latex formation from latent surfactant systems, while US9624333B2 emphasizes staged monomer feed, potassium stearate emulsification, and agglomeration of rubber particles.
- For acrylonitrile content, US7592409B2 only states that the B block may comprise acrylonitrile and methacrylonitrile with 1,3-butadiene or isoprene, without a numeric ACN range; US9624333B2 explicitly sets the graft sheath at 10 to 30 wt % acrylonitrile and/or methyl methacrylate.
- US9624333B2 provides the more direct low-ACN compositional guidance for NBR-related materials, while US7592409B2 is more of an enabling process patent for aqueous controlled polymerization of nitrile-rubber-containing blocks.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR-related synthesis is most strongly supported by aqueous emulsion routes with staged monomer addition and surfactant/initiator control. US9624333B2 is the most compositionally relevant patent because it explicitly discloses an acrylonitrile-containing sheath at 10 to 30 wt % and a butadiene-rich emulsion-prepared rubber substrate, while US7592409B2 contributes a controlled microemulsion/block-copolymer methodology that can accommodate acrylonitrile/butadiene segments. Based on the extracted evidence, the most defensible synthesis direction for low-ACN NBR is water-based emulsion polymerization with controlled feed strategy, potassium persulfate initiation, and careful surfactant/soap stabilization; however, the exact low-ACN rubber grade range is not directly quantified in US7592409B2 and is only indirectly supported in US9624333B2 through the acrylonitrile-containing graft sheath.
