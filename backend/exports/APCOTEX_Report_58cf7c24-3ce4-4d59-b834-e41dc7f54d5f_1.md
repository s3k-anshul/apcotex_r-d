# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT PROCESS PATENTS LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence on hydrogenated nitrile-butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on routes that control residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and processability while preserving the characteristic oil, heat, and ozone resistance of nitrile rubber-derived elastomers. The patent set spans two major manufacturing philosophies: post-polymerization modification of NBR by hydrogenation, and integrated degradation/hydrogenation strategies that first reduce NBR chain length and then hydrogenate the remaining olefinic sites. Across the landscape, the target is consistently defined as HNBR or liquid HNBR with reduced Mooney viscosity, narrower or deliberately broadened molecular-weight distribution depending on end use, and controlled acrylonitrile content and hydrogenation degree.

The older patents emphasize catalyst-enabled molecular-weight reduction of NBR prior to or during hydrogenation. EP2473281B1 discloses simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation of nitrile rubber using ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems in hydrogen, aiming to produce low-molecular-weight HNBR with low residual double bond content. US7951875B2 and US20230340237A1 both describe two-step degradation/hydrogenation workflows in chlorobenzene using Zhan-type catalysts, with the latter targeting liquid HNBR having ACN 15-50%, hydrogenation 75-99.5%, Mw 3,000-60,000, and PDI 2.0-8.0. These routes demonstrate that catalyst choice, hydrogen pressure, temperature, and degradation time strongly govern final Mw, PDI, and hydrogenation degree.

In contrast, EP2478021B1 focuses on the precursor NBR synthesis in organic solvent by free-radical solution polymerization with modifiers/RAFT-like control, followed optionally by hydrogenation. Although it is not a direct HNBR hydrogenation patent, it is relevant because precursor microstructure, monomer feed, and molecular-weight control determine downstream hydrogenation behavior and final HNBR properties. US8957161B2 is adjacent rather than direct: it discloses salt-accelerated metathesis of nitrile rubber using Grubbs-type catalysts, which is relevant as a chain-scission tool for preparing lower-MW HNBR precursors. Overall, the evidence supports solution-phase hydrogenation in chlorobenzene or related solvents, ruthenium-based metathesis/hydrogenation catalysts or Zhan catalysts, and careful control of degradation severity to balance fluidity, mechanical strength, and residual unsaturation.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473281B1 and US20230340237A1 both target low-molecular-weight HNBR, but EP2473281B1 uses simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation while US20230340237A1 uses sequential degradation then hydrogenation.
- US7951875B2 also uses metathesis-based chain scission before hydrogenation, but emphasizes co-olefin-assisted degradation and reports product Mw around 54 kg/mol and PDI near 2.0.
- US20230340237A1 operates in chlorobenzene with Zhan catalyst at 80° C. degradation and 150° C. hydrogenation under 8 MPa H2, whereas US7951875B2 reports MCB solvent, 80° C. reactor temperature, and ethylene or 1-hexene co-olefins.
- EP2473281B1 reports catalyst loadings in phr and metal ppm, with HNBR 1 showing Mooney viscosity 33.3 and RDB down to 0.4% after 240 min; this is a more integrated one-pot strategy than the two-step routes.
- EP2478021B1 is upstream of the hydrogenation patents: it controls NBR precursor synthesis in solution using Vazo® 88, DoPAT, and DMAc, which can influence downstream hydrogenation behavior and final HNBR properties.
- US8957161B2 is mechanistically adjacent rather than a direct HNBR hydrogenation patent; it shows salt-accelerated metathesis with Grubbs II catalyst and supports the broader chain-degradation toolbox used to prepare lower-MW HNBR precursors.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence shows that successful HNBR synthesis depends on controlling precursor unsaturation, chain length, and hydrogenation severity. The strongest direct routes are simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation with ruthenium/osmium catalysts (EP2473281B1) and sequential degradation/hydrogenation with Zhan-type catalysts in chlorobenzene (US20230340237A1), both of which can deliver low Mw, low residual double bond content, and acceptable PDI. For practical low-MW HNBR, the evidence supports solution processing in chlorobenzene or monochlorobenzene-class solvents, catalyst dosages in the low phr or sub-percent range, hydrogenation at elevated pressure (8 MPa in the liquid-HNBR patent), and degradation temperatures around 80° C. followed by hydrogenation near 150° C. Where precursor control is needed, solution polymerization of NBR with controlled regulator dosage and monomer feed is important. Overall, the best-supported synthesis strategy is to combine controlled NBR precursor synthesis with either one-pot metathesis/hydrogenation or staged degradation/hydrogenation to reach Mw roughly 8,000-60,000, hydrogenation 91-99%, and PDI around 2-6 for liquid or processable HNBR grades.
