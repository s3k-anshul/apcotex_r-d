# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESSING PATENT LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes nine patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), spanning direct hydrogenation of NBR, simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes, catalyst-residue control, and downstream compounding or application-driven processing. The target material is defined as nitrile-butadiene rubber with reduced residual unsaturation, typically retaining the nitrile-containing backbone while converting a substantial fraction of butadiene-derived double bonds to saturated linkages to improve heat, oil, and ozone resistance. Across the selected patents, the dominant synthesis strategies are: (1) homogeneous noble-metal hydrogenation of NBR in solution or cement, often using ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium catalysts; (2) one-step hydrogenation plus metathesis to lower molecular weight and narrow polydispersity; and (3) post-hydrogenation processing into blends, tires, or bonded flexible pipes where HNBR performance is leveraged.

The landscape shows two major process directions. First, metathesis-enabled depolymerization/hydrogenation patents (US7919563B2, US7772328B2, EP2473280B1, US9150669B2) aim to reduce molecular weight and Mooney viscosity while maintaining the HNBR microstructure. These disclosures emphasize ruthenium/osmium carbene catalysts, co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, and reaction conditions around 80 °C and elevated pressure. Second, conventional hydrogenation patents (EP3827029A1) focus on catalyst selection, catalyst loading, and especially catalyst removal/recovery to avoid discoloration and aging penalties; they specify Ru, Pd, and Rh loadings and broad hydrogenation windows. The remaining patents are application/process patents that incorporate HNBR into blends or bonded structures and disclose practical compounding parameters such as peroxide type, scorch time, acrylonitrile content, residual double bond content, and cure temperature.

Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that combine controlled hydrogenation with either metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction or careful catalyst management. The most technically relevant parameters are moderate hydrogenation temperatures, sufficient hydrogen pressure, low catalyst loadings, and post-reaction purification or catalyst recovery to preserve aging performance and color stability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- The metathesis-assisted HNBR patents (US7919563B2, US7772328B2, EP2473280B1, US9150669B2) all rely on ruthenium/osmium catalysts and elevated temperature, with 80°C explicitly shown in the low-MW examples and hydrogen/co-olefin assistance used to drive chain scission.
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 disclose very similar process windows: 15 wt.-% or 6 wt.-% cement, 6.9×10^5 Pa ethylene, 150 g 1-hexene in one example, and catalyst loadings of 0.05-0.25 phr; both report Mw reductions to the 50-60 kg/mol range and PDI near 2.0 in the best case.
- EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 compare catalyst systems directly; EP2473280B1 shows Grubbs III outperforming Grubbs II and Wilkinson's catalyst at equal metal content, while US9150669B2 shows Hoveyda/Arlt-type catalysts outperforming Wilkinson's catalyst in hydrogenation rate and molecular-weight reduction.
- EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 both emphasize simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis, but EP2473280B1 reports lower residual double bond contents more rapidly with Grubbs III, whereas US9150669B2 highlights the benefit of catalyst/co-catalyst selection and reports PDI values down to 2.34.
- EP3827029A1 differs from the metathesis patents by focusing on conventional hydrogenation with Ru/Pd/Rh loadings in the ppm range and broad conditions of 60-200°C, 700000-15000000 Pa, and 1-24 h, with catalyst removal/recovery as a central process issue.
- The application patents (US20130303700A1, US10759885B2, US10731020B2, US10641418B2) do not primarily synthesize HNBR but show how partially hydrogenated or hydrogenated rubber is compounded: metathesis blending with silicone, hydrogenated branched-diene precursor design, hollow-particle lightweight molding, and peroxide-vulcanized bonded pipe construction.
- US10641418B2 is the most application-specific HNBR disclosure, requiring Mooney 05, 150°C scorch time of at least 10 minutes and vulcanization at at least 160°C to achieve strong PVDF adhesion, whereas US10731020B2 uses HNBR only as one possible base rubber in a lightweight molding composition.
- Across the synthesis patents, low residual unsaturation is consistently linked to performance: EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 target low RDB, while US10641418B2 specifies residual double bond content below 2% for the HNBR compound used in the pipe.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two main HNBR manufacturing strategies: direct hydrogenation with careful catalyst management, and simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis to lower molecular weight and Mooney viscosity. For synthesis, the strongest evidence favors ruthenium-based homogeneous catalysts, hydrogenation temperatures around 80°C for metathesis-assisted routes, and catalyst loadings in the low phr or ppm range, with co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene used to promote controlled chain scission. For conventional hydrogenation, broad windows of 60-200°C and 700000-15000000 Pa are disclosed, but catalyst residue removal is critical to preserve aging and color stability. For downstream HNBR compounding, peroxide systems with scorch times of at least 10 minutes and cure temperatures of 160-175°C appear beneficial in demanding bonded structures. Overall, the best-supported synthesis parameters are low catalyst loading, controlled hydrogen pressure, moderate temperature, and post-reaction purification or catalyst recovery to achieve low residual unsaturation, low Mooney viscosity, and good long-term performance.
