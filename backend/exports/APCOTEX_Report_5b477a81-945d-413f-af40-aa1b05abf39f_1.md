# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: POLYMERIZATION, RECOVERY, AND POWDER-FORM PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) preparation and post-polymerization handling, with emphasis on acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymerization and process control. The target material is low acrylonitrile NBR, defined here as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile incorporation relative to standard grades, typically sought to improve low-temperature flexibility while retaining oil and fuel resistance. Across the selected patents, the disclosed inventions span continuous emulsion polymerization, latex coagulation/powder recovery, and stabilized latex recovery with pH-controlled reslurrying. The landscape shows that NBR is predominantly produced by aqueous emulsion polymerization of acrylonitrile and 1,3-butadiene, with water serving as the polymerization medium in all three patents, though the downstream objectives differ substantially.

US5770660A focuses on a continuous, multi-vessel emulsion polymerization strategy in which butadiene is incrementally added to maintain a high acrylonitrile/butadiene monomer ratio and achieve a latex with about fifty percent bound acrylonitrile. The patent emphasizes composition control through staged butadiene feed, steady-state conversion management, and recovery operations including coagulation, washing, dewatering, drying, and baling. EP0071240A1 is centered on direct production of NBR powder from latex by using sodium lauryl sulfate as emulsifier and a critical magnesium/aluminum sulfate coagulant system; it demonstrates that powder-size coagulum can be obtained from NBR latices with bound acrylonitrile contents around 31–34.6%. US5708132A does not disclose NBR synthesis conditions, but it is relevant to NBR manufacturing because it improves recovery of nitrile rubber latex through antioxidant stabilization, salt/acid coagulation, wash-water reslurrying, and pH adjustment to reduce cure variability and improve storage stability. Collectively, the patents show that low-to-mid acrylonitrile NBR is typically made by aqueous emulsion copolymerization, while downstream water-based coagulation and washing steps are critical to product form, stability, and processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US5770660A and EP0071240A1 both use aqueous emulsion/latex NBR chemistry, but US5770660A is a continuous polymerization/composition-control patent while EP0071240A1 is a latex coagulation and powder-recovery patent.
- US5770660A discloses much higher target acrylonitrile incorporation, centered around about 50 wt% bound acrylonitrile, whereas EP0071240A1 reports NBR latices at 31-34.6 wt% bound acrylonitrile.
- US5770660A controls composition by staged butadiene addition across multiple reactors; EP0071240A1 controls product form by sodium lauryl sulfate emulsifier selection and Mg/Al sulfate coagulation chemistry.
- US5708132A is not a synthesis patent but a recovery/stabilization patent; it uses coagulation, wash-water reslurrying, and pH adjustment to improve the properties of recovered nitrile rubber.
- All three patents rely on water-based processing, but only US5770660A and EP0071240A1 explicitly disclose polymerization in aqueous emulsion; US5708132A focuses on post-polymerization latex recovery and washing.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The selected patents show that NBR manufacturing is predominantly aqueous-emulsion based, with composition control and recovery chemistry being the main levers for product quality. US5770660A provides the strongest disclosure on monomer-feed control for high-bound-acrylonitrile NBR, EP0071240A1 demonstrates direct powder recovery from NBR latex with low-to-mid acrylonitrile content, and US5708132A improves latex recovery and post-coagulation stability through antioxidant and pH management. Together, they define a practical process landscape for NBR synthesis and handling, even though only US5770660A directly targets composition control at the polymerization stage.
