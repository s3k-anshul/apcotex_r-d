# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This technical report synthesizes patent evidence for Hydrogenated Acrylonitrile NBR (HNBR), analyzing 12 primary patents published between 2009 and 2021. The research scope focuses on the catalytic hydrogenation of nitrile rubbers and the preparation of low molecular weight or functionalized HNBR variants. Key technical approaches include metathesis-mediated degradation using ruthenium-based catalysts (such as Grubbs, Hoveyda, and Arlt complexes) to control molecular weight, and heterogeneous catalytic systems (such as Raney Cobalt and supported Palladium) for hydrogenation and functionalization. Process trends reveal a strong emphasis on achieving low gel content (<5%), controlled Mooney viscosity, and introducing aminomethyl groups to enable alternative dianhydride-based crosslinking pathways. This report provides a comprehensive cross-patent comparison of reaction conditions, catalyst efficiencies, and resulting polymer properties.

#### Patent 3

**Patent Details**
- Patent Number: US9079979
- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of hydrogenated nitrile rubber
- Assignee: Not disclosed
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2015
- Material Type: Hydrogenated Nitrile Rubber (HNBR)
- Relevance to target: Compares hydrogenation performance of Grubbs II, Grubbs III, and Wilkinson's catalysts.

**Hydrogenation Method**
- Catalyst Type: Grubbs III / Grubbs II / Wilkinson's (Comparison of hydrogenation catalysts)
- Catalyst Amount: 0.055 - 0.080 phr (HNBR 1 to 4)
- Metal Content: 65.6 - 91.3 ppm (Catalyst metal content)
- Reaction Time: 120 min (Grubbs III hydrogenates faster in first 120 min)

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Grubbs III catalyst at 0.057 phr (65.6 ppm metal) achieved 0.4% residual double bonds (RDB) after 120 min.
- Wilkinson's catalyst at 0.06 phr achieved 3.1% RDB after 120 min.
- Mooney viscosity of the resulting HNBR ranged from 20.2 to 69.3 depending on the catalyst and degradation.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates that Grubbs III catalyst provides faster hydrogenation kinetics than Grubbs II or Wilkinson's catalyst at equivalent metal contents.

**Synthesis Method**

- Hydrogenation Temperature: 60 - 190 °C (Preferably 100 to 180 °C)

- Gel Content: < 10 % (Preferably less than 5%)

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**

**Technical Relevance**

**Trend Analysis:**
- Catalyst Selection: Metathesis-based ruthenium catalysts (Grubbs II/III, Hoveyda, Arlt) are preferred for simultaneous degradation and hydrogenation to produce low molecular weight HNBR, whereas Raney Cobalt is utilized for functionalization (aminomethylation) and supported Pd is used for standard hydrogenation.
- Process Conditions: Hydrogenation temperatures typically range from 80 °C to 160 °C, with pressures varying widely from 500 psi (approx. 34 bar) for metathesis degradation up to 120 bar for Raney Cobalt-catalyzed aminomethylation.
- Molecular Weight Control: Metathesis degradation processes successfully reduce the weight-average molecular weight (Mw) from ~296 kg/mol down to ~54 kg/mol, significantly lowering Mooney viscosity to improve processability.
- Functionalization and Crosslinking: Aminomethyl-functionalized HNBR (1-5 wt% amine content) enables alternative crosslinking with dianhydrides (e.g., pyromellitic dianhydride) at 170-180 °C, achieving high tensile strength (up to 27.1 MPa) without peroxide or sulfur.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent landscape for Hydrogenated Acrylonitrile NBR (HNBR) reveals a strong focus on process optimization, molecular weight reduction, and functionalization. Metathesis degradation using ruthenium-based catalysts (Grubbs, Hoveyda) is a dominant trend for producing low molecular weight HNBR with improved processability. Additionally, aminomethyl functionalization using Raney Cobalt catalysts introduces novel crosslinking pathways via dianhydrides, offering peroxide-free vulcanization options. Gaps in the disclosed patent data include detailed long-term dynamic mechanical properties and specific emulsifier/stabilizer formulations used during the initial polymerization stage.
