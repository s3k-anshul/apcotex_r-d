# NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SYNTHESIS, COMPOSITION, AND PROCESSING OF NITRILE BUTADIENE RUBBER AND RELATED FORMULATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a primary patent set centered on nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile-rubber embodiments, with emphasis on preparation, composition control, and downstream processing. Across the selected patents, the target material is defined either as NBR itself, highly saturated nitrile rubber compositions derived from NBR, or NBR-containing blends and functionalized rubber systems where acrylonitrile-butadiene identity remains central. The landscape spans classical emulsion polymerization of NBR, metathesis-based degradation of NBR in organic solvent, and multiple formulation patents that tune NBR performance through silica chemistry, crosslinking packages, and filler architecture.

The polymerization-oriented disclosures show that NBR can be prepared by aqueous emulsion routes using water, acrylonitrile, 1,3-butadiene, emulsifiers, initiators, and chain-transfer agents, with composition control expressed through nitrile content ranges and monomer feed ratios. Other patents focus on post-polymerization modification or processing of NBR, including metathesis degradation in chlorobenzene with 1-hexene and ruthenium catalysts, where the goal is to reduce molecular weight and improve processability before hydrogenation. Several patents are not synthesis routes per se but are highly relevant because they define formulation windows for NBR compounds: silica CaO content, silica pH, crosslinking agents, peroxide cure systems, and laminar inorganic fillers are used to improve compression set, tensile properties, gasoline permeation resistance, or electro-mechanical behavior.

A recurring trend is the use of water in emulsion polymerization, latex handling, coagulation, washing, or flocculation, while solvent-based patents keep the core reaction medium organic even when water appears in workup. Composition trends include acrylonitrile content windows from about 15 wt% to 60 wt% depending on the embodiment, and performance-driven selection of fillers and crosslinking systems to balance oil resistance, elasticity, processability, and heat resistance. Overall, the selected patents collectively show that NBR innovation is driven by both polymerization chemistry and downstream compounding strategies, with water-rich latex processing dominating synthesis disclosures and formulation engineering dominating property optimization.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US8957161B2 uses NBR as a degradation substrate in chlorobenzene solution, whereas EP2009050B1 and EP2463335B1 are latex/emulsion-based nitrile-rubber composition patents with water-centered processing.
- EP2009050B1 explicitly discloses nitrile-rubber monomer content windows of 15 to 55 wt% (preferred) and 20 to 50 wt% (particularly preferred), while US3297792A reports blend-component acrylonitrile contents of 20%, 35%, 40%, and 50% in acrylonitrile-diene copolymers.
- US8957161B2 and EP2027920B1 are metathesis/catalyst patents; only US8957161B2 directly discloses NBR composition data, while EP2027920B1 is catalyst-centric and does not disclose NBR composition values.
- US8133948B2 and EP2009050B1 are compounding-focused: both use silica/crosslinking packages to improve heat resistance or compression set, but only EP2009050B1 includes a direct nitrile-rubber production example.
- US11347156B2 and US3297792A both use NBR-family rubbers as functional components in blends or layered structures rather than as synthesis targets; their acrylonitrile content disclosures are tied to material selection rather than polymerization control.
- US9376537B2 and US10087293B2 are latex-processing patents; both rely on water-rich media and downstream coagulation/washing or drying, but neither is a conventional NBR synthesis disclosure.
- Across the primary set, the most explicit acrylonitrile-content disclosures appear in EP2009050B1, US11347156B2, and US3297792A; the remaining patents either do not disclose NBR composition values or disclose them only indirectly through non-target embodiments.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, NBR innovation is split between true synthesis/production disclosures and downstream performance engineering. EP2009050B1 is the clearest NBR-production patent, giving an aqueous emulsion route and explicit nitrile-monomer content windows, while US8957161B2 addresses NBR degradation in organic solvent and US3297792A shows acrylonitrile-diene copolymers as blend components. The remaining patents mainly optimize NBR-containing formulations through silica chemistry, latex processing, or functional composite architectures. Overall, the set shows that acrylonitrile content is most often controlled at the polymer-design stage, whereas later patents emphasize compounding, filler selection, and process medium management to tune workability, compression set, and barrier or electro-mechanical performance.
