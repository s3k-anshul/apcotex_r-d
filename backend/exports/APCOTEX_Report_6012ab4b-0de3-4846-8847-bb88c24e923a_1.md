# HIGH ACRYLONITRILE NBR: ORGANIC-SOLVENT FREE-RADICAL AND RELATED COPOLYMERIZATION ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to high acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on solution-based and composition-controlled routes that can support elevated acrylonitrile incorporation. The target material is defined as NBR with increased acrylonitrile or bound acrylonitrile content relative to standard grades, typically achieved by controlling the acrylonitrile/butadiene monomer feed, conversion, and regulator level during copolymerization. Across the patent set, the dominant disclosed chemistry is free-radical copolymerization of at least one conjugated diene with at least one alpha,beta-unsaturated nitrile in organic solvent, often with optional termonomers and molecular-weight regulators. One patent focuses on solution polymerization in specific solvent mixtures, while another discloses solution polymerization in organic solvents using RAFT-type regulators and staged initiator addition. A third patent, although not an NBR disclosure, provides a useful comparator for controlled solution copolymerization in organic media using a neodymium Ziegler-Natta catalyst system and demonstrates how feed composition and catalyst design can be used to tune diene copolymer microstructure and incorporation behavior.

The landscape shows two major process trends for high-acrylonitrile NBR: first, organic-solution polymerization as an alternative to conventional emulsion polymerization, intended to avoid latex isolation before downstream hydrogenation and to improve process integration; second, deliberate control of monomer feed and reaction time to manage conversion, molecular weight, and composition drift. The EP and US NBR patents both emphasize organic solvents, free-radical initiation, and molecular-weight control, with one explicitly using dodecyl mercaptan-type regulators in the background and the other using a RAFT regulator (DoPAT) with Vazo 88 initiator. The examples show acrylonitrile charges around 37 phm in one case and controlled conversion endpoints of 39% and 48% in others, indicating that composition and conversion are central levers. The comparator patent demonstrates solution polymerization with 1-phenylbutadiene/butadiene using a neodymium catalyst system, highlighting the broader relevance of controlled monomer feed and catalyst selection for diene copolymer synthesis.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2478021B1 and US20140235744A1 both disclose free-radical nitrile rubber synthesis in organic solvent, but EP2478021B1 provides concrete acrylonitrile/butadiene example charges and RAFT-type control, whereas US20140235744A1 emphasizes a two-solvent system and improved time-conversion behavior.
- EP2478021B1 uses Vazo 88 and DoPAT with solution polymerization in DMAc at 100°C and 3 bar butadiene; US20140235744A1 does not extract example initiator data, but claims a main-solvent fraction of 70 to 99.9 vol% and discusses prior-art conversion limits around 40% over 40 h.
- EP2478021B1 explicitly reports acrylonitrile at 37 phm and butadiene at 63 phm in Example 1, supporting a high-ACN feed strategy; US20140235744A1 does not expose explicit monomer ratios in the extracted evidence.
- EP2478021B1 includes optional termonomers such as HEA and HDODA and staged initiator addition in selected examples, indicating broader composition control; US20140235744A1 focuses more on solvent architecture than on termonomer design.
- EP2727948B1 is mechanistically different, using a neodymium Ziegler-Natta catalyst system for diene copolymerization rather than nitrile-rubber free-radical chemistry, but it illustrates how solution polymerization, catalyst composition, and feed level can control incorporation and microstructure.
- Among the three, EP2478021B1 is the most directly applicable to high-acrylonitrile NBR because it explicitly polymerizes acrylonitrile with butadiene in solution and reports conversion, temperature, pressure, and regulator levels.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports organic-solution free-radical copolymerization as the most relevant route to high-acrylonitrile NBR, with composition control achieved by acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratio, regulator selection, and conversion management. EP2478021B1 is the strongest direct disclosure, showing acrylonitrile at 37 phm, butadiene at 63 phm, 100°C polymerization, 3 bar butadiene, and RAFT-type control in DMAc, while US20140235744A1 reinforces the value of solvent-mixture design for improved time-conversion performance. Based on the evidence, a practical high-ACN NBR synthesis window would center on solution polymerization in an organic solvent, controlled monomer feed with elevated acrylonitrile fraction, and careful molecular-weight regulation; staged initiator addition and optional termonomers can be used when broader property tuning is required.
