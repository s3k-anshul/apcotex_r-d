# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION-PHASE STYRENE-BUTADIENE RUBBER WITH TIN-MODIFIED CONTINUOUS POLYMERIZATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes the patent evidence for low-styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) with emphasis on polymerization route, monomer feed, and process control. The selected patent landscape in the provided manifest is narrow and centers on a single primary patent, US6313232B1, which discloses continuous solution polymerization of a conjugated diene monomer or a diene/vinyl aromatic copolymer in hydrocarbon solvent using an organolithium initiator. Although the patent is not explicitly framed as a low-styrene invention, it is directly relevant because it covers butadiene/styrene copolymerization, the core chemistry used to make SBR, and it provides process conditions that influence copolymer architecture and downstream tire performance.

The disclosed synthesis approach is a continuous hydrocarbon-solution process with staged tin-compound addition during polymerization and again after withdrawal of the polymerization mixture. The patent emphasizes that adding tin compounds at 25–80% conversion, preferably 50–80%, and then again inline after reactor discharge improves hysteresis-related properties, including reduced Payne effect and lower tan delta at 50 °C. The evidence also identifies the use of organolithium initiators, hydrocarbon solvents such as hexane, and tin compounds including tin tetrachloride, dibutyltin dichloride, and tributyltin chloride. The examples show post-reaction termination with isopropanol, solvent removal by drum drying, and compounding with carbon black and conventional tire additives.

From a low-styrene SBR perspective, the patent is most useful as a process-control reference rather than a composition-specific low-styrene disclosure. It demonstrates how continuous solution polymerization and post-polymerization tin treatment can be used to tailor rubber performance in butadiene/styrene systems, which is relevant to industrial SBR grades where styrene content is adjusted to meet rolling resistance and wet traction targets.
