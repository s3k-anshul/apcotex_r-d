# CARBOXYLATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND SYNTHESIS/USE ANALYSIS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to carboxylated nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymers are used in heat-activable adhesive systems. The primary patent in the manifest does not disclose a standalone polymerization recipe for making the carboxylated NBR; instead, it focuses on formulation and end-use performance of an adhesive tape for flexible printed circuit boards (FPCBs). The target material is defined as an acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer, used with epoxy resin at a weight ratio greater than 1.5 and without an additional nonpolymer hardener. The evidence indicates that the carboxylated nitrile rubber functions as both elastomer and reactive component, enabling heat-induced crosslinking with epoxy resin while maintaining low flow at elevated temperature.

Across the disclosed examples, the patent compares a carboxylated NBR/epoxy formulation against a formulation containing unmodified nitrile rubber and a hardener, and against a formulation with added polyterpene resin. The carboxylated NBR examples show improved bond strength and temperature stability, while all examples pass solder-bath resistance. The patent also specifies that suitable nitrile rubbers may have acrylonitrile contents of 15% to 50% by weight, and that the adhesive crosslinks above 150°C. The synthesis/process emphasis is therefore on solution coating of adhesive compositions, drying, lamination at 100°C, and final bonding at 200°C under 1.3 MPa for one hour, rather than on polymer synthesis.

Overall, the patent landscape here is formulation-driven: carboxylated NBR is treated as a reactive elastomer for high-temperature adhesive tapes, not as a separately synthesized polymer in the examples. The most important process trend is the use of acid functionality to eliminate the need for external epoxy hardener while preserving flexibility, solvent processability, and thermal resistance in FPCB bonding applications.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports carboxylated NBR as a reactive, formulation-enabling elastomer rather than a separately detailed synthesis target in this patent set. The key disclosed design space is an acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer with 15% to 50% acrylonitrile, combined with epoxy resin at a weight ratio greater than 1.5 and processed from solution into heat-activable adhesive tapes. For practical synthesis/formulation, the most defensible parameters from the record are acid-modified NBR, epoxy resin, butanone solution coating, drying to a 25 μm layer in Example 1, lamination at 100°C, final bonding at 200°C and 1.3 MPa for 1 hour, and crosslinking above 150°C. The patent indicates that carboxylation improves bond strength and temperature stability while eliminating the need for an external epoxy hardener.
