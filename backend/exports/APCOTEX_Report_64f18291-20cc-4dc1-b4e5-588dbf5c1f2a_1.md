# HIGH ACRYLONITRILE NBR: ORGANIC-SOLVENT FREE-RADICAL COPOLYMERIZATION AND COMPOSITION-CONTROLLED NITRILE RUBBER SYNTHESIS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to high acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on synthesis routes that control acrylonitrile incorporation through monomer feed, solvent choice, initiator/regulator selection, and conversion management. The single primary patent in the manifest, EP2478021B1, discloses solution-phase free-radical preparation of nitrile rubbers in organic solvents using a conjugated diene, an α,β-unsaturated nitrile, and optional additional copolymerizable monomers, together with a modifier/regulator system. The patent is not limited to a single high-AN grade, but its examples explicitly demonstrate acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymerization in dimethylacetamide (DMAc) with controlled radical chemistry, as well as terpolymer variants containing hydroxyethyl acrylate, HDODA, and styrene. The disclosed examples show practical composition control via monomer charge ratios expressed in parts by weight per 100 parts monomer mixture, with acrylonitrile and butadiene fed at 37 phm and 63 phm in Example 1, and polymerization initiated at 100°C under 3 bar butadiene pressure. The process also uses oxygen exclusion, argon degassing, and post-polymerization precipitation in ethanol containing stabilizer. Across the examples, the patent emphasizes that solution polymerization can be used to prepare nitrile rubbers and functionalized nitrile rubbers with tailored molecular weight and architecture, including low-molecular-weight RAFT macromers and block/terpolymer structures. For high acrylonitrile NBR synthesis, the key technical message is that composition is governed primarily by monomer ratio and controlled radical conditions rather than by post-modification. The evidence supports solution polymerization in organic solvent, controlled initiator/regulator dosing, and conversion monitoring as the main process levers for achieving nitrile rubbers with targeted acrylonitrile content and manageable viscosity.

## 4. CONCLUSION
EP2478021B1 supports solution-phase free-radical synthesis as a viable route to nitrile rubbers with controlled composition and molecular weight. For high acrylonitrile NBR, the most evidence-based parameters are acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratio control, oxygen-free operation, organic solvent use (DMAc), polymerization at 100°C, and controlled radical initiation/regulation with Vazo® 88 and DoPAT. The patent suggests that composition tuning is achieved during polymerization rather than by downstream modification, making it relevant for designing high-AN NBR recipes with manageable viscosity and tailored architecture.
