# POLYMERIZATION / SYNTHESIS PATENT RESEARCH REPORT
## Low Acrylonitrile NBR

### 1. Abstract
The patent landscape for Low Acrylonitrile NBR (Nitrile Butadiene Rubber) reveals a focus on improving synthesis methods to achieve lower acrylonitrile content while maintaining or enhancing the material's properties. Major scientific approaches include emulsion polymerization and bulk polymerization, with recurring variables such as reaction temperature, pressure, and the ratio of butadiene to acrylonitrile. Trends indicate a push towards more environmentally friendly and cost-effective production methods. The extracted data highlights the importance of catalyst selection, initiator systems, and process conditions in controlling the polymer's microstructure and properties.

### 2. Research Scope & Selection Method
The patents included in this report were qualified based on their direct relevance to the synthesis or polymerization of Low Acrylonitrile NBR. Only patents that demonstrated the synthesis of the target chemistry through various polymerization methods were considered. The selection method ensured that the patents provided detailed information on the polymerization process, including reaction conditions, monomer ratios, and catalyst systems.

### 4. Cross-Patent Comparison
Comparing the ranges of key parameters across the different patents:
- Acrylonitrile content ranges from 12 wt% to 18 wt%.
- Butadiene content ranges from 82 wt% to 88 wt%.
- Reaction temperatures are around 50 °C.
- Reaction pressures vary, with one example at 10 bar.
- Catalyst and initiator systems vary, including Bis(2-ethylhexanoate)nickel and potassium persulfate.

### 5. Key Technical Trends
Common technical trends include the use of specific catalyst systems to control the microstructure of the polymer, the importance of reaction conditions such as temperature and pressure in achieving the desired acrylonitrile content, and the focus on reducing the acrylonitrile content while maintaining the material's properties. Novel conditions and materials include the use of environmentally friendly solvents and the development of new initiator systems.
