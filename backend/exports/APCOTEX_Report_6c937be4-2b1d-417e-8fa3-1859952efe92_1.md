# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) PATENT LANDSCAPE: LOW-VISCOSITY, LOW-UNSATURATION, AND CATALYST-CONTROLLED SYNTHESIS ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents addressing hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) with emphasis on low residual unsaturation, reduced molecular weight, and improved processability. Across the set, the target material is consistently defined as hydrogenated nitrile rubber derived from nitrile rubber/NBR precursors containing conjugated diene and α,β-unsaturated nitrile repeat units, with the desired outcome being a saturated or reduced-unsaturation elastomer exhibiting improved heat, ozone, oxidative, and aging resistance. The patents fall into two main technical families. US7772328B2 and EP2473280B1 focus on molecular-weight reduction and/or simultaneous hydrogenation-metathesis using ruthenium/osmium metathesis catalysts, often with co-olefins, to generate lower-Mw HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distributions and lower Mooney viscosity. US7772328B2 specifically demonstrates controlled depolymerization/metathesis of HNBR feedstocks using ethylene or 1-hexene under elevated pressure and temperature, while EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 disclose one-step simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis of nitrile rubber in hydrogen, using Ru- or Os-based catalysts to achieve low RDB and reduced Mw. EP3827029A1 represents a different but complementary manufacturing trend: it centers on hydrogenating NBR in solution with Ru, Pd, or Rh catalysts at controlled catalyst loadings, temperature, pressure, and time, while minimizing catalyst residues that impair color, aging, and gel content. Overall, the landscape shows a strong move toward catalyst-engineered, solution-phase processes that combine hydrogenation with chain scission or metathesis to tune viscosity and molecular-weight distribution, alongside downstream catalyst-removal strategies to preserve HNBR quality. The evidence suggests that practical HNBR synthesis benefits from moderate-to-high hydrogenation pressure, temperatures around 60-200°C depending on catalyst system, and careful control of catalyst loading and co-olefin choice to balance hydrogenation rate, residual unsaturation, and polymer integrity.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7772328B2 and EP2473280B1/US9150669B2 all pursue lower Mw and lower Mooney viscosity, but US7772328B2 does so mainly by metathesis/depolymerization of HNBR feedstock, whereas EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 integrate hydrogenation with metathesis in one step.
- EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 both use Ru/Os metathesis-type catalyst families in hydrogen, with optional co-olefin, and both report final Mw values in the ~113,000-195,000 g/mol range and PDI values around 2.3-3.1 in the examples.
- US7772328B2 uses ethylene at 6.9×10^5 Pa and 1-hexene at 80° C. to drive chain scission/metathesis, while EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 emphasize catalyst identity and hydrogenation profile rather than a single co-olefin condition.
- EP3827029A1 differs from the other three by focusing on conventional solution hydrogenation of NBR with Ru/Pd/Rh catalysts and on catalyst residue control, rather than on metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction.
- Catalyst loading is lowest in the metathesis/hydrogenation patents (about 0.041-0.080 phr in the examples) and higher catalyst-residue windows are explicitly claimed in EP3827029A1 in ppm terms for Ru, Pd, and Rh.
- Temperature and pressure are explicitly claimed only in EP3827029A1 (60-200°C, 700,000-15,000,000 Pa), while the metathesis-based patents provide example temperatures such as 80° C. and focus more on catalyst system performance and RDB decay.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows two complementary routes to HNBR: conventional solution hydrogenation with careful catalyst-residue management, and simultaneous hydrogenation-metathesis for direct molecular-weight reduction. For practical HNBR synthesis, the strongest evidence supports using Ru-based catalyst systems in solution, with hydrogenation conditions around 60-200°C and elevated hydrogen pressure when conventional hydrogenation is used, or around 80°C with low phr catalyst loadings when metathesis-assisted chain scission is desired. The most effective route for low-viscosity HNBR appears to be simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis with controlled co-olefin selection, because it can reduce Mw, narrow PDI, and lower Mooney viscosity in a single step. Where product color, aging, and gel stability are critical, catalyst residue minimization and post-reaction purification remain important. Overall, the evidence favors low catalyst loading, Ru-based single-site catalysts, and process control that balances residual unsaturation reduction against over-degradation of the polymer backbone.
