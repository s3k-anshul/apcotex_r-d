# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS DEVELOPMENT ACROSS SELECTIVE HYDROGENATION AND METATHESIS ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, with emphasis on selective hydrogenation of NBR, low-molecular-weight HNBR production, and catalyst systems that combine metathesis with hydrogenation. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated nitrile rubber derived from acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber, with the desired attributes of improved heat, ozone, and oil resistance, low residual unsaturation, and controlled molecular weight/Mooney viscosity. The patent landscape separates into two major process families. First, conventional solution hydrogenation of NBR is disclosed using ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium catalysts under elevated temperature and hydrogen pressure, with attention to catalyst loading, residual double bond reduction, and catalyst-removal issues. Second, several patents address a more integrated route in which metathesis and hydrogenation occur simultaneously or sequentially using Ru/Os carbene catalysts, often with co-olefins and catalyst pretreatment, to reduce molecular weight while hydrogenating the polymer backbone. Across the selected patents, the key formulation/process trends are: use of organic solvent solution processing; hydrogen pressures from 4.137 MPa to 15,000,000 Pa; temperatures commonly around 120 °C or within 60–200 °C; catalyst loadings reported in phr or ppm; and explicit control of residual double bond content, Mooney viscosity, and polydispersity. The metathesis-based patents emphasize low-Mw HNBR with narrower molecular weight distributions and improved processability, while the catalyst-composition patent highlights pretreatment of Ru catalysts with vinyl ethyl ether to improve hydrogenation efficiency and preserve molecular weight. The more recent EP patent focuses on reducing catalyst residues and improving ageing properties by using low noble-metal levels and avoiding post-hydrogenation removal steps. Collectively, these disclosures show that HNBR synthesis is moving toward more selective, lower-residue, and process-integrated hydrogenation strategies that balance high conversion with molecular-weight retention and downstream performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10500578B2 and EP3827029A1 both use solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR, but US10500578B2 emphasizes Ru catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether and simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation, whereas EP3827029A1 focuses on low ppm noble-metal loading and ageing/catalyst-residue control.
- US7919563B2, US7772328B2, EP2473281B1, and US9150669B2 all use metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction, but EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 uniquely combine metathesis and hydrogenation in a single step under hydrogen, while US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 describe metathesis followed by hydrogenation.
- The metathesis-based patents report low Mooney viscosities and reduced Mw: US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 show Mooney values as low as 3 and PDI down to 1.90, while EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 target Mw 20,000 to 250,000 and PDI below 3.0 with residual double bond content below 5%.
- US10500578B2 uses hydrogen pressures of 4.137 MPa and 6.895 MPa at 120 °C, whereas EP3827029A1 claims a broader hydrogenation window of 60 to 200 °C and 700000 to 15000000 Pa.
- Catalyst systems differ substantially: US10500578B2 centers on Ru/Os carbene complexes with co-catalyst pretreatment; EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 use formula (I) Ru/Os metathesis/hydrogenation catalysts; EP3827029A1 uses Ru, Pd, or Rh compounds for conventional hydrogenation; US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 rely on Ru/Os metathesis catalysts plus co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows two viable HNBR synthesis strategies: conventional solution hydrogenation of NBR with noble-metal catalysts, and metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by or concurrent with hydrogenation. For high-performance HNBR, the strongest evidence supports low residual double bond content below 5%, hydrogenation temperatures around 120 °C, and hydrogen pressures in the 4.137 to 6.895 MPa range for the catalyst-composition route, or 60 to 200 °C and 700000 to 15000000 Pa for broader hydrogenation practice. For low-Mw, highly processable HNBR, the metathesis-based patents recommend Ru/Os catalysts with co-olefins and, in the simultaneous route, hydrogen present during metathesis to achieve Mw 20,000 to 250,000, Mooney viscosity below 10 to 50, and PDI below 2.5 to 3.0. Catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether is a clear performance enhancer, improving hydrogenation rate and helping preserve molecular weight. Overall, the best-supported synthesis parameters are selective hydrogenation in solution, controlled noble-metal loading, and either sequential or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation depending on whether the priority is maximum unsaturation removal or molecular-weight reduction.
