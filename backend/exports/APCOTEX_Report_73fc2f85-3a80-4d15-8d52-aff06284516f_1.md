# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
The synthesis and formulation of low-to-medium acrylonitrile carboxylated nitrile butadiene rubber (XNBR) latexes are designed to mimic the physical properties of natural rubber—specifically high elongation, low modulus, and high tensile strength—for thin-walled elastomeric articles such as medical examination gloves. The patent landscape, represented by US9926439B2, US8470422B2, and EP2010602B1, establishes a core technology based on blending two distinct XNBR terpolymers to achieve synergistic mechanical and processing performance. The primary polymerization and formulation approach involves blending a first XNBR terpolymer (which provides a lower elastic modulus and softness) with a second XNBR terpolymer (which exhibits superior film-forming capabilities) in a compositional ratio ranging from 60:40 to 40:60, with a 50:50 ratio being preferred. The resulting blended polymer composition features a low-to-medium acrylonitrile content between 17% and 45% by weight, a methacrylic acid content of less than 15% by weight, and the remaining balance being butadiene. To achieve the desired elastomeric properties, a dual-crosslinking system is employed. Ionic crosslinking of the carboxylic acid groups is controlled using 0.5 to 1.5 parts of a metallic oxide (typically zinc oxide) combined with 0 to 1.5 parts of an alkali agent providing monovalent ions. Covalent crosslinking of the butadiene segments is achieved using 0.5 to 1.5 parts sulfur and 0.5 to 1.5 parts of a rubber accelerator system, specifically exemplified by a combination of 0.5 phr diphenyl guanidine (DPG), 0.25 phr zinc mercaptobenzothiazole (ZMBT), and 0.25 phr zinc diethyldithiocarbamate (ZDEC). Process trends highlight the control of glove thickness to a thin range of 0.05 to 0.11 mm to minimize the force required for stretching. Furthermore, EP2010602B1 introduces a post-polymerization surface modification involving an exterior coating of silicone emulsion, silicone oil, or glycerin, alongside a controlled drying temperature profile of 80°C to under 100°C to optimize surface release and tactile properties.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Monomer Content and Ratios: All three patents (US9926439B2, US8470422B2, and EP2010602B1) specify a carboxylated nitrile-butadiene rubber (XNBR) blend with an acrylonitrile (AN) content of 17% to 45% by weight and a methacrylic acid (MAA) content of less than 15% by weight, with butadiene making up the remainder.
- Terpolymer Blending Strategy: The patents consistently employ a blend of two distinct XNBR terpolymers in a compositional ratio ranging from 60:40 to 40:60 (optimally 50:50). The first terpolymer is characterized by a lower elastic modulus to impart softness, while the second terpolymer exhibits superior film-forming properties to facilitate dip-processing.
- Crosslinking and Vulcanization Systems: The formulation chemistry is identical across the documents, utilizing a dual-crosslinking system: ionic crosslinking via 0.5 to 1.5 parts of a metallic oxide (such as zinc oxide) combined with 0 to 1.5 parts of an alkali agent (total combined > 1.0 part), and covalent crosslinking via 0.5 to 1.5 parts sulfur and 0.5 to 1.5 parts of a multi-component accelerator system.
- Specific Accelerator Package: The examples in all three patents specify a highly defined accelerator package consisting of 0.5 phr diphenyl guanidine (DPG), 0.25 phr zinc mercaptobenzothiazole (ZMBT), and 0.25 phr zinc diethyldithiocarbamate (ZDEC) to achieve optimal vulcanization kinetics and physical properties.
- Key Distinctions and Process Innovations: While US9926439B2 and US8470422B2 focus on the core latex blend and glove article properties, EP2010602B1 introduces a critical post-processing step. It characterizes the elastomeric glove by an exterior coating of a silicone emulsion, silicone oil, glycerin solution, or fluorocarbon release agent, and specifies a drying temperature profile of 80°C to below 100°C for the gelled glove on the mold.
- Physical Performance Targets: All three patents target a force-strain profile mimicking natural rubber, specifically achieving a low force at 400% elongation (typically 1.5 to 1.7 N compared to 1.3 N for natural rubber) and high tensile strength (30 to 58 MPa) at reduced glove thicknesses (0.05 to 0.11 mm).

## 4. CONCLUSION
The synthesis of low-acrylonitrile carboxylated nitrile butadiene rubber (XNBR) with natural rubber-like characteristics is successfully realized through a synergistic blending strategy. By combining a low-modulus XNBR terpolymer with a high-film-forming XNBR terpolymer in a 50:50 ratio, manufacturers can overcome the traditional trade-off between softness and processability. Recommended synthesis and formulation parameters derived from the patent evidence include maintaining an overall acrylonitrile content of 17-45 wt%, methacrylic acid content below 15 wt%, and utilizing a dual-crosslinking system comprising 0.5-1.5 phr zinc oxide, 0.5-1.5 phr sulfur, and a balanced accelerator package of 0.5 phr DPG, 0.25 phr ZMBT, and 0.25 phr ZDEC. Additionally, incorporating an exterior silicone or glycerin coating and maintaining drying temperatures between 80°C and 100°C optimizes the surface properties and structural integrity of the resulting thin-walled (0.05-0.11 mm) elastomeric articles.
