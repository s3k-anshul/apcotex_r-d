# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT PROCESS PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how NBR precursors are transformed into hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated products and how process conditions are tuned to control residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and catalyst residues. Across the selected patents, the target material is consistently defined as a nitrile rubber derived from at least one conjugated diene and at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile, with hydrogenation of the diene-derived double bonds to improve heat, oil, and ozone resistance. Several patents focus on lowering Mooney viscosity and narrowing molecular weight distribution by combining metathesis-driven chain scission with subsequent hydrogenation, while others address catalyst selection and post-treatment to improve ageing stability or reduce residual noble metals.

The landscape is dominated by two major synthesis strategies. First, metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction is disclosed in multiple patents, typically using ruthenium-based Grubbs/Hoveyda-type catalysts, sometimes with co-olefins, followed by hydrogenation to yield low-MW HNBR. These routes emphasize simultaneous or sequential depolymerization and hydrogenation, with reported temperatures commonly in the 60–200 °C range and hydrogen pressures from 700,000 to 15,000,000 Pa. Second, catalyst-management patents focus on improving HNBR quality after hydrogenation by minimizing or removing Ru, Rh, or Pd residues using filtration, adsorption, ion exchange, membranes, or ionic liquids. A separate formulation patent addresses guanidine-free curing systems for HNBR compounds, reflecting downstream processing needs rather than synthesis.

Overall, the evidence indicates that the most effective HNBR synthesis routes combine controlled metathesis degradation to reduce feedstock viscosity, followed by hydrogenation under moderate-to-high pressure with ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium catalysts. The most important process trends are catalyst selectivity, low residual double bond content, retention of polymer integrity, and practical work-up methods that preserve ageing performance and reduce discoloration.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7772328B2 and EP2473281B1 both use metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction before or during hydrogenation, but EP2473281B1 explicitly claims simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis in hydrogen, whereas US7772328B2 emphasizes sequential low-MW HNBR preparation with co-olefins.
- US7951875B2 and US7772328B2 disclose very similar solution-processing examples with monochlorobenzene, ethylene at 6.9×10^5 Pa, 80°C, and catalyst loadings of 0.05-0.25 phr; both show that lower solids and co-olefin choice strongly affect Mooney viscosity and Mw.
- US20210340285A1 and EP3827029A1 define essentially the same hydrogenation window: Ru/Pd/Rh catalyst loadings in the tens of ppm, 60-200°C, 700000-15000000 Pa, and 1-24 h, with the key difference being jurisdiction and publication format.
- US7737233B2 and US8957161B2 are catalyst-activation patents for nitrile-rubber metathesis; US7737233B2 focuses on alkaline earth metal chlorides, while US8957161B2 highlights bromide salts such as LiBr and CsBr.
- EP2519350B1 is the most catalyst-centric disclosure, providing a broad family of Ru complexes for metathesis/depolymerization, whereas the HNBR-focused patents mainly optimize process conditions and catalyst residues rather than catalyst structure.
- EP2292687B1 is downstream and formulation-oriented rather than synthetic: it uses HNBR in guanidine-free cure systems, while the other patents focus on making the HNBR precursor or hydrogenated polymer itself.
- US7923518B2 is the least directly hydrogenation-related; it addresses NBR precursor coagulation, calcium/chlorine content, and storage stability, which can still influence downstream HNBR manufacture.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows that the most effective HNBR synthesis strategies combine controlled NBR depolymerization or metathesis with subsequent or simultaneous hydrogenation under carefully selected catalyst and pressure conditions. For low-MW HNBR, the strongest evidence supports solution metathesis using ruthenium-based catalysts, often with co-olefins and catalyst-activation salts, followed by hydrogenation at roughly 60-200°C and 700000-15000000 Pa. Catalyst loading in the tens of ppm for Ru/Pd/Rh systems is repeatedly associated with practical hydrogenation performance and improved ageing behavior. For precursor quality, NBR coagulation and residual-ion control remain important, while downstream compounding benefits from guanidine-free cure systems. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are: metathesis in solution with Ru catalysts and optional co-olefin, hydrogenation at 60-150°C when simultaneous processing is desired, or 60-200°C for conventional hydrogenation, with catalyst levels minimized but sufficient to maintain conversion and product stability, and with post-process attention to noble-metal residue control.
