# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes technical data from fifteen key patents to map the synthesis, modification, and application landscape of hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR). The research scope spans the evolution of HNBR production from traditional emulsion polymerization followed by multi-step degradation and hydrogenation, to advanced solution-based polymerization using RAFT modifiers and single-pot simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation. The patent landscape reveals a strong focus on controlling polymer molecular weight and polydispersity to improve processability without compromising the material's inherent heat, ozone, and chemical resistance. Major polymerization and modification approaches highlight the transition from Wilkinson's catalyst-mediated hydrogenation to highly active ruthenium-based carbene catalysts (such as Grubbs-type and novel six-coordinate Ru-N complexes) that facilitate simultaneous chain cleavage and double-bond reduction. Additionally, the incorporation of specialized termonomers, such as maleic acid monoethyl ester and alkoxyalkyl acrylates, is shown to optimize the balance between low-temperature flexibility and oil swelling, expanding HNBR's utility in high-performance automotive drive belts, battery separators, and wearable electronics.

- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of hydrogenated nitrile rubber
- Assignee: Not disclosed in the available patent text.

- Relevance: Simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis of nitrile rubber using specific ruthenium or osmium catalysts.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**

- Catalyst Amount: 0.057 phr (HNBR 1), 0.080 phr (HNBR 2), 0.06 phr (HNBR 3), 0.055 phr (HNBR 4)

**Technical Relevance**

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Catalyst Evolution: Traditional systems (EP2292687B1, EP3063192B1) utilize Wilkinson's catalyst [RhCl(PPh3)3] with triphenylphosphine (TPP) co-catalyst for selective hydrogenation, whereas newer approaches (US9079979B2, EP2473281B1, EP2519350B1) employ advanced ruthenium carbene complexes (Grubbs II/III and six-coordinate Ru-N complexes) to drive simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation.
- Process Configurations: Patents such as US7919563B2 and EP2473278B1 detail a two-step process where NBR is first metathesized to reduce molecular weight and subsequently hydrogenated. In contrast, EP2473281B1 and US9079979B2 establish single-step, simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation processes that significantly reduce cycle times and energy consumption.
- Monomer and Termonomer Systems: Standard HNBR compositions are contrasted with carboxylated HNBR (HXNBR) terpolymers containing maleic acid monoethyl ester cure-site monomers (EP2292687B1, US7572861B2) and alkoxyalkyl acrylate-modified HNBR (EP3063192B1), which are specifically designed to optimize low-temperature flexibility down to -35°C and minimize oil swelling.
- Solvent and Reaction Media: Monochlorobenzene (MCB) is the dominant solvent utilized across both metathesis and hydrogenation reactions (US7919563B2, EP2473278B1, EP3063192B1, EP2519350B1). However, toluene is preferred for polymer segment interchange reactions to produce block copolymers (EP2057205B1, EP2061827B1), and dimethylacetamide (DMAc) is highlighted for solution-based RAFT polymerization (US9023914B2).

## 4. CONCLUSION
The synthesized patent data demonstrates that the molecular weight, polydispersity, and performance characteristics of hydrogenated NBR can be finely tuned by selecting specific catalytic systems and process configurations. While traditional Wilkinson's catalyst remains highly effective for pure selective hydrogenation, modern applications increasingly rely on ruthenium carbene complexes to achieve simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in a single step. This single-pot approach, typically conducted in monochlorobenzene at temperatures between 130°C and 140°C, successfully yields low molecular weight HNBR with narrow polydispersity indices (< 2.5) and low Mooney viscosities, significantly enhancing processability. Furthermore, the integration of cure-site termonomers and the development of solution-polymerized NBR via RAFT chemistry represent key advancements that streamline production and enable high-value applications in energy storage and advanced elastomer composites.
