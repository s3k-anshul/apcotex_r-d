# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON COMPOSITION CONTROL, EMULSION POLYMERIZATION, AND POST-POLYMER MODIFICATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), focusing on how acrylonitrile content, monomer feed strategy, and downstream processing influence rubber performance. The target material is low-ACN NBR, generally understood as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile content relative to conventional grades, often in the approximate 10–18 wt% range, where composition control is critical for balancing oil resistance, low-temperature flexibility, and processability. The patent set spans two major synthesis themes: direct copolymer preparation by emulsion or solution polymerization, and post-polymer modification/degradation routes that alter molecular weight or functionality without changing the base NBR backbone.

US9650502B2 is the most directly relevant to low-ACN NBR preparation, disclosing emulsion polymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene with staged acrylonitrile feeding, alkyl thiol chain-transfer control, and pH-managed latex coagulation/washing. The examples explicitly show low initial acrylonitrile charges followed by continuous feed, conversion-controlled booster additions, and coagulation with magnesium sulfate followed by alkaline washing. EP2145681B1 and US7875683B2 address metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of NBR, enabling lower-Mn feedstocks and narrower distributions for subsequent hydrogenation or functionalization; these are not composition-setting patents, but they are important for processing low-viscosity NBR derived from controlled copolymer feeds. EP1825913B1 further develops metathesis catalyst systems by adding salts such as LiBr or CsBr to accelerate ring-closing metathesis, with broader implications for NBR metathesis efficiency. US10011664B2 discloses metathesis functionalization of nitrile rubbers using functionalized allyl compounds and ruthenium/osmium carbene catalysts, showing a route to modified NBR materials. EP2727948B1 is less directly related to acrylonitrile content, but it demonstrates solution polymerization with neodymium catalyst systems and controlled monomer feed in conjugated diene copolymerization, reinforcing the broader process-control paradigm used in elastomer design.

Overall, the evidence favors semi-continuous emulsion polymerization with controlled acrylonitrile feed, thiol chain transfer, and carefully managed coagulation/washing as the most practical route to low-ACN NBR, while metathesis patents provide complementary routes for molecular-weight tailoring and downstream functionalization.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9650502B2 is the only patent in the set that directly controls NBR composition during polymerization; it uses emulsion polymerization with staged acrylonitrile feed, whereas the other patents focus on post-polymer metathesis or unrelated diene copolymerization.
- US9650502B2 emphasizes low-temperature emulsion polymerization at 7 °C with continuous acrylonitrile feed and thiol chain transfer, while EP2727948B1 uses solution polymerization at 65 °C with a neodymium catalyst system and no acrylonitrile.
- The metathesis patents (US7875683B2, EP2145681B1, EP1825913B1, US10011664B2) are process-modification patents rather than composition-setting patents; they target molecular-weight reduction, catalyst efficiency, or functionalization of existing NBR.
- US7875683B2 and EP2145681B1 both address NBR degradation by metathesis, but US7875683B2 adds transition-metal compounds of formula M(OZ)m, while EP2145681B1 uses BF3-derived additive complexes with donor molecules.
- EP1825913B1 focuses on salt-promoted metathesis and explicitly shows LiBr and CsBr accelerating conversion, whereas US7875683B2 and EP2145681B1 focus on catalyst-system composition rather than salt acceleration.
- US10011664B2 differs from the degradation patents by using metathesis for functionalization with allyl compounds, indicating a route to modified NBR rather than lower-Mn NBR.
- Across the set, the most explicit composition-control variables are monomer feed ratio, staged addition, conversion control, and pH management in US9650502B2; the other patents mainly disclose catalyst systems, temperatures, and reaction times for downstream transformations.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is best approached through controlled emulsion copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene, with staged acrylonitrile feeding, low-temperature operation, and chain-transfer control as shown most clearly in US9650502B2. For downstream processing, metathesis-based patents provide useful tools for lowering molecular weight, narrowing distribution, or introducing functionality, but they do not replace composition control during polymerization. Based on the disclosed data, the most defensible synthesis strategy is semi-continuous emulsion polymerization with explicit acrylonitrile feed control, pH around 8–9 during polymerization, and coagulation/washing under alkaline conditions to obtain processable low-ACN NBR with good vulcanization behavior.
