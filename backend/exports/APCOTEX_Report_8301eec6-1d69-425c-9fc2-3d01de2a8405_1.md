# LOW STYRENE SBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND SYNTHESIS ANALYSIS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report analyzes patent evidence relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), with emphasis on synthesis routes that control styrene incorporation, polymer microstructure, and downstream processability. The single primary patent in the manifest, US12037432B2, is not a dedicated low-styrene SBR disclosure; rather, it describes a solution-polymerized modified styrene-butadiene copolymer and a post-polymerization stabilization strategy intended to suppress Mooney viscosity increase during steam hot-water treatment and storage. The patent is relevant because it uses anionic solution polymerization of styrene and 1,3-butadiene in hydrocarbon solvent, followed by terminal modification with an alkoxysilane-based modifier and condensation with a modification stabilizer. This approach is representative of modern SBR manufacturing where monomer feed, polar additive selection, initiator choice, and terminal functionalization are used to tune rubber properties.

The disclosed example uses a 20 L autoclave reactor charged with 270 g styrene, 710 g 1,3-butadiene, and 5,000 g n-hexane, with 1.3 g polar additive and 4 mmol n-butyllithium at 40°C. After about 20 minutes, 20 g additional 1,3-butadiene is injected for terminal capping, then 4 mmol of a silane modifier is added, followed by 15 minutes reaction and quench with ethanol and BHT. A subsequent 15-minute condensation with a modification stabilizer is performed before hot-water stripping at 100°C and roll drying. Comparative data show that the stabilizer improves processability metrics, especially Mooney viscosity, while maintaining similar styrene and vinyl contents.

Overall, the evidence supports solution-polymerized SBR as the dominant route for controlled styrene incorporation, but the specific patent does not demonstrate a low-styrene grade; the styrene content reported is about 27 wt%, which is above typical low-styrene targets. The main process trend is post-polymerization stabilization of modified SBR to preserve handling properties after functionalization and thermal workup.

## 4. CONCLUSION
US12037432B2 shows a solution-polymerized modified SBR route using n-butyllithium, hydrocarbon solvent, polar additive, terminal capping, silane modification, and stabilizer condensation. The evidence indicates that the key synthesis lever is not low styrene content itself, but controlled copolymerization and post-modification stabilization to preserve processability after hot-water stripping. Based on the disclosed data, the most defensible synthesis recommendation for low-styrene SBR development is to retain solution polymerization with precise styrene/butadiene feed control and terminal functionalization, while reducing styrene feed below the reported 27 wt% level and maintaining the stabilizer strategy to limit Mooney viscosity rise during finishing and storage.
