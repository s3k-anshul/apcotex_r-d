# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This comprehensive technical report synthesizes the polymerization, metathesis, and formulation landscape of Hydrogenated Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (HNBR) based on fifteen key patents. The research scope covers the transition from standard high-viscosity acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) to low molecular weight, highly processable HNBR. The selected patent landscape reveals two primary polymerization and modification approaches: (1) a two-step process involving metathetic degradation of NBR in the presence of a co-olefin (such as 1-hexene or ethylene) followed by catalytic hydrogenation, and (2) a single-step, simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation process. Key formulation and process trends highlight the use of highly active ruthenium-based carbene catalysts (e.g., Grubbs, Hoveyda, and Grela catalysts) to control molecular weight and polydispersity. Additionally, the report covers post-hydrogenation treatments, such as the oxidation of residual phosphine cocatalysts to phosphine oxides to improve vulcanizate properties, and the optimization of residual catalyst concentrations to enhance thermal oxidative aging without requiring complex purification steps. Finally, industrial applications of HNBR in high-temperature pipeline insulation and additive manufacturing (3D printing) are detailed, demonstrating the polymer's versatile performance profile. The report provides a deep dive into the reaction parameters, including monomer ratios, catalyst loadings, temperatures, pressures, and reaction times, offering a valuable reference for polymer scientists and research analysts working on advanced elastomeric materials. By analyzing these diverse patent disclosures, this report establishes a clear correlation between synthesis conditions and final polymer properties, guiding the development of next-generation HNBR formulations.

#### Patent 1
- Patent Number: EP2473281B1
- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of low molecular weight hydrogenated nitrile rubber
- Assignee: Not disclosed in the available patent text.
- Jurisdiction: EP
- Publication Year: 2014
- Relevance: Describes a process for producing low molecular weight HNBR by simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis of NBR in the presence of hydrogen and a ruthenium or osmium catalyst.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**
- Monomer: 1,3-butadiene (conjugated diene)
- Monomer: acrylonitrile (unsaturated nitrile)
- Catalyst: Ruthenium or osmium complex of formula (I)
- Co-olefin: Optional
- Reaction Type: Simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- The patent describes a simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis process of nitrile rubber (NBR) in the presence of hydrogen and a ruthenium or osmium catalyst of formula (I).
- The process yields hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) with a molecular weight (Mw) in the range of 20,000 to 250,000 g/mol.
- The resulting HNBR has a Mooney viscosity (ML 1+4 @ 100°C) in the range of 1 to 50 and a narrow molecular weight distribution (polydispersity index).

**Technical Relevance**
Provides a single-step method for simultaneous metathesis (molecular weight reduction) and hydrogenation of NBR to produce low-viscosity HNBR without requiring a separate degradation step.

#### Patent 3
- Patent Number: US20210340285A1
- Patent Title: Method for producing hydrogenated nitrile rubber and hnbr compositions thereof
- Assignee: Not disclosed in the available patent text.
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2021
- Relevance: Discloses a method for producing HNBR with good aging properties by controlling the residual amount of noble metal catalysts (Ru, Pd, Rh) without requiring complex catalyst removal steps.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**
- Monomer Content: 40% to 90% by weight of a conjugated diene, 10% to 60% by weight of an alpha,beta-unsaturated nitrile
- Catalyst: Ruthenium compound, Palladium compound, or Rhodium compound
- Ruthenium Concentration: 10 ppm to 200 ppm (preferably 42 ppm to 79 ppm) based on NBR
- Palladium Concentration: 20 ppm to <67 ppm (or 44 ppm to <67 ppm) based on NBR
- Rhodium Concentration: 50 ppm to <270 ppm (preferably 79 ppm to 170 ppm) based on NBR
- Temperature: 60 to 200 °C
- Pressure: 700,000 Pa to 15,000,000 Pa (7 to 150 bar)
- Reaction Time: 1 to 24 hours

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- The method subjects NBR in solution to hydrogenation using controlled low amounts of Ru, Pd, or Rh catalysts.
- Specific ruthenium catalysts include RuHCl(CO)(PPh3)3, Grubbs first-generation, and Grubbs second-generation catalysts.
- The resulting HNBR exhibits improved aging properties: gel content increases by less than 10% and Mooney viscosity increases by less than 40% after aging for 4 days at 140°C.

**Technical Relevance**
Eliminates the need for expensive and complex catalyst removal steps by optimizing the catalyst concentration to a level that does not detrimentally affect the thermal oxidative aging of the HNBR.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Monomer Content and Ratios: US20210340285A1 specifies NBR containing 40% to 90% by weight of conjugated diene and 10% to 60% by weight of unsaturated nitrile. EP2292687B1 utilizes a terpolymer (NBR 1) with 60.4 wt% butadiene, 39.6 wt% acrylonitrile, and a maleic acid monoethyl ester termonomer.
- Polymerization and Degradation Processes: US9023914B2 describes the direct synthesis of NBR in organic solvents (DMAc) using RAFT modifiers, avoiding emulsion coagulation. In contrast, US7919563B2, EP2473278B1, and US9593168B2 focus on the metathetic degradation of pre-formed NBR in monochlorobenzene using co-olefins like 1-hexene or ethylene to reduce molecular weight prior to or during hydrogenation.
- Catalyst Systems: EP2473281B1 and US9079979B2 utilize ruthenium or osmium complexes for simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation. US9593168B2 demonstrates that the Grela catalyst achieves complete NBR degradation in 185 minutes compared to over 425 minutes for the Grubbs II catalyst.
- Post-Hydrogenation and Additives: US20160326272A1 and US7572861B2 address residual triphenylphosphine (TPP) from homogeneous hydrogenation. US20160326272A1 uses oxidizing agents (e.g., sodium hypochlorite) to convert TPP to phosphine oxide to improve modulus, while US7572861B2 utilizes the molecularly dispersed TPP as an in-situ crosslinking activator.

## 4. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, the synthesis of high-performance Hydrogenated Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (HNBR) requires precise control over molecular weight, polydispersity, and residual catalyst chemistry. The evidence indicates that simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation using advanced ruthenium carbene catalysts (such as Grubbs III or Grela-type complexes) offers a highly efficient, single-step route to low-viscosity HNBR (Mooney viscosity 1 to 50, Mw 20,000 to 250,000 g/mol). For applications requiring superior aging properties, maintaining residual noble metal concentrations within strict limits (e.g., Ru at 10–200 ppm, Pd at 20–67 ppm) avoids the need for costly catalyst removal while preventing gelation. Furthermore, converting residual phosphine cocatalysts to phosphine oxides via post-hydrogenation oxidation significantly enhances the modulus and compression set of the resulting vulcanizates. Recommended synthesis parameters include conducting reactions in monochlorobenzene at temperatures of 100–138°C, pressures of 5–8.5 MPa, and utilizing 2–4 phr of 1-hexene as a co-olefin for molecular weight control.
