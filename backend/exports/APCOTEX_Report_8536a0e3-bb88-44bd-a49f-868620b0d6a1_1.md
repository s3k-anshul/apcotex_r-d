# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS LANDSCAPE FROM SELECTED PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes eight patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how hydrogenation level, molecular weight, catalyst choice, and downstream processing are controlled to tailor performance. The target material is defined as nitrile rubber derived from conjugated diene/α,β-unsaturated nitrile copolymers that have been fully or partially hydrogenated to improve heat resistance, oil resistance, ozone/weather resistance, and processability. Across the selected patents, two major synthesis directions emerge: conventional selective hydrogenation of NBR in solution or latex using noble-metal catalysts, and metathesis-assisted hydrogenation/depolymerization using ruthenium/osmium catalysts to reduce molecular weight and narrow polydispersity in a single step. A third theme is not primary synthesis but formulation/process integration, where HNBR is compounded or bonded into high-performance articles such as silicone blends or PVDF-lined flexible pipes.

The patents on low-molecular-weight HNBR (US7951875B2, US7772328B2, US9150669B2) focus on simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis in the presence of hydrogen and co-olefins, using ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems related to Grubbs/Hoveyda/Arlt/Wilkinson-type chemistry. These disclosures emphasize lower Mw, narrower MWD, and reduced Mooney viscosity relative to conventional HNBR feedstocks. EP2473280B1 similarly uses simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis, but specifically compares catalyst families and shows that catalyst identity strongly affects hydrogenation rate and residual double bond content. In contrast, EP3827029A1 addresses conventional HNBR hydrogenation with ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium compounds and focuses on minimizing catalyst residues while preserving ageing properties; it highlights the tradeoff between catalyst loading, reaction time, and post-removal complexity. US20220073666A1 shifts to latex hydrogenation of PEG acrylate-functionalized NBR, followed by coagulation, to produce processable functional HNBR terpolymers. Finally, US20130303700A1 and US10641418B2 demonstrate how HNBR is used in blends and bonded pipe structures, with process windows centered on peroxide curing, scorch time, adhesion, and thermal stability rather than polymer formation itself.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7951875B2, US7772328B2, US9150669B2, and EP2473280B1 all use simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis concepts, but EP2473280B1 explicitly compares catalyst families and hydrogenation profiles, while the US patents emphasize low-MW product targets and co-olefin effects.
- US7951875B2 and US7772328B2 disclose essentially the same process window: 80° C., 500 psi co-olefin pressure, and catalyst loadings of 0.05-0.25 phr, with ethylene or 1-hexene as co-olefins.
- US9150669B2 differs by focusing on catalyst-system optimization and residue management; it uses 0.041-0.060 phr catalyst and adds a triphenylphosphine co-catalyst in one comparative case.
- EP2473280B1 reports catalyst amounts of 0.055-0.080 phr and metal contents of 65.6-91.3 ppm, with Grubbs III showing faster hydrogenation than Grubbs II or Wilkinson's catalyst at equal metal content.
- US20220073666A1 is a latex-hydrogenation route rather than a metathesis route, and it uses broader composition windows for PEG acrylate-functionalized NBR (10-60 wt% nitrile, 20-80 wt% diene, 10-60 wt% PEG acrylate).
- EP3827029A1 uses conventional solution hydrogenation with Ru/Pd/Rh catalysts at 60-200°C and 0.7-15 MPa, emphasizing catalyst residue control rather than molecular-weight reduction by metathesis.
- US20130303700A1 and US10641418B2 are downstream application patents: the former uses partly hydrogenated NBR in silicone blends with metathesis catalyst, while the latter uses HNBR in peroxide-vulcanized PVDF-bonded flexible pipes.
- Across the set, lower residual unsaturation is associated with better service performance, but processability is improved either by lowering Mw/Mooney via metathesis-assisted hydrogenation or by tailoring compound scorch time and cure conditions.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows two dominant HNBR synthesis strategies: conventional selective hydrogenation of NBR in solution or latex, and simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis to reduce molecular weight and Mooney viscosity in one step. For low-viscosity HNBR, the strongest evidence supports ruthenium/osmium metathesis-capable catalysts, co-olefin-assisted processing, and reaction temperatures around 80° C. with catalyst loadings in the 0.04-0.25 phr range. For conventional hydrogenation, the evidence supports 60-200°C, 0.7-15 MPa hydrogen pressure, and careful catalyst-residue management to preserve ageing properties. Functional HNBR latex routes are also viable, especially for PEG acrylate-containing copolymers. Overall, the best-supported synthesis targets are low residual double bond content, Mw in the 20,000-250,000 g/mol range, and controlled Mooney viscosity, with downstream cure/bonding performance improved by maintaining adequate scorch time and using peroxide systems matched to the application.
