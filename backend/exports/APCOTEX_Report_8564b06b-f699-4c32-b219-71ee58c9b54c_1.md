# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION, EMULSION, AND FUNCTIONALIZED COPOLYMER ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes nine primary patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and closely related styrene-butadiene copolymers. The target is defined as SBR materials with styrene content below conventional grades, emphasizing reduced styrene incorporation, lower aromatic monomer fraction, and controlled monomer feed ratios. Across the landscape, the patents span both solution polymerization and emulsion polymerization, with several also addressing downstream compounding, oil replacement, filler interaction, and post-polymerization functionalization. A recurring theme is that low-styrene SBR is not only a composition target but also a process target: styrene content, sequence distribution, molecular weight, and microstructure are tuned to balance rolling resistance, wet grip, wear resistance, and processability.

The solution-polymerization patents (US11591428B2, EP1274737B1, EP2778184A1, US10174133B2, US6984706B2, US7612144B2) disclose hydrocarbon-solvent systems using organolithium initiators, polar modifiers, and in several cases controlled monomer addition, branching, or terminal functionalization. These routes generally provide tighter control over styrene content and vinyl content, with disclosed styrene levels ranging from about 20 to 40 wt% in many embodiments and specific examples near 20–26 wt% for low-styrene embodiments. The emulsion patent US8530017B2 uses water-based radical emulsion polymerization with a reactive emulsifier, showing that low-styrene SBR can also be produced in aqueous media while introducing polar functionality. US6365668B1 focuses on post-polymerization carboxylation of solution SBR and demonstrates how bound styrene content and filler compatibility can be combined with downstream water/steam stripping and silica masterbatch formation. US20060167160A1 is primarily a formulation patent, but it includes a high-styrene SBR reference and a low-styrene-relevant liquid SBR blend component, illustrating how styrene level and resin/oil selection affect tire performance.

Overall, the evidence supports low-styrene SBR synthesis via solution polymerization as the most controllable route, with emulsion polymerization remaining important where aqueous processing and reactive emulsifiers are desired. Recommended synthesis parameters from the evidence include hydrocarbon solvent solution polymerization, organolithium initiation, controlled styrene feed in the low-to-mid 20 wt% range where disclosed, and downstream coagulation/steam stripping or solvent removal as appropriate.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Solution-polymerized patents generally disclose lower and more controllable styrene levels than the emulsion patent, with low-styrene examples around 20-26 wt% in US11591428B2, US6365668B1, and US7612144B2.
- US8530017B2 uses aqueous emulsion polymerization with styrene feed at 10-50 wt% of total reactants, but the examples are centered at 260-280 g styrene with 710 g butadiene, i.e. a moderate-styrene emulsion SBR route.
- EP1274737B1 and US6984706B2 emphasize solution polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent with styrene contents around 25% in the characterized materials, while EP1274737B1 also shows continuous feeds as low as 3 wt% styrene in the reactor feed.
- US7612144B2 and US11591428B2 both demonstrate low-styrene solution SBR around 20-26% styrene, but US7612144B2 adds sulfur-containing initiator chemistry for head-group functionality, whereas US11591428B2 focuses on tapered sequence distribution and low molecular weight.
- US6365668B1 is distinctive in combining low-styrene solution SBR starting materials with post-polymerization carboxylation and silica masterbatching, showing that low styrene can be paired with filler affinity improvements.
- US20060167160A1 is primarily a compounding patent rather than a synthesis patent, but it confirms practical tire-compound use of SBR at 30% styrene and references an emulsion SBR at 23.5% styrene as a comparative material.
- Across the solution patents, hydrocarbon solvents such as hexane and cyclohexane dominate, with coagulation/steam stripping or isopropanol coagulation used for recovery; the emulsion patent instead uses water as the polymerization medium and stripping/workup medium.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports low-styrene SBR synthesis primarily through hydrocarbon solution polymerization, where styrene content can be held near 20-26 wt% while maintaining control over molecular weight, vinyl content, and sequence distribution. Emulsion polymerization remains viable for functionalized SBR, but the strongest low-styrene synthesis control appears in the solution-route patents. Recommended parameters from the extracted evidence are hydrocarbon solvent solution polymerization, organolithium initiation, styrene feed in the low-to-mid 20 wt% range where disclosed, controlled termination or branching as needed, and downstream coagulation or steam stripping for recovery. For tire applications, the patents indicate that low-styrene SBR benefits from tailored microstructure and, when needed, post-functionalization or filler-compatible modifiers to preserve processability and performance.
