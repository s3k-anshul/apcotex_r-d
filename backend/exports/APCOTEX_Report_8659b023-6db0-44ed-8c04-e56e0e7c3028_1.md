# PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT ON NITRILE BUTADIENE RUBBER (NBR) SYNTHESIS, COMPOSITION, AND PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related NBR-based compositions, with emphasis on synthesis, preparation, compounding, and performance control. The landscape spans both direct NBR material design and downstream formulations that use NBR as a key elastomeric component. Across the set, the patents address different target embodiments: semiconductive rubber rollers and electro-conductive members that incorporate NBR as a blend or domain rubber, sealing rubber materials based on NBR/PVC blends, NBR compositions for rubber-metal laminates, highly saturated nitrile rubber compositions built from hydrogenated NBR fractions, and hydrogenated NBR compositions tuned for oil/fuel resistance and low-temperature performance.

The disclosed polymerization and preparation approaches are diverse. Two patents focus on conventional NBR composition engineering rather than monomer polymerization: one uses NBR blended with PVC and sulfur vulcanization, while another uses NBR with tailored carbon black packages and oven vulcanization for rubber-metal laminates. The highly saturated nitrile rubber patent discloses emulsion polymerization of acrylonitrile/butadiene-based latexes, followed by hydrogenation, coagulation, drying, and compounding with polyamide resin in a twin-screw extruder. The hydrogenated NBR patent similarly relies on hydrogenated NBR feedstocks with specified acrylonitrile content and iodine number, compounded with ester plasticizer and organic peroxide, then press/oven vulcanized. The electro-conductive member patent is structurally focused, using NBR and SBR as phase-separated rubbers in matrix/domain architectures rather than describing NBR synthesis itself.

Major formulation trends include precise control of acrylonitrile content, hydrogenation level, carbon black type and loading, plasticizer level, and crosslinking system. Water appears prominently in the emulsion-polymerization and hydrogenation routes, including aqueous soap solutions, coagulation, washing, and latex handling, but is absent from the solvent-based or dry compounding patents except where used in evaluation or workup. Overall, the evidence indicates that NBR performance is most strongly tuned through monomer composition, hydrogenation state, and compounding architecture rather than through a single universal synthesis route.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- All six patents are formulation- or application-driven rather than direct monomer-feed synthesis patents, but EP2692788B1 uniquely discloses the aqueous emulsion polymerization and hydrogenation route for NBR-derived feedstocks.
- Acrylonitrile content is explicitly specified in US8277692B2 (31.5% for the added NBR), US8846804B2 (42.5% for the high-nitrile NBR in Example 1), US9688834B2 (34% for the NBR in Example 1), EP2692788B1 (35.6 wt% for a1; 40.5 wt% for a2; 36 wt% for the methacrylic-acid variant), US7772305B2 (25-44 wt% in the claims; examples at 36 wt%), and US11347156B2 (multiple NBR grades from 15% to 41.5%).
- The most synthesis-relevant process conditions are in EP2692788B1: emulsion polymerization at 5°C, hydrogenation at 3 MPa and 50°C, coagulation, washing, and drying. US7772305B2 instead emphasizes downstream peroxide curing of hydrogenated NBR at 170°C press vulcanization and 160°C oven vulcanization.
- US8846804B2 and US9688834B2 both use sulfur-cured NBR compositions, but US8846804B2 combines NBR with PVC to control precipitation and compression set, whereas US9688834B2 focuses on carbon black architecture for reinforcement and surface smoothness in rubber-metal laminates.
- US8277692B2 and US11347156B2 are electrophotographic/application patents where NBR is a component in multi-rubber systems; both rely on compounding and phase/curing design rather than NBR polymerization. US11347156B2 spans a wider NBR grade range and explicitly links NBR/SBR pairing to matrix-domain behavior.
- Water use is most extensive in EP2692788B1, where it is part of the polymerization medium and later used for coagulation and washing. In US8846804B2 and US7772305B2, water appears only in testing/workup contexts, while US9688834B2 is essentially water-free in the disclosed processing route.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that NBR performance is controlled primarily through acrylonitrile content, hydrogenation level, and compounding architecture rather than through a single universal synthesis route. For direct NBR synthesis, EP2692788B1 provides the clearest aqueous emulsion polymerization and hydrogenation pathway, with acrylonitrile contents around 35.6-40.5 wt% in the disclosed highly saturated nitrile rubbers and explicit hydrogenation at 3 MPa and 50°C. For practical NBR formulations, the most useful parameters are the acrylonitrile windows in US7772305B2 (25-44 wt%) and the specific NBR grades in US8846804B2, US9688834B2, and US8277692B2. Recommended evidence-based synthesis/formulation targets are: use aqueous emulsion polymerization for precursor NBR when synthesis is required; maintain acrylonitrile content in the mid-range for balanced oil resistance and low-temperature behavior; and tune downstream properties with controlled filler, plasticizer, and crosslinking systems. For sealing and laminate applications, sulfur-cured NBR/PVC or NBR/carbon-black systems are strongly supported, while electrophotographic applications favor NBR as one phase in a multi-rubber architecture rather than as a standalone polymer.
