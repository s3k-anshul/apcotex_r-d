# HYDROGENATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON HNBR SYNTHESIS, HYDROGENATION, AND PROCESS/COMPOSITION CONTROL

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a patent landscape centered on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) and closely related process technologies. The target is defined as nitrile-butadiene rubber precursors and their hydrogenated, partially hydrogenated, or fully hydrogenated derivatives, with emphasis on synthesis, hydrogenation, residual unsaturation control, molecular-weight control, and manufacturing robustness. Across the selected patents, the dominant technical theme is solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous noble-metal catalysts, especially ruthenium-, rhodium-, and palladium-based systems, with strong attention to catalyst loading, hydrogen pressure, temperature, and post-reaction catalyst removal. One key route (EP2473281B1) combines metathesis and hydrogenation in a single process to reduce molecular weight while hydrogenating the polymer, using ruthenium/osmium complexes and hydrogen in the presence of optional co-olefin. Another route (EP3827029A1) focuses on hydrogenating NBR in solution with controlled noble-metal content and operating windows designed to improve ageing properties while minimizing catalyst residues. EP2292687B1 is not a synthesis patent, but it is highly relevant because it discloses HNBR-based vulcanizable compositions and explicitly defines HNBR precursor and hydrogenation characteristics, including hydrogenation degree, Mooney viscosity, and cure-package design. US11131058B2 likewise is formulation/process-oriented, showing how carboxy-modified NBR/HNBR latex systems are combined with resorcinol-formaldehyde and polycarbodiimide chemistry for cord treatment, indirectly reflecting HNBR compatibility in industrial rubber systems. The remaining patents are less directly synthetic but still informative: US9321910B2 and US10385647B2 show HNBR in demanding end-use compositions, while EP2519350B1 and EP3137587B1 are largely outside the HNBR synthesis domain. Overall, the landscape indicates that practical HNBR manufacture depends on balancing hydrogenation efficiency, catalyst economy, residual metal control, and preservation of polymer properties, while downstream formulations exploit HNBR’s heat and oil resistance in high-temperature sealing, belt, and swellable-rubber applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473281B1 and EP3827029A1 are the most synthesis-focused HNBR patents: EP2473281B1 emphasizes simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation for molecular-weight reduction, while EP3827029A1 emphasizes conventional solution hydrogenation with controlled noble-metal loading and catalyst removal.
- EP2473281B1 discloses very low catalyst loadings in phr and tracks residual double bond content over 0-240 min, whereas EP3827029A1 defines catalyst content in ppm, hydrogenation at 60-200°C, 700000-15000000 Pa, and 1-24 h.
- US9321910B2 and EP2292687B1 are formulation patents rather than synthesis patents; both define HNBR as a high-hydrogenation elastomer and provide cure-package/process windows, but neither introduces a new hydrogenation route.
- US10385647B2 uses HNBR in swellable packers and highlights hydrogenation levels of about 70-91% and unsaturation of about 9-30%, showing a less fully hydrogenated elastomer than the 95-100% hydrogenation claimed in EP2292687B1.
- US11131058B2 is the most indirect HNBR reference: it does not synthesize HNBR, but it explicitly allows carboxy-modified hydrogenated acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer latex in a cord-treatment system.
- EP2519350B1 is catalyst-centric and only indirectly relevant to HNBR; it mentions depolymerization of NBR and hydrogenation of HNBR at 60-150°C, but the examples are focused on Ru-complex synthesis rather than rubber hydrogenation.
- EP3137587B1 is outside the HNBR technical field and serves as a non-relevant control patent in this manifest.
- Across the relevant patents, the recurring process themes are solution-phase hydrogenation, noble-metal catalyst management, residual unsaturation control, and downstream formulation compatibility for high-temperature rubber goods.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that HNBR manufacturing is dominated by solution-phase hydrogenation of NBR using noble-metal catalysts, with two major technical directions: simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation for molecular-weight reduction (EP2473281B1) and conventional hydrogenation with tight catalyst-residue control (EP3827029A1). For practical HNBR production, the strongest evidence supports hydrogenation in the range of 60-200°C and 700000-15000000 Pa, with catalyst contents in the tens of ppm and reaction times of 1-24 h, while aiming for very low residual double bond content and minimal catalyst carryover. For high-performance end use, the formulation patents indicate that highly hydrogenated HNBR, typically 95-100% hydrogenation, is preferred for heat- and oil-resistant vulcanizates, and that cure packages, microgels, and retarder systems can be used to tune processing and final properties. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are: solution hydrogenation of NBR with ruthenium/rhodium/palladium catalysts in the low-ppm range, operation near 138°C and 85 bar where demonstrated, and post-reaction catalyst removal or minimization to preserve ageing performance. Where molecular-weight reduction is desired, the simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation route is the most distinctive disclosed option.
