# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: SOLUTION-POLYMERIZED NITRILE RUBBER WITH CONTROLLED ACRYLONITRILE CONTENT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on synthesis routes that control acrylonitrile incorporation during copolymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile. The primary patent, CN115725020A, discloses a solution polymerization process designed to avoid soap-derived impurities typical of emulsion NBR and to produce a colorless, transparent raw rubber with improved processing and vulcanizate properties. The patent positions solution polymerization as an industrially viable alternative to emulsion routes, using a mixed solvent system composed of a benzene solvent and a ketone solvent, with cooling before initiation and subsequent radical polymerization under low temperature and moderate pressure.

The disclosed composition window for the monomer feed is broad, with butadiene and acrylonitrile mass percentages of 60-80% and 40-20%, respectively, which encompasses low-acrylonitrile grades at the lower end of acrylonitrile content. The examples show that varying the acrylonitrile charge and solvent/co-solvent selection changes the combined acrylonitrile content in the raw rubber from 27.3% to 41.1%, while maintaining high monomer conversion (>85% in the solution examples). The process also uses chain-transfer control via tert-dodecyl mercaptan and initiators such as PMHP or PHP, with reaction temperatures below 8°C and reaction times of 12-14 h.

Overall, the patent landscape in the provided manifest is limited to one primary patent, but the evidence strongly indicates that low-ACN NBR can be approached through solution polymerization with careful monomer ratio control, solvent selection, low-temperature initiation, and mercaptan-mediated molecular-weight regulation. The key trend is the replacement of emulsion polymerization with a cleaner solution process to improve purity and downstream vulcanizate performance while retaining high conversion and tunable acrylonitrile incorporation.

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Example 1: Baseline solution polymerization in toluene/methyl ethyl ketone at <8°C gave 85.7% conversion, Mooney viscosity 48.6, and combined acrylonitrile content 27.3%.
- Example 2: Increasing acrylonitrile charge to 230 g and adjusting solvent/mercaptan/initiator levels maintained high conversion (86.4%) and raised combined acrylonitrile content to 30.2%.
- Example 3: Ethylbenzene/acetone solvent system with 260 g acrylonitrile achieved 87.4% conversion and 33.6% combined acrylonitrile content.
- Example 4: Benzene/acetone system with 280 g acrylonitrile produced 87.7% conversion and 35.4% combined acrylonitrile content.
- Example 5: Chlorobenzene/methyl ethyl ketone system with 325 g acrylonitrile gave the highest conversion in the examples (88.7%) and the highest combined acrylonitrile content (41.1%).

**Technical Relevance**
This patent is directly relevant because it provides a solution-polymerized NBR route that controls acrylonitrile incorporation through monomer feed and solvent selection, while avoiding emulsion-derived soap impurities. Although the examples span moderate to relatively high acrylonitrile contents, the disclosed composition window and process controls are applicable to low-acrylonitrile NBR design.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports solution polymerization as a practical route to cleaner NBR with tunable acrylonitrile content and improved processing properties. For low-acrylonitrile NBR, the most relevant controls are the butadiene/acrylonitrile feed ratio, mixed benzene/ketone solvent selection, low-temperature initiation below 8°C, and mercaptan-based molecular-weight regulation. The patent demonstrates that high monomer conversion (>85%) is achievable while varying acrylonitrile charge and solvent system, making it a useful process basis for low-ACN NBR synthesis and scale-up.
