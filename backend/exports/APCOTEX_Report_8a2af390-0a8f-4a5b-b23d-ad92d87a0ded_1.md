# HYDROGENATED NBR: LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT AND FUNCTIONALIZED HNBR PREPARATION PROCESSES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents addressing hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, with emphasis on lowering molecular weight, controlling residual unsaturation, and improving processability while preserving the characteristic heat, ozone, and oil resistance of HNBR. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated nitrile-butadiene rubber derived from NBR precursors, including conventional NBR and PEG-acrylate-functionalized NBR. Across the selected patents, two major process families emerge: (1) simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation of nitrile rubber in the presence of hydrogen and ruthenium- or osmium-based catalysts, and (2) latex hydrogenation of functionalized NBR followed by coagulation to obtain processable HNBR copolymers. The first approach is represented by US7951875B2 and EP2473281B1, both focused on reducing molecular weight and narrowing molecular weight distribution during hydrogenation, with EP2473281B1 explicitly claiming simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in hydrogen atmosphere. The second approach, US20220073666A1, targets PEG acrylate-HNBR copolymers and emphasizes latex hydrogenation of PEG acrylate-NBR latex, followed by coagulation, to retain functionalization while improving processing behavior. The patents collectively show that HNBR synthesis is not limited to simple post-hydrogenation of NBR; instead, process design can integrate chain scission via metathesis, catalyst selection to avoid nitrile reduction, and latex-state hydrogenation to manage viscosity and downstream handling. Key formulation/process trends include use of co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene in metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction, ruthenium catalysts with phosphine or N-heterocyclic carbene ligation, hydrogen pressures in the multi-megapascal range for latex hydrogenation, and monomer windows spanning acrylonitrile, conjugated dienes, and PEG acrylate comonomers. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis strategies that balance hydrogenation degree, residual double-bond content, and molecular weight control to achieve improved processability without sacrificing the performance profile that makes HNBR valuable in seals, hoses, and oilfield elastomers.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7951875B2 and EP2473281B1 both focus on lowering molecular weight and Mooney viscosity during HNBR-related processing, but US7951875B2 emphasizes metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction with co-olefins such as ethylene and 1-hexene, whereas EP2473281B1 emphasizes simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in hydrogen atmosphere.
- EP2473281B1 provides the clearest hydrogenation-performance data, with residual double bond content reduced to 0.4% at 240 min for the inventive catalyst, while US7951875B2 reports strong molecular-weight reduction and PDI narrowing but does not present the same explicit RDB time profile.
- US20220073666A1 differs from the other two patents by focusing on PEG acrylate-functionalized NBR latex and a hydrogenation-then-coagulation route rather than metathesis-based chain scission.
- Catalyst systems differ substantially: EP2473281B1 uses ruthenium/osmium compounds in hydrogen, US7951875B2 relies on metathesis catalysts with co-olefins, and US20220073666A1 cites group 8/9/10 transition-metal catalysts for latex hydrogenation in prior-art context.
- Process state also differs: US20220073666A1 is latex-based, while US7951875B2 and EP2473281B1 operate on dissolved/cement or solution-phase nitrile rubber.
- Temperature and pressure disclosure is strongest in US20220073666A1 for latex hydrogenation context (80° C. to 160° C., 3 000 000 to 8 000 000 Pa), while US7951875B2 explicitly uses 80° C. and 500 psi co-olefin pressure in the metathesis examples.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports three complementary HNBR synthesis strategies: simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation for direct molecular-weight reduction, metathesis-assisted chain scission with co-olefins, and latex hydrogenation of functionalized NBR followed by coagulation. For low-MW HNBR, the strongest evidence favors ruthenium-based simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation under hydrogen with catalyst loadings in the 0.041-0.060 phr range and reaction times up to 240 min, which can drive RDB below 1% and reduce Mooney viscosity substantially. For processability-focused HNBR, latex hydrogenation at 80-160° C. and 3-8 MPa hydrogen pressure is a practical route, especially when preserving PEG acrylate functionality. Overall, the recommended synthesis direction from the evidence is to combine controlled hydrogenation with either metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction or latex-state processing, while targeting low residual unsaturation, moderate catalyst loading, and monomer compositions that maintain nitrile content and diene-derived performance.
