# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report evaluates patent evidence relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on composition control, monomer feed strategy, and emulsion polymerization process design. The target material is a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer with reduced acrylonitrile fraction relative to conventional NBR grades, typically achieved by controlling acrylonitrile feed, conversion profile, and reactor staging. In the provided manifest, only one primary patent was identified: US10208151B2. Although the patent is not directed to standalone low-ACN NBR, it is highly relevant because it discloses a diene-based rubber latex prepared by staged emulsion polymerization of 1,3-butadiene with controlled addition of emulsifier, initiator, electrolyte, molecular weight regulator, and cross-linking agent, followed by grafting with acrylonitrile/styrene to form ABS. The disclosed process demonstrates how rubber-phase latex properties can be tuned through monomer charge, cross-linker type, addition timing, temperature staging, and conversion endpoints. Key process trends include two-step polymerization, nitrogen-substituted reactor operation, use of potassium rosinate/potassium oleate emulsification, potassium persulfate initiation, tertiary dodecyl mercaptan molecular-weight control, and termination at high conversion (90%+). The examples show that changing the cross-linking agent from propylene glycol diacrylate n=7 to n=13 and adjusting addition timing changes gel content and particle size while preserving large latex particle size (~3100 Å). For low-ACN NBR synthesis, the patent is useful as a process-control reference for emulsion copolymerization and latex property management, even though it does not explicitly disclose low acrylonitrile NBR compositions.

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Example 1: Produced large-size rubber latex with 78% gel content and 3100 Å particle size using (propylene glycol)7 diacrylate, then grafted acrylonitrile/styrene to ABS latex with 97% conversion and 41% graft rate.
- Example 2: Replacing the cross-linker with (propylene glycol)13 diacrylate lowered gel content to 76% while maintaining 3100 Å particle size.
- Example 3: Using (propylene glycol)13 diacrylate in a split-addition mode further reduced gel content to 70% while preserving 3100 Å particle size.
- Comparative Example 1: A non-crosslinked latex was prepared for comparison and then used in ABS grafting.
- Comparative Example 2: Adding polypropylene glycol diacrylate (Mn=6,000) at 0.2 part by weight produced a large-size latex of 3100 Å for comparison.

**Technical Relevance**
This patent is relevant because it provides a detailed emulsion-polymerization framework for controlling diene rubber latex morphology, conversion, and grafting behavior, all of which are important when designing low-ACN NBR or related nitrile rubber systems. While it does not explicitly claim low acrylonitrile NBR, its staged addition strategy and composition control principles are directly transferable to acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymer synthesis.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence base contains one primary patent, US10208151B2, which is not a direct low-acrylonitrile NBR disclosure but is technically useful for nitrile-rubber process design. The strongest transferable lessons are staged emulsion polymerization, controlled monomer and emulsifier addition, use of a molecular-weight regulator, and conversion-based termination to manage latex size and gel content. For low-ACN NBR synthesis, the most relevant evidence-supported process direction is careful feed-ratio control in emulsion polymerization, with staged addition and temperature management to maintain composition uniformity and polymer properties. However, no patent in the provided manifest explicitly discloses a low-acrylonitrile NBR composition, so any low-ACN target specification must be inferred from broader nitrile-rubber process principles rather than directly claimed composition data.
