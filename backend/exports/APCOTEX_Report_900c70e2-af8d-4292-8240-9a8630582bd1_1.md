# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS CONTROL: PATENT LANDSCAPE REVIEW

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on how NBR precursors are transformed into hydrogenated products while preserving polymer integrity and controlling residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and catalyst residues. Across the landscape, the core target is HNBR derived from acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber or related nitrile rubbers, typically defined by partial to near-complete hydrogenation of butadiene-derived double bonds, with resulting improvements in heat, oil, ozone, and chemical resistance. The patents fall into two broad technical groups: (1) catalytic hydrogenation of NBR to HNBR, often using ruthenium, rhodium, palladium, or Wilkinson-type systems under elevated hydrogen pressure and temperature; and (2) metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction of NBR, sometimes combined with hydrogenation in a one-pot or simultaneous process to produce lower-Mooney, lower-Mw HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distributions. A recurring process theme is the need to reduce residual double bonds while avoiding gel formation, excessive nitrile reduction, or catalyst contamination that degrades aging performance and color. Several patents focus on catalyst selection and loading, including Grubbs-type, Hoveyda-type, Wilkinson’s catalyst, and other Ru complexes, while others emphasize post-reaction purification or catalyst recovery to improve product stability. Formulation-oriented patents also show how HNBR is used in conductive pastes and vulcanizable mixtures, where alkali-metal content, filler type, and cure-package selection influence processing and end-use properties. Overall, the evidence indicates that the most effective HNBR synthesis routes combine controlled hydrogenation conditions, careful catalyst choice, and, where needed, pre-hydrogenation metathesis to lower molecular weight without sacrificing polymer microstructure.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9079979B2 and EP2473281B1 both disclose simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes for HNBR, but EP2473281B1 emphasizes Hoveyda/Arlt-type catalysts and lower final Mooney viscosity, while US9079979B2 compares Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson's catalyst performance.
- US9079979B2 and EP3827029A1 both use noble-metal hydrogenation chemistry, but EP3827029A1 focuses on catalyst residue control and aging stability with explicit Ru/Pd/Rh ppm windows, whereas US9079979B2 focuses on one-pot hydrogenation/metathesis and polymer-property outcomes.
- US8957161B2 and US7737233B2 are upstream metathesis-enabling patents rather than direct hydrogenation patents; both address catalyst activation for NBR degradation, with US8957161B2 using salt additives such as LiBr/CsBr and US7737233B2 using alkaline earth metal chlorides.
- EP2519350B1 extends the metathesis toolbox by introducing new Ru complexes and explicitly claims NBR depolymerization followed by hydrogenation to HNBR, bridging catalyst design and polymer processing.
- EP2292687B1 is formulation-oriented rather than synthesis-oriented, but it provides a concrete HNBR hydrogenation example at 138 °C and 85 bar H2 and shows how HNBR properties translate into vulcanizable compounds.
- US10119045B2 is the least synthesis-focused of the set; it uses HNBR as a binder in conductive paste and highlights the importance of low alkali-metal content and controlled nitrile content for performance.
- Across the hydrogenation-focused patents, the recurring process window is elevated temperature, high hydrogen pressure, and ruthenium-based catalysts, with product quality strongly tied to residual double bond content, Mooney viscosity, and catalyst residue management.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set shows two complementary routes to HNBR: direct hydrogenation of NBR and metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by hydrogenation or simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis. The strongest evidence supports ruthenium-based catalyst systems operated under elevated temperature and hydrogen pressure, with careful control of catalyst loading and residue removal to preserve aging performance and avoid discoloration or gel formation. For low-Mooney HNBR, the evidence favors preconditioning NBR to below about 30 Mooney units or using one-pot metathesis/hydrogenation systems that deliver Mw in the 20,000-250,000 g/mol range and residual double bonds below about 1%. Practical synthesis windows supported by the evidence include hydrogenation at roughly 60-200 °C and 700000-15000000 Pa, with catalyst loadings in the tens of ppm for noble metals. For process development, the most robust strategy is a ruthenium-catalyzed hydrogenation route with strong attention to catalyst recovery, or a metathesis-plus-hydrogenation route when lower molecular weight and narrower MWD are required.
