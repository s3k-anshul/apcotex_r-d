# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS CONTROL: PATENT LANDSCAPE REVIEW

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) synthesis, with emphasis on how NBR precursors are hydrogenated, how residual unsaturation is controlled, and how molecular weight or processability is tuned. The target material is hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated NBR derived from butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymers, with the desired outcome of improved heat, ozone, and oil resistance while retaining manageable viscosity and low gel content. The patent landscape spans three main process families: conventional solution hydrogenation of NBR using ruthenium catalysts and additives to suppress molecular-weight growth; simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes that reduce molecular weight while hydrogenating in one step; and catalyst-pretreated metathesis/hydrogenation systems that improve hydrogenation rate and preserve polymer molecular weight. A separate application focuses on blending partially hydrogenated NBR with silicone rubber using metathesis catalysts, showing the compatibility of HNBR with reactive blending strategies rather than precursor synthesis itself. Across the hydrogenation patents, the disclosed process variables include hydrogen pressures from 4.137 MPa to 1200 psi, temperatures from 80°C to 145°C, catalyst loadings from 0.015 wt% to 0.25 phr or 500 ppm, and reaction times from 2 to 24 hours depending on catalyst system and target conversion. Additives such as water plus carboxylic acids, or vinyl ethyl ether pretreatment of Ru metathesis catalysts, are repeatedly used to control gelation, molecular-weight increase, or catalyst activity. The metathesis-based patents emphasize Ru/Os catalysts of Grubbs/Hoveyda/Grela/Zhan type, often with co-olefins such as ethylene or vinyl ethyl ether, to achieve lower Mw and narrower PDI. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that balance high hydrogenation degree with controlled chain scission or minimal crosslinking, and it highlights catalyst/additive engineering as the key lever for scalable HNBR manufacture.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10500578B2, EP2473280B1, US9150669B2, and US7772328B2 all use Ru/Os catalyst systems to combine hydrogenation with metathesis or catalyst pretreatment, but US7772328B2 and US7951875B2 emphasize molecular-weight reduction more explicitly than the others.
- EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 both report low residual double bond contents below about 1% in some examples, with hydrogenation times of 2-4 h and temperatures of 120°C, whereas US10500578B2 reaches 95-99% hydrogenation under similar 120°C / 4.137 MPa conditions after catalyst pretreatment.
- US10500578B2 uses vinyl ethyl ether pretreatment and reports catalyst-to-co-catalyst ratios of 42.4 and 72.4, while EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 rely on Grubbs/Hoveyda/Arlt/Wilkinson comparisons without the same explicit VEE pretreatment strategy.
- US7772328B2 and US7951875B2 use co-olefins such as ethylene at 6.9×10^5 Pa and 1-hexene at 80°C to drive metathesis-based chain scission, achieving Mw as low as 59000 g/mol and Mooney viscosity as low as 3.
- EP0588099A1 is mechanistically different: it is a conventional solution hydrogenation patent that uses water plus organic additives to suppress Mw growth and gelation, rather than metathesis-assisted depolymerization.
- Across the hydrogenation-focused patents, catalyst loadings are low: 0.015-0.15 wt% in EP0588099A1, 0.041-0.060 phr in US9150669B2, 0.055-0.080 phr in EP2473280B1, and 0.057-0.080 phr in US10500578B2.
- The most aggressive viscosity reduction is seen in the metathesis-based low-MW patents (US7772328B2 and US7951875B2), while EP0588099A1 focuses on maintaining viscosity control during hydrogenation rather than intentionally lowering Mw.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows two viable synthesis directions for hydrogenated NBR: conventional selective hydrogenation with additives to suppress gelation and molecular-weight growth, and metathesis-assisted hydrogenation to deliberately lower Mw and Mooney viscosity. For high-performance HNBR, the most supported conditions are Ru/Os catalyst systems, 120°C-class temperatures, hydrogen pressures around 4.137 MPa or 1200 psi, and reaction times of 2-4 h when catalyst activity is optimized. For low-MW HNBR, the strongest evidence favors simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis with co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, or catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethyl ether, which can reduce Mw into the 30,000-150,000 g/mol range and PDI toward 2.0-2.5. If the goal is simply to preserve processability during hydrogenation, water plus selected organic acids in EP0588099A1 is the clearest control strategy. Overall, the best-supported synthesis parameters are low Ru loading, controlled temperature near 120°C, hydrogen pressure in the 4-7 MPa range, and catalyst/additive engineering tailored to whether the objective is saturation only or combined saturation plus chain-length reduction.
