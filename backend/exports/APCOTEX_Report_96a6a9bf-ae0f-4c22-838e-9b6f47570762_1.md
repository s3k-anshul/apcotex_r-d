# LOW STYRENE SBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION-CONTROLLED AND FUNCTIONALIZED STYRENE-BUTADIENE RUBBERS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), emphasizing materials in which styrene content is reduced relative to conventional SBR and/or tightly controlled through monomer feed, polymerization route, or post-polymerization functionalization. The selected patent set spans classic solution- and emulsion-polymerized SBR tread compositions, modern functionalized SBR architectures for silica-filled tire compounds, and broader conjugated-diene polymer platforms that explicitly define low vinyl-aromatic content windows. Across the landscape, the target is not simply “SBR” but low-styrene or styrene-lean SBR-like elastomers that preserve processability while balancing wet grip, rolling resistance, abrasion resistance, and hysteresis.

The patents show three dominant technical approaches. First, solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene with organolithium initiation is used to control styrene incorporation, vinyl content, molecular weight, and microstructure; this is seen in both older tread-rubber compositions and newer functionalized elastomers. Second, end-group or chain-mid functionalization is used to improve filler interaction, especially with silica, through silanol, silane, sulfur-silane, trithiocarbonate, or polyether-containing motifs. Third, some patents move beyond simple SBR to broader diene elastomers or polymer composites, but still disclose styrene-butadiene copolymer examples and composition ranges directly relevant to low-styrene SBR design.

Key formulation trends include silica reinforcement with silane coupling agents, use of aromatic or hydrocarbon oils, staged mixing to manage processability, and curing systems based on sulfur/accelerator packages. Process trends include batch and continuous solution polymerization, partial termination or coupling to create functional architectures, and post-polymerization modification at controlled temperatures. Overall, the evidence supports low-styrene SBR designs in the approximate 5-25 wt% styrene range, with solution polymerization, controlled vinyl content, and silica-compatible end-group functionality as the most relevant synthesis directions.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene with organolithium initiation is the dominant direct SBR synthesis route across the set (US4866131A, US8492479B2, EP2139703B1, US10676543B2, EP2596963B1, EP2719549B1).
- Explicit low-to-moderate styrene examples cluster around ~18-25 wt% styrene: EP2596963B1 reports 17.97 wt% in Sample 1 and 21.5-21.9 wt% in other samples; EP2719549B1 reports 17.97 wt% in the comparative functionalized SBR and 23.46-23.73 wt% in other samples; US8492479B2 Example 4 reports 25 wt% styrene.
- US4866131A uses a low molecular weight butadiene/aromatic vinyl copolymer blended with a high-styrene SBR, whereas the newer patents favor direct functionalization of solution SBR to improve silica interaction and tread performance.
- EP2139703B1 and US8492479B2 emphasize mid-chain or chain-end silanol/polyether functionality for silica-filled tread compounds, while US10676543B2 emphasizes high trans-1,4 microstructure plus alkoxysilane modification.
- US10676543B2 is the most microstructure-focused, explicitly requiring trans-1,4 bond content of 80 wt% or more; EP2596963B1 and EP2719549B1 also report trans/vinyl/styrene data but with lower styrene contents near the low-styrene boundary.
- Silica reinforcement and silane coupling are recurring formulation themes in the functionalized SBR patents (EP2139703B1, US10676543B2, EP2596963B1, EP2719549B1, EP3070120B1), indicating that low-styrene SBR is often optimized in silica-filled tread systems rather than carbon-black-only systems.
- Process conditions are generally mild to moderate solution-polymerization temperatures: 40-45 °C in US8492479B2 and EP2139703B1 batch examples, 65 °C in EP2719549B1, 70 °C in EP2139703B1 continuous example, and 80-90 °C in US10676543B2.
- Several patents rely on post-polymerization termination or coupling to introduce functionality: polyether-silane coupling in US8492479B2, methyltrichlorosilane coupling in EP2139703B1, alkoxysilane modification in US10676543B2, and ES/CTES termination in EP2719549B1.
- Older US4866131A focuses on blending a low molecular weight copolymer with a diene rubber, while later patents increasingly integrate the low-styrene SBR into a single functionalized polymer architecture to improve processability and filler interaction.
- US20260001972A1 broadens the landscape by defining a vinyl aromatic monomer content window of 1-18 wt%, which is directly compatible with low-styrene design targets even though the patent is not limited to SBR.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports low-styrene SBR development primarily through solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene with tight control of monomer feed, microstructure, and end-group functionality. The most practical synthesis window from the patent set is a styrene content near 18-25 wt% for many tire-grade examples, with lower values explicitly supported by the broader 1-18 wt% vinyl-aromatic window in US20260001972A1. For performance-balanced low-styrene SBR, the strongest evidence favors solution polymerization at about 40-70 °C, organolithium initiation, and silica-compatible functionalization such as silanol, polyether-silane, or alkoxysilane end groups. Where abrasion resistance is critical, high trans-1,4 microstructure and controlled modification ratio are beneficial. For tread compounding, silica plus silane coupling, sulfur/accelerator cure systems, and staged mixing are recurring best practices. Overall, the recommended synthesis direction is a solution-polymerized SBR with reduced styrene content, controlled vinyl/trans microstructure, and post-polymerization functionality designed to improve silica interaction and maintain processability.
