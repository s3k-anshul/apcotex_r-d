# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS DEVELOPMENT: CATALYTIC HYDROGENATION, LOW-VISCOSITY ROUTES, AND FUNCTIONALIZED HNBR LATEX PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), focusing on how NBR precursors are converted to partially or fully hydrogenated products and how process choices affect residual unsaturation, viscosity, and downstream performance. The target material is HNBR derived from acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymers, including conventional NBR, low-molecular-weight HNBR, and functionalized HNBR terpolymers. Across the patent set, the dominant synthesis theme is selective hydrogenation of diene unsaturation in solution or latex, typically using noble-metal catalysts from groups 8–10, with strong emphasis on catalyst selectivity, catalyst removal, and control of gel formation or aging stability. EP3248988B1 is centered on homogeneous rhodium-catalyzed hydrogenation of nitrile groups and tandem hydrogenation in solution, using RhH(PiPr3)3 with phosphine additives to suppress side reactions and gel formation. US20210340285A1 addresses HNBR production with ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium compounds at low ppm catalyst levels, aiming to improve aging properties by minimizing residual noble metals and avoiding post-hydrogenation purification. US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 pursue a different route: metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber prior to or in conjunction with hydrogenation, yielding low-Mooney HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distributions. US11827726B2 focuses on latex hydrogenation of PEG acrylate-functionalized NBR followed by coagulation, combining HNBR formation with retained functional comonomer content to improve processability and tailor properties. US10731020B2 is not a direct HNBR synthesis patent, but it is relevant as it explicitly includes hydrogenated nitrile rubber among base rubbers for vulcanization-molding compositions and provides processing constraints that reflect the importance of Mooney viscosity control in HNBR applications. Overall, the landscape shows two major process directions: direct catalytic hydrogenation of NBR/HNBR precursors in solution or latex, and precursor modification by metathesis or functional comonomer incorporation to manage viscosity and application performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP3248988B1 and US20210340285A1 both use noble-metal catalysis in solution, but EP3248988B1 emphasizes unsupported rhodium with phosphine additive for selective nitrile hydrogenation, whereas US20210340285A1 emphasizes low-ppm Ru/Pd/Rh loading and catalyst-residue control for aging stability.
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 both use metathesis to reduce molecular weight of nitrile rubber before/while making HNBR; the disclosed examples show ethylene or 1-hexene co-olefins, 80° C. reactor temperature, and catalyst loadings of 0.05–0.25 phr.
- The metathesis patents target low Mooney viscosity and narrow PDI, while the direct hydrogenation patents target high degree of hydrogenation, low residual unsaturation, and suppression of gel formation.
- US11827726B2 differs from the other HNBR patents by hydrogenating PEG acrylate-functionalized NBR latex rather than conventional solution polymer; it preserves functional comonomer content while converting diene unsaturation.
- US20210340285A1 and EP3248988B1 both address catalyst selectivity and side reactions, but US20210340285A1 is more focused on post-hydrogenation aging and noble-metal residue management.
- US10731020B2 is downstream-processing oriented rather than synthetic; it shows that HNBR application performance is strongly influenced by base-rubber Mooney viscosity and compound formulation, not only by hydrogenation chemistry.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two principal HNBR synthesis strategies: direct catalytic hydrogenation of NBR/HNBR precursors in solution or latex, and precursor modification by metathesis to lower molecular weight and improve processability. For direct hydrogenation, the most relevant parameters are selective noble-metal catalysis, solution or latex processing, hydrogenation temperatures around 60 to 200° C., pressures in the MPa range, and careful control of catalyst residues to avoid gel formation and aging loss. For processability-focused HNBR, the metathesis patents show that low Mooney viscosity and narrow molecular-weight distribution can be achieved using Ru/Os catalysts with co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene at about 80° C. and low phr catalyst loadings. Functionalized latex hydrogenation, as in PEG acrylate-HNBR, broadens the design space for specialty elastomers. Based on the evidence, the most robust synthesis targets are: selective hydrogenation in solution or latex, low residual unsaturation, minimal noble-metal contamination, and precursor molecular-weight control to keep final Mooney viscosity in a processable range.
