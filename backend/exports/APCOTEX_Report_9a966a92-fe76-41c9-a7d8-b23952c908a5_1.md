# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION CONTROL, SOLUTION POLYMERIZATION, AND LOW-MOONEY RUBBER DESIGN

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related low-viscosity nitrile rubber systems. The target is defined as NBR grades with reduced acrylonitrile content and/or composition control strategies that enable low-ACN elastomers with manageable processability, while avoiding hydrogenated, carboxylated, or otherwise excluded variants unless they are directly relevant as downstream analogs. Across the selected patents, the landscape spans three distinct but related themes: (1) low-Mooney nitrile rubber generated by metathesis followed by hydrogenation, (2) solution-phase free-radical NBR synthesis in organic solvents using modifier/RAFT-like control concepts, and (3) swellable rubber formulations that incorporate nitrile elastomers, including hydrogenated nitrile elastomers, as part of a high-performance downhole composition.

EP1554342B1 focuses on a two-step route to low-Mooney hydrogenated nitrile rubber, where a statistical butadiene-acrylonitrile terpolymer is first metathesized in monochlorobenzene under ethylene pressure and then hydrogenated with Wilkinson’s catalyst at elevated temperature and hydrogen pressure. The disclosed feedstock includes 47 mol% butadiene and 21 mol% acrylonitrile, showing that low-ACN nitrile backbones can be converted into highly processable low-viscosity HNBR. EP2478021B1 discloses solution polymerization of nitrile rubbers in organic solvents, using acrylonitrile, 1,3-butadiene, and modifiers/initiator systems to control molecular weight and enable direct synthesis of NBR in solution rather than conventional emulsion polymerization. It emphasizes solvent choice, regulator concentration, conversion control, and post-polymerization coagulation. US10385647B2 is not a synthesis patent for NBR itself, but it is relevant because it specifies swellable rubber compositions containing nitrile elastomer and butadiene rubber, with explicit ranges for hydrogenated nitrile elastomer hydrogenation, Mooney viscosity, and SAP loading; it demonstrates how nitrile-based elastomers are formulated for high-temperature, high-pressure service.

Overall, the evidence suggests that low-ACN NBR synthesis is best approached through careful monomer feed control in solution or emulsion polymerization, with downstream viscosity tailoring via molecular-weight control or metathesis/hydrogenation when HNBR analogs are acceptable. The most actionable process trends are solvent-based polymerization, controlled initiator/modifier use, and explicit management of Mooney viscosity and conversion to obtain processable nitrile rubber grades.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP1554342B1 and EP2478021B1 both address nitrile-rubber composition control, but EP1554342B1 uses a low-Mooney precursor followed by metathesis/hydrogenation, whereas EP2478021B1 uses direct solution free-radical polymerization of acrylonitrile/butadiene in organic solvent.
- EP1554342B1 explicitly discloses a starting terpolymer with 21 mol% acrylonitrile, while EP2478021B1 discloses acrylonitrile feed at 37 phm and butadiene at 63 phm in Example 1; both are consistent with low-to-moderate ACN NBR precursor design rather than high-ACN grades.
- EP1554342B1 relies on ethylene pressure during metathesis and hydrogen pressure during hydrogenation; EP2478021B1 relies on 3 bar monomer pressure, 100°C polymerization temperature, and modifier/initiator control in solution.
- EP1554342B1 emphasizes Mooney viscosity reduction to below 10 for the hydrogenated low-Mooney component, while EP2478021B1 emphasizes molecular-weight control through regulator concentration and conversion management to obtain processable NBR.
- EP2478021B1 uses organic solvent polymerization with DMAc and post-polymerization coagulation, whereas EP1554342B1 uses monochlorobenzene for both metathesis and hydrogenation; both are solvent-based but serve different process objectives.
- US10385647B2 is formulation-focused rather than synthesis-focused, but it confirms that nitrile elastomers with about 70-91% hydrogenation and Mooney viscosity about 47-72 are suitable in swellable packer compositions alongside powder SAP.
- Across the set, the most explicit low-ACN composition evidence is in EP1554342B1 (21 mol% acrylonitrile substrate), while the most explicit direct NBR synthesis evidence is in EP2478021B1.
- The patents collectively suggest that low-ACN NBR development benefits from controlled monomer feed ratios, solvent-based polymerization, and downstream viscosity tailoring when hydrogenated analogs are acceptable.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low acrylonitrile NBR development is best supported by solvent-based polymerization with explicit monomer-ratio control and, where needed, downstream molecular-weight reduction or hydrogenation to improve processability. EP2478021B1 provides the clearest direct synthesis route for NBR in organic solvent, while EP1554342B1 demonstrates how a nitrile-containing precursor with 21 mol% acrylonitrile can be converted into a low-Mooney rubber via metathesis and hydrogenation. US10385647B2 is not a synthesis patent, but it confirms practical formulation windows for nitrile elastomers in demanding applications. Based on the evidence, recommended synthesis parameters for low-ACN NBR are controlled acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratios, solution polymerization in an oxygen-free organic solvent, temperature near 100°C for radical polymerization, and viscosity control via regulator or post-polymerization modification when low-Mooney performance is required.
