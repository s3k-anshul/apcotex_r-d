# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: EMULSION POLYMERIZATION, COMPOSITION CONTROL, AND WORK-UP TRENDS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) manufacture, with emphasis on low-acrylonitrile and composition-controlled embodiments. The selected landscape spans one patent centered on controlled emulsion/block-copolymer technology that explicitly includes nitrile-rubber blocks containing acrylonitrile and butadiene, and one patent focused directly on nitrile rubbers produced by emulsion polymerization and subsequent coagulation/washing work-up. Across the set, the dominant synthesis mode is aqueous emulsion polymerization, often using surfactant systems, initiators such as potassium persulfate, and chain-transfer or molecular-weight-control agents such as tert-dodecyl mercaptan. The patents show two distinct but complementary trends: first, composition control during polymerization via staged or controlled monomer addition to tune acrylonitrile/butadiene incorporation; second, downstream isolation strategies that strongly affect storage stability and residual inorganic content, especially calcium and chlorine levels.

The NBR-focused patent discloses latices A, B, and C with acrylonitrile contents of 27, 12.5/6.5, and 44 parts by weight per 100 parts monomer mixture, respectively, and polymerization temperatures of 17, 18, and 20 °C. It further demonstrates that coagulation and washing conditions, including the use of calcium-containing water, influence storage stability and residual calcium/chlorine in the rubber. The block-copolymer patent is relevant because it explicitly defines a B block comprised of acrylonitrile/methacrylonitrile and isoprene or butadiene, and its examples show aqueous microemulsion polymerization with water, potassium persulfate, phosphate, and potassium hydroxide, followed by latex formation and stripping. Overall, the extracted evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR development is driven by emulsion-process control, monomer-feed staging, and careful post-polymerization work-up rather than by nonaqueous synthesis routes.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7592409B2 is a general controlled emulsion/block-copolymer platform that only indirectly touches nitrile-rubber chemistry through a claimed B block containing acrylonitrile/methacrylonitrile and butadiene/isoprene; it does not disclose an NBR composition value for the target material.
- US7923518B2 is the only patent in the set that directly discloses NBR recipes with explicit acrylonitrile loadings, including a low-ACN latex B formulation using 12.5/6.5 parts acrylonitrile and staged feed addition.
- Both patents use aqueous emulsion/microemulsion media, but US7592409B2 emphasizes in-situ surfactant generation and controlled radical/block architecture, whereas US7923518B2 emphasizes conventional NBR emulsion polymerization plus coagulation/washing work-up.
- US7923518B2 discloses much more process detail for NBR manufacture: monomer ratios, emulsifier package, chain-transfer agent t-DDM, initiator system, temperatures of 17-20 °C, and conversion levels around 74.5-75%.
- Only US7923518B2 provides downstream isolation data tied to product quality, including calcium content, chlorine content, washing temperature at 20 °C, and storage stability not more than 3 Mooney units.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, the strongest direct evidence for low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis comes from US7923518B2, which discloses butadiene/acrylonitrile emulsion recipes including a low-ACN staged-feed latex B formulation and links composition to storage-stable rubber after coagulation and washing. US7592409B2 is relevant mainly as an enabling aqueous controlled-emulsion/block-copolymer platform that includes nitrile-rubber block chemistry, but it does not directly disclose a low-ACN NBR target composition. Overall, the extracted record shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR development is driven by emulsion polymerization recipe design, staged monomer addition, and careful post-polymerization work-up.
