# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: COMPOSITION CONTROL, BARRIER STRUCTURES, AND ELASTOMERIC APPLICATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys a five-patent landscape relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) and adjacent elastomeric technologies. The target is defined as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile content or composition control over acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratio, with emphasis on industrially relevant synthesis, emulsion or solution copolymerization, and downstream performance in hoses, seals, and high-demand elastomeric components. Across the manifest, only one patent directly addresses NBR as a hose material (EP2438340B1), while the others provide adjacent formulation or application context rather than direct NBR polymerization routes.

The strongest direct relevance comes from EP2438340B1, which identifies NBR among preferred non-fluorinated rubber inner-tube materials for flexible fuel hose and pairs it with a branched PA6 barrier layer and impact modifier to achieve low permeation and improved flexibility. US10443339B2 is also relevant at the application level, describing elastomeric components for bore tools and packers in harsh environments and explicitly naming NBR among candidate elastomers, though it focuses on nanoplatelet reinforcement rather than NBR synthesis. US20200239671A1 is a wear-resistant rubber composition patent centered on natural rubber, polybutadiene, and hydroxy-terminated polybutadiene; it is only indirectly relevant as a formulation/process benchmark for durable elastomer systems. US7871546B2 concerns vegetable-oil dielectric fluids and is not chemically related to NBR synthesis, but it illustrates the broader patent environment for elastomer-adjacent industrial materials. US20080108752A1 discloses controlled free-radical block copolymerization of styrene and maleic anhydride using TEMPO/BPO chemistry; it is not an NBR patent, but it is useful as a process reference for controlled copolymerization, monomer-feed management, and molecular-weight control.

Overall, the evidence suggests that low-acrylonitrile NBR is most often treated as a materials-selection problem in end-use patents rather than a dedicated synthesis patent in this set. The most transferable process themes are composition control, barrier-layer integration, and reinforcement/compatibilization strategies rather than explicit low-ACN polymerization recipes.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Only EP2438340B1 directly uses NBR in a target application; the other patents are adjacent materials or process references rather than low-ACN NBR synthesis disclosures.
- US20080108752A1 provides the clearest composition-control analogy: monomer ratio, stable-free-radical/initiator ratio, temperature, and time are explicitly tuned to control copolymer microstructure and molecular weight.
- US20200239671A1 and EP2438340B1 are formulation/application patents, emphasizing compounded elastomer performance rather than monomer feed control; both rely on multi-component rubber systems and property targets.
- US10443339B2 focuses on harsh-environment elastomer service and explicitly lists NBR as a candidate elastomer, but the disclosed innovation is nanoplatelet reinforcement, not acrylonitrile-content adjustment.
- US7871546B2 is outside the NBR chemistry space and contributes only general industrial materials context; it does not disclose polymerization, emulsifier, initiator, or chain-transfer conditions relevant to NBR synthesis.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The manifest contains only one patent that directly names NBR in a relevant product architecture, EP2438340B1, which supports NBR as a non-fluorinated inner-tube elastomer in low-permeation fuel hose. The remaining patents are useful mainly as adjacent references for elastomer compounding, controlled copolymerization, and harsh-environment service requirements. For low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis specifically, the evidence here does not provide a dedicated polymerization recipe; the most transferable process lesson is from controlled free-radical copolymerization in US20080108752A1, where monomer ratio, stable-free-radical/initiator ratio, temperature, and reaction time govern composition and molecular weight. Based on the evidence, the most defensible synthesis guidance is to prioritize feed-ratio control and tight process control during copolymerization, then validate the resulting low-ACN NBR in multilayer or reinforced formulations for fuel, seal, and downhole applications.
