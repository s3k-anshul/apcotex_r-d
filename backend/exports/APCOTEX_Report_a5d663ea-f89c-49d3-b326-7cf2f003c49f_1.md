# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE AND PROCESS ANALYSIS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews two primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) synthesis and formulation, with emphasis on low-acrylonitrile composition control and process routes that influence acrylonitrile incorporation. The target material is low-acrylonitrile NBR, generally understood as NBR grades below about 20 wt% acrylonitrile, although the provided patent set includes one process patent focused on high-bound-acrylonitrile NBR and one formulation patent that uses NBR as a component in swellable rubber compositions. The landscape therefore spans both direct copolymerization chemistry and downstream rubber compounding, but only one patent directly addresses monomer feed/composition control during NBR polymerization.

US5770660A discloses a continuous free-radical emulsion copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene using multiple reaction vessels and incremental butadiene addition to maintain a controlled unconverted-monomer ratio. Although the patent is aimed at high bound acrylonitrile NBR, it is technically important because it demonstrates how staged monomer addition, continuous transfer, and emulsion polymerization can stabilize composition distribution and conversion behavior in NBR manufacture. The examples contrast conventional batch, incremental batch, and continuous operation, showing that continuous processing improves tensile strength and environmental resistance relative to batch routes.

US10385647B2 is not a synthesis patent for NBR itself, but it is relevant because it incorporates a nitrile elastomer, including NBR, into a swellable rubber composition with butadiene rubber, sulfur curing agent, silica, and powder super absorbent polymer. The patent emphasizes formulation control, high SAP loading, and curing conditions that preserve mechanical integrity under brine, temperature, and pressure. It also discloses a nitrile rubber elastomer option with Mooney viscosity ML 1+4 (100°C) of about 47 to about 72 and discusses hydrogenated nitrile elastomer variants, which are outside the target but useful as formulation context.

Overall, the evidence suggests that low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis would most plausibly be achieved by adjusting acrylonitrile feed ratio and maintaining emulsion polymerization conditions that avoid late-stage butadiene depletion. The strongest process lessons are staged monomer addition, controlled conversion, and continuous reactor operation; however, the manifest does not include a patent explicitly optimized for low-acrylonitrile NBR.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US5770660A is a direct NBR copolymerization patent, while US10385647B2 is a downstream compounding patent that uses NBR as one elastomer component in swellable packers.
- US5770660A controls acrylonitrile/butadiene composition by staged monomer addition in continuous emulsion polymerization; US10385647B2 controls performance by formulation design, SAP loading, and curing conditions rather than monomer feed.
- US5770660A explicitly discloses acrylonitrile/butadiene feed ratios of about 80/20 and unconverted-monomer ratios of about 70-90/30-10; US10385647B2 does not disclose NBR monomer composition or polymerization conditions.
- US5770660A uses multiple reaction vessels and incremental butadiene addition; US10385647B2 uses a nitrile elastomer with butadiene rubber, sulfur curing agent, silica, and powder SAP.
- US5770660A reports conversions from 24% to 82% in the continuous example and bound acrylonitrile content of 48.8%; US10385647B2 reports curing at 330°F and 70 psi for 2.5 hours and swell testing at 200-275°F in 5% and 16% brine.
- For low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis, US5770660A is the more informative process reference because it addresses monomer ratio control directly, even though its target composition is high bound acrylonitrile.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence contains one direct NBR polymerization process and one formulation patent using NBR in swellable rubber articles. For low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis, the most useful guidance comes from US5770660A: maintain emulsion polymerization with controlled acrylonitrile/butadiene feed, avoid late-stage butadiene depletion, and use staged or continuous addition to stabilize composition. Although the patent is aimed at high bound acrylonitrile, the same process logic can be inverted to support lower acrylonitrile incorporation by reducing acrylonitrile feed and preserving monomer-ratio control throughout conversion. US10385647B2 is relevant mainly as application context, showing that NBR can be compounded with sulfur, silica, and powder SAP for high-temperature brine service. Based on the evidence, the recommended synthesis direction for low-ACN NBR is controlled emulsion copolymerization with careful acrylonitrile feed management, staged monomer addition, and conversion control to prevent composition drift.
