# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This technical report synthesizes patent literature regarding the synthesis, compounding, and application of Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (Low Acrylonitrile NBR). The research scope encompasses the polymerization methodologies, structural modifications, and formulation trends of diene-based elastomers containing low levels of acrylonitrile (typically under 30% by mass). Low Acrylonitrile NBR is highly valued for its low-temperature flexibility, oil resistance, and compatibility with polar fillers like silica. The selected patent landscape reveals three primary polymerization and modification approaches: cold emulsion polymerization, anionic solution copolymerization, and olefin metathesis-driven segment interchange. Emulsion polymerization remains the dominant industrial method, utilizing rosin/fatty acid emulsifiers, mercaptan chain transfer agents, and organic peroxide initiators at low temperatures (e.g., 10°C) to achieve random monomer distribution and narrow glass transition widths. Anionic solution polymerization, initiated by functionalized lithium amides, is utilized to introduce tertiary amine or pyrrolidine groups, significantly enhancing filler-elastomer interactions and reducing hysteresis. Olefin metathesis, utilizing Grubbs-type catalysts, offers a route to novel block copolymers via segment interchange of hydrogenated NBR (HNBR). In terms of application, Low Acrylonitrile NBR is heavily featured in electro-conductive matrix-domain structures for electrophotographic members, where its specific volume resistivity and compatibility with epichlorohydrin or other polar rubbers prevent transfer defects and image fogging. This report provides a comprehensive analysis of these synthetic methodologies and compounding formulations to guide the development of high-performance Low Acrylonitrile NBR materials.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Monomer Content Ranges: US6562929B2 specifies a highly randomized terpolymer containing 9 to 30% by mass of acrylonitrile, 10 to 50% styrene, and 20 to 81% butadiene. US11137716B2 utilizes Nipol DN401LL, a commercial low-acrylonitrile NBR grade (~18% acrylonitrile), while US11971683B2 utilizes N215SL, another low-acrylonitrile NBR grade.
- Polymerization Processes: Emulsion polymerization is detailed in US7041761B2 for functionalized diene rubbers. Anionic solution polymerization is utilized in US5567815A, US6693160B1, and US6995224B2 to prepare functionalized diene elastomers. US8557921B2 introduces a distinct metathesis-driven segment interchange process in solution to form block copolymers from HNBR.
- Emulsifier and Initiator Systems: US7041761B2 details a cold emulsion system (10°C) using disproportionated rosin acid and unsaturated fatty acid potassium salts as emulsifiers, initiated by pinane hydroperoxide. In contrast, the anionic solution systems (US5567815A, US6693160B1) utilize n-butyllithium or functionalized lithium amides in hydrocarbon solvents at elevated temperatures.
- Chain-Transfer and Coagulation: US7041761B2 utilizes tert-dodecylmercaptan as a chain-transfer agent to control molecular weight and coagulates the latex using 10% sulfuric acid at pH 3-4. The solution polymerization patents typically terminate reactions with alcohols (e.g., isopropyl alcohol) and recover the polymer by vacuum stripping or solvent precipitation.
- Compounding and Applications: US11137716B2 and US11971683B2 focus on compounding low-acrylonitrile NBR into matrix-domain electro-conductive layers with epichlorohydrin rubbers, utilizing sulfur vulcanization systems with zinc oxide and stearic acid to control electrical impedance and physical properties.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The synthesis of high-performance Low Acrylonitrile NBR requires precise control over monomer randomness, molecular weight distribution, and functionalization. Based on the patent evidence, cold emulsion polymerization at 10°C using disproportionated rosin acid emulsifiers and tert-dodecylmercaptan chain transfer agents is recommended for producing highly randomized terpolymers with narrow glass transition widths (<20°C) and Mooney viscosities of 20-200. For applications requiring low hysteresis and high filler interaction, anionic solution copolymerization with amine-functionalized monomers (such as PES or PAMS) initiated by functionalized lithium amides is highly effective. Compounding low-acrylonitrile grades (such as Nipol DN401LL or N215SL) into matrix-domain structures with epichlorohydrin rubbers provides optimal electro-conductive properties for specialized engineering applications. Future research should focus on optimizing metathesis-driven segment interchange to create thermoplastic NBR elastomers with tailored block architectures.
