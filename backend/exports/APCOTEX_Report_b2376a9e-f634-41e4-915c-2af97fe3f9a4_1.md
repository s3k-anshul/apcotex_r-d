# PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT FOR HIGH-ACRYLONITRILE NBR-RELATED DISCLOSURES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents retrieved under a high-acrylonitrile NBR search profile, with emphasis on how each disclosure relates to nitrile rubber composition control, degradation, or block-copolymer construction. The target material is high-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), generally understood as a butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer enriched in acrylonitrile to increase polarity, oil resistance, and gas-barrier performance relative to standard NBR. Among the selected patents, only US7875683B2 directly addresses nitrile rubber as the substrate and is therefore the most technically relevant to NBR processing; it discloses metathetic degradation of nitrile rubber using a metal-carbene metathesis catalyst combined with a transition-metal additive to reduce molecular weight before hydrogenation. US7592409B2 is relevant by composition and process analogy: it discloses controlled emulsion polymerization of block polymers containing an A block and a B block where the B block can include acrylonitrile and isoprene, and it provides detailed in-situ emulsification, RAFT/NMP-type control, and seed-latex strategies that are transferable to controlled nitrile-rubber architectures. US9296850B2 is only indirectly relevant, since it focuses on renewable isoprene production and polyisoprene/copolymer claims that merely list acrylonitrile among many possible comonomers; it does not disclose NBR synthesis. Across the landscape, the dominant process themes are controlled emulsion polymerization, in-situ surfactant generation, and metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction. The evidence suggests that high-ACN NBR development is most strongly supported by patents teaching precise monomer/comonomer control, aqueous emulsion processing, and post-polymerization molecular-weight tailoring rather than by direct high-ACN NBR examples.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7875683B2 is the only patent that directly uses nitrile rubber as the substrate; it focuses on post-polymerization molecular-weight reduction rather than monomer feed control.
- US7592409B2 provides the most relevant synthesis methodology for composition/architecture control, using in-situ emulsification, RAFT/NMP/ATRP-type control, and seed-latex strategies; it explicitly mentions acrylonitrile-containing B blocks.
- US9296850B2 is only tangentially related: it centers on renewable isoprene production and polyisoprene claims, with acrylonitrile appearing only as one optional comonomer in a broad claim list.
- No patent in the set discloses a direct high-acrylonitrile NBR copolymerization recipe with explicit acrylonitrile wt% feed ratios or final ACN content.
- Temperature windows in the relevant process patents cluster around 65-75°C for controlled emulsion polymerization in US7592409B2, while US7875683B2 emphasizes metathesis degradation followed by hydrogenation rather than polymerization temperature.
- US7875683B2 reports prior-art catalyst loadings in ppm Ru and phr catalyst terms, highlighting the catalyst-intensity issue in nitrile-rubber degradation; US7592409B2 instead uses persulfate/phosphate/KOH aqueous systems and organic control agents such as dibenzyltrithiocarbonate or PBN.
- US7592409B2 repeatedly achieves complete conversion within about 1.5 to 7 hours depending on recipe, whereas US7875683B2 discusses long reaction times as a limitation of prior metathesis degradation processes.
- For high-ACN NBR development, the most transferable evidence is controlled aqueous emulsion processing with surfactant generation and block-architecture control from US7592409B2, combined with molecular-weight tailoring concepts from US7875683B2.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set does not contain a direct high-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis example, but it does provide two useful technical directions: controlled emulsion/block-copolymer methodology and nitrile-rubber molecular-weight reduction. For synthesis of high-ACN NBR, the strongest evidence supports aqueous controlled polymerization with tight monomer-feed control, in-situ emulsification, and low-polydispersity control agents, while US7875683B2 indicates that post-polymerization metathesis can be used to tune molecular weight and processability of nitrile rubber feedstocks. Based on the evidence, the most defensible recommended approach is controlled emulsion copolymerization of butadiene with acrylonitrile using a surfactant-controlled aqueous system at roughly 65-75°C, with careful control of initiator and chain-transfer/control-agent loading, followed if needed by molecular-weight adjustment to maintain processability.
