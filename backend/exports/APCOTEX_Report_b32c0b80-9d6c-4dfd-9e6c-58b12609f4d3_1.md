# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS LANDSCAPE FROM SELECTED PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), focusing on how NBR precursors are converted to partially or highly hydrogenated elastomers with reduced residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil resistance, and improved processability. The selected patent landscape spans two major technical directions: direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution or specialized reactors, and coupled metathesis/hydrogenation routes used to lower molecular weight before or during hydrogenation. Across the set, the target material is consistently defined as a nitrile rubber copolymer or terpolymer containing at least one conjugated diene and at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile, optionally with additional comonomers; hydrogenation is used to convert some or all diene-derived C=C bonds to saturated backbone units while preserving nitrile functionality.

The patents show several process families. One group emphasizes reactor/process intensification, including spinning disk reactor hydrogenation with hydrogen pressures from 3.5 to 100 bar and homogeneous catalysts based on Rh/Ru and related metals. Another group focuses on simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation using ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems in the presence of hydrogen and optional co-olefins, enabling single-step reduction of molecular weight and residual double bond content. A third group uses metathesis first, then hydrogenation, to obtain low-MW HNBR with narrow molecular weight distributions. Additional patents address upstream NBR synthesis in organic solvents or solvent mixtures, including emulsion and solution polymerization with mercaptan chain transfer control, because precursor molecular weight strongly influences final HNBR viscosity. Finally, one patent addresses post-hydrogenation purification by removing ruthenium residues with functionalized ion-exchange resins, and two patents apply partially hydrogenated NBR in carbon nanotube dispersions, where residual double bond content and monomer composition are tuned for dispersion performance.

Overall, the evidence indicates that the most relevant synthesis levers for HNBR are controlled NBR precursor composition, selective hydrogenation conditions, catalyst choice, hydrogen pressure, and, where needed, metathesis-assisted molecular weight reduction. The strongest process trend is toward lower-viscosity, lower-residual-unsaturation HNBR made either by intensified hydrogenation or by one-step/ two-step metathesis-hydrogenation sequences.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Direct hydrogenation patents (US7666950B2) emphasize reactor engineering and hydrogen pressure control, while simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation patents (EP2473281B1, US9150669B2) combine chain scission and saturation in one step to lower Mw and Mooney viscosity.
- The low-MW HNBR patents (US7772328B2, US7951875B2) use metathesis first and then hydrogenation; they report product Mw around 54 kg/mol and Mooney viscosity as low as 3 in the best example, showing the benefit of precursor degradation before saturation.
- Catalyst systems differ substantially: US7666950B2 broadly allows Rh/Ru/Ti/Pt/Ir/Pd/Re/Os/Co/Cu catalysts, whereas EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 focus on Ru/Os compounds for simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation, and US8957161B2 uses salt-accelerated metathesis catalyst systems.
- Process conditions vary from batch hydrogenation pressures of 65-90 bar in the background of US7666950B2 to a claimed 3.5-100 bar window in the spinning-disk reactor process; the simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation patents do not center on high-pressure batch operation but on catalyst efficiency and single-step conversion.
- Precursor composition control is explicit in US7666950B2 and EP2554558A1: NBR typically contains 40-90 wt.% diene and 10-60 wt.% nitrile, while the solvent-polymerization patent uses 70-99.9 vol% main solvent and 100°C polymerization for NBR synthesis.
- Post-treatment is addressed only in US7846995B2, which removes Ru residues to 10 ppm after 66 h at about 100°C using 0.5 g ion-exchange resin, highlighting downstream purification as a separate but important HNBR manufacturing step.
- The CNT-dispersion patents (US10686185B2, EP3348582B1) are not synthesis patents for HNBR itself, but they define useful partially hydrogenated NBR windows: RDB 0.5-40 wt.% and α,β-unsaturated nitrile content 20-50 wt.% for formulation performance.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports three practical synthesis routes for hydrogenated NBR: direct hydrogenation of NBR in intensified reactors, metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by hydrogenation, and simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation in the presence of hydrogen. For conventional HNBR manufacture, the most defensible parameters are NBR feedstocks with about 40-90 wt.% diene and 10-60 wt.% nitrile, hydrogenation under Rh/Ru-based catalysis, and hydrogen pressures in the 3.5-100 bar range when using intensified reactor designs. For low-viscosity HNBR, the strongest evidence favors pre-metathesis or simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation using Ru/Os catalyst systems, optional co-olefins, and conditions that drive Mw into the 20,000-250,000 range with Mooney viscosity below 50 and residual double bond content near the low end of the disclosed ranges. Downstream, ruthenium residue removal by functionalized ion-exchange resin is recommended when metathesis catalysts are used. Overall, the best-supported synthesis strategy is to tailor the NBR precursor first, then use catalyst-efficient hydrogenation or combined metathesis/hydrogenation to achieve low-RDB, low-Mooney HNBR.
