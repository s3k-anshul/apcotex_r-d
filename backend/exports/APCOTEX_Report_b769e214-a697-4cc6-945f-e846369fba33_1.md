# PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This technical report synthesizes polymerization and synthesis data from six key patents focusing on Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (Low ACN NBR). The scope of this research covers the production of nitrile rubbers with bound acrylonitrile contents typically ranging from 27.9% to 36.6%. The selected patent landscape reveals two primary technical focuses: first, low-temperature (7&deg;C to 8&deg;C) emulsion polymerization processes utilizing redox initiator systems (such as diisopropylbenzene hydroperoxide or p-menthane hydroperoxide combined with iron(II) salts) and incremental chain-transfer agent (TDM) dosing to control molecular weight and Mooney viscosity; second, highly controlled post-polymerization coagulation and washing processes. A significant portion of the patent art focuses on adjusting latex pH and selecting specific coagulation salts (magnesium chloride, calcium chloride, or sodium chloride) to regulate the residual cation profile (ion index) of the final rubber. This precise ion control is shown to directly optimize vulcanization kinetics, reduce Mooney scorch, and improve the corrosion resistance and aging properties of the resulting vulcanizates.

### POLYMERIZATION RECIPE EXTRACTIONS

#### Patent 1

**Patent Details**
- Patent Number: US9650502
- Patent Title: Process for the preparation of nitrile rubbers
- Assignee: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2017
- Polymer Type: Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR)
- Relevance to target: Discloses a low-temperature emulsion polymerization process for NBR with low bound ACN (27.9% - 28.1%) using incremental additions of t-dodecyl mercaptan (TDM) to control Mooney viscosity.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**
- Polymerization process: Cold emulsion polymerization
- Monomer system: Acrylonitrile and Butadiene
- Monomer ratio: 500 g ACN / 4.37 kg Butadiene (approx. 10.3 wt% ACN in feed) or 815 g ACN / 4.0 kg Butadiene (approx. 16.9 wt% ACN in feed)
- Water amount: 9.92 kg deionized water
- Emulsifier: Potassium stearate and DNMK (disproportionated rosin acid potassium salt)
- Emulsifier loading: 0.53 kg of 5.4 wt% potassium stearate solution, 0.440 kg DNMK
- Initiator: Diisopropylbenzene hydroperoxide (DIHP) and Sodium formaldehyde sulfoxylate (FSS)
- Initiator loading: 6 g DIHP, 139 g FSS aqueous solution
- Catalyst / activator: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Chain-transfer agent: t-Dodecyl mercaptan (TDM)
- Chain-transfer dosage: 18.72 g initially, with booster additions of 2.34 g at 37% and 59% conversion (or 21.85 g initially and 5.45 g booster)
- Polymerization temperature: 7&deg;C
- Pressure: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- pH: Latex pH of 8.5 - 8.6
- Reaction time: 7 hours
- Conversion: 70% (stopped with hydroxylamine sulfate)
- Coagulation conditions: Coagulated using magnesium sulfate (4 kg saturated solution in 43 kg water) at elevated temperature, followed by washing at pH 11.5-11.8 (using KOH)
- Post-treatment: Filtered and dried in an air oven at 100&deg;C for 24 hours (residual humidity < 1%)
- Raw polymer properties: Bound ACN content of 27.9% - 28.1% (Latex A/C) or 33.9% (Latex B); Mooney viscosity of 29.0 - 60.5 (ML 1+4 @ 100&deg;C)

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Latex A was prepared with 500 g ACN, 4.37 kg butadiene, and 18.72 g TDM, yielding 28.1% bound ACN and 60.5 Mooney viscosity.
- Latex C was prepared similarly to Latex A, yielding 27.9% bound ACN and 29.0 Mooney viscosity.
- Latex B was prepared with 815 g ACN, 4.0 kg butadiene, and 21.85 g TDM, yielding 33.9% bound ACN and 32.1 Mooney viscosity.
- Coagulation was performed using magnesium sulfate, followed by washing at pH 11.5-11.8 with KOH to control residual emulsifier and ash content.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates a low-temperature (7&deg;C) emulsion polymerization process for low-ACN nitrile rubbers (27.9-28.1% bound ACN) using incremental additions of TDM to control molecular weight and Mooney viscosity.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates that the choice of coagulation salt (MgCl2 vs. CaCl2) and the resulting cation ratio in the rubber significantly influence Mooney scorch and vulcanization rates.

#### Patent 5

**Patent Details**
- Patent Number: US8436091
- Patent Title: Nitrile rubbers
- Assignee: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Jurisdiction: US
- Publication Year: 2013
- Polymer Type: Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR) / Carboxylated NBR (XNBR)
- Relevance to target: Discloses a low-temperature emulsion polymerization process using a specific redox initiator system (Trigonox NT 50 and Fe(II) chloride or sulfate) to produce NBR with controlled ACN content and Mooney viscosity.

**Polymerization / Synthesis Method**
- Polymerization process: Cold emulsion polymerization
- Monomer system: Butadiene, Acrylonitrile, and 2-Hydroxyethyl methacrylate (HEMA)
- Monomer ratio: Butadiene: 61.4, Acrylonitrile: 36, HEMA: 2.6 parts by weight
- Water amount: 220 parts by weight (total water); 2.1 kg water for 1.25 kg monomer autoclave batches
- Emulsifier: Sodium lauryl sulphate (Texapon K-12)
- Emulsifier loading: 3.0 parts by weight (range 0.2 to 15 parts by weight)
- Initiator: p-Menthane hydroperoxide (Trigonox NT 50) and Fe(II)SO4 or Fe(II)Cl2 redox system with Rongalit C
- Initiator loading: 0.02 parts by weight Trigonox NT 50; Premix containing 0.986 g Fe(II)SO4*7H2O or 0.845 g Fe(II)Cl2*4H2O and 2.0 g Rongalit C in 400 g water
- Catalyst / activator: EDTA (equimolar to Fe(II)), Na2SO4 (0.12 parts by weight)
- Chain-transfer agent: t-DDM (tertiary dodecyl mercaptan)
- Chain-transfer dosage: 0.54 parts by weight
- Polymerization temperature: 8.0&deg;C &plusmn; 0.5&deg;C
- Pressure: 50 bar to 150 bar (for hydrogenation, if applicable)
- pH: 7.0 &plusmn; 1.0
- Reaction time: 6.5 to 7.0 hours
- Conversion: 84.2% to 86.1% (stopped with diethylhydroxylamine)
- Coagulation conditions: Latex admixed with 50% dispersion of Vulkanox BKF (0.3 wt% on NBR solids) before coagulation
- Post-treatment: Stabilized with Wingstay 29/Naugawhite dispersion (0.18 parts by weight)
- Raw polymer properties: Bound ACN content of 35.3% - 36.6%; Mooney viscosity of 25 - 142 MU (ML 1+4 @ 100&deg;C); Tg of -23.1&deg;C to -25.8&deg;C

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Nitrile rubber G (using FeSO4 premix) achieved 86.1% conversion in 7.0 hours, yielding a polymer with 36% ACN.
- Nitrile rubber H (using FeCl2 premix) achieved 84.2% conversion in 6.5 hours.
- The use of FeCl2 instead of FeSO4 in the redox system resulted in distinct polymer properties, showing the sensitivity of the redox catalyst system.

**Technical Relevance**
Provides a detailed recipe for low-temperature emulsion polymerization of NBR with ~36% ACN using a p-menthane hydroperoxide/Fe(II) redox system to control conversion and molecular weight.

- Emulsifier: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Emulsifier loading: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Initiator: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Initiator loading: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence

- Chain-transfer agent: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence

- Polymerization temperature: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence

- pH: Latex pH adjusted to 5.8 (using HCl) or 8.4 - 9.5 (using KOH) before coagulation
- Reaction time: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Conversion: Not disclosed in the analyzed patent evidence
- Coagulation conditions: Coagulated using NaCl (15-26 wt% salt) or CaCl2 (0.3-1.2 wt% salt) at temperatures of 20&deg;C to 50&deg;C
- Post-treatment: Vulcanized in a 1.5 L laboratory mixer and evaluated for vulcanization rate (t90 - t10) and mechanical properties
- Raw polymer properties: Ion indices (Ca, Na, Mg, K levels); vulcanizates show stress at 30% elongation of 4.2 - 10.2 MPa and elongation at break of 535 - 651%

**Relevant Experimental Evidence**
- Latex pH was adjusted using 5% aqueous KOH or 5% aqueous HCl before coagulation.
- Coagulation with NaCl (e.g., examples C6-C9) required higher salt concentrations (15-26 wt%) and resulted in higher residual sodium levels (up to 960 ppm).
- Coagulation with CaCl2 (e.g., examples C10-C13) required much lower salt concentrations (0.3-1.2 wt%) and resulted in high calcium levels (~1200 ppm) and higher vulcanization rates.

**Technical Relevance**
Demonstrates how pre-coagulation pH adjustment and salt selection (NaCl vs. CaCl2) control the residual ion profile, directly affecting the vulcanization rate and mechanical performance.
