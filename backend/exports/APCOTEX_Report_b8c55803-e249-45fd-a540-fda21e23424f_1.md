# LOW-STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes three primary patents relevant to low-styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) synthesis and use. The target is defined as SBR embodiments with styrene content below conventional commercial grades, emphasizing reduced aromatic monomer fraction and butadiene-biased copolymer composition. Across the selected patents, two major synthesis families appear: aqueous emulsion polymerization for specialized E-SBR and solution/anionic polymerization for functionalized SBR. EP1967387B1 discloses an aqueous emulsion polymerization prepared E-SBR with 12–16% bound styrene, specifically positioned as a low-styrene elastomer for tire sidewall rubber compositions. US7612144B2 focuses on anionic solution polymerization of styrene/butadiene using sulfur-containing initiators, enabling functional head groups and improved filler interaction; the disclosed SBR examples contain about 21.7–22.2% styrene, which is not low-styrene by the target definition but is still relevant for process and end-group control. US8865829B2 discloses solution polymerization of styrene and butadiene followed by functional termination, with a comparative non-functionalized SBR and functionalized SBRs used in silica/carbon black compounds; the polymerization is conducted in hydrocarbon solvent at 65°C with conversion above 98%. Overall, the landscape shows that low-styrene SBR can be achieved through controlled aqueous emulsion polymerization with reduced bound styrene, while solution polymerization patents emphasize chain-end functionality, filler affinity, and rolling-resistance improvements rather than low styrene content itself. Formulation trends include use in tire sidewalls or tread compounds, reinforcement with carbon black and/or silica, and downstream mixing/cure protocols designed to translate polymer microstructure into lower hysteresis and improved dynamic performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP1967387B1 is the only patent that directly discloses a low-styrene SBR target, with 12–16% bound styrene and a preferred 13–15% range; the other two patents disclose SBRs around 21.7–22.2% styrene or do not state a low-styrene embodiment.
- EP1967387B1 uses aqueous emulsion polymerization with an iron/hydroperoxide redox catalyst system, whereas US7612144B2 and US8865829B2 use solution/anionic polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent.
- US7612144B2 and US8865829B2 both emphasize chain-end functionality and filler interaction; US7612144B2 uses sulfur-containing anionic initiators, while US8865829B2 uses functional terminators after living anionic polymerization.
- US7612144B2 discloses styrene contents of 21.7–22.2% in the SBR examples, which is substantially higher than the low-styrene range in EP1967387B1.
- US8865829B2 discloses polymerization at 65°C with conversion above 98% and termination with isopropanol or functional terminators; its compounding studies focus on silica/carbon black reinforcement and hysteresis reduction rather than styrene reduction.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The strongest evidence for low-styrene SBR synthesis is EP1967387B1, which directly discloses an aqueous emulsion polymerization prepared E-SBR with 12–16% bound styrene, preferably 13–15%, and a Tg of -60 to -70°C. The other patents are valuable process references for solution-polymerized SBR and functionalization strategies, but their disclosed SBR examples are not low-styrene embodiments. For low-styrene SBR development, the evidence supports aqueous emulsion polymerization with controlled styrene feed and redox initiation as the most directly relevant route, while solution-polymerization patents suggest complementary approaches for end-group control and filler interaction optimization.
