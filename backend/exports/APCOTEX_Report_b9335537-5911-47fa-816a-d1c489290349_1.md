# HYDROGENATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON LOW-MOONEY HNBR, METATHESIS-BASED MOLECULAR WEIGHT REDUCTION, AND FUNCTIONALIZED HNBR APPLICATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents centered on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) synthesis, molecular-weight control, and downstream formulation use. The target material is defined as nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) that has been partially or fully hydrogenated to reduce residual unsaturation while retaining the nitrile-containing backbone needed for oil, heat, and ozone resistance. Across the selected patents, two major synthetic strategies dominate: (1) post-polymerization hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous catalysts, especially rhodium- and ruthenium-based systems, and (2) metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction of NBR prior to or concurrent with hydrogenation to produce lower-Mooney, narrower-distribution HNBR. Several patents emphasize that conventional HNBR suffers from high Mooney viscosity and broad molecular-weight distribution, motivating catalyst systems and process conditions that lower Mw, PDI, and residual double-bond content without introducing unwanted functional groups or excessive gelation.

The landscape includes patents focused on low molecular weight HNBR obtained by metathesis degradation followed by hydrogenation, as well as a single-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation route using ruthenium or osmium complexes. Other patents address partially hydrogenated nitrile rubbers with a defined ratio of solution viscosity to molecular weight, and microgel-containing vulcanizable compositions based on HNBR for improved high-temperature mechanical performance. A later patent extends HNBR into battery technology by using epoxy-functionalized, optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber as a fluorine-free cathode binder. Across the portfolio, the major process trends are use of homogeneous metal catalysts, optional co-olefins, solvent-based cement processing, controlled hydrogenation levels, and formulation additives such as carbon black, peroxide, unsaturated carboxylic acids, and microgels. The evidence collectively shows that HNBR synthesis is being optimized not only for saturation level, but also for processability, molecular-weight narrowing, and compatibility with demanding end-use applications.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2, US7772328B2, US20130345366A1, and US9150669B2 all target low-Mooney, low-PDI HNBR, but US9150669B2 is the only one explicitly claiming simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis in a single step.
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 emphasize metathesis degradation followed by hydrogenation, with co-olefin use and cement/solvent processing; US20130345366A1 extends this by defining a specific SV/Mw ratio window for partially hydrogenated nitrile rubbers.
- Catalyst families differ across the HNBR synthesis patents: US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 discuss Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson-type hydrogenation catalysts; US20130345366A1 and US9150669B2 focus on ruthenium/osmium metathesis catalysts, including Grubbs-type systems.
- Reaction conditions in the low-MW HNBR patents are broadly similar in using solvent-based cements and elevated temperatures around 80°C to 105°C, but the exact catalyst loadings vary from 0.041 to 0.060 phr in US9150669B2 and 0.05 to 0.25 phr in US7919563B2/US7772328B2.
- US9321910B2 and EP3900086B1 are not synthesis patents; they show how HNBR is leveraged in vulcanizates and battery binders, respectively, highlighting the downstream value of controlled hydrogenation and functionalization.
- EP2057205B1 is the only patent in the set that uses partially hydrogenated nitrile/butadiene rubber as a metathesis reagent for meta-block copolymer formation rather than as the final HNBR product.
- US8957161B2 is a catalyst-additive patent that can support HNBR synthesis indirectly by improving metathesis activity, but it does not itself disclose HNBR product properties.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that the most effective HNBR synthesis strategies in this patent set are metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of NBR followed by hydrogenation, and in one case simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation in a single step. The most useful product targets are low-Mw HNBR with Mw roughly 20,000 to 250,000, Mooney viscosity below about 50, and reduced PDI, with hydrogenation levels commonly in the 50% to 97% range. For practical synthesis, the strongest evidence supports solvent-based cement processing, optional co-olefin use, and ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems; catalyst loading and reaction time must be balanced to avoid gelation while achieving low residual unsaturation. For downstream performance, microgel-containing peroxide-cured formulations improve high-temperature mechanical properties, and functionalized HNBR derivatives can serve as fluorine-free battery binders. Overall, the recommended synthesis direction is controlled metathesis degradation of NBR to lower molecular weight, followed by selective hydrogenation under homogeneous catalytic conditions, with careful control of catalyst type, co-olefin, and hydrogenation extent to preserve processability and mechanical integrity.
