# CARBOXYLATED NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: DIRECT COPOLYMERIZATION AND PROCESS-CONTROLLED LATEX TERMINATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents relevant to carboxylated nitrile rubber (carboxylated NBR) within the 2010–2024 US/EP search scope. The target material is defined by incorporation of carboxyl functionality into the nitrile-butadiene rubber backbone, typically through copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene with an unsaturated carboxylic acid monomer such as methacrylic acid or acrylic acid. The selected patents show two distinct but complementary technology directions: one patent directly discloses a carboxylated NBR synthesis route, while the other is a non-carboxylated styrene-butadiene copolymer patent that is only tangentially related and serves mainly as a contrast case for acid-monomer-free hot polymerization.

US20200283557A1 is the key primary patent for the target compound. It discloses emulsion/latex polymerization of acrylonitrile, 1,3-butadiene, and methacrylic acid in water, using t-dodecyl mercaptan as chain-transfer agent, cumene hydroperoxide as initiator, and a pressure reactor held at 5 °C. The inventive focus is not the carboxylation chemistry itself, but the termination strategy: a nitrous acid salt combined with a water-insoluble hydroquinone is added at a controlled conversion to suppress discoloration and stabilize the latex and final rubber. The examples map the effect of sodium nitrite dosage and hydroquinone co-terminator level on pH, conversion drift, Mooney viscosity drift, discoloration, and scorch stability.

US9815984B2 does not disclose carboxylated NBR synthesis; instead, it teaches hot polymerization of styrene-butadiene copolymers in the absence of acid monomers, followed by sulfur curing and use in asphalt systems. It is included because it provides a useful boundary condition for the search space: hot polymerization without carboxyl monomers is explicitly non-target, but its process parameters and latex handling illustrate how acid-free rubber dispersions are managed in related elastomer technology.

Overall, the patent landscape indicates that the most relevant synthesis route for carboxylated NBR is direct free-radical emulsion polymerization of butadiene/acrylonitrile with a carboxylic acid comonomer, followed by carefully controlled polymerization termination and coagulation. The strongest evidence supports methacrylic-acid-containing formulations, low-temperature initiation around 5 °C, and low-dose nitrite/hydroquinone termination to minimize discoloration while preserving storage stability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US20200283557A1 is a direct carboxylated NBR patent; US9815984B2 is explicitly non-carboxylated and therefore outside the target chemistry.
- US20200283557A1 uses acrylonitrile/butadiene/methacrylic acid monomers, whereas US9815984B2 uses styrene/butadiene only and excludes acid monomers.
- US20200283557A1 is a low-temperature emulsion polymerization at 5 °C with chain-transfer agent and peroxide/redox initiation; US9815984B2 teaches hot polymerization at 40 °C or greater, with some embodiments at 50 °C or greater.
- US20200283557A1 emphasizes polymerization termination control using sodium nitrite plus water-insoluble hydroquinone; US9815984B2 emphasizes sulfur curing and asphalt blending rather than termination chemistry.
- US20200283557A1 reports conversion-dependent termination and latex pH values around 3.8 to 5.6; US9815984B2 reports formulation and performance metrics for asphalt blends, not monomer conversion or coagulation conditions.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports direct free-radical emulsion polymerization of acrylonitrile, butadiene, and a carboxylic acid comonomer, with methacrylic acid clearly demonstrated, as the most relevant route to carboxylated NBR. The strongest process lesson from the target patent is that low-temperature polymerization at 5 °C combined with controlled termination using a nitrous acid salt and a water-insoluble hydroquinone can suppress discoloration and improve storage stability. Based on the disclosed examples, sodium nitrite at 0.025 to 0.15 parts per 100 parts monomer, together with 0.1 to 1.0 part hydroquinone on a monomer basis, is the most evidence-backed termination window. The second patent is not a synthesis route for carboxylated NBR, but it reinforces that acid-free hot polymerization belongs to a different technology class. For carboxylated NBR manufacture, the recommended evidence-based starting point is acrylonitrile/butadiene/methacrylic acid emulsion polymerization at about 5 °C with chain-transfer control and nitrite/hydroquinone termination before coagulation.
