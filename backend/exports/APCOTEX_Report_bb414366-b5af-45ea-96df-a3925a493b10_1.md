# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report evaluates patent evidence relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), defined here as acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymers with reduced acrylonitrile incorporation relative to conventional grades. The required patent manifest contains one primary patent, US11896953B2, which does not disclose NBR elastomer synthesis but instead describes hydrophilic-lipophilic balance (HLB) copolymers for solid phase extraction. It is nevertheless compositionally relevant because it uses acrylonitrile as the hydrophilic monomer in a divinylbenzene-acrylonitrile copolymer and explicitly discusses monomer-ratio control, porogen selection, and reproducibility across a broad range of hydrophobic/hydrophilic feed ratios. The disclosed polymerization approach is a copolymerization of at least one hydrophobic monomer, preferably divinylbenzene, with at least one hydrophilic monomer, preferably acrylonitrile, in the presence of both nonpolar and polar porogens. The patent emphasizes that acrylonitrile is more effectively incorporated across a broader range of monomer ratios than N-vinylpyrrolidone, and that the resulting copolymers can be used in conventional or simplified SPE workflows without conditioning and equilibration steps. Key formulation trends include use of mixed porogens, especially o-xylene with 4-methyl-2-pentanol, and broad hydrophobic-to-hydrophilic monomer ratios from 10:90 to 90:10 wt.%, with a preferred 30:70 to 70:30 wt.% window. Although not a direct low-ACN NBR synthesis disclosure, the patent provides useful evidence on acrylonitrile incorporation control in copolymer systems and on how monomer ratio and porogen environment influence polymer structure and performance.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The available patent evidence does not disclose a direct low-acrylonitrile NBR elastomer synthesis route. The single primary patent instead demonstrates acrylonitrile-containing copolymer formation in an HLB adsorbent context, with explicit control over monomer ratio and porogen environment. For low-ACN NBR development, the most transferable lesson is that acrylonitrile incorporation can be tuned through feed composition and solvent/porogen selection; however, no elastomer-specific polymerization conditions, initiators, or conversion data are provided here. Based on the evidence, the most defensible recommendation is to use controlled copolymer feed ratios and a mixed solvent environment when targeting reduced acrylonitrile incorporation, while recognizing that additional NBR-specific patents would be needed to define practical elastomer synthesis parameters.
