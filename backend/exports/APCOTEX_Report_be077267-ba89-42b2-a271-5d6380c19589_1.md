# PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR HIGH-ACRYLONITRILE NBR SYNTHESIS AND CONTROLLED EMULSION/ORGANIC-SOLVENT POLYMERIZATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents relevant to high-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) synthesis and process control. The target material is a butadiene–acrylonitrile copolymer enriched in acrylonitrile, typically requiring careful monomer feed control, polymerization temperature management, and molecular-weight regulation to achieve the desired high-ACN grade while maintaining processability. EP2478021B1 focuses directly on nitrile rubber production in organic solvents using free-radical polymerization of a conjugated diene, an α,β-unsaturated nitrile, and optional comonomers in the presence of a modifier; the patent emphasizes solution-phase synthesis, RAFT-like control chemistry, and examples using acrylonitrile/butadiene feeds at 37/63 phm under 3 bar and 100°C. It also discloses terpolymer variants incorporating hydroxyethyl acrylate or HDODA and post-polymerization isolation by ethanol precipitation. US7592409B2 is not an NBR manufacturing patent per se, but it is highly relevant because it discloses controlled emulsion polymerization technology that explicitly includes block polymers containing acrylonitrile/methacrylonitrile and conjugated diolefin segments, including acrylonitrile/butadiene-based B blocks. The patent’s core contribution is in-situ emulsifier formation from latent surfactant/surfactant activator pairs, enabling controlled emulsion polymerization with conventional aqueous media, rapid initiation, and stable latex formation. Across the two patents, the main process trends are: solution polymerization in organic solvent with modifier-mediated control for direct NBR synthesis, and controlled emulsion polymerization with in-situ emulsification for architecturally complex acrylonitrile-containing block copolymers. The evidence supports high-ACN NBR synthesis strategies centered on monomer ratio selection, oxygen exclusion, controlled radical initiation, and solvent/emulsion process design rather than hydrogenation or post-modification routes.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2478021B1 is the more direct high-ACN NBR synthesis disclosure: it uses acrylonitrile/butadiene solution polymerization in organic solvent, with explicit monomer feed levels of 37 phm ACN and 63 phm butadiene in Example 1.
- US7592409B2 is broader and more platform-oriented: it does not exemplify NBR synthesis directly, but its claims explicitly include acrylonitrile/methacrylonitrile plus conjugated diolefin blocks, making it relevant to NBR-based block copolymers rather than conventional random NBR.
- EP2478021B1 uses organic-solvent free-radical polymerization with modifier/RAFT-like control chemistry (DoPAT) and initiator Vazo® 88, whereas US7592409B2 centers on controlled emulsion polymerization with in-situ emulsifier formation and controlled radical methods such as RAFT, NMP, ATRP, and DT.
- EP2478021B1 operates at 100°C and 3 bar with polymerization times of 6-9 hours in the examples, while US7592409B2 commonly uses 65-75°C and reaches complete conversion in roughly 1.5-7 hours depending on the control system.
- EP2478021B1 isolates polymer by ethanol precipitation and vacuum drying after solvent polymerization; US7592409B2 generally forms stable latexes and uses stripping/coagulation only in selected examples.
- For high-acrylonitrile NBR, EP2478021B1 provides the clearest evidence for direct composition control, while US7592409B2 provides a useful emulsion-processing framework for acrylonitrile-containing block copolymers and latex-based manufacturing.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates two complementary synthesis directions for high-acrylonitrile NBR-related materials. The most directly relevant route is EP2478021B1, which discloses solution polymerization of acrylonitrile and 1,3-butadiene in DMAc with controlled radical chemistry, oxygen exclusion, 3 bar butadiene feed, and 100°C initiation; this is the strongest patent evidence for direct NBR manufacture with tunable acrylonitrile content. US7592409B2 contributes a broader controlled-emulsion platform that explicitly encompasses acrylonitrile/diolefin block polymers and demonstrates rapid, stable latex formation via in-situ emulsifier generation. Based on the evidence, the recommended synthesis parameters for high-ACN NBR are: acrylonitrile-rich monomer feed control, rigorous oxygen removal, controlled radical initiation, and either organic-solvent solution polymerization for direct NBR or controlled emulsion polymerization when block architecture or latex processing is desired.
