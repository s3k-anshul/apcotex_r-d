# HYDROGENATED NBR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS DEVELOPMENT: HYDROGENATION, METATHESIS, AND LOW-MOONEY ROUTE PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of primary patents addressing hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, process intensification, and property control. The target material is HNBR derived from nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on reduced residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil resistance, and elastomeric performance. Across the selected patents, the landscape spans three major technical directions: direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution or specialized reactors, simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation to lower molecular weight before or during saturation, and post-hydrogenation modification or downstream use in blends and compositions. The patents collectively define HNBR as a partially or fully hydrogenated nitrile rubber with residual double bond content typically in the low single-digit to teens range, while also highlighting the importance of controlling Mooney viscosity, molecular weight, and gel formation.

The dominant polymerization/synthesis approaches include homogeneous hydrogenation with noble-metal catalysts such as rhodium, ruthenium, palladium, and cobalt-based systems; heterogeneous hydrogenation using supported catalysts; and metathesis-assisted routes using ruthenium/osmium carbene catalysts or related catalyst systems. Several patents focus on process engineering rather than catalyst chemistry, including spinning disk reactor hydrogenation and catalyst recovery/removal strategies to improve product aging and color. Others emphasize simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis to achieve low molecular weight HNBR in a single step, with co-olefin-assisted chain scission and narrow molecular weight distributions. A separate patent addresses reduction of nitrile groups in already hydrogenated rubber using Raney cobalt, showing that HNBR can be further functionalized to amino-containing polymers.

Formulation and process trends include solution-phase operation in chlorobenzene or THF/dioxane mixtures, hydrogen pressures from sub-MPa to tens of MPa, temperatures from about 60 to 220°C depending on route, and catalyst loadings reported in phr or ppm. The evidence consistently shows that catalyst choice, catalyst residue control, and reaction-time management are central to balancing hydrogenation degree, molecular-weight retention, and final HNBR performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 both use simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis of NBR with ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems, but EP2473280B1 reports a slightly broader low-Mooney target window (Mooney 1-50, PDI <2.8) and US9150669B2 reports Mooney 1-50 with PDI <3.0.
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 both pursue low-Mooney HNBR via metathesis followed by hydrogenation; US7919563B2 claims PDI <2.5 while US7772328B2 claims PDI <2.5 and a stricter Mooney viscosity of less than 10.
- US7666950B2 is process-engineering focused, using a spinning disk reactor and hydrogen pressures of 3.5-100 bar, whereas the metathesis-based patents rely on ruthenium/osmium catalyst chemistry rather than reactor intensification.
- EP3116916B1 is distinct from the other patents because it does not primarily make HNBR from NBR; instead it reduces nitrile groups on already hydrogenated rubber using Raney cobalt at 170-220°C and 0.5-25 MPa H2.
- EP3827029A1 emphasizes catalyst residue control and aging performance, with Ru/Pd/Rh loadings in the tens to hundreds of ppm, while the other hydrogenation patents focus more on molecular-weight control or reactor/catalyst selection.
- EP2316860B1 addresses precursor NBR synthesis by emulsion polymerization with Fe(II) chloride redox initiation, providing the feedstock platform for downstream HNBR hydrogenation rather than the hydrogenation step itself.
- Across the hydrogenation-focused patents, solution-phase processing is common, but the process windows differ: EP3827029A1 uses 60-200°C and 0.7-15 MPa, EP3116916B1 uses 170-220°C and 0.5-25 MPa, and US7666950B2 broadens hydrogen pressure to 3.5-100 bar in a spinning disk reactor.
- Catalyst systems vary substantially: Grubbs/Hoveyda/Wilkinson-type metathesis catalysts dominate the low-Mooney routes, while noble-metal hydrogenation catalysts and Raney cobalt dominate direct hydrogenation or post-functionalization routes.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that HNBR synthesis is most effectively advanced by two complementary strategies: direct hydrogenation of NBR under carefully controlled catalyst and reactor conditions, and metathesis-assisted routes that lower molecular weight before or during hydrogenation. For low-Mooney HNBR, the strongest evidence supports simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis using ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems, with catalyst loadings in the 0.04-0.08 phr range and reaction times on the order of 1-4 h producing substantial reductions in residual double bonds and molecular weight. For conventional hydrogenation, solution-phase operation with noble-metal catalysts remains important, but catalyst residue control is critical for aging and color stability. Reactor intensification via spinning disk reactors and careful hydrogen-pressure management can reduce equipment burden and improve process economics. If the goal is post-functionalized HNBR, Raney cobalt hydrogenation of nitrile groups is a viable route, but it requires high temperature and pressure and only partial nitrile reduction. Overall, the most evidence-supported synthesis parameters are: controlled NBR precursor composition, low-to-moderate Mooney feedstock, ruthenium-based metathesis/hydrogenation catalysts for low-Mooney products, and rigorous management of catalyst residues when noble-metal hydrogenation is used.
