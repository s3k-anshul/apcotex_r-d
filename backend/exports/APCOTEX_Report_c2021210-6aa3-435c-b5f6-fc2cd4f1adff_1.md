# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOLECULAR-WEIGHT PROCESS PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes five primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on routes that reduce residual unsaturation, improve heat/oil resistance, and control molecular weight and processability. The patent landscape splits into two major themes: (1) direct hydrogenation of NBR using noble-metal catalysts under controlled pressure, temperature, and catalyst loading, and (2) combined metathesis/hydrogenation strategies that reduce molecular weight during or before hydrogenation to obtain lower-Mooney HNBR. Across the set, the target material is consistently defined as an NBR-derived elastomer containing at least one conjugated diene and at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile, with hydrogenation levels typically described as partial to near-complete saturation of the diene-derived double bonds. Several patents explicitly target low residual double bond content (RDB), narrower polydispersity, and lower Mooney viscosity than conventional HNBR grades.

US7951875B2 and EP3827029A1 focus on hydrogenation of NBR in solution using ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium catalysts, with explicit attention to catalyst residue management, aging stability, and product discoloration. EP3827029A1 provides the clearest operating window, specifying catalyst metal contents, hydrogenation temperature, pressure, and time. EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 disclose single-step or coupled metathesis/hydrogenation processes using ruthenium/osmium catalysts, optionally with co-olefins, to simultaneously cleave unsaturation and hydrogenate the polymer, thereby lowering molecular weight and narrowing molecular weight distribution. US8957161B2 is adjacent rather than direct HNBR hydrogenation: it teaches salt-accelerated metathesis of nitrile rubber using ruthenium metathesis catalysts, which is relevant because metathesis degradation is a precursor step used in low-MW HNBR manufacture.

Overall, the evidence indicates that effective HNBR synthesis benefits from solution-phase processing, ruthenium-based catalyst systems, controlled hydrogen pressure and temperature, and deliberate management of catalyst loading and post-reaction purification. For low-MW HNBR, the strongest evidence supports combining metathesis with hydrogenation and using feedstocks with moderate Mooney viscosity to preserve processability while achieving low RDB and reduced molecular weight.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US8957161B2 is a metathesis-acceleration patent for nitrile rubber degradation, while US7951875B2, EP2473280B1, and US9150669B2 integrate metathesis with hydrogenation to directly generate low-MW HNBR.
- EP3827029A1 is the most conventional hydrogenation patent in the set: it uses solution hydrogenation of NBR with Ru/Pd/Rh catalysts and focuses on catalyst residue control rather than metathesis-driven chain scission.
- Catalyst families differ by function: US8957161B2 uses Ru/Os metathesis catalysts plus salts; US7951875B2 and the two US/EP low-MW HNBR patents use Ru/Os metathesis/hydrogenation catalysts; EP3827029A1 uses Ru, Pd, or Rh hydrogenation catalysts.
- Operating windows are most explicit in EP3827029A1 (60 to 200°C, 700000 to 15000000 pascals, 1 to 24 hours) and US7951875B2 (80°C, 500 psi co-olefin, 6.9×10^5 Pa ethylene, 0.05 to 0.25 phr catalyst).
- Property targets converge on low residual unsaturation and improved processability: EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 explicitly target Mw 20,000 to 250,000 and Mooney viscosity 1 to 50, while US7951875B2 reports Mooney values as low as 3 and PDI down to 1.90.
- Co-olefin use is explicit in US7951875B2 and US9150669B2, supporting chain-transfer/metathesis control; EP2473280B1 also allows optional co-olefin, whereas EP3827029A1 does not emphasize co-olefin-assisted chain scission.
- Catalyst residue management is a major differentiator: EP3827029A1 centers on minimizing and removing noble-metal residues, while the metathesis/hydrogenation patents emphasize catalyst choice and reaction design to achieve the desired molecular-weight reduction in situ.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence shows two viable HNBR synthesis strategies: direct solution hydrogenation of NBR with Ru/Pd/Rh catalysts, and single-step or staged metathesis/hydrogenation using Ru/Os catalysts to lower molecular weight while saturating residual double bonds. For conventional HNBR production, EP3827029A1 provides the clearest practical hydrogenation window and catalyst-metal ranges, with strong emphasis on minimizing residual noble metals and preserving aging performance. For low-MW HNBR, EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 provide the strongest evidence that simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation can deliver Mw around 20,000 to 250,000 g/mol, Mooney viscosity in the 1 to 50 range, and low PDI, especially when catalyst identity and co-olefin selection are controlled. US7951875B2 further supports the use of ethylene or 1-hexene, moderate temperature, and low phr catalyst loadings to achieve substantial molecular-weight reduction. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are solution-phase processing, Ru-based catalysts for low-MW routes, hydrogenation temperatures around 60 to 200°C, hydrogen pressures in the 700000 to 15000000 pascal range, and deliberate control of catalyst loading and co-olefin feed to balance hydrogenation rate, residual unsaturation, and polymer integrity.
