# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: EMULSION, SOLUTION, AND FUNCTIONALIZED COPOLYMER ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), with emphasis on explicit low-styrene targets, styrene-content control, and process routes that preserve or improve performance in tire and rubber applications. The target is defined here as SBR with reduced styrene fraction, typically in the low single-digit to low-teens wt% range for emulsion SBR, or as low-to-moderate styrene solution SBR architectures designed to deliver low hysteresis, improved filler interaction, or tailored tread/bead-filler performance. The patent landscape spans two major synthesis families: emulsion polymerization of low-styrene SBR using emulsifier systems tuned to suppress coagulum and improve polymerization rate, and solution polymerization of styrene-butadiene copolymers using organolithium initiation, polar modifiers, and end-group functionalization or chain-end coupling to improve silica/carbon black interaction.

US10407566B2 is the clearest direct low-styrene disclosure, specifying 5-10 wt% styrene and an emulsifier mixture of aliphatic organic acid plus sulfonate-based compound, with Example 1 using 94:6 butadiene:styrene feed at 10°C and 80% conversion. EP2719549B1 and US7763696B2 are solution-polymerization disclosures that are not low-styrene-focused per se, but they provide important control over styrene incorporation, molecular weight, and microstructure through TMEDA/n-BuLi systems, continuous reactors, and salt-assisted cyclic-vinyl formation. US6313232B1 shows continuous solution SBR with midstream and post-reactor tin addition to reduce Payne effect and hysteresis. US20110112212A1 is a tire composition patent using modified SBR and modified BR with silica/carbon black blends, relevant to low-styrene SBR because it demonstrates how modified SBR grades are deployed in tread formulations. EP3732243A1 discloses low molecular weight tapered SBR with 20-30 wt% styrene and controlled styrene sequencing, while US8492479B2 teaches polyether-block functionalized diene copolymers, including SBR, for improved filler interaction and hysteresis reduction.

Overall, the evidence supports low-styrene SBR synthesis by emulsion polymerization with carefully balanced emulsifiers, or by solution polymerization with controlled monomer feed, polar modifier, and post-polymer functionalization to compensate for reduced styrene content in final tire performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10407566B2 is the only patent in the set that explicitly targets low styrene SBR at 5-10 wt% styrene; the others mainly use moderate styrene levels or focus on functionalization rather than low styrene content.
- US10407566B2 uses emulsion polymerization with an aliphatic organic acid/sulfonate emulsifier pair, whereas EP2719549B1, US7763696B2, US6313232B1, EP3732243A1, and US8492479B2 rely on solution polymerization or anionic living-polymer routes.
- US10407566B2 emphasizes latex coagulation and washing after emulsion polymerization; the solution patents emphasize reactor residence time, organolithium initiation, polar modifiers, and post-polymer end-group modification or coupling.
- US7763696B2 and EP2719549B1 both use organolithium/TMEDA-type solution polymerization, but US7763696B2 uniquely adds alkali metal salts to raise cyclic vinyl content, while EP2719549B1 focuses on ES/CTES end-group functionalization.
- US6313232B1 differs from the others by using staged tin addition during and after continuous polymerization to reduce Payne effect and hysteresis rather than directly controlling styrene content.
- EP3732243A1 specifies a tapered SBR with 20-30 wt% styrene and low molecular weight, which is higher in styrene than the low-styrene target but relevant for sequencing control and oil-replacement behavior.
- US8492479B2 and EP2719549B1 both aim to improve filler interaction through functionalization; US8492479B2 uses polyether blocks linked via silicon chemistry, while EP2719549B1 uses ES/CTES chain-end modification.
- Across the solution patents, temperatures cluster around 40-80 °C, with continuous or batch reactors, organolithium initiators, and hydrocarbon solvents being common; emulsion low-styrene SBR is instead run at low temperature with emulsifier optimization.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two practical synthesis directions for low styrene SBR. The most direct route is emulsion polymerization as disclosed in US10407566B2, where 5-10 wt% styrene, a 94:6 butadiene:styrene feed in Example 1, and a mixed aliphatic organic acid/sulfonate emulsifier system gave low coagulum and acceptable polymerization time. For solution-based routes, the patents show that low-styrene or styrene-controlled SBR can be made by organolithium polymerization with polar modifiers, continuous reactors, and post-polymer functionalization to recover filler interaction and hysteresis performance. Recommended evidence-based parameters are: styrene content near 5-10 wt% for true low-styrene emulsion SBR; low-temperature emulsion polymerization around 10 °C with emulsifier blending; or, for solution SBR, 40-80 °C polymerization with organolithium initiation, TMEDA or similar polar agents, and optional end-group functionalization or tin/silicon modification to improve tire-relevant properties.
