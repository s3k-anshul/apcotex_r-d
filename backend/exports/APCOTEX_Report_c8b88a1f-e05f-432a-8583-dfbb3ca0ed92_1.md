# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) synthesis and use, with emphasis on compositions and processing routes that preserve rubber-grade performance while reducing acrylonitrile content relative to conventional higher-ACN grades. The patent set does not disclose a dedicated low-ACN NBR polymerization process; instead, it shows how NBR is selected, blended, or processed in end-use formulations where acrylonitrile content is controlled as a material property. EP3828231A1 discloses a halogen-free cable sheath composition based on an EVA/NBR/AEM blend, explicitly allowing NBR grades with acrylonitrile contents of 31-35 wt%, 36-41 wt%, or 42-46 wt%, thereby covering a lower-ACN rubber window within a performance-driven cable formulation. US10385647B2 focuses on swellable packer elastomers and uses a nitrile elastomer together with butadiene rubber, sulfur cure, silica, and powder super absorbent polymer; the examples emphasize high-temperature/high-pressure brine swelling, and the disclosure notes a hydrogenated nitrile elastomer with 70-91% hydrogenation and Mooney viscosity ML(1+4) of 47-72, but it is not a low-ACN synthesis patent. US10731020B2 is a vulcanization-molding rubber composition patent centered on hollow particles and base rubber selection; it lists nitrile rubber among many possible base rubbers and specifies a base-rubber Mooney viscosity range of 5-90 at 100°C, again without a dedicated NBR polymerization route. Across the set, the dominant trends are formulation-based performance tuning, use of polar elastomer blends, and processability control via viscosity, filler loading, and cure conditions rather than monomer-feed synthesis. For low-ACN NBR development, the evidence supports selecting NBR grades in the 31-41 wt% acrylonitrile range when balancing oil resistance, flexibility, and cold performance, then optimizing downstream compounding and cure to maintain mechanical integrity.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP3828231A1 is the only patent that explicitly defines NBR by acrylonitrile-content windows, including 31-35 wt% and 36-41 wt% ACN, which are the closest disclosures to a low-ACN NBR target.
- US10385647B2 and US10731020B2 use nitrile rubber as a formulation component but do not disclose a dedicated acrylonitrile-content range for NBR synthesis; their focus is on end-use performance and processing.
- EP3828231A1 is a halogen-free cable composition with EVA/NBR/AEM blending and high flame-retardant filler loading, whereas US10385647B2 is a swellable packer elastomer with powder SAP and US10731020B2 is a hollow-particle lightweight molding composition.
- US10385647B2 discloses the most detailed cure/service conditions: 330°F cure at 70 psi for 2.5 hours, then brine exposure at 275°F and about 300 psi, with performance targets of at least 5000 psi differential pressure.
- US10731020B2 emphasizes base-rubber Mooney viscosity control (5-90 at 100°C) and hollow-particle further expansion ratio (20-80%), showing a processing-driven rather than composition-driven approach to rubber performance.
- Across the three patents, the recurring formulation trend is blending NBR with other polar or functional components to balance oil resistance, flexibility, swelling, or dimensional stability rather than altering NBR monomer feed during polymerization.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is most clearly represented here as a selectable commercial grade rather than as a newly synthesized polymer. The strongest direct guidance comes from EP3828231A1, which explicitly includes NBR grades at 31-35 wt% and 36-41 wt% acrylonitrile, supporting low-ACN selection for halogen-free cable compounds. US10385647B2 and US10731020B2 reinforce the importance of downstream compounding: nitrile elastomers perform well when paired with appropriate fillers, cure systems, SAP or hollow-particle additives, and tightly controlled viscosity and thermal processing. Based on the disclosed evidence, a practical low-ACN NBR strategy is to select NBR in the 31-41 wt% acrylonitrile range, then tune blend ratios, filler loading, and cure conditions to preserve oil resistance, flexibility, and processability in the final rubber article.
