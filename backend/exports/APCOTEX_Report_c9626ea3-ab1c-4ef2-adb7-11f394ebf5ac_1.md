# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR COMPOSITION CONTROL, MOLECULAR-WEIGHT REDUCTION, AND DOWNSTREAM FORMULATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile-rubber-derived materials. The target material is defined as NBR with reduced acrylonitrile content relative to standard grades, while retaining elastomeric performance and processability. Across the selected patents, the landscape spans three main technical directions: direct copolymer composition design, post-polymerization molecular-weight reduction, and formulation engineering for downstream use. EP3063192B1 and EP2463335B1 are the most composition-centric disclosures, describing nitrile-containing copolymers or nitrile rubber compositions with explicit acrylonitrile ranges and additional monomer/filler design intended to balance oil resistance, low-temperature flexibility, and gasoline permeation resistance. EP2145681B1 and EP2473279B1 focus on metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber, using ruthenium/osmium catalyst systems and, in one case, a boron fluoride additive system, to generate lower-MW nitrile rubbers or HNBR precursors with improved processability. EP2492306B1 addresses cross-linkable nitrile rubber compositions, emphasizing master-batch preparation of aromatic polyvalent amine crosslinkers to improve dispersion and suppress foaming during steam crosslinking. US10385647B2 is not a synthesis patent for NBR itself, but it is relevant as a formulation patent using nitrile elastomer/HNBR in swellable packers, showing how nitrile-rubber-based compositions are tuned with powder superabsorbent polymers, silica, sulfur cure systems, and brine/temperature-resistant processing windows. Overall, the evidence indicates that low-ACN NBR development is driven by careful monomer-ratio control, selective hydrogenation where needed, and downstream compounding strategies that preserve oil resistance while improving flexibility, viscosity, and application-specific durability. The most directly relevant synthesis parameters are acrylonitrile contents in the low-to-moderate range, butadiene-rich backbones, and process routes that avoid excessive degradation while enabling manageable molecular weight and cure behavior.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP3063192B1 and EP2463335B1 are the most composition-focused disclosures: EP3063192B1 allows 10 to 60 wt% acrylonitrile with optional acrylate termonomers, while EP2463335B1 uses 25 to 60 wt% acrylonitrile plus 0 to 30 wt% cationic monomer and laminar filler for gasoline-barrier performance.
- EP2145681B1 and EP2473279B1 both use metathesis to reduce nitrile-rubber molecular weight, but EP2473279B1 explicitly combines metathesis with hydrogenation in monochlorobenzene and reports low-Mw HNBR values, whereas EP2145681B1 emphasizes a catalyst system containing metathesis catalyst plus BF3-type additive complexes.
- EP2473279B1 discloses the most explicit low-MW product data among the degradation patents, with Example 5 giving Mn 8,500-9,300 g/mol and Mw 12,000-13,700 g/mol, while Example 7 gives Mn 19,000 g/mol and Mw 34,000 g/mol.
- EP2492306B1 is distinct from the others because it is a compounding/crosslinking patent rather than a polymerization patent; its key innovation is master-batch dispersion of aromatic polyvalent amine crosslinkers at 130°C to suppress foaming.
- US10385647B2 is formulation-driven and uses nitrile elastomer/HNBR as a swellable-packers base, with powder SAP loadings of 125 to 225 phr and service conditions of 200°F to 275°F and up to 5000 psi, showing downstream application requirements rather than synthesis conditions.
- Across the set, acrylonitrile content windows remain broad in the composition patents, but the process patents focus on lowering molecular weight and viscosity to improve processability without sacrificing nitrile-rubber performance.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR development is best approached through controlled copolymer composition, followed by process or formulation steps that preserve elastomeric performance while improving processability and end-use durability. The most directly useful synthesis windows are acrylonitrile contents in the low-to-moderate range, especially 10 to 60 wt% depending on the application, with butadiene-rich backbones and, where needed, selective hydrogenation. For lower viscosity and better handling, metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction is strongly supported, particularly when paired with hydrogenation and solvent systems such as monochlorobenzene. For practical compounding, master-batch dispersion of crosslinkers and carefully selected filler/SAP systems are important to avoid foaming, leaching, and poor cure behavior. Based on the evidence, a recommended low-ACN NBR strategy is to target acrylonitrile in the lower end of the disclosed windows, maintain butadiene as the major comonomer, and use controlled post-polymerization molecular-weight reduction only as needed to reach processable Mooney viscosity while preserving oil resistance and cure response.
