# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR): PATENT LANDSCAPE ON HYDROGENATION, LOW-MOONEY GRADES, AND CATALYST-RESIDUE CONTROL

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes six primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), focusing on how NBR precursors are transformed into hydrogenated derivatives with reduced residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil/ozone resistance, and better processability. The landscape splits into two major technical directions: (1) direct hydrogenation of NBR using noble-metal catalysts under controlled pressure, temperature, and catalyst loading, and (2) simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation routes designed to lower molecular weight and Mooney viscosity in a single step. A third theme is catalyst-residue management, where the objective is not only to hydrogenate efficiently but also to minimize residual Ru/Rh/Pd that can impair ageing, discoloration, and gel stability.

US20210340285A1 and EP3827029A1 disclose a solution hydrogenation process for NBR using ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium compounds, with explicit operating windows of 60 to 200°C, 700,000 to 15,000,000 Pa, and 1 to 24 hours. These patents emphasize that catalyst amount and post-reaction residue control are central to achieving HNBR with improved ageing properties. US7951875B2 and EP2473281B1 instead target low-molecular-weight HNBR via metathesis-assisted degradation/hydrogenation, using ruthenium-based catalyst systems and co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene; the disclosed examples show substantial reductions in Mw, Mn, PDI, and Mooney viscosity. US8957161B2 is related more indirectly: it teaches salt-accelerated metathesis of nitrile rubber using Ru catalysts, which is relevant as a precursor degradation strategy for HNBR feedstocks. US9150669B2 further develops the simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation concept and demonstrates that catalyst choice strongly affects hydrogenation rate and final polymer properties.

Across the patents, the dominant process trends are solution-phase operation, ruthenium-centered catalyst chemistry, use of co-olefins to drive metathesis, and explicit attention to residual double bond content and catalyst residues. The evidence supports a synthesis strategy combining controlled hydrogenation conditions with catalyst systems that either preserve polymer integrity or intentionally reduce molecular weight before/while hydrogenating.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US8957161B2 focuses on salt-accelerated Ru-catalyzed metathesis of nitrile rubber, whereas US7951875B2, EP2473281B1, and US9150669B2 use metathesis/hydrogenation to directly generate low-MW HNBR.
- US20210340285A1 and EP3827029A1 are the only patents in the set that explicitly define direct hydrogenation windows for NBR to HNBR: 60 to 200°C, 700,000 to 15,000,000 Pa, and 1 to 24 hours, with Ru/Pd/Rh catalyst loadings in the ppm range.
- US7951875B2 and US9150669B2 both disclose catalyst screening at 65.6 ppm metal content and show that catalyst identity strongly affects RDB decay, Mw, Mn, PDI, and Mooney viscosity after 240 min.
- EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 both claim low-MW HNBR with Mw 20,000 to 250,000, Mooney 1 to 50, and PDI below 3.0; EP2473281B1 reports a preferred feed Mooney of 28 to 40, while US9150669B2 emphasizes the same product window but with a single-step simultaneous process.
- The metathesis-based patents rely on ruthenium/osmium catalyst families and optionally co-olefins; US7951875B2 explicitly uses ethylene at 6.9×10^5 Pa and 1-hexene, while the other metathesis patents describe co-olefin use more generally.
- Catalyst-residue and ageing concerns are central in US20210340285A1 and EP3827029A1, which link noble-metal content to discoloration and ageing performance, whereas the metathesis patents prioritize molecular-weight reduction and residual double bond control.
- Across the set, the most consistent process trend is solution-phase processing with ruthenium-centered chemistry; the most consistent product trend is lowering residual unsaturation while reducing Mooney viscosity and narrowing molecular-weight distribution.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two complementary synthesis strategies for HNBR: direct solution hydrogenation of NBR with controlled Ru/Pd/Rh loading and temperature/pressure windows, and simultaneous metathesis-hydrogenation to produce low-MW, low-Mooney HNBR in a single step. For industrially robust HNBR synthesis, the strongest evidence favors solution hydrogenation at 60 to 200°C and 700,000 to 15,000,000 Pa with ppm-level noble-metal catalysts when ageing stability and catalyst-residue control are priorities. When low viscosity and improved processability are the main targets, the metathesis/hydrogenation patents indicate that ruthenium-based catalyst systems, optionally with co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, can reduce Mw into the 20,000 to 250,000 range and Mooney viscosity into the 1 to 50 range while maintaining low residual double bond content. The most defensible recommended synthesis parameters from the evidence are: solution-phase operation, Ru-centered catalyst systems in the tens of ppm range, hydrogenation at 60 to 200°C under 0.7 to 15 MPa, and, for low-MW grades, simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation with co-olefin assistance.
