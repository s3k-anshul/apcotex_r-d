# HYDROGENATED NBR PATENTS: SYNTHESIS, HYDROGENATION CONTROL, AND FORMULATION TRENDS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a patent landscape centered on hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR) and closely related hydrogenated nitrile rubber materials, with emphasis on precursor composition, hydrogenation severity, catalyst choice, and downstream compounding. The target material is defined as NBR-derived copolymers in which a substantial fraction of butadiene unsaturation is hydrogenated to improve heat, ozone, oil, and fuel resistance while retaining processability and mechanical integrity. Across the selected patents, the dominant technical themes are: (i) direct hydrogenation of NBR in solution or latex using noble-metal catalysts, (ii) simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis routes to reduce molecular weight and narrow molecular-weight distribution, (iii) control of residual unsaturation to balance cure response and aging, and (iv) formulation of HNBR into vulcanizates, hoses, seals, and nanocomposites for demanding automotive and oilfield uses.

The landscape includes early application-driven disclosures such as fuel-hose internal layers based on partially hydrogenated NBR with at least 50% hydrogenation of diene units, and later process patents focused on catalyst efficiency and catalyst-residue management. One patent discloses low-molecular-weight HNBR produced by simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis using ruthenium/osmium catalysts, with catalyst loading, hydrogen pressure, and reaction time explicitly controlled. Another patent targets hydrogenation in the presence of Ru, Pd, or Rh compounds and specifies catalyst ppm ranges, temperature, pressure, and time to improve aging properties while avoiding post-hydrogenation catalyst-removal steps. Formulation-oriented patents show that HNBR performance can be tuned with sulfur/tellurium vulcanization systems, PVC or liquid NBR blending, reactive silicone oils, and functionalized fillers. Overall, the evidence indicates that the most important synthesis variables for HNBR are hydrogenation degree, catalyst identity and loading, solvent-phase processing, and post-reaction purification or compounding strategy.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US4486480A and US5569719A both emphasize partially hydrogenated NBR with high diene saturation, but US4486480A focuses on fuel-hose durability while US5569719A focuses on vulcanizate heat build-up and cure promotion.
- US7951875B2 and US9150669B2 both target low-molecular-weight HNBR, but US7951875B2 uses simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis with explicit catalyst loading and co-olefin conditions, whereas US9150669B2 emphasizes alternative Ru/Os catalyst systems and reports lower Mw/PDI outcomes.
- EP3827029A1 is the most catalyst-residue-focused hydrogenation patent, specifying Ru/Pd/Rh ppm windows and broad temperature/pressure/time ranges, while US4486480A and US5569719A are more application/formulation oriented.
- US5569719A and EP2660285B1 both connect hydrogenated nitrile rubber to compounding performance; US5569719A uses sulfur/tellurium cure chemistry, whereas EP2660285B1 uses reactive silicone oils, polyamine/peroxide crosslinking, and a prior hydrogenation step with Pd catalyst.
- EP2660285B1 and US11131058B2 both involve carboxylated nitrile systems, but EP2660285B1 uses carboxylated highly saturated nitrile rubber as a final elastomer, while US11131058B2 uses carboxy-modified hydrogenated NBR latex as an adhesive component.
- US10465064B2 and US10731020B2 are downstream application patents rather than synthesis patents; both include HNBR as a usable elastomer, but US10465064B2 targets nanocomposite wear/high-temperature performance and US10731020B2 targets lightweight molded products with hollow particles.
- EP4215556A4 is the least experimentally detailed of the set in the provided evidence, but its title indicates liquid HNBR and likely overlaps with the low-Mw direction seen in US7951875B2 and US9150669B2.
- Across the set, the recurring synthesis levers are hydrogenation degree, catalyst identity/loading, solvent-phase processing, and post-hydrogenation compounding to balance residual unsaturation, cure response, and aging resistance.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that hydrogenated NBR synthesis is best controlled by managing hydrogenation degree, catalyst system, and molecular-weight retention. For direct HNBR production, the strongest process evidence supports solution-phase hydrogenation with noble-metal catalysts at roughly 60 to 200°C and elevated hydrogen pressure, while low-molecular-weight HNBR can be obtained by combining hydrogenation with metathesis using Ru/Os catalysts and carefully tuned catalyst loading. For application performance, the most useful HNBR targets are high diene saturation with residual unsaturation low enough to improve heat, ozone, and oil resistance but not so low that cure becomes difficult; the patents suggest that partial hydrogenation levels around 50% or higher are a minimum practical threshold, with much higher hydrogenation often preferred. Recommended evidence-based synthesis parameters are: solution hydrogenation, noble-metal catalyst ppm in the tens to low hundreds, hydrogen pressure in the MPa range, temperature around 50 to 200°C depending on catalyst, and reaction times from 1 to 24 hours. Downstream, sulfur/tellurium cure systems, reactive silicone oils, and functionalized fillers are effective routes to convert HNBR into durable hoses, seals, and nanocomposites.
