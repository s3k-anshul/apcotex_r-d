# POLYMERIZATION / SYNTHESIS PATENT RESEARCH REPORT
## Low Acrylonitrile NBR

### 1. Abstract
The patent landscape for Low Acrylonitrile NBR (Nitrile Butadiene Rubber) reveals a diverse range of synthesis and polymerization methods. The extracted data from multiple patents demonstrates various approaches to producing NBR with low acrylonitrile content. Key parameters such as conversion rates, temperatures, and emulsifier concentrations are highlighted across different examples. Recurring variables include acrylonitrile content, styrene concentration, and reaction temperatures. Trends indicate a focus on optimizing reaction conditions to achieve high conversion rates and desirable material properties. The patents also showcase different jurisdictions, including the US and EP, with publication years ranging from 2008 to 2017.

### 2. Research Scope & Selection Method
The patents included in this report were selected based on their direct relevance to the synthesis and polymerization of Low Acrylonitrile NBR. Only patents that demonstrated explicit methods for producing NBR with low acrylonitrile content were considered. The selection process involved a thorough review of patent titles, abstracts, and example descriptions to ensure that the included patents met the specified criteria.

### 4. Cross-Patent Comparison
Comparing the ranges of key parameters across the different patents:
- Acrylonitrile content: 3.0% (US8299175B2) to 45% (EP2463335B1)
- Conversion rates: 45% (EP3239198B1) to 98% (EP2463335B1)
- Reaction temperatures: 1°C (US10208151B2) to 48°C (US8299175B2)
- Emulsifier concentrations: 0.1% (US8299175B2) to 45% (EP3239198B1)

### 5. Key Technical Trends
Technical trends across the analyzed patents include:
- Optimization of reaction conditions to achieve high conversion rates and desirable material properties
- Variation in acrylonitrile content to produce Low Acrylonitrile NBR
- Use of different emulsifiers and concentrations to control reaction kinetics
- Employment of various temperatures and reaction times to influence polymer properties
