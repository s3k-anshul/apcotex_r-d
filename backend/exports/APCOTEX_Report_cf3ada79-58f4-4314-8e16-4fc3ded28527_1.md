# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR CONTROLLED COPOLYMERIZATION, SOLVENT POLYMERIZATION, AND NBR-CONTAINING FORMULATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys a five-patent landscape relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related NBR-containing materials. The target is defined as NBR grades with reduced acrylonitrile content, typically below about 20 wt% ACN, where monomer ratio control, feed strategy, and polymerization conditions are used to tune composition, molecular weight, and processability. The selected patents span direct NBR synthesis in organic solvent, emulsion-based grafting and SAN/ABS-type systems that incorporate butadiene-acrylonitrile rubber domains, and formulation patents that use carboxyl-terminated butadiene-acrylonitrile rubber as a toughener in epoxy composites. Although not all patents are dedicated to low-ACN NBR, they collectively disclose the process levers most relevant to controlling acrylonitrile incorporation and downstream performance.

US9023914B2 is the most directly relevant synthesis disclosure: it describes free-radical solution polymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile in organic solvent with modifier substances, using explicit monomer ratios, initiator loading, temperature, pressure, reaction time, and precipitation isolation. The examples show a 37/63 phm ACN/butadiene feed, 100 °C polymerization, 3 bar butadiene addition, and 9 h reaction time, with variants adjusting modifier and solvent to tune molecular weight and conversion. US9624333B2 discloses emulsion graft copolymerization of styrene/acrylonitrile onto agglomerated butadiene rubber substrates, with explicit SAN feed composition, temperatures, initiator addition, and precipitation conditions; it is relevant as a process analogue for acrylonitrile incorporation control in rubber-rich systems. US9909003B2 and US10947337B2 are formulation patents that explicitly use butadiene acrylonitrile rubber or CTBN-type tougheners in epoxy systems, showing how NBR chemistry is leveraged in composite and adhesive applications. US11813892B2 is less directly related to NBR, but it provides controlled radical polymerization and monomer-functionalization concepts that are useful as broader process references for architecture control.

Overall, the evidence supports solution or emulsion copolymerization with careful monomer feed control, moderate-to-high temperatures, and chain-transfer/modifier management as the main routes to low-ACN NBR. The most actionable synthesis parameters are those in US9023914B2, while the other patents mainly inform downstream use and formulation compatibility.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9023914B2 is the only patent in the set that directly discloses NBR synthesis; it uses solution free-radical copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene in organic solvent, whereas the other patents are mainly formulation or graft-copolymer documents.
- The acrylonitrile feed in US9023914B2 is explicitly 37 phm in Example 1, with butadiene at 63 phm; this is a relatively butadiene-rich feed, but still not clearly in the low-ACN range by the target definition.
- US9023914B2 uses dimethylacetamide as solvent, Vazo® 88 initiator, DoPAT modifier, 100° C. polymerization temperature, 3 bar butadiene pressure, and 9 h reaction time; these are the most actionable synthesis conditions in the set.
- US9624333B2 discloses aqueous emulsion grafting of styrene/acrylonitrile onto agglomerated butadiene rubber, with potassium persulfate initiation and 68° C. to 80° C. processing; it is process-adjacent rather than a direct NBR recipe.
- US9909003B2 and US10947337B2 both use butadiene acrylonitrile rubber or CTBN-type tougheners in epoxy formulations, showing downstream compatibility of NBR-derived elastomers but not monomer-feed control for low-ACN synthesis.
- US11813892B2 is mechanistically unrelated to NBR but provides controlled radical polymerization concepts (RAFT, chain-transfer control) that are relevant as broader synthesis tools for architecture control.
- Across the set, the recurring process themes are monomer ratio control, temperature control, and molecular-weight management; however, only US9023914B2 explicitly couples these to NBR preparation.
- Coagulation/precipitation is explicitly disclosed in US9023914B2 (ethanol precipitation) and US9624333B2 (magnesium sulfate precipitation), while the epoxy patents focus on curing and composite fabrication rather than polymer isolation.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence indicates that the most credible route to low-acrylonitrile NBR is controlled free-radical copolymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile, preferably in solution when precise molecular-weight and architecture control are needed. US9023914B2 provides the strongest direct guidance: acrylonitrile/butadiene feed control, 100° C. polymerization, inerting, modifier-assisted chain control, and precipitation isolation. For low-ACN targeting, the evidence supports reducing acrylonitrile feed below the 37 phm example level while maintaining the same process discipline around temperature, pressure, and modifier loading. The remaining patents are valuable mainly as application and process analogues: US9624333B2 for emulsion grafting and acrylonitrile incorporation in rubber-rich systems, and US9909003B2/US10947337B2 for downstream use of NBR-derived tougheners in epoxy composites. Recommended synthesis parameters based on the evidence are solution polymerization in an oxygen-free organic solvent, moderate-to-high temperature around 100° C., controlled butadiene feed under pressure, and explicit monomer-ratio adjustment to lower acrylonitrile content while monitoring conversion and molecular weight.
