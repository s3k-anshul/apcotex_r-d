# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: COMPOSITION CONTROL, HYDROGENATION, AND HIGH-PERFORMANCE RUBBER FORMULATIONS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile elastomer systems. The target is interpreted as NBR compositions with reduced acrylonitrile content relative to conventional grades, while preserving oil resistance, cold resistance, processability, and mechanical performance. Across the selected patents, the disclosed chemistry spans both direct NBR copolymerization and post-polymerization hydrogenation to highly saturated nitrile rubbers (HSNBR/HNBR-like materials), with acrylonitrile contents explicitly ranging from about 14 wt% to 36 wt% in the examples and claims. The landscape shows two major formulation directions: (1) composition tuning of the rubber backbone through monomer-feed control and hydrogenation to achieve a desired nitrile content and iodine value, and (2) reinforcement/crosslinking packages that compensate for lower nitrile content or altered saturation by using silica, zinc dimethacrylate, fillers, plasticizers, polyamine crosslinkers, and in one case cellulose nanofiber or powder super absorbent polymer additives.

EP3187530B1 emphasizes highly saturated nitrile rubber containing 5 to 50 wt% nitrile monomer units and a low-iodine backbone, then reinforces it with cellulose nanofiber master batches and polyamine crosslinking. US8133948B2 focuses on highly saturated nitrile rubber with 10 to 60 wt% nitrile units, especially 15 to 55 wt%, combined with metallic carboxylate salts and treated silica to improve heat resistance and tensile stress. EP3351591B1 discloses a family of nitrile-group-containing highly saturated copolymer rubbers with 14 to 20.5 wt% nitrile units in the composition claims, prepared by emulsion polymerization followed by hydrogenation, then formulated with fillers and plasticizers for cold/oil resistance. US10385647B2 is not a synthesis patent for NBR, but it is relevant as a formulation patent showing that nitrile elastomers, including hydrogenated nitrile elastomers, can be used with powder SAPs to achieve high swell performance under brine and high-temperature conditions. Overall, the evidence supports low-ACN NBR design through precise monomer ratio control, hydrogenation level control, and downstream compounding choices rather than a single synthesis route.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP3187530B1 and EP3351591B1 both use emulsion polymerization followed by Pd-catalyzed hydrogenation, but EP3187530B1 centers on a highly saturated nitrile rubber with 35.6 wt% acrylonitrile units in the example, whereas EP3351591B1 explicitly targets lower nitrile contents such as 15 to 24 wt% in the synthesis examples and claims 14 to 20.5 wt%.
- US8133948B2 and EP3351591B1 both define highly saturated nitrile rubbers with iodine value limits, but US8133948B2 is broader on nitrile content (10 to 60 wt%) and emphasizes silica/zinc dimethacrylate reinforcement, while EP3351591B1 emphasizes composition control and blending of different rubbers to tune cold/oil resistance.
- EP3187530B1 uses cellulose nanofiber master batches and a polyamine crosslinker (2,2-bis[4-(4-aminophenoxy)phenyl]propane) with DBU, whereas US8133948B2 uses zinc dimethacrylate, silica, zinc oxide, and organic peroxide crosslinking.
- Hydrogenation conditions are similar in EP3187530B1 and EP3351591B1: both use 3 MPa hydrogen pressure, 50°C, and 6 hours with palladium catalyst, but EP3351591B1 varies palladium loading from 1,000 to 650 ppm to modulate iodine value from 10 to 75.
- US10385647B2 is formulation-focused rather than synthesis-focused; it uses nitrile elastomers, including hydrogenated nitrile elastomers with 70 to 91% hydrogenation, together with powder SAPs at 125 to 210 phr to achieve swell performance in brine, which is orthogonal but relevant to end-use compounding of low-ACN nitrile systems.
- Across the patents, lower acrylonitrile content is associated with improved cold resistance but requires compensating formulation strategies: reinforcement fibers in EP3187530B1, silica/zinc dimethacrylate in US8133948B2, and filler/plasticizer balancing in EP3351591B1.
- Coagulation and isolation methods vary: EP3187530B1 uses sodium chloride or magnesium sulfate coagulation of latex master batches, while EP3351591B1 uses methanol coagulation and vacuum drying after hydrogenation.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR and related highly saturated nitrile rubbers are best approached through controlled emulsion copolymerization, followed where needed by hydrogenation to reduce unsaturation while preserving nitrile functionality. The most directly relevant composition window is EP3351591B1, which explicitly centers on 14 to 20.5 wt% nitrile units and demonstrates practical synthesis examples at 15 to 24 wt% acrylonitrile. EP3187530B1 and US8133948B2 support the broader design space for highly saturated nitrile rubbers, showing that performance can be recovered through reinforcement and crosslinking packages even when nitrile content is adjusted. For low-ACN synthesis, the evidence favors emulsion polymerization at about 5°C with controlled monomer feed, mercaptan chain transfer, and Pd hydrogenation at 3 MPa and 50°C for 6 hours, with nitrile content tuned near 15 to 20 wt% where cold resistance is prioritized. For end-use formulations, silica, zinc dimethacrylate, plasticizers, and appropriate crosslinking systems are important to maintain tensile, heat, and oil resistance.
