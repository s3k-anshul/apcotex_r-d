# CARBOXYLATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR SYNTHESIS AND PROCESSING

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes two primary patents relevant to carboxylated nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), focusing on both direct synthesis of carboxyl group-containing nitrile rubber and downstream use of carboxylated NBR in heat-activable adhesive systems. EP3604355A1 discloses a solvent-based polymerization route in which a monomer mixture containing an α,β-ethylenically unsaturated nitrile monomer and a carboxyl group-containing monomer is polymerized, then terminated with a specific combination of a nitrous acid salt and a water-insoluble hydroquinone before coagulation. The key technical objective is to obtain carboxylated nitrile rubber with reduced discoloration and stable storage behavior, while maintaining effective polymerization termination at low terminator dosage. US20100147462A1 is not a synthesis patent per se, but it is highly relevant because it uses acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymers as the elastomeric component in heat-activable adhesive tapes for flexible printed circuit boards. It confirms the industrial importance of carboxylated NBR and provides formulation constraints that preserve flexibility, solvent processability, and high-temperature bond performance. Across the two patents, the dominant process themes are copolymerization with carboxyl-containing monomers, solvent/emulsion-style rubber production, controlled termination/coagulation, and formulation of carboxylated NBR with epoxy resins for thermally activated bonding. The evidence indicates that methacrylic acid or related carboxyl monomers are preferred for introducing functionality, while nitrous acid salt/hydroquinone termination chemistry is used to suppress discoloration and viscosity drift. In application-oriented formulations, the carboxylated NBR fraction is used as the reactive elastomeric hardener for epoxy resin, with acrylonitrile content in the 15% to 50% by weight range and the adhesive designed to crosslink above 150°C. Overall, the patents show that carboxylated NBR is valued both as a directly synthesized functional rubber and as a reactive polar elastomer for high-adhesion, heat-resistant adhesive systems.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP3604355A1 is a direct synthesis patent for carboxylated nitrile rubber, while US20100147462A1 is an application/formulation patent using acid-modified NBR as a reactive adhesive component.
- EP3604355A1 explicitly incorporates a carboxyl group-containing monomer into the nitrile rubber backbone; US20100147462A1 relies on pre-existing acid- or acid anhydride-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer.
- EP3604355A1 emphasizes polymerization termination chemistry: nitrous acid salt plus water-insoluble hydroquinone, with nitrous acid salt at 0.15 parts by weight or less per 100 parts monomer mixture.
- US20100147462A1 emphasizes adhesive formulation ratios and thermal processing: elastomer/epoxy resin weight ratio greater than 1.5, no additional nonpolymer hardener, lamination at 100°C, pressing at 200°C and 1.3 MPa.
- The monomer composition in EP3604355A1 example data is acrylonitrile 25 parts, 1,3-butadiene 68 parts, and methacrylic acid 7 parts; US20100147462A1 cites commercial nitrile rubbers with acrylonitrile contents of 26% and 41% by weight and acid modification of 7% by weight in one example.
- EP3604355A1 reports latex pH values of 3.8 to 5.6 and storage-related conversion/viscosity changes as quality metrics; US20100147462A1 reports adhesive performance metrics such as T-peel, static peel at 70°C, and solder bath resistance.
- EP3604355A1 uses coagulation after termination to isolate the solid rubber; US20100147462A1 uses solution coating onto release paper followed by drying and later heat activation/crosslinking in the bondline.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two complementary routes for carboxylated NBR: direct synthesis by copolymerization with a carboxyl-containing monomer, and downstream use of acid-modified NBR as a reactive elastomer in epoxy-based heat-activable adhesives. For synthesis, the strongest evidence favors a nitrile/butadiene/methacrylic acid-type composition with controlled termination using a nitrous acid salt and water-insoluble hydroquinone, keeping nitrous acid salt at 0.15 parts by weight or less per 100 parts monomer mixture and hydroquinone at 0.1 to 1.0 part by weight. For application, carboxylated NBR is most useful when paired with epoxy resin at an elastomer/resin ratio greater than 1.5 and processed under heat above 150°C. Overall, the patents indicate that carboxylated NBR should be synthesized with careful termination and coagulation control to minimize discoloration and storage instability, then formulated as a polar, reactive elastomer for high-adhesion, heat-resistant products.
