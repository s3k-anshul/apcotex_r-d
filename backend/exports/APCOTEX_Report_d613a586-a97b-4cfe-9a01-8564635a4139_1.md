# HIGH-ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SYNTHESIS, DEGRADATION, AND FORMULATION-DRIVEN PROCESSING ROUTES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys patents relevant to high-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how acrylonitrile-rich nitrile elastomers are prepared, modified, or incorporated into downstream formulations. The target material is defined as NBR with elevated acrylonitrile content relative to standard grades, typically requiring careful control of butadiene/acrylonitrile feed composition, polymerization route, and post-polymerization processing to preserve desired polarity, oil resistance, and processability. The patent set spans three distinct technical directions: direct emulsion polymerization of acrylonitrile/butadiene copolymers, post-polymerization metathesis degradation of nitrile rubber to lower molecular weight or alter architecture, and formulation/composite patents that use NBR or HNBR as a functional elastomeric component.

The most directly relevant synthesis disclosure is the emulsion polymerization route described in US7592409B2, which explicitly states that the B block can comprise acrylonitrile and a conjugated diolefin such as 1,3-butadiene, and that controlled emulsion polymerization can be used to make block architectures incorporating nitrile-rubber segments. EP2057205B1 and EP2473279B1 are not synthesis patents for high-ACN NBR per se, but they are highly relevant because they show how nitrile rubbers are transformed by metathesis, including use of Grubbs II catalyst and hydrogenation with Wilkinson’s catalyst, enabling molecular-weight reduction and property tuning. EP3327047A1 demonstrates RAFT-based controlled polymerization in emulsion and explicitly includes acrylonitrile among the monomer classes, indicating a broader controlled-polymerization toolbox that could be adapted to nitrile-containing systems. The remaining patents are formulation/process patents: EP2561011B1 uses butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer as a nitrile resin base in conductive composites, US9909003B2 uses carboxyl-terminated butadiene acrylonitrile rubber in fire-retardant epoxy prepregs, and US10716877B2 uses NBR in nanocellulose-reinforced elastomer composites.

Across the landscape, the dominant trends are emulsion-based synthesis, controlled radical polymerization, metathesis-based molecular-weight adjustment, and heavy emphasis on downstream compounding with fillers, coagents, and reinforcing phases. For high-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis, the evidence supports monomer-ratio control in emulsion polymerization as the most direct route, while metathesis and formulation patents provide useful process/property context rather than primary synthesis recipes.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7592409B2 is the only patent in the set that explicitly frames acrylonitrile-containing polymer blocks as part of the synthesis claims; it uses controlled emulsion polymerization with in-situ emulsifier formation and RAFT/NMP/ATRP-compatible control chemistry.
- EP2473279B1 and EP2057205B1 are post-polymerization modification patents: both use metathesis chemistry on nitrile-rubber-containing substrates, but EP2473279B1 focuses on molecular-weight reduction/hydrogenation, whereas EP2057205B1 focuses on segment interchange to create meta-block copolymers.
- EP3327047A1 is a controlled radical polymerization patent with explicit acrylonitrile claim language, but its examples are styrene/AESO/AECO-based; it is broader in polymerization platform but less directly nitrile-rubber-specific than US7592409B2.
- The formulation patents (EP2561011B1, US9909003B2, US10716877B2) all use nitrile rubber or butadiene-acrylonitrile rubber as a matrix/toughener, but none disclose synthesis of high-ACN NBR; they mainly show compounding, filler loading, and cure/process conditions.
- Across the set, emulsion processing dominates the synthesis-relevant disclosures, while metathesis and compounding patents emphasize solvent choice, catalyst loading, temperature, and downstream property tuning rather than monomer feed composition.
- No patent in the set provides a direct high-ACN NBR recipe with explicit acrylonitrile wt% feed ratio and polymerization kinetics; the closest synthesis guidance comes from controlled emulsion polymerization and controlled radical polymerization disclosures.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that the most practical synthesis route for high-acrylonitrile NBR is controlled emulsion copolymerization of acrylonitrile with butadiene, with precise monomer-feed control and aqueous process management. US7592409B2 provides the strongest direct process framework for acrylonitrile-containing block structures, while EP3327047A1 broadens the controlled-polymerization toolbox to RAFT-based emulsion systems that explicitly include acrylonitrile monomers. EP2473279B1 and EP2057205B1 are valuable for post-synthesis molecular-weight and architecture tuning of nitrile rubbers, but they do not define the primary synthesis of high-ACN grades. Based on the disclosed evidence, recommended synthesis parameters are: emulsion polymerization in water, controlled radical or controlled emulsion conditions, careful monomer-ratio management, and downstream molecular-weight adjustment only if needed. For application development, the formulation patents show that nitrile rubbers are compatible with fillers, nanofillers, and reactive matrices, supporting use in high-performance elastomer composites.
