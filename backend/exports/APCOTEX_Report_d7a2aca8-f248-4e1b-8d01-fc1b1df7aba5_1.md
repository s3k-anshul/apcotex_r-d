# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON COMPOSITION CONTROL, CONTINUOUS POLYMERIZATION, AND POST-POLYMERIZATION MODIFICATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how acrylonitrile incorporation is controlled during copolymerization and how downstream processing affects performance. The target material is a butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymer with acrylonitrile content below standard NBR grades, typically in the low-ACN regime where monomer feed ratio, conversion profile, and polymerization mode strongly influence final composition. Across the selected patents, the landscape spans three main technical directions: (1) direct NBR synthesis in emulsion or solution using controlled monomer feeds and solvent systems, (2) continuous or staged-feed polymerization strategies to manage composition drift and bound acrylonitrile content, and (3) post-polymerization modification of NBR by metathesis or hydrogenation, which is relevant because low-ACN feedstocks are often chosen to balance oil resistance, flexibility, and processability in the final rubber or hydrogenated derivative.

The most composition-relevant disclosures are the continuous/staged polymerization patents, especially those teaching incremental butadiene addition and multi-vessel continuous operation to maintain a high and more uniform bound acrylonitrile content while avoiding premature butadiene depletion. One patent discloses a continuous process with an initial acrylonitrile/butadiene charge of about 80/20 parts and maintenance of unconverted monomers in the range of about 70–90 parts acrylonitrile to 30–10 parts butadiene. Another teaches batch and continuous incremental addition strategies, including 38 parts acrylonitrile with 62 parts butadiene, 5 °C polymerization, and 90% conversion termination. A solution-polymerization patent emphasizes use of at least two solvents, with the main solvent at 70–99.9 vol%, and mercaptan regulators to improve time-conversion behavior.

The remaining patents are indirectly relevant: metathesis catalysts and catalyst-salt systems enable molecular-weight reduction of NBR prior to hydrogenation, while hydrogenation catalyst patents focus on preserving molecular weight during conversion to HNBR. Together, these disclosures show that low-ACN NBR synthesis is most effectively achieved by feed-ratio control, staged or continuous monomer addition, low-temperature free-radical polymerization, mercaptan-based molecular-weight regulation, and careful coagulation/hydrogenation handling to preserve polymer quality.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US5770660A and US20140235744A1 are the most composition-direct patents: both teach free-radical copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene, but US5770660A emphasizes continuous staged-feed operation while US20140235744A1 emphasizes solvent-mixture solution polymerization and mercaptan regulation.
- US5770660A uses high acrylonitrile feed ratios and incremental butadiene addition to maintain bound acrylonitrile near 48.8-59.5 wt%, whereas US20140235744A1 uses a 38/62 acrylonitrile/butadiene feed in emulsion/solution processing and focuses on processability and hydrogenation compatibility rather than maximizing bound ACN.
- US10781270B2 and US9605088B2 are downstream hydrogenation patents; both use organic-medium hydrogenation of NBR, but US10781270B2 focuses on mercapto-residue control and coagulation temperature, while US9605088B2 focuses on catalyst pretreatment to suppress metathesis and preserve molecular weight.
- US8362154B2 and US8536277B2 are metathesis/degradation patents; both target molecular-weight reduction of NBR before hydrogenation, but US8362154B2 compares Grubbs III vs Grubbs II with 1-hexene, while US8536277B2 adds salts to boost catalyst activity.
- US8957161B2 and US8536277B2 both use salt effects to enhance metathesis, but US8957161B2 specifically demonstrates LiBr and CsBr acceleration in model metathesis and discusses nitrile-rubber degradation, whereas US8536277B2 claims a broader salt:catalyst ratio window and includes tin-salt examples.
- US9060566B2 is formulation-focused rather than synthesis-focused; it confirms practical NBR composition windows in footwear and cites a commercial NBR with approximately 34 wt% acrylonitrile, which is above the low-ACN target but useful as an application benchmark.
- Across the set, the most relevant synthesis levers for low-ACN NBR are monomer feed ratio, staged or continuous butadiene addition, low-temperature free-radical polymerization, mercaptan-based molecular-weight control, and careful coagulation/hydrogenation conditions to preserve polymer quality.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is best approached through controlled free-radical copolymerization of acrylonitrile and butadiene, with composition governed primarily by feed ratio, staged addition, and conversion management. The strongest direct synthesis guidance comes from continuous/staged-feed patents, which show that maintaining a high acrylonitrile-to-butadiene ratio in the unconverted monomer pool and using incremental butadiene addition can stabilize composition and improve polymer properties. For practical low-ACN production, the most defensible parameter set from the evidence is low-temperature emulsion or solution polymerization near 5° C., acrylonitrile/butadiene feeds in the approximate 38/62 to 80/20 range depending on target composition, mercaptan or related molecular-weight regulators at low ppm to sub-part levels, and coagulation in the 10-60° C. range before any hydrogenation step. If downstream hydrogenation is required, the data support careful control of residual mercapto compounds and catalyst pretreatment to avoid molecular-weight loss and maintain reactivity. Overall, the patent set suggests that composition control during polymerization is the key determinant for low-ACN NBR, while metathesis and hydrogenation patents provide enabling downstream processing routes.
