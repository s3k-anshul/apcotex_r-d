# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews three primary patents relevant to low-acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) and closely related nitrile elastomer formulations. The target material is a butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymer with reduced acrylonitrile content, typically pursued to balance low-temperature flexibility, processability, and oil resistance while avoiding hydrogenated, carboxylated, epoxidized, or chlorinated variants unless directly relevant as comparative materials. The selected patents do not disclose a dedicated low-ACN NBR polymerization route in the narrow sense; instead, they address composition design and end-use performance of nitrile elastomers in demanding applications.

US9926439B2 discloses a carboxylated nitrile-butadiene rubber latex formulation based on blending two acrylonitrile terpolymers, with the blended acrylonitrile content stated as 17-45 wt% and the terpolymer ratio at 60:40 to 40:60. It emphasizes soft, natural-rubber-like force-strain behavior in dipped articles and provides a process context for latex compounding, ionic crosslinking, and sulfur vulcanization. US10385647B2 focuses on swellable packer compositions using a nitrile elastomer together with butadiene rubber, sulfur curing agent, silica, and powder super absorbent polymer; it is relevant because it explicitly includes nitrile rubber as a matrix option and gives practical high-temperature/high-pressure service conditions, though the patent is centered on swellable downhole seals rather than NBR synthesis. US10443339B2 is a broader elastomeric-material patent for bore tools and packers reinforced with carbon-based nanoplatelets; it mentions NBR among candidate elastomers but does not disclose NBR synthesis or composition control.

Across the landscape, the dominant trends are formulation-driven rather than monomer-feed-driven: blending of nitrile polymers to tune modulus, use of latex dipping and coagulant processes, sulfur/accelerator cure systems, and reinforcement or swell-control additives for downhole durability. For low-ACN NBR synthesis specifically, the evidence supports composition control by monomer ratio as the key lever, but the patents here provide limited direct polymerization recipes.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US9926439B2 is the only patent in the set that explicitly controls acrylonitrile content; it states 17-45 wt% ACN and a 60:40 to 40:60 blend ratio of two acrylonitrile terpolymers.
- US10385647B2 uses nitrile elastomer as a formulation component in swellable packers, but the patent emphasis is on SAP loading, brine swelling, and downhole durability rather than monomer composition.
- US10443339B2 is a reinforcement/materials patent for bore tools and packers; it mentions NBR as a possible elastomer but provides no NBR composition or polymerization recipe.
- Process-wise, US9926439B2 relies on latex blending, coagulant dipping, ionic crosslinking, and sulfur vulcanization; US10385647B2 relies on cured swellable rubber formulations with powder SAP; US10443339B2 relies on elastomeric matrices reinforced with carbon-based nanoplatelets.
- Temperature and pressure service conditions are most explicit in US10385647B2 and US10443339B2, with US10385647B2 showing 200-275°F brine testing and >5000 psi resistance, and US10443339B2 citing HPHT conditions up to 138 MPa and 205°C.
- For low-ACN NBR synthesis, the strongest evidence is composition tuning in US9926439B2; the other two patents are application-oriented and only indirectly informative.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence base shows that low-acrylonitrile NBR development is driven primarily by composition control rather than by novel monomer-polymerization chemistry in these patents. US9926439B2 provides the clearest compositional guidance, with blended acrylonitrile terpolymers at 17-45 wt% ACN and a 60:40 to 40:60 blend ratio, supporting soft, natural-rubber-like behavior while retaining nitrile performance. US10385647B2 and US10443339B2 are valuable application patents for downhole elastomer service, but they do not materially advance low-ACN NBR synthesis itself.

Based on the disclosed evidence, the most defensible synthesis/design direction for low-ACN NBR is to target the lower end of the nitrile-content window while maintaining latex processability and cure balance, then validate performance in brine, heat, and pressure. For practical formulation work, the data support using latex-based processing, controlled ionic crosslinking, and sulfur cure systems, with downstream reinforcement or SAP additions only as needed for the final application. The patents collectively suggest that low-ACN NBR should be optimized by monomer ratio and formulation architecture, not by aggressive post-modification.
