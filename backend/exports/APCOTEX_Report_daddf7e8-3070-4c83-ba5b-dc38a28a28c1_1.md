# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS CONTROL ACROSS CATALYST-DRIVEN HYDROGENATION PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes five patents focused on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), with emphasis on selective hydrogenation of nitrile-butadiene rubber (NBR) precursors to reduce residual unsaturation while retaining polymer integrity and processability. The target material is consistently defined as HNBR derived from at least one conjugated diene and at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile, typically acrylonitrile-butadiene rubber, with desired properties including improved heat, oil, and ozone resistance and low residual double bond (RDB) content. The selected patent landscape spans two major synthesis strategies: conventional solution hydrogenation of NBR using noble-metal catalysts, and simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation routes that reduce molecular weight and narrow molecular-weight distribution in a single step. Across the patents, ruthenium-, osmium-, rhodium-, palladium-, and Wilkinson-type catalyst systems are disclosed, with strong emphasis on catalyst composition, catalyst loading, hydrogen pressure, temperature, reaction time, and co-catalyst or co-olefin effects.

US10508156B2 discloses catalyst compositions based on ruthenium or osmium complexes pretreated with vinyl ether co-catalysts to improve hydrogenation activity and preserve molecular weight during HNBR formation. US7772328B2 and EP2473280B1 both address low-molecular-weight HNBR via simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation, using Ru/Os metathesis catalysts and optional co-olefins to cleave polymer chains while saturating residual double bonds. EP2473280B1 compares Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson’s catalyst and shows faster hydrogenation with Grubbs III at equal metal content. US9150669B2 extends this concept with alternative Ru/Os catalyst structures and demonstrates that catalyst choice strongly affects hydrogenation rate, RDB reduction, and molecular-weight retention. EP3827029A1 focuses less on the hydrogenation chemistry itself and more on production quality, catalyst residue control, and aging stability, specifying noble-metal loading windows and reaction conditions for producing HNBR with improved aging properties.

Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that combine selective hydrogenation with catalyst systems engineered to control metathesis, gel formation, catalyst residue, and molecular-weight loss. The most robust process window in the evidence uses solution-phase hydrogenation in organic solvent, hydrogen pressures around 4.137–8.4 MPa, temperatures from 120–140°C in the examples, and catalyst/co-catalyst pretreatment to tune activity and product properties.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10508156B2 emphasizes catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ether co-catalysts to improve hydrogenation activity while preserving molecular weight, whereas EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 focus on simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation to intentionally lower molecular weight and Mooney viscosity.
- US10508156B2 uses solution hydrogenation at 120°C and 4.137 MPa in the main examples, while EP2473280B1 and US9150669B2 use 140°C and 8.4 MPa in their comparative hydrogenation profiles.
- EP3827029A1 is distinct in that it centers on noble-metal loading windows and catalyst residue/aging control rather than metathesis-driven molecular-weight reduction.
- Across the metathesis-based patents, ruthenium/osmium catalysts are the dominant platform; EP2473280B1 explicitly compares Grubbs III, Grubbs II, and Wilkinson's catalyst, while US9150669B2 compares Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson-type systems.
- US7772328B2 and EP2473280B1 both disclose co-olefin-assisted chain scission before or during hydrogenation, with ethylene and 1-hexene used to reduce Mw and PDI.
- US10508156B2 shows that vinyl ethyl ether pretreatment can accelerate hydrogenation and reduce PDI relative to untreated catalyst systems, supporting catalyst activation as a key process lever.
- EP3827029A1 specifies broad but explicit catalyst concentration ranges: Ru 10-200 ppm, Pd 20-<67 ppm, Rh 50-<270 ppm, with hydrogenation at 60-200°C for 1-24 h.
- The patents collectively indicate that low residual unsaturation, controlled catalyst loading, and careful management of metathesis side activity are central to producing processable HNBR with acceptable molecular weight and aging stability.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows two viable HNBR synthesis paradigms: direct selective hydrogenation of NBR with optimized noble-metal catalysts, and one-step metathesis/hydrogenation to deliberately lower molecular weight and Mooney viscosity. For high-performance HNBR, the strongest evidence supports solution-phase processing in organic solvent with hydrogenation temperatures around 120-140°C, hydrogen pressures around 4.137-8.4 MPa, and catalyst systems based on Ru/Os or related noble-metal complexes. Catalyst pretreatment with vinyl ethers or use of carefully chosen co-catalysts can improve hydrogenation rate and help preserve molecular weight, while co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene can be used when molecular-weight reduction is desired. EP3827029A1 further indicates that catalyst residue control is important for aging stability and product appearance. Based on the disclosed evidence, recommended synthesis parameters are: solution hydrogenation, Ru/Os-based catalyst systems, hydrogen pressure in the 4-8.4 MPa range, temperature near 120-140°C, and explicit control of catalyst loading and pretreatment to balance hydrogenation efficiency, residual unsaturation, and final rheology.
