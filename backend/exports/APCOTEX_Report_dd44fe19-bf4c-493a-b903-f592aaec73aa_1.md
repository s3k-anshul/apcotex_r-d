# LOW STYRENE SBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE FOR CONTROLLED STYRENE INCORPORATION, HYDROGENATION, AND TIRE-COMPOUND PERFORMANCE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report surveys patents relevant to low styrene styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and closely related styrene-containing diene elastomers used in tire compounds. The target is a styrene-butadiene copolymer with reduced styrene incorporation relative to conventional SBR grades, typically achieved through controlled monomer feed, random copolymerization, solution polymerization, hydrogenation, or functionalized anionic initiation. Across the selected patents, the dominant technical themes are not only lowering or tuning styrene content, but also preserving wet grip, abrasion resistance, rolling resistance, processability, and filler compatibility in silica- and carbon-black-filled formulations.

The landscape includes patents that directly synthesize SBR with explicit styrene/butadiene feed ratios and hydrogenation levels, as well as patents that use low molecular weight styrene-butadiene copolymers, hydrogenated SBR, or styrene-containing multicomponent copolymers as performance modifiers. Several disclosures emphasize solution polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent with n-butyllithium and polar modifiers such as THF or TMEDA, followed by termination, hydrogenation, or functional end-group introduction. Other patents focus on post-polymerization compounding strategies: silica, silane coupling agents, liquid resins, and sulfur are used to balance traction, abrasion, and processability. Functional initiator patents show how sulfur-containing lithio initiators can generate SBR with tailored headgroups that improve filler interaction and reduce hysteresis.

Overall, the evidence indicates that low styrene SBR performance is best achieved by combining moderate-to-low styrene content with controlled molecular weight, narrow-to-moderate dispersity, and either hydrogenation or functionalization to recover reinforcement lost by reducing styrene. In tire compounds, the most consistent formulation trends are silica/carbon black hybrid reinforcement, silane coupling, and careful control of oil/resin loading and curing package. The patents collectively support a design space centered on solution-polymerized SBR with styrene content around the lower conventional range, hydrogenation where needed for durability, and filler-compatible end-group chemistry for improved compound performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10907034B2 and US10428203B2 both disclose solution-polymerized SBR using n-hexane, n-butyllithium, and THF/TMEDA, with styrene at 60 g and butadiene at 140 g in the main synthesis examples; both then use hydrogenation to tune performance.
- US10907034B2 reports a 30 wt% styrene SBR and hydrogenated variants with 16-30 wt% styrene-equivalent composition, while US10428203B2 reports a 30 wt% styrene SBR and hydrogenation levels of 60-95 mol %; both sit at the upper end of conventional SBR styrene content rather than the low-styrene target.
- US7253235B2 is the clearest low-styrene disclosure, with preparation examples at 5 wt%, 12 wt%, 25 wt%, and 40 wt% styrene and low molecular weights from 8,000 to 83,000, making it more directly aligned with low-styrene SBR than the hydrogenated tire patents.
- US7462677B2 and US6057397A focus on functionality and silica compatibility rather than styrene reduction itself; both are relevant because low styrene SBR often needs end-group or hydroxyl functionality to recover filler reinforcement.
- US10759885B2 and US11021595B2 broaden the styrene-containing elastomer space by combining styrene with branched dienes or ethylene/non-conjugated olefin units; these are more about performance balancing than strict low-styrene SBR, but they show how styrene can be diluted or structurally offset.
- US8993664B2 and US10392497B2 are compounding patents: the former uses liquid resin plus fine silica and sulfur, while the latter uses hydroxyaryl-functional polymer/silica coupling. Both are important downstream tools for low styrene SBR formulations.
- US7829624B2 adds a process route for one-pot liquid polymer/nanoparticle blends, useful for tire compounds containing SBR-based liquid polymers, but it does not directly optimize styrene content.
- Across the set, the most common tire-compound reinforcement package is silica plus silane coupling agent, often with carbon black and oil/resin; hydrogenation and functionalization are the main polymer-level strategies used to offset performance losses from lower styrene content.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports low styrene SBR development through three complementary strategies: direct solution polymerization with controlled styrene feed, hydrogenation or structural modification to recover durability, and filler-compatible functionalization to preserve reinforcement. The most directly relevant low-styrene disclosure is US7253235B2, which includes styrene contents as low as 5 wt% in low molecular weight styrene-butadiene copolymers. For tire-grade systems, the strongest supporting process conditions are solution polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent with n-butyllithium and a polar modifier such as THF or TMEDA, followed by careful termination and, where needed, hydrogenation or end-group modification. On the formulation side, silica/silane coupling, liquid resin assistance, and hydroxyl- or sulfur-functional polymer architectures are repeatedly shown to improve wet grip, abrasion resistance, and processability. A practical evidence-based design window for low styrene SBR is therefore a solution-polymerized styrene-butadiene copolymer in the lower conventional styrene range, combined with silica/silane reinforcement and either hydrogenation or functional end-group chemistry to maintain tire performance.
