# LOW MOLECULAR WEIGHT NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND PROCESS-CONTROL SYNTHESIS REVIEW

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews the provided patent set against the target of low molecular weight NBR (nitrile-butadiene rubber) synthesis and process control. The manifested patents do not directly disclose NBR preparation routes; instead, they illustrate adjacent polymerization and manufacturing strategies relevant to controlling molecular weight, viscosity, architecture, and product handling in polymer systems. US10167348B2 is the most mechanistically relevant, describing solution anionic polymerization of 1,1-disubstituted alkene monomers with explicit control over initiator ratio, temperature, solvent choice, monomer feed, and quench conditions, producing homopolymers, random copolymers, and block copolymers with tunable molecular weight distributions. US7683134B2 demonstrates continuous reactive extrusion maleation of polypropylene, showing how feed ratios, peroxide initiator rate, screw speed, and vacuum can be used to tune molecular weight and grafting efficiency in a melt process. US8470957B2 focuses on continuous microreactor production of monomer-like bischloroformates and low-repeating-unit oligomers, emphasizing flow rate, temperature, and stoichiometric control to suppress side reactions and obtain low-degree oligomeric products. US9434846B2 discloses emulsion polymerization of HASE thickeners with surfactant-resistant rheology, using nitrogen purge, 80°C batch hold, ammonium persulfate initiation, and chaser chemistry to convert residual monomer to oligomer and reduce VOCs. Across the set, the dominant process trends are solution or emulsion polymerization, continuous or semi-continuous feed control, explicit initiator/monomer ratio management, temperature control, and post-reaction quenching or stripping. For low-MW NBR synthesis, the evidence supports prioritizing controlled radical or anionic solution polymerization concepts, tight monomer-to-initiator or chain-transfer control, and solvent/temperature regimes that limit runaway viscosity and broadened molecular-weight distributions.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US10167348B2 and US9434846B2 both rely on solution/emulsion polymerization with explicit control of initiator chemistry and temperature, but US10167348B2 emphasizes anionic living control and molecular-weight tuning, whereas US9434846B2 emphasizes emulsion thickener performance and residual monomer conversion.
- US10167348B2 provides the clearest molecular-weight control levers for low-MW targets: monomer-to-activator ratios of about 1000:1 or 100:1, temperatures from about 23° C. to −78° C., and solvent-dependent Mn values from about 2,000 to 2,000,000 daltons.
- US7683134B2 uses a continuous melt extrusion route rather than solution polymerization; molecular weight is influenced by screw speed, peroxide feed rate, and maleic anhydride feed, with Mn values spanning about 30,000 to 71,000 in the examples.
- US8470957B2 is an oligomer-control patent rather than a rubber patent, but it shows that low repeating-unit products can be obtained by tight stoichiometric and flow control, with average repeating units n around 1.06 to 1.32 in the examples.
- Across the set, the most transferable low-MW design principles are: controlled initiator/activator ratio, low-temperature reaction control, continuous or staged monomer addition, and post-reaction quenching or termination to freeze molecular weight.
- None of the patents directly disclose NBR monomer ratios of butadiene to acrylonitrile or NBR-specific chain-transfer agents; therefore, the evidence is best interpreted as process analogues for molecular-weight control rather than direct NBR recipes.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent set does not include a direct low-MW NBR synthesis disclosure, but it does provide strong process analogues for controlling molecular weight and viscosity in polymer manufacture. The most relevant evidence supports solution polymerization with tight monomer-to-initiator control, low or subambient temperatures when needed, staged monomer addition, and rapid quenching to arrest growth. For a low-MW NBR program, the best-supported synthesis strategy from this evidence would be controlled solution polymerization with carefully limited initiator or chain-transfer activity, solvent selection that moderates heat and viscosity, and process conditions that avoid runaway conversion and broad molecular-weight distributions. Continuous or semi-continuous operation with in-line monitoring would be preferable if scale-up and reproducibility are priorities. Based on the disclosed examples, the most transferable parameter set is a low-temperature, solvent-based controlled radical or anionic process with explicit feed-ratio management and immediate termination once the desired Mn window is reached.
