# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: EMULSION, SOLUTION, FUNCTIONALIZED, AND PROCESS-CONTROL APPROACHES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven primary patents relevant to styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) synthesis and production, with emphasis on low-styrene embodiments and process routes that can support reduced styrene incorporation. The landscape spans aqueous emulsion polymerization, organic-solution polymerization, functionalized SBR latex preparation, oil-extension of SBR, modified diene polymer design for tire performance, battery-binder SBR microspheres, and continuous-process reaction blocking for SBR elastomers. Across the set, the target material is not always explicitly labeled as low-styrene SBR; instead, relevance arises from patents that disclose SBR preparation, monomer feed control, or SBR-derived materials where styrene content can be tuned.

The strongest direct synthesis disclosures are the emulsion-polymerized SBR patents (US8530017B2, US9902837B2, US12129321B2, and EP0295519A1) and the solution-polymerized SSBR patent (EP2657262B1). US8530017B2 teaches radical emulsion polymerization of styrene and butadiene using reactive emulsifiers, with styrene feed explicitly in the 10-50 wt% range and butadiene in the 45-85 wt% range. US9902837B2 and US12129321B2 both disclose aqueous emulsion systems with water as the polymerization medium and later coagulation/washing steps; US9902837B2 further shows functional monomer incorporation and oil extension, while US12129321B2 focuses on porous cross-linked SBR microspheres for lithium-ion battery binders. EP2657262B1 is the key non-aqueous route, disclosing anionically initiated solution polymerization in hexane with 70/30 butadiene/styrene monomer feed and subsequent oil extension/steam stripping.

Overall, the patent set indicates that low-styrene SBR development is most plausibly achieved by controlling styrene feed ratio in emulsion or solution polymerization, using reactive emulsifiers or functional monomers to preserve performance at lower styrene levels, and applying downstream oil extension, coagulation, stripping, or crosslinking to tailor processability and end-use properties.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US8530017B2 and US9902837B2 both use aqueous emulsion polymerization, but US8530017B2 explicitly claims styrene feed at 10-50 wt% of total reactants, while US9902837B2 Example 6 reports 21.0 wt% bound styrene in a functionalized SBR.
- EP2657262B1 and US6313232B1 are both solution-polymerization disclosures in hydrocarbon solvent, but EP2657262B1 gives a clear 70/30 butadiene/styrene monomer feed, whereas US6313232B1 focuses on tin-compound addition timing and does not disclose a specific styrene fraction in the extracted evidence.
- US12129321B2 and US9902837B2 both rely on water-based latex/emulsion processing and later coagulation/washing, but US12129321B2 is directed to porous battery-binder microspheres with very low styrene charge (25 parts styrene vs 75 parts butadiene in Example 1), while US9902837B2 targets oil-extended functionalized SBR for tire applications.
- US8614272B2 is a non-aqueous solution route with explicit monomer charge in moles and a post-polymerization end-modification step; it differs from the emulsion patents by using cyclohexane and precipitation/reprecipitation rather than latex coagulation.
- EP0295519A1 is process-control only: it discloses continuous SBR line blocking at 5-10 °C and solid-content control around 24.8-25.2%, but no monomer ratio or polymer composition target is provided in the extracted evidence.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the patent set, the most actionable low-styrene SBR synthesis guidance comes from emulsion and solution polymerization disclosures that explicitly control styrene feed. US8530017B2 provides the clearest emulsion-process range (10-50 wt% styrene), EP2657262B1 provides a solution-process benchmark (70/30 butadiene/styrene feed), and US9902837B2 shows that functionalized aqueous SBR can be prepared with bound styrene around 21 wt%. For low-styrene SBR development, the evidence supports using controlled styrene feed reduction in either emulsion or solution polymerization, with reactive emulsifiers or functional monomers used to preserve silica compatibility and end-use performance. Downstream coagulation, washing, stripping, and oil extension are recurring process elements that help convert the latex or cement into processable rubber.
