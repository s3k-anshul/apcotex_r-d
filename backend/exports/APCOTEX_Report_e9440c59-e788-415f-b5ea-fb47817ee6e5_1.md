# Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber
## SCIENTIFIC PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

### Abstract
This report synthesizes technical information from five European patents related to Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR), with a particular focus on aspects relevant to low acrylonitrile content, processing, and modification. The patent landscape reveals diverse approaches to NBR synthesis and post-modification, including emulsion polymerization, solution polymerization in organic solvents, and advanced techniques like Reversible Addition-Fragmentation Chain Transfer (RAFT) polymerization. Key scientific approaches involve precise control over reaction conditions, monomer compositions, and the use of various additives to tailor material properties.

Recurring variables across the patents include polymerization temperature (ranging from 0°C to 160°C), reaction time (from minutes to several hours), and pressure (up to 150 bar for hydrogenation or butadiene addition). Monomer ratios, particularly acrylonitrile and butadiene, are critical, with acrylonitrile content often specified in weight percentages (e.g., 5-80% by weight of α,β-unsaturated nitriles). The use of various initiators (peroxidic, azo, RAFT agents), regulators (e.g., dodecylmercaptan), and catalysts (rhodium-containing for hydrogenation, redox systems for polymerization) is consistently highlighted. Post-polymerization modifications such as hydrogenation and plasticization are also detailed, with specific conditions for pH adjustment (e.g., 8.5-12 for latex, then 5-8 for hydrogenation) and temperature.

Trends indicate a strong emphasis on controlling molecular weight (Mn, Mw) and polydispersity index (PDI) to achieve desired polymer characteristics. Gel content and glass transition temperature (Tg) are frequently measured material properties, reflecting the impact of synthesis conditions. The incorporation of carboxy groups and subsequent hydrogenation is explored for enhanced performance. Furthermore, the control of particle size distribution in emulsion polymerization and the use of various emulsifiers, stabilizers, crosslinkers, and fillers are common themes. One patent, EP3715409, specifically details sulfur-modified chloroprene rubber, which, while not NBR, provides insights into general rubber modification strategies and processing parameters that could be analogous or comparative in the broader rubber industry. Overall, the patents demonstrate a concerted effort to optimize NBR production for improved efficiency, controlled microstructure, and enhanced end-use properties.

### Cross-Patent Synthesis Trends

The analyzed patents reveal several common and distinct technical trends in the synthesis and modification of Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR) and related elastomers.

**Common Technical Trends:**

1.  **Polymerization Methods:** Emulsion polymerization is a prevalent method, particularly for NBR synthesis (EP2316860, EP3715409). However, solution polymerization in organic solvents is also explored (EP2478021), offering potential advantages in molecular weight control and reduced gel content. RAFT (Reversible Addition-Fragmentation Chain Transfer) polymerization is highlighted in EP2478021 as a method to achieve controlled molecular weights (Mn, Mw) and narrow polydispersity indices (PDI), indicating a move towards more sophisticated polymerization techniques for tailored polymer architectures.

2.  **Reaction Conditions:**
    *   **Temperature:** A wide range of temperatures is employed depending on the specific process step. Polymerization temperatures typically range from 0°C to 100°C (EP2478021, EP2316860, EP3715409), with some processes, like hydrogenation or specific initiator choices, extending to 150°C-160°C (EP2478021, EP2316860, EP3715409). Post-processing steps like plasticization (50°C in EP3715409) and crosslinking (160°C in EP3715409) also have specific temperature requirements.
    *   **Pressure:** Hydrogenation reactions consistently involve elevated pressures, with ranges up to 150 bar (EP2478021, EP2316860). Butadiene addition during polymerization can also occur under pressure, e.g., 3 bar (EP2478021).
    *   **Time:** Polymerization times vary significantly, from 6-22 hours (EP2478021) to 4-15 hours (EP2316860), depending on desired conversion and monomer content. Hydrogenation typically takes 2-10 hours (EP2478021, EP2316860).
    *   **pH Control:** For carboxy group-containing NBR, precise pH adjustment (8.5-12 for latex, then 5-8 for hydrogenation) is crucial for efficient hydrogenation and lower iodine values (EP3744739). The pH of the aqueous emulsified liquid at polymerization start is also critical (10.5 or more in EP3715409).

3.  **Raw Materials & Additives:**
    *   **Monomers:** Acrylonitrile and butadiene are the primary monomers. The proportion of α,β-unsaturated nitriles (acrylonitrile) can range from 5 to 80% by weight (EP2316860). Terpolymers are also mentioned, with additional monomers up to 40% by weight (EP2478021, EP2316860).
    *   **Initiators & Regulators:** Peroxidic and azo initiators are common (EP2478021). RAFT agents like DoPAT, DiBenT, and CPDA are used for controlled polymerization (EP2478021). Redox systems involving Fe(II) salts (Fe(II)SO4, Fe(II)Cl2) and reducing agents like Rongalit C are also employed (EP2478021, EP2316860).
    *   **Emulsifiers:** Various emulsifiers are used, including potassium disproportionated rosinate, sodium lauryl sulfate (EP3715409), and selectively hardened tallow fatty acid salts (Edenor HTiCT) (EP2478021, EP2316860). Amounts typically range from 0.2 to 15 parts by weight based on monomers (EP2316860).
    *   **Crosslinkers & Accelerators:** Polyamine crosslinkers (0.2-20 parts by weight) and crosslinking accelerators (0.5-10 parts by weight) are mentioned (EP2478021). Sulfur, sulfenamides, xanthogen disulfides, ethylene thiourea, and dipentamethylenethiuramtetrasulfide are used for sulfur-modified chloroprene rubber (EP3715409).
    *   **Stabilizers:** Pyrocatechol/hydroquinone (EP2478021) and hindered phenols like Vulkanox BKF (EP2478021, EP2316860) are used to prevent degradation.
    *   **Fillers & Plasticizers:** Carbon black and zinc oxide are common fillers (EP3715409). Plasticizers are used, typically up to 50 parts by mass (EP3715409).

4.  **Material Properties & Characterization:**
    *   **Molecular Weight:** Mn, Mw, and PDI are routinely measured (EP2478021).
    *   **Gel Content:** Measured after dissolution in solvents like methyl ethyl ketone (MEK) (EP2478021).
    *   **Glass Transition Temperature (Tg):** Important for understanding polymer flexibility, with ranges from -70°C to +10°C (EP2316860).
    *   **Particle Size Distribution (PSD):** Crucial for emulsion polymers, with efforts to achieve narrow distributions (EP1247820).
    *   **Mooney Viscosity:** A key indicator of processability for crude rubbers (EP3715409).

**Interesting or Novel Conditions, Materials, or Properties:**

*   **Carboxy Group-Containing Hydrogenated NBR (EP3744739):** This patent specifically focuses on a method to produce hydrogenated NBR with carboxy groups, emphasizing a two-stage pH adjustment (8.5-12 then 5-8) during heating and hydrogenation to achieve lower iodine values and higher production efficiency. This highlights a targeted modification for specific performance benefits.
*   **RAFT Polymerization in Organic Solvents (EP2478021):** The use of RAFT agents (DoPAT, DiBenT, CPDA) in organic solvents like dimethylacetamide allows for precise control over molecular weight and polydispersity, leading to NBRs with tailored properties. This is a more advanced polymerization technique compared to traditional free-radical methods.
*   **Cyclodextrins for Narrow Particle Size Distribution (EP1247820):** The incorporation of randomly methylated β-cyclodextrin (RAMEB) during semi-continuous emulsion polymerization is a novel approach to achieve narrow particle size distributions and reduce coagulum formation, which is critical for latex stability and product quality.
*   **Detailed Redox Initiator Systems (EP2316860):** This patent provides detailed formulations for redox initiator systems, including specific amounts of Fe(II) salts and Rongalit C, and their molar ratios to reducing agents, indicating fine-tuning of initiation for optimal polymerization.
*   **Sulfur-Modified Chloroprene Rubber (EP3715409):** While not NBR, this patent details a sulfur-modified chloroprene rubber composition. The use of sulfenamides and xanthogen disulfides as plasticizers and modifiers, along with specific polymerization and plasticization temperatures, offers insights into advanced rubber modification strategies that could be adapted or compared to NBR systems for properties like abrasion resistance, compression set, and heat build-up.
