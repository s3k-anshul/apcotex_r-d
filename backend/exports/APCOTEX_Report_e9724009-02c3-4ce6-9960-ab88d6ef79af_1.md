# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR), with emphasis on how acrylonitrile content is controlled during polymerization to obtain grades typically below about 20 wt% ACN. The single primary patent in the manifest, CN119978233A, discloses a low-temperature emulsion polymerization route for nitrile rubber in which butadiene and acrylonitrile are copolymerized with a reactive anti-aging agent containing amide functionality. The process uses staged or continuous acrylonitrile addition, a composite emulsifier system, a molecular weight regulator, and termination at controlled conversion to manage composition drift and final polymer properties. The patent explicitly targets a combined acrylonitrile content of 17-20%, placing it squarely in the low-ACN NBR domain.

The disclosed formulation trends are consistent with industrial low-ACN NBR practice: low-temperature emulsion polymerization, controlled monomer feed, and use of a mixed emulsifier package based on disproportionated rosin soap, linear alkylbenzene sulfonic acid, and naphthalene sulfonate formaldehyde condensate. The initiator system is based on dicumyl peroxide with a reducing agent/chelating agent package, and tertiary dodecyl mercaptan is used as a molecular weight regulator. The patent further emphasizes adding acrylonitrile in multiple portions, with a preferred four-stage feed tied to conversion milestones, to stabilize composition and improve heat-oxidative aging resistance.

Overall, the evidence indicates that low acrylonitrile NBR synthesis is best achieved by careful feed scheduling rather than simply lowering the initial monomer charge. The patent suggests that a butadiene/acrylonitrile ratio of 80-83/17-20 parts, polymerization at 5-15°C, and termination near 70-76% conversion are effective conditions for producing low-ACN NBR with improved thermal-oxidative durability for oil-resistant sealing applications.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The available evidence shows that low acrylonitrile NBR is best produced by low-temperature emulsion copolymerization of butadiene and acrylonitrile with tight feed control. For the disclosed primary patent, the most relevant synthesis window is 80-83 parts butadiene and 17-20 parts acrylonitrile, polymerized at 5-15°C, with acrylonitrile added in multiple stages and termination near 70-76% conversion. A mixed emulsifier system and mercaptan chain-transfer agent support stable latex formation and molecular weight control. These conditions are consistent with producing low-ACN NBR suitable for oil-resistant sealing applications with improved heat-oxidative aging resistance.
