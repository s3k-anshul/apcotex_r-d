# HYDROGENATED NITRILE BUTADIENE RUBBER (HNBR): HYDROGENATION PROCESSES, CATALYST SYSTEMS, AND POST-PROCESSING TRENDS ACROSS PRIMARY PATENTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents focused on hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR) and closely related manufacturing routes. The target material is HNBR itself, including liquid HNBR, low-molecular-weight HNBR, and HNBR compositions, with emphasis on how hydrogenation is performed, what catalyst systems are used, and how residual catalyst or polymer properties are managed. Across the set, the dominant transformation is catalytic hydrogenation of unsaturated nitrile rubber (NBR) in solution, with one patent also disclosing simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis to reduce molecular weight. Another patent focuses on producing HNBR with improved ageing properties by controlling noble-metal catalyst type and loading, while the most recent patent addresses liquid HNBR and appears to rely on hydrogenation chemistry and catalyst-related processing rather than worked examples.

The landscape is centered on noble-metal catalysis, especially ruthenium, palladium, and rhodium compounds, with explicit attention to catalyst residue management because residual metals can impair ageing, discoloration, and product performance. Solution polymerization/hydrogenation in organic solvent is the core medium in the disclosed preparation routes; water is not part of the hydrogenation medium in the extracted evidence, though downstream washing/coagulation is mentioned in prior-art discussion within one patent. The patents collectively show a strong trend toward process simplification: avoiding elaborate catalyst-removal steps, tuning catalyst loading to balance hydrogenation rate and ageing stability, and in one case using catalysts that enable concurrent hydrogenation and metathesis to obtain lower molecular weight HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distribution.

Key disclosed process windows include hydrogenation temperatures of 60–200°C, pressures of 700,000–15,000,000 Pa, and reaction times of 1–24 hours in one patent; another uses monochlorobenzene solution at 130–138°C and 85 bar with 100 psi H2 degassing. Example data show that catalyst identity and loading strongly affect residual double bond reduction, molecular weight, and Mooney viscosity. Overall, the evidence supports solution-phase catalytic hydrogenation of NBR precursors as the principal route to HNBR, with catalyst selection and post-reaction handling being the main levers for product quality.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- All four patents are centered on HNBR, but only EP2473281B1 explicitly discloses a quantitative hydrogenation outcome via residual double bond (0.4% for HNBR 1 after 240 min).
- EP3827029A1 and US20210340285A1 focus on noble-metal residue effects and ageing behavior of HNBR rather than the hydrogenation extent itself; both use solution-based organic media and emphasize catalyst identity/loading.
- EP2473281B1 uses monochlorobenzene solution at 85 bar and 138°C with simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis, whereas EP3827029A1 claims broader hydrogenation windows of 60–200°C, 700,000–15,000,000 Pa, and 1–24 h.
- Catalyst systems differ: EP3827029A1 centers on Ru/Pd/Rh compounds with ppm-level loadings; EP2473281B1 compares Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson-type ruthenium catalysts with phr-level loadings; US20210340285A1 studies Ru/Pd/Rh additives in finished HNBR compositions.
- Water is not part of the core reaction medium in any of the extracted inventive examples; where water-related operations appear, they are only in prior-art discussion or absent entirely.
- EP2473281B1 is the only patent in the set that clearly links catalyst choice to both hydrogenation kinetics and molecular-weight reduction, making it the most process-specific HNBR synthesis disclosure in the group.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The extracted evidence shows that HNBR is predominantly made by solution-phase catalytic hydrogenation of NBR in organic solvent, with noble-metal catalysts being the dominant technology family. The strongest synthesis disclosure is EP2473281B1, which demonstrates simultaneous hydrogenation and metathesis in monochlorobenzene under hydrogen, yielding low-molecular-weight HNBR with low residual double bond content. EP3827029A1 provides a broader industrial hydrogenation window and highlights the importance of controlling Ru/Pd/Rh catalyst residues to preserve ageing performance, while US20210340285A1 shows that residual noble metals in HNBR compositions measurably affect ageing behavior. EP4215556A4 is relevant to liquid HNBR but the extracted evidence is too sparse to recover detailed process parameters. Based on the evidence, the most defensible synthesis recommendation is solution hydrogenation of NBR in an organic solvent with carefully selected noble-metal catalyst systems, controlled temperature and pressure, and explicit attention to catalyst residue management to balance hydrogenation rate, residual unsaturation, molecular weight, and ageing stability.
