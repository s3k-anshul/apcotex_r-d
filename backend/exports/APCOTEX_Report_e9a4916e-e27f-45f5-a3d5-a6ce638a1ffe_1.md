# CARBOXYLATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE AND SYNTHESIS/TERMINATION CONTROL IN EMULSION POLYMERIZATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews the disclosed preparation of carboxylated nitrile rubber (carboxylated NBR; carboxyl group-containing nitrile rubber) from the provided patent set, with emphasis on direct synthesis and process control. The target material is defined as an acrylonitrile/butadiene-based rubber containing incorporated carboxyl functionality, typically introduced by copolymerization with a carboxyl-bearing monomer such as methacrylic acid. The single primary patent in the manifest, EP3604355B1, focuses not on the monomer feed itself but on how to terminate the polymerization and recover the latex without discoloration or post-storage instability. The disclosed route is a solvent-based polymerization of a monomer mixture comprising an α,β-ethylenically unsaturated nitrile monomer and a carboxyl group-containing monomer, followed by termination using a specific combination of nitrous acid salt and water-insoluble hydroquinone, then coagulation of the latex. The patent identifies that conventional terminators can lead to discoloration and poor storage stability, whereas the claimed terminator pair, used at controlled loading, suppresses conversion drift, Mooney viscosity increase, and discoloration. The examples compare multiple terminator combinations and show that nitrous acid salt plus water-insoluble hydroquinone gives low increases in conversion and viscosity after storage at 5°C and 50°C, with improved color. The broader technical trend is that carboxylated NBR performance is strongly influenced not only by monomer selection and incorporation of acid functionality, but also by late-stage polymerization termination chemistry and latex handling. For practical synthesis, the evidence supports using acrylonitrile with a carboxyl monomer such as methacrylic acid, terminating at a predetermined conversion with ≤0.15 parts by weight nitrous acid salt per 100 parts monomer mixture, and 0.1–1.0 part by weight water-insoluble hydroquinone, followed by coagulation to isolate the solid rubber.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports carboxylated NBR preparation by copolymerizing acrylonitrile, butadiene, and a carboxyl monomer such as methacrylic acid, then carefully terminating the latex polymerization before coagulation. The key process insight from EP3604355B1 is that a nitrous acid salt plus water-insoluble hydroquinone, used at low loading, improves termination efficiency while minimizing discoloration and post-storage increases in conversion and Mooney viscosity. Based on the disclosed data, the most defensible synthesis guidance is to keep nitrous acid salt at 0.15 parts by weight or less per 100 parts monomer mixture, use water-insoluble hydroquinone at 0.1–1.0 part by weight, and coagulate only after the latex has been terminated to a pH of 7 or below.
