# HYDROGENATED NBR: PATENT LANDSCAPE ON HYDROGENATION, MOLECULAR-WEIGHT CONTROL, AND HNBR FORMULATION

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes a set of patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile butadiene rubber (HNBR), spanning upstream NBR polymerization, hydrogenation process control, catalyst selection/recovery, and downstream compounding. The target material is defined as nitrile rubber in which the butadiene-derived unsaturation is selectively reduced to produce partially or fully hydrogenated elastomeric products with controlled residual double-bond content, molecular weight, and viscosity. Across the selected patents, the dominant technical themes are: (1) solution hydrogenation of NBR using homogeneous noble-metal catalysts such as rhodium, ruthenium, or palladium systems; (2) simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation concepts intended to reduce molecular weight while hydrogenating; (3) in-line or process analytical control to stop hydrogenation at a defined conversion; and (4) formulation strategies that exploit HNBR’s thermal and chemical resistance in vulcanizates, conductive pastes, and adhesive systems.

The strongest synthesis-focused disclosures are EP2473281B1, which teaches simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation of nitrile rubber in the presence of hydrogen using ruthenium/osmium complexes to obtain low-molecular-weight HNBR with narrower molecular-weight distribution, and US6307624B1, which discloses pressurized hydrogenation of NBR with homogeneous or heterogeneous catalysis while monitoring Raman spectra to determine the degree of hydrogenation in real time. EP3827029A1 addresses catalyst loading and post-hydrogenation purification, emphasizing ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium compounds at controlled ppm levels to improve ageing properties while minimizing residual noble metals. EP2292687B1 and US9321910B2 are formulation patents that define HNBR as a high-performance elastomer and specify hydrogenation degree, Mooney viscosity, and compounding systems for durable vulcanizates. US7795344B2 and US20180022951A1 are not direct HNBR synthesis patents, but they disclose hydrogenation of diene-containing nano-particles and the use of HNBR as a conductive-rubber component, respectively, showing how hydrogenated nitrile chemistry is integrated into advanced materials. Overall, the landscape favors solution-phase hydrogenation, noble-metal catalysis, careful control of residual unsaturation, and downstream stabilization/compounding to preserve ageing resistance and processability.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- EP2473281B1 and EP3827029A1 both target HNBR process optimization, but EP2473281B1 emphasizes simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation for molecular-weight reduction, whereas EP3827029A1 emphasizes conventional solution hydrogenation with controlled noble-metal ppm levels and catalyst removal concerns.
- US6307624B1 focuses on partial hydrogenation control using in situ Raman spectroscopy, while EP3827029A1 focuses on catalyst loading and ageing performance; both address process reproducibility, but by different control strategies.
- EP2292687B1 and US9321910B2 are downstream formulation patents: both define HNBR as a high-temperature elastomer, but EP2292687B1 gives a concrete hydrogenation example (138°C, 85 bar, 4 h) whereas US9321910B2 mainly specifies composition windows and microgel/peroxide curing.
- US20180022951A1 includes an upstream NBR emulsion polymerization example and then hydrogenation to HNBR for conductive films; unlike the core synthesis patents, its HNBR use case is functional materials rather than bulk elastomer production.
- US7795344B2 demonstrates hydrogenation of diene-containing nano-particle surface layers at 120°C and about 792 kPa H2, which is mechanistically related to selective hydrogenation but not to conventional NBR-to-HNBR manufacturing.
- Catalyst families differ across the synthesis patents: EP2473281B1 uses ruthenium/osmium metathesis complexes, US6307624B1 uses rhodium/phosphine or heterogeneous catalysts, and EP3827029A1 allows ruthenium, palladium, or rhodium compounds.
- Process temperatures span from 60-200°C in EP3827029A1, 135°C in US6307624B1, and 138°C in EP2292687B1, indicating that industrial HNBR hydrogenation commonly operates in the 100-150°C range.
- Residual unsaturation control is explicit in EP2473281B1 (RDB values down to 0.4%) and US6307624B1 (Raman-based endpoint control), while EP3827029A1 emphasizes ageing stability and low residual catalyst rather than direct RDB monitoring.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the patent set, the most actionable HNBR synthesis guidance comes from solution-phase hydrogenation patents that tightly control catalyst type, catalyst loading, temperature, pressure, and endpoint. EP3827029A1 provides the broadest industrial operating window for direct HNBR hydrogenation with ruthenium/palladium/rhodium catalysts, while US6307624B1 shows that in situ Raman monitoring can improve reproducibility of partial hydrogenation. EP2473281B1 adds a distinct route in which metathesis and hydrogenation are combined to lower molecular weight and narrow dispersity, which is valuable when lower-viscosity HNBR is desired. The evidence supports operating in the 60-150°C range, under elevated hydrogen pressure, with catalyst loadings in the low-ppm to low-phr regime depending on catalyst class, and with explicit control of residual double bonds and post-reaction catalyst removal. For downstream performance, the patents consistently indicate that high hydrogenation degree, controlled Mooney viscosity, and low residual metal content are important for ageing resistance and processability.
