# CARBOXYLATED NBR (XNBR) SYNTHESIS AND POST-FUNCTIONALIZATION PATENT REVIEW

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews patent evidence relevant to carboxylated nitrile rubber (XNBR), with emphasis on how carboxyl functionality is introduced into an NBR backbone and how the resulting material is subsequently processed. The single primary patent in the manifest, US20030109642A1, does not describe the initial emulsion copolymerization of XNBR; instead, it addresses a downstream transformation of carboxylated nitrile rubber, including hydrogenated carboxylated nitrile rubber (HXNBR), by simultaneous crosslinking and derivatization in the presence of coupling/condensing agents. The disclosed chemistry is centered on reacting the carboxyl-containing polymer with carbodiimides and related condensing agents to form crosslinked/derivatized networks while preserving the polymer backbone. The patent explicitly discusses polymers derived from conjugated dienes, unsaturated nitriles, and unsaturated carboxylic acids, and it highlights the challenge of maintaining carboxyl functionality during hydrogenation and subsequent processing.

The selected patent landscape therefore captures a key post-polymerization route for XNBR-derived materials rather than a direct synthesis route. The major process trend is the use of coupling/condensing agents, especially dicyclohexylcarbodiimide, to convert carboxyl groups into derived functionalities while simultaneously crosslinking the rubber. The evidence also shows that the polymer may be optionally hydrogenated, with selective hydrogenation of carbon-carbon double bonds emphasized to avoid reduction of nitrile and carboxyl groups. Formulation/process conditions disclosed in the examples include milling at 40°C, incorporation of 0.9 g or 2.4 g DCC into 24 g or 20 g HXNBR, and pressing at 150°C and 40,000 psi for 2 minutes in one example. Overall, the patent is technically relevant because it demonstrates a practical route for stabilizing and modifying XNBR/HXNBR after carboxyl incorporation, which is important for performance tuning in seals, hoses, belts, and other demanding rubber applications.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence shows that XNBR-related technology in the manifest is focused on post-functionalization rather than direct carboxylated NBR synthesis. US20030109642A1 demonstrates that carboxylated nitrile rubbers, including hydrogenated variants, can be simultaneously crosslinked and derivatized using coupling/condensing agents such as dicyclohexylcarbodiimide. The most actionable disclosed conditions are milling at 40°C, DCC addition in the range of 0.9 to 2.4 g for 20 to 24 g polymer in the examples, and hot pressing at 150°C and 40,000 psi for 2 minutes. For synthesis and processing of XNBR-derived materials, the evidence supports selective hydrogenation of the diene double bonds while preserving nitrile and carboxyl groups, followed by carbodiimide-based derivatization/crosslinking to tune final properties.
