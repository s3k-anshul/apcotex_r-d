# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND LOW-MOONEY PROCESS PATENTS: CATALYSTS, REACTOR MODES, AND DEGRADATION-CONTROL STRATEGIES

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes patent evidence on hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR) preparation, with emphasis on solution hydrogenation of NBR precursors and on process routes that lower molecular weight and Mooney viscosity while preserving elastomeric performance. Across the selected patents, the target material is consistently defined as a nitrile rubber derived from a conjugated diene and an alpha,beta-unsaturated nitrile, subsequently hydrogenated to reduce residual double bonds and improve heat, ozone, oil, and ageing resistance. The landscape splits into two major technical directions: direct hydrogenation of NBR to HNBR using noble-metal catalysts, and tandem or pre-degradation strategies that first reduce polymer molecular weight by metathesis before hydrogenation or, in some cases, combine both transformations in a single reactor concept.

The hydrogenation-focused patents disclose homogeneous catalyst systems based on ruthenium, rhodium, and palladium, often in monochlorobenzene solution, with hydrogen pressures ranging from low single-digit bar in spinning-disk operation to 1200 psi in batch high-pressure reactors and temperatures commonly between 130 and 138 C, or more broadly 60 to 200 C. Several patents emphasize catalyst loading control in the ppm or phr range and the importance of minimizing catalyst residues to avoid discoloration and ageing penalties. Other patents focus on reactor engineering, including spinning disk reactors, to reduce hydrogen pressure requirements and equipment cost.

A second cluster of patents addresses low-molecular-weight HNBR by metathesis-driven chain scission of NBR or by segment interchange between unsaturated polymers. These disclosures use Grubbs-type ruthenium catalysts, Hoveyda/Arlt variants, Wilkinson-type hydrogenation catalysts, and salt additives such as LiBr or CaCl2 to tune metathesis activity. Reported outcomes include substantial reductions in Mw, narrower polydispersity, and lower Mooney viscosity, with some examples achieving Mooney values near 3 to 40 and Mw reductions from the 200,000-300,000 g/mol range to roughly 50,000-190,000 g/mol. Overall, the evidence supports HNBR synthesis routes that combine controlled unsaturation reduction, catalyst selection, and process intensification to balance conversion, molecular weight, and final rubber performance.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Direct hydrogenation patents (EP3827029A1, US9079979B2, US7666950B2) focus on Ru/Rh/Pd catalyst systems, with EP3827029A1 emphasizing ppm-level noble-metal control and ageing stability, US9079979B2 emphasizing simultaneous hydrogenation/metathesis, and US7666950B2 emphasizing spinning-disk reactor intensification.
- Low-Mw HNBR patents (US7919563B2, EP2473281B1, US7772328B2) all use metathesis-assisted chain scission before or during hydrogenation; US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 use Grubbs/Wilkinson-type systems with co-olefins, while EP2473281B1 compares Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson catalysts.
- Reaction media are consistently aromatic chlorinated solvents, especially monochlorobenzene/chlorobenzene, across the hydrogenation and metathesis patents; solution concentrations commonly range from 4% to 15% solids or 6% to 15% cement.
- Hydrogenation conditions vary widely: EP3827029A1 claims 60 to 200 C and 700000 to 15000000 Pa, US9079979B2 uses 130 to 138 C and 85 bar, and US7666950B2 demonstrates 3.5 to 10 bar in a spinning disk reactor with 138 C hydrogenation.
- Catalyst loadings are generally low in direct hydrogenation patents, often in phr or ppm ranges, while metathesis/degradation patents use 0.041 to 0.25 phr catalyst or 8000 to 13000 ppm catalyst/co-catalyst systems.
- Co-olefin use is a recurring lever in the metathesis-based patents: ethylene and 1-hexene are the most common, with 1-hexene often used at 2 phr or 150 g scale to promote chain scission.
- Salt additives are a distinct trend in the degradation patents US8957161B2 and US7737233B2, where LiBr, LiCl, LiI, and CaCl2 are used to modulate metathesis activity and final Mw; this strategy is absent from the direct hydrogenation patents.
- Property outcomes differ by route: direct hydrogenation patents mainly target ageing stability and catalyst residue control, whereas metathesis-assisted patents explicitly report Mw reductions to roughly 54000 to 195000 g/mol and Mooney viscosity reductions down to 3 to 41.
- US8557921B2 is the most structurally different disclosure, using HNBR as a reagent in meta-block copolymer formation rather than as the final product; it nevertheless confirms HNBR compatibility with metathesis chemistry.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The evidence supports two practical HNBR synthesis paradigms: direct solution hydrogenation of NBR with noble-metal catalysts, and metathesis-assisted molecular-weight reduction followed by hydrogenation. For direct HNBR manufacture, the strongest evidence favors Ru/Rh/Pd catalyst systems in chlorinated aromatic solvents, with hydrogenation at roughly 60 to 200 C and elevated pressure, while careful control of catalyst residue is important for ageing performance. For low-Mooney HNBR, the most effective route is metathesis chain scission of NBR using Grubbs-type ruthenium catalysts, optionally with ethylene or 1-hexene co-olefins, followed by hydrogenation; salt additives such as CaCl2 or LiCl can further accelerate degradation and lower final Mw. Based on the disclosed data, a robust synthesis strategy would combine a low-residue hydrogenation catalyst, monochlorobenzene solvent, controlled solids loading around 4% to 15%, and either a spinning-disk or high-pressure solution reactor depending on throughput and pressure constraints. For lowest viscosity products, the metathesis-first route with optimized catalyst loading and salt assistance appears most effective.
