# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews the provided patent evidence for low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) synthesis and processing, with emphasis on how acrylonitrile content is controlled to balance low-temperature flexibility and oil resistance. The single patent in the manifest, EP2145681B1, is not a direct low-ACN copolymerization disclosure; instead, it addresses post-polymerization molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber by metathesis using ruthenium- or osmium-based catalyst systems combined with Lewis-acid/acid adducts. The patent defines NBR broadly as copolymers or terpolymers of at least one α,β-unsaturated nitrile and at least one conjugated diene, and it is relevant because lower molecular weight NBR/HNBR grades are often sought for improved processability in low-ACN elastomer applications. The disclosed process is aimed at reducing Mooney viscosity and number-average molecular weight of nitrile rubber feedstocks prior to or in conjunction with hydrogenation, rather than changing acrylonitrile feed ratio during polymerization. The evidence highlights the use of metathesis catalysts based on ruthenium or osmium, with optional compounds of formula (Z) such as BF3, BF2Cl, BFCl2, BF2Br, BFBr2, BF2(OC2H5), BF(OC2H5)2, BF2(CH3), or BF(CH3)2, combined with donor solvents or acids including water, diethyl ether, ethylamine, THF, n-propanol, formic acid, acetic acid, trifluoroacetic acid, trichloroacetic acid, sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, trifluoromethanesulphonic acid, or toluenesulphonic acid. The broader technical context notes that commercially available HNBR often has Mooney viscosity ML 1+4 at 100°C of 55 to 120 and Mn of about 200,000 to 700,000, while lower-viscosity materials below ML 55 or Mn below 200,000 are desirable for processing. Overall, the patent landscape here supports downstream molecular-weight tailoring of nitrile rubbers, but does not provide direct low-ACN copolymerization recipes.
