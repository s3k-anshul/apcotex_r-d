# LOW STYRENE SBR PATENT LANDSCAPE: SOLUTION AND EMULSION ROUTES, FEED CONTROL, AND FUNCTIONALIZED COPOLYMER VARIANTS

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes four primary patents centered on styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and styrene-butadiene copolymer manufacture, with emphasis on low-styrene or reduced-styrene embodiments relevant to low styrene SBR. The selected patents span solution polymerization, continuous two-zone feed control, barium-based catalyst systems, and post-polymerization functionalization of solution SBR. Across the set, the target material is consistently a butadiene-rich elastomeric copolymer, but the disclosed styrene contents vary by process and embodiment, ranging from about 10 wt% in a targeted low-vinyl SSBR example to 20-30 wt% in a tapered low-molecular-weight copolymer and 10/90 styrene/butadiene feed compositions that yield random SBR. The landscape shows that styrene reduction is not always the sole objective; instead, patents often optimize microstructure, randomness, molecular weight, and terminal functionality while maintaining tire-relevant properties such as storage modulus, wear resistance, and low vinyl content.

The major polymerization approaches are solution-based. US6372863B1 teaches continuous two-reactor solution polymerization with split-feed control to suppress tapered block formation and achieve random SBR with low vinyl content. US6103842A discloses solution polymerization in hydrocarbon solvent using a barium alkoxide/lithium alkoxide/organolithium catalyst system to produce highly random SBR with low vinyl and high trans character. US11591428B2 focuses on low molecular weight tapered styrene-butadiene copolymers prepared in hexane with organolithium initiation and sodium dodecylbenzenesulfonate, then isolated by coagulation and drying. US10174133B2 describes modified SSBR prepared in hexane with n-butyllithium, polar additive, terminal butadiene capping, and silane-containing modifier, followed by ethanol termination and water-assisted solvent removal. Overall, the patents show a strong process-control trend: monomer feed ratio, staged addition, catalyst/modifier selection, and downstream isolation are used to tune styrene distribution and performance rather than merely lowering styrene content alone.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US6372863B1 and US6103842A both disclose solution SBR in hydrocarbon solvent, but US6372863B1 emphasizes split-feed continuous reactor control to suppress tapered block formation, whereas US6103842A emphasizes a barium alkoxide/lithium alkoxide/organolithium catalyst system to drive randomization and high trans character.
- US11591428B2 also uses solution polymerization, but its focus is low molecular weight tapered styrene-butadiene copolymer with styrene content around 25-27 wt%, higher than the 10 wt% example in US6103842A and the 18 wt% target in US6372863B1.
- US10174133B2 differs from the other three by adding a post-polymerization modification step with a silane-containing modifier and by using ethanol termination plus water-assisted solvent removal; the other patents primarily focus on polymerization recipe and reactor control.
- Styrene content spans from 10 wt%/10 percent styrene in US6103842A to 18 wt% in US6372863B1 and 25.7-26.6% in US11591428B2; US10174133B2 does not clearly disclose the final styrene content of the target polymer in the extracted evidence.
- All four patents use organic solvent-based solution processes rather than aqueous emulsion polymerization, but only US6372863B1 and US10174133B2 explicitly disclose downstream water-related steps in the extracted evidence.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the selected primary patents, low-styrene SBR is approached mainly through solution polymerization with careful control of monomer feed, catalyst system, and reactor staging rather than through a dedicated low-styrene claim alone. The most directly low-styrene disclosure is US6103842A, which reports a 10/90 styrene/butadiene solution SBR and a broader series up to 20/80 and beyond. US6372863B1 provides a continuous two-reactor split-feed process with a targeted 18 wt% styrene SSBR and low-vinyl/random microstructure control. US11591428B2 contributes a tapered low-molecular-weight copolymer platform with styrene around 25-27 wt% and performance benefits in rubber compounds. US10174133B2 adds a functionalized SSBR route with solution polymerization, terminal capping, and water-assisted isolation. Together, the patents show that low-styrene SBR development is tightly linked to solution-process architecture, monomer feed management, and downstream handling.
