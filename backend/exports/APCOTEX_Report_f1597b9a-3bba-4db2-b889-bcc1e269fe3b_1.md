# HYDROGENATED NBR (HNBR) SYNTHESIS AND PROCESS PATENTS LANDSCAPE

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report synthesizes seven primary patents relevant to hydrogenated nitrile rubber (HNBR), focusing on routes that either directly hydrogenate NBR or combine hydrogenation with molecular-weight reduction to improve processability. The target material is hydrogenated nitrile rubber derived from nitrile rubber precursors containing conjugated diene and α,β-unsaturated nitrile units, with emphasis on low residual unsaturation, improved heat/oil/ozone resistance, and controlled molecular weight or Mooney viscosity. Across the patent set, two major process families emerge. First, several patents (US7919563B2, US7772328B2, EP2473281B1, US9150669B2) disclose metathesis-based depolymerization of NBR/HNBR precursors using ruthenium- or osmium-based catalysts, often with co-olefins such as ethylene or 1-hexene, to reduce molecular weight and narrow polydispersity. EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 are especially notable for simultaneous metathesis and hydrogenation in the presence of hydrogen, enabling one-step formation of low-MW HNBR with low residual double bond content. Second, US10759885B2 and US10717906B2 are not direct HNBR synthesis patents but are relevant to hydrogenated elastomer technology: US10759885B2 discloses hydrogenation of branched conjugated diene copolymers (e.g., myrcene/farnesene/styrene systems) to improve tire performance, while US10717906B2 uses partially hydrogenated hydrocarbon resins in adhesive formulations, illustrating broader formulation trends around hydrogenated unsaturation for stability and performance. US7846995B2 addresses downstream purification by removing ruthenium residues from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using functionalized ion-exchange resins, an important process-enabling step for catalyst-sensitive HNBR production. Overall, the landscape shows a progression from conventional two-step metathesis-then-hydrogenation toward single-step hydrogenation/metathesis, with strong attention to catalyst choice, co-olefin selection, residual double bond control, and post-reaction metal removal.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US7919563B2 and US7772328B2 both use metathesis-based molecular-weight reduction of nitrile rubber with ruthenium/osmium catalysts, optional co-olefins, and elevated temperature; both emphasize low MW and low Mooney HNBR, but US7772328B2 explicitly claims Mooney <10 and Mw 40,000 to 220,000 in dependent claims.
- EP2473281B1 and US9150669B2 are the key single-step HNBR patents: both run nitrile rubber in hydrogen with Ru/Os catalysts and optional co-olefin, aiming for simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation rather than sequential processing.
- US9150669B2 provides the most explicit comparative catalyst data, including Hoveyda, Arlt, and Wilkinson-type systems, 65.6 ppm metal content, and RDB decay over 0 to 240 min; EP2473281B1 is more claim- and concept-focused in the extracted evidence.
- The metathesis-only patents (US7919563B2, US7772328B2) use co-olefins such as ethylene and 1-hexene at 80°C, with catalyst loadings from 0.05 to 0.25 phr and ethylene pressure of 6.9×10^5 Pa or 500 psi, whereas the simultaneous hydrogenation patents emphasize hydrogen atmosphere and catalyst identity over detailed pressure/time windows in the extracted text.
- US7846995B2 is a downstream purification patent rather than a synthesis patent; it complements the synthesis patents by addressing Ru residue removal from optionally hydrogenated nitrile rubber using ion-exchange resins.
- US10759885B2 and US10717906B2 are not NBR patents, but they show broader hydrogenation trends in elastomers and resins: hydrogenation is used to improve processability, aging resistance, and ozone stability in related polymer systems.
- Across the HNBR-focused patents, the dominant process trend is moving from two-step metathesis then hydrogenation toward one-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation, with catalyst selection and residue control becoming central to manufacturability.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The patent evidence supports two practical synthesis directions for hydrogenated NBR: controlled metathesis of nitrile rubber to lower molecular weight, and single-step simultaneous metathesis/hydrogenation to generate low-RDB HNBR more efficiently. The strongest HNBR-specific evidence favors Ru/Os catalyst systems, optional co-olefins, and hydrogen atmosphere, with target product windows around Mw 20,000 to 250,000 and Mooney viscosity from about 1 to 50. For process development, the most evidence-backed parameters are moderate temperatures near 80°C, catalyst loadings in the 0.05 to 0.25 phr range for metathesis-based depolymerization, and careful control of hydrogenation catalyst choice to avoid excessive nitrile reduction. Post-reaction purification with functionalized ion-exchange resins is also important to reduce residual ruthenium. Overall, the best-supported route for HNBR synthesis is a Ru-catalyzed one-step hydrogenation/metathesis process, followed by metal-removal cleanup, when the goal is low residual unsaturation, lower viscosity, and improved processability.
