# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews two primary patents relevant to nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR) with emphasis on low-acrylonitrile embodiments and NBR-centered processing or formulation. The selected patents span two distinct technical directions: one patent is centered on an electrophotographic electro-conductive member that explicitly uses NBR as one of the diene-based rubbers in a matrix/domain architecture, while the other patent focuses on low molecular weight nitrile rubber polymers and their use in polymer composites, including a route that starts from nitrile polymer feedstock and proceeds through metathesis and hydrogenation. Across the set, the disclosed NBR-related material definitions are broader than a dedicated low-ACN synthesis patent, but both documents provide useful compositional and processing context for NBR materials. The first patent discloses NBR in a mixed-rubber electro-conductive layer, with examples showing NBR grades of different nitrile contents, including 15%, 18.0%, 27.5%, 35%, 40.0%, and 41.5% in the materials section, and comparative examples using NBR as either domain or matrix rubber. The second patent discloses a statistical butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer substrate with 34 mol% acrylonitrile and describes solvent-based metathesis in monochlorobenzene followed by hydrogenation to produce lower-Mooney nitrile rubber feedstocks for composites. Major process trends include solution-phase chemistry in organic solvent for molecular-weight reduction/hydrogenation, and compounding/open-mill processing for final articles. Major formulation trends include use of carbon black, zinc oxide, sulfur or peroxide curatives, and auxiliary additives to tune viscoelasticity, conductivity, and processability. Overall, the landscape indicates that low-acrylonitrile NBR is represented more through disclosed NBR grades and downstream processing/composite use than through a dedicated low-ACN polymerization recipe in the extracted evidence.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- US11347156B2 is a formulation/application patent using commercial NBR grades, with explicitly listed nitrile contents spanning 15% to 41.5%; the lowest disclosed grade is 15% nitrile content (NBR (1)).
- US7262244B2 is a synthesis/process patent for nitrile rubber feedstock and discloses a starting butadiene-acrylonitrile copolymer with 34 mol% acrylonitrile, which is substantially higher than the lowest NBR grades listed in US11347156B2.
- US11347156B2 is solid-state compounding/molding of rubber compositions, whereas US7262244B2 is an organic-solvent solution process using monochlorobenzene followed by hydrogenation.
- US7262244B2 discloses explicit catalyst and reaction conditions for metathesis/hydrogenation (0.25 phr Grubb's catalyst, 0.075 phr Wilkinson's catalyst, 500 psi ethylene, 1200 psi hydrogen), while US11347156B2 focuses on rubber pairing, SP-value difference, and conductive particle loading rather than polymerization chemistry.
- Both patents use NBR as a functional material platform, but only US7262244B2 provides a direct synthetic route to modified nitrile rubber feedstock; US11347156B2 provides downstream performance context for NBR-containing materials.

## 4. CONCLUSION
Across the two selected primary patents, low-acrylonitrile NBR is represented more by disclosed commercial grades and downstream use than by a dedicated low-ACN polymerization recipe. US11347156B2 explicitly lists NBR grades down to 15% nitrile content and uses NBR in a matrix/domain electro-conductive member, while US7262244B2 discloses a solvent-based metathesis/hydrogenation route starting from a 34 mol% acrylonitrile butadiene copolymer to produce low-Mooney nitrile rubber feedstocks for composites. Together, they show both material selection and processing routes relevant to NBR performance, with the clearest low-ACN evidence coming from the NBR grade list in US11347156B2.
