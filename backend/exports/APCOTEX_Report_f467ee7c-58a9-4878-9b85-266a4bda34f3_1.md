# POLYMERIZATION / SYNTHESIS PATENT RESEARCH REPORT
## Low Acrylonitrile NBR

### 1. Abstract
The patent landscape for Low Acrylonitrile NBR (nitrile butadiene rubber) reveals a diverse range of synthesis and polymerization methods. The extracted data from multiple patents demonstrates various approaches to producing Low Acrylonitrile NBR, including emulsion polymerization, graft copolymerization, and hydrogenation. Recurring variables such as acrylonitrile content, styrene content, and reaction conditions (e.g., temperature, pressure, and retention time) are critical in determining the properties of the resulting polymer. Trends in the data suggest a focus on developing Low Acrylonitrile NBR with improved properties, such as increased thermal stability, chemical resistance, and processability. The patents also highlight the use of different monomers, initiators, and additives to achieve these goals.

### 2. Research Scope & Selection Method
The patents included in this report were selected based on their relevance to the synthesis and polymerization of Low Acrylonitrile NBR. Only patents that demonstrated direct synthesis or polymerization of the target chemistry were included. The extraction data was carefully reviewed to ensure that only relevant parameters were included in the analysis.

### 4. Cross-Patent Comparison
Comparing the ranges of key parameters across the different patents reveals:
* Acrylonitrile content: 10-24 wt % (US9085686B2)
* Styrene content: 5-76 wt % (US9085686B2, US9562156B2)
* Reaction temperature: 2 minute (US9562156B2)
* Reaction pressure: 6 bar (US9562156B2)
* Reaction yield: 60-90 % (US9085686B2, US9562156B2)

### 5. Key Technical Trends
The analyzed patents demonstrate a focus on developing Low Acrylonitrile NBR with improved properties, such as increased thermal stability, chemical resistance, and processability. The use of different monomers, initiators, and additives is a common trend across the patents. Additionally, the development of bio-based ABS polymer compositions and the use of nanomaterials in PC/ABS polymers are notable trends.
