# LOW ACRYLONITRILE NBR PATENT LANDSCAPE REPORT

## 1. ABSTRACT
This report reviews the provided patent set for evidence relevant to low acrylonitrile nitrile butadiene rubber (NBR). The target is a compositionally modified NBR grade with reduced acrylonitrile content, typically achieved by controlling butadiene/acrylonitrile copolymerization feed ratio, polymerization conditions, and downstream formulation choices. In the supplied manifest, however, the two primary patents do not disclose direct low-ACN NBR synthesis routes. Instead, they show adjacent uses of NBR-derived materials in elastomeric downhole seals and in fiber-cord adhesion systems, both of which rely on NBR chemistry but are not focused on monomer incorporation control.

US10443339B2 discloses elastomeric components for bore tools and packers reinforced with carbon-based nanoplatelets, with explicit emphasis on high-temperature/high-pressure service and chemical resistance. The patent mentions NBR among suitable elastomers in the broader background, but its inventive contribution is reinforcement of elastomeric matrices rather than NBR polymerization or acrylonitrile-content tuning. US11131058B2 discloses a cord manufacturing method using a first treatment agent containing a resorcin-formaldehyde condensate, a carboxy-modified latex, and a polycarbodiimide curing agent; the latex may be a carboxy-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer latex. This patent is relevant as a formulation/process disclosure involving NBR latex in adhesion treatment, but again not as a synthesis patent for low-ACN NBR.

Overall, the landscape in the provided evidence is formulation- and application-oriented rather than monomer-feed-oriented. The major process trends are: use of NBR as a component in reinforced elastomeric systems; use of carboxy-modified NBR latex in aqueous adhesive treatments; and emphasis on durability, flexibility, and environmental resistance. No patent in the manifest explicitly discloses a low-acrylonitrile NBR copolymerization recipe, acrylonitrile wt% target, or monomer feed ratio for producing low-ACN NBR.

## 3. CROSS-PATENT COMPARISON & SYNTHESIS TRENDS
- Both patents are application/formulation patents rather than direct low-ACN NBR synthesis patents.
- US10443339B2 focuses on reinforced elastomeric downhole components using carbon-based nanoplatelets; US11131058B2 focuses on aqueous cord-treatment formulations using carboxy-modified NBR latex.
- US10443339B2 discloses a nanoplatelet loading below approximately 10 wt% and service temperatures up to 205-260 degrees C. in HPHT contexts; US11131058B2 discloses treatment-agent composition ranges such as 15-20 wt% rubber composition and 1-10 parts by mass curing agent per 100 parts rubber component.
- US11131058B2 is the only patent in the set that explicitly names acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer latex, but it does not specify acrylonitrile content or monomer feed ratio.
- Neither patent provides a copolymerization recipe, initiator system, emulsifier system, chain-transfer agent, or acrylonitrile wt% target for producing low-acrylonitrile NBR.

## 4. CONCLUSION
The supplied patent set does not contain a direct disclosure of low-acrylonitrile NBR synthesis. The closest evidence is US11131058B2, which uses carboxy-modified acrylonitrile-butadiene copolymer latex in an aqueous RFL-type treatment system, and US10443339B2, which references NBR as a service elastomer in reinforced downhole components. For low-ACN NBR synthesis, the evidence base here is insufficient to recommend a specific polymerization recipe; however, the broader patent context supports aqueous emulsion-based NBR processing and formulation control. Based on the target definition, the most relevant synthesis parameters to seek in additional patents would be butadiene/acrylonitrile feed ratio, acrylonitrile content below about 20 wt% (often 10-18 wt%), emulsion polymerization conditions, and molecular-weight control via chain-transfer management.
