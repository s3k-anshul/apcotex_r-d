# Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber
## SCIENTIFIC PATENT RESEARCH REPORT

### Abstract
This report synthesizes technical data extracted from multiple patents pertaining to the synthesis and properties of Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR). The analyzed patents primarily focus on emulsion and solution polymerization techniques, followed by crucial post-polymerization modifications such as hydrogenation. A recurring theme across the relevant patents is the precise control over monomer composition, particularly maintaining low acrylonitrile (ACN) content, typically ranging from 21 to 38 wt%, alongside butadiene and other functional monomers like mono-n-butyl maleate and n-butyl acrylate.

Reaction conditions are meticulously detailed, with polymerization temperatures varying from 5°C (initiation) to 100°C, and post-treatment heating steps reaching up to 85°C. Hydrogenation reactions, a critical step for enhancing NBR's thermal and oxidative stability, are typically conducted between 50°C and 150°C, often under elevated pressures ranging from 3 MPa to 150 bar. Reaction times are highly variable, from minutes for degassing and kneading to several hours (6-40 hours for polymerization, 2-10 hours for hydrogenation). pH control is highlighted as essential, especially for carboxy group-containing NBR, where pH is adjusted to 8.5-12 for heating and then 5-8 for hydrogenation.

Various catalytic systems and modifiers are employed to achieve desired polymer characteristics. These include azo and peroxidic initiators, molecular weight modifiers like t-dodecylmercaptan, and advanced techniques such as Reversible Addition-Fragmentation Chain Transfer (RAFT) polymerization using regulators like dibenzoyl trithiocarbonate and cumylphenyl dithioacetate to control molecular weight and polydispersity. Palladium-based catalysts are consistently used for hydrogenation. Polymer properties such as conversion rates (ranging from 9.2% to 95%), number average molecular weight (Mn from 6,600 to 66,000 g/mol), and polydispersity index (PDI from 1.3 to 2.3 for NBR) are closely monitored. Glass transition temperatures (Tg) for NBR typically fall within -28.5°C to -19.6°C. The report underscores the importance of achieving high conversion while maintaining low gel content and narrow particle size distribution, often facilitated by semi-continuous monomer addition and the use of cyclodextrins.

### Cross-Patent Synthesis Trends

The analysis of patents related to Low Acrylonitrile Nitrile Butadiene Rubber (NBR) reveals several key trends in monomer composition, polymerization methods, reaction conditions, and polymer properties. While some extracted data pertains to other polymer systems (Polylactic Acid, Ethylene Multi-block Copolymers), the core insights for NBR are drawn from EP2478021, EP3744739, and general polymerization principles from EP1247820.

**1. Monomer Composition:**
A consistent feature of "Low Acrylonitrile" NBR is the relatively low content of acrylonitrile (ACN) units. Patents EP2478021 and EP3744739 show ACN content ranging from 21 wt% to 38 wt% in the final polymer. Butadiene (1,3-butadiene) is the primary comonomer, typically present in higher proportions (e.g., 39 wt% in EP3744739). Some NBR formulations incorporate additional monomers to introduce specific functionalities, such as carboxy groups. Examples include mono-n-butyl maleate (5-6 parts by weight, or 5 wt% in the final polymer) and n-butyl acrylate (31-35 parts by weight, or 35 wt% in the final polymer) as seen in EP3744739. The total amount of different copolymerizable monomers is generally kept below 50 wt%, preferably below 10 wt% (EP3744739).

**2. Polymerization Methods:**
Both emulsion and solution polymerization techniques are described for NBR synthesis.
- **Emulsion Polymerization:** EP3744739 details emulsion polymerization in a dispersive medium, emphasizing the preparation of a latex. This method often involves emulsifiers (0.1-10 parts by weight relative to 100 parts of monomer mixture) and water (80-500 parts by weight relative to 100 parts of monomer mixture).
- **Solution Polymerization:** EP2478021 describes NBR production in organic solvents like dimethylacetamide.
- **Semi-continuous Addition:** EP1247820, while demonstrating with MMA and styrene, highlights the benefit of semi-continuous monomer addition to achieve narrow particle size distribution and reduce coagulum content (<1.5%). This principle is relevant for controlling morphology in NBR emulsion polymerization.

**3. Reaction Conditions:**
- **Temperature:** Polymerization temperatures for NBR vary, with examples at 5°C (initiation, EP3744739), 13°C (EP2478021), and 100°C (EP2478021). Post-polymerization heating steps for carboxy-NBR latex are performed at 50-85°C (EP3744739). Hydrogenation reactions are typically conducted at 50-150°C (EP2478021, EP3744739).
- **Time:** Polymerization times range from 6 hours (EP2478021, EP3744739) to 40 hours (EP2478021), with some examples aiming for high conversion (50%) in less than 10 hours. Hydrogenation reaction times are typically 2-10 hours (EP2478021, EP3744739). Other steps like pH adjustment stirring (1 hour), heating under reduced pressure (2-3 hours), and palladium removal stirring (3 hours) are also specified.
- **Pressure:** Butadiene addition can occur under 3 bar pressure (EP2478021). Hydrogenation reactions require elevated pressures, ranging from 3 MPa (EP3744739) to 50-150 bar (EP2478021). Reduced pressure conditions (0.03-0.08 MPa below atmospheric) are used for heating steps (EP3744739).
- **pH Control:** For carboxy group-containing NBR, precise pH adjustment is crucial. The latex is heated at a pH of 8.5-12, and subsequently, the pH is adjusted to 5-8 for the hydrogenation reaction (EP3744739).

**4. Catalysis and Regulation:**
- **Initiators:** Peroxidic and azo initiators are commonly used (EP2478021). Cumene hydroperoxide is mentioned as a polymerization initiator (EP3744739). The amount of polymerization initiator is typically 0.01-2 parts by weight relative to 100 parts of the monomer mixture (EP3744739).
- **Molecular Weight Modifiers/Regulators:** t-dodecylmercaptan is used as a molecular weight modifier (0.6-0.75 parts by weight, EP3744739). RAFT (Reversible Addition-Fragmentation Chain Transfer) regulators like dibenzoyl trithiocarbonate (DiBenT) and cumylphenyl dithioacetate (CPDA) are employed to achieve controlled molecular weights and narrow polydispersities (EP2478021).
- **Hydrogenation Catalysts:** Palladium catalysts (e.g., 1 wt% palladium acetate acetone solution) are consistently used for the hydrogenation of NBR (EP3744739).

**5. Solvents and Dispersive Media:**
Water is a primary dispersive medium for emulsion polymerization (EP3744739, EP1247820). Organic solvents include dimethylacetamide (DMAc) for solution polymerization (EP2478021), toluene or monochlorobenzene for hydrogenation (EP2478021), and methyl ethyl ketone/isopropyl alcohol mixtures for analytical purposes (EP3744739).

**6. Polymer Properties:**
- **Conversion:** Polymerization conversion ratios for NBR range from 9.2% to 56% in some examples (EP2478021), with preferred ranges for emulsion polymerization being 60-95%, and more specifically 65-90% (EP3744739).
- **Molecular Weight (Mn) and Polydispersity Index (PDI):** Mn values for NBR examples range from 6,600 to 66,000 g/mol, with corresponding PDI values from 1.3 to 2.3 (EP2478021). The use of RAFT regulators is shown to yield low PDI values (1.3-1.9). For MMA/Styrene systems, Mn ranges from 31,000 to 62,000 g/mol, and PDI from 1.3 to 6.46 (EP1247820).
- **Glass Transition Temperature (Tg):** NBR polymers exhibit low Tg values, typically between -28.5°C and -19.6°C (EP2478021).
- **Gel Content:** Low gel content is a desirable property, with values typically below 5.0% (EP2478021, EP1247820).
- **Mooney Viscosity:** Carboxy group-containing hydrogenated NBR can have a polymer Mooney viscosity (ML1+4, 100°C) of 15 to 100 (EP3744739).
- **Particle Size Distribution (PSD):** For polymer particles, narrow PSD is achieved with semi-continuous monomer addition and the presence of cyclodextrin (RAMEB), leading to average particle sizes (APS) in the range of 267-588 nm (EP1247820).

**7. Post-Polymerization Treatments:**
- **Heating:** NBR latex can undergo a heating step (50-85°C for 0.5-10 hours) under controlled pH (8.5-12) and sometimes reduced pressure (EP3744739).
- **Hydrogenation:** This is a crucial step to improve NBR properties, performed in the presence of a hydrogenation catalyst (e.g., palladium) at specific temperatures (50-150°C) and pressures (3 MPa to 150 bar), with pH adjusted to 5-8 (EP2478021, EP3744739).
- **Catalyst Removal:** After hydrogenation, complexing agents like dimethylglyoxime are used to precipitate and remove palladium catalysts (EP3744739).
- **Drying:** Polymers are typically dried in vacuum at moderate temperatures (e.g., 60°C for 12 hours) (EP3744739).

**8. Additives and Formulation Components:**
Various additives are mentioned for NBR compositions:
- **Crosslinking Agents:** Polyamine crosslinkers (0.2-20 parts by weight) and general crosslinkers (1-20 phr) are used (EP2478021, EP3744739).
- **Accelerators:** Crosslinking accelerators (0.5-10 parts by weight) are employed (EP2478021).
- **Filler Activators:** Used in amounts of 0-10 phr (EP2478021).
- **Antioxidants:** Mentioned as components in some formulations (EP2478021).
